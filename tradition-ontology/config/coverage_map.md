A. The Vedic family
A1. The Vedic hymns, ritual and ancillary sciences

* Ṛgveda: the ten maṇḍalas, with special attention to:
   * the philosophical hymns: the riddle hymn 1.164, the creation hymn (Nāsadīya) 10.129, Puruṣa Sūkta 10.90, Hiraṇyagarbha 10.121, the hymn of Speech (Vāk) 10.125, the long-haired sage (Keśin) 10.136
   * the hymns to Rudra, Soma, Agni and Varuṇa, and the concepts ṛta and satya
* Sāmaveda as the root of chant and sound theory.
* Yajurveda, both Black and White, including the Śatarudrīya (Śrī Rudram).
* Atharvaveda: the healing hymns, the Vrātya book, the Skambha hymns, the hymn to prāṇa.
* The Brāhmaṇas (Śatapatha, Aitareya, Taittirīya): ritual theory, prāṇa, the fire altar.
* The Āraṇyakas: the internalized sacrifice, including the inner fire offering (prāṇāgnihotra).
* The Vedāṅgas: phonetics (śikṣā), meter (chandas), grammar (Pāṇini), etymology (Yāska's Nirukta), ritual (kalpa), timekeeping (jyotiṣa).
* Core concepts: ṛta, satya, yajña, tapas, Brahman in its early sense, prāṇa, vāk, soma, and the internalization of ritual.

A2. The Upanishads

* The principal thirteen: Īśa, Kena, Kaṭha, Praśna, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya, Taittirīya, Aitareya, Chāndogya, Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Śvetāśvatara, Kauṣītaki, Maitrī.
* Their key teachings to capture explicitly:
   * the four great sayings (mahāvākyas), each with its source
   * "not this, not this" (neti neti)
   * the five sheaths (Taittirīya)
   * the three states and the fourth (Māṇḍūkya)
   * Yama and Naciketas, and the chariot image (Kaṭha)
   * the two birds on one tree (Muṇḍaka)
   * the dialogues: Yājñavalkya with Maitreyī and with Janaka; Uddālaka with Śvetaketu; Prajāpati with Indra and Virocana; Satyakāma Jābāla
   * the contest of prāṇa with the senses
   * the doctrine of the five fires, and the paths of the ancestors and of the gods
   * the sixfold yoga of the Maitrī
   * the yoga chapter of the Śvetāśvatara, and its Rudra-Śiva
* The Muktikā canon of 108, in groups:
   * The twenty Yoga Upanishads: Advayatāraka, Amṛtabindu, Amṛtanāda, Brahmavidyā, Darśana, Dhyānabindu, Haṃsa, Kṣurikā, Mahāvākya, Nādabindu, Pāśupatabrahma, Śāṇḍilya, Tejobindu, Triśikhibrāhmaṇa, Maṇḍalabrāhmaṇa, Varāha, Yogacūḍāmaṇi, Yogakuṇḍalī, Yogaśikhā, Yogatattva
   * The Sannyāsa Upanishads (including Jābāla, Paramahaṃsa, Āruṇi, Nāradaparivrājaka, Turīyātīta, Avadhūta)
   * The Śaiva, Śākta and Vaiṣṇava Upanishads
   * The general Vedānta Upanishads

A3. The Gītās and epic teachings

* Bhagavad Gītā, all eighteen chapters, including:
   * action, knowledge, devotion and meditation
   * the person of steady wisdom (sthitaprajña)
   * the field and its knower (ch. 13)
   * the three guṇas (ch. 14)
   * the three kinds of faith, food, austerity and giving (ch. 17)
   * the universal form (ch. 11)
* The Mahābhārata's teaching sections: Mokṣadharma (early Sāṃkhya and Yoga), Anugītā, Sanatsujātīya, Vidura's counsel, the Yakṣa's questions.
* Bhāgavata Purāṇa: the Uddhava Gītā (with Dattātreya's twenty-four teachers) and the Kapila Gītā.
* The other Gītās: Aṣṭāvakra, Avadhūta, Ṛbhu, Īśvara (Kūrma Purāṇa), Devī (Devī Bhāgavata), Śiva, Gaṇeśa, Rāma (Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa), Guru (Skanda Purāṇa).
* Yoga Vāsiṣṭha, in its full and Laghu versions:
   * its six books
   * the seven stages of knowledge (bhūmikās)
   * its teaching stories (Līlā, Karkaṭī, Bhuśuṇḍa, Cūḍālā)
* Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa.

A4. The Purāṇas and Āgamas (for their yoga, devotion, death and cosmology sections)

* Purāṇas: Bhāgavata, Viṣṇu, Śiva, Liṅga, Skanda, Kūrma, Mārkaṇḍeya (with the Devī Māhātmya), Devī Bhāgavata, Brahmāṇḍa (with the Lalitopākhyāna), Padma, Nārada, Agni, Garuḍa (its section on death and the afterlife).
* Āgamas and tantras:
   * the twenty-eight Śaiva Siddhānta Āgamas and their secondary texts
   * the sixty-four Bhairava tantras
   * the Vaiṣṇava Pāñcarātra Saṃhitās (Ahirbudhnya, Jayākhya, Lakṣmī Tantra) and the Vaikhānasa texts
   * the Śākta tantras

A5. The six schools (darśanas)

* Sāṃkhya.
   * Texts: Kapila (legendary), Āsuri, Pañcaśikha; Īśvarakṛṣṇa's Sāṃkhya Kārikā with Gauḍapāda's commentary, the Māṭhara Vṛtti, the Yuktidīpikā and Vācaspati's Tattvakaumudī; the Sāṃkhya Sūtra with Vijñānabhikṣu.
   * Concepts: the twenty-five principles, listed in full; pre-existence of the effect (satkāryavāda); the three guṇas; the three kinds of suffering (from oneself, from other beings, from the cosmic order); liberation by discriminating knowledge (viveka-khyāti).
* Yoga.
   * Texts: Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras (four books) with Vyāsa, Vācaspati Miśra's Tattvavaiśāradī, Bhoja's Rājamārtaṇḍa, Vijñānabhikṣu's Yogavārttika, and the commentary attributed to Śaṅkara (attribution disputed).
   * Concepts: citta and its five activities (vṛttis); practice and dispassion; Īśvara and Oṃ; the nine obstacles and their five accompaniments; the four attitudes (friendliness, compassion, gladness, equanimity); kriyā yoga; the five afflictions and their four states (dormant, attenuated, interrupted, active); the eight limbs with each ethical rule and observance; samyama; the three transformations; the powers (Vibhūti Pāda); the kinds of samādhi (with reasoning, reflection, bliss, I-am-ness; with and without seed; the cloud of dharma); kaivalya.
* Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika.
   * Texts: Gautama's Nyāya Sūtra with Vātsyāyana, Uddyotakara, Vācaspati and Udayana; Gaṅgeśa's Tattvacintāmaṇi (Navya-Nyāya); Kaṇāda's Vaiśeṣika Sūtra; Praśastapāda.
   * Concepts: the sixteen categories; the means of knowledge; the five-membered inference; the self as a substance; liberation as the end of pain; atoms; Udayana's arguments for Īśvara.
* Mīmāṃsā.
   * Texts: Jaimini's Mīmāṃsā Sūtra; Śabara; Kumārila; Prabhākara.
   * Concepts: dharma known through Vedic injunction; the unseen result (apūrva); the intrinsic validity of knowledge; the six marks of purport; the eternality of the Veda and of sound.
* Vedānta: Bādarāyaṇa's Brahma Sūtras (four chapters), the root for every Vedānta school.

A6. The Vedānta schools

* Advaita.
   * Texts and teachers:
      * Gauḍapāda's Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (non-origination, ajātivāda)
      * Śaṅkara: his commentaries on the Brahma Sūtras, the principal Upanishads and the Gītā; the Upadeśa Sāhasrī; and the works attributed to him (Vivekacūḍāmaṇi, Ātma Bodha, Aparokṣānubhūti, Bhaja Govindam, Nirvāṇa Ṣaṭkam, Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotram)
      * Maṇḍana Miśra's Brahmasiddhi
      * Sureśvara's Naiṣkarmyasiddhi
      * Padmapāda's Pañcapādikā and the Vivaraṇa school (Prakāśātman)
      * Vācaspati's Bhāmatī school; Sarvajñātman
      * Śrīharṣa's Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya; Citsukha
      * Vidyāraṇya's Pañcadaśī and Jīvanmuktiviveka
      * Madhusūdana Sarasvatī's Advaitasiddhi and his Gītā commentary
      * Appayya Dīkṣita; Sadānanda's Vedāntasāra; Dharmarāja's Vedāntaparibhāṣā
      * the Tripura Rahasya; the Dṛg-Dṛśya Viveka
   * Concepts: māyā; ignorance (avidyā) and the debate over where it resides; superimposition (adhyāsa); apparent transformation (vivarta); the three levels of reality; liberation while living and at death; the four qualifications and the six virtues; hearing, reflection and deep contemplation; the method of superimposition and retraction (adhyāropa-apavāda); the witness (sākṣin); the three bodies and five sheaths; the three states and the fourth; the three theories of how the one appears as many (limitation, reflection, semblance).
* Viśiṣṭādvaita.
   * Texts and teachers: Nāthamuni, Yāmuna, Rāmānuja (Śrībhāṣya, Gītā Bhāṣya, Vedārthasaṅgraha, the three Gadyas), Vedānta Deśika (Rahasyatrayasāra), Piḷḷai Lokācārya; the split between the northern and southern schools over grace, known as the "monkey" and "cat" theories.
   * Concepts: the world and souls as God's body; the three realities (sentient, insentient, God); devotion and surrender (bhakti, prapatti); God's five forms.
* Dvaita.
   * Texts and teachers: Madhva (Brahma Sūtra Bhāṣya, Anuvyākhyāna, Gītā Bhāṣya), Jayatīrtha (Nyāyasudhā), Vyāsatīrtha (Nyāyāmṛta).
   * Concepts: the five differences; the gradation of souls; eternal distinction.
* The other schools of difference-and-non-difference:
   * Bhāskara (Bhedābheda)
   * Nimbārka (Dvaitādvaita)
   * Vallabha (Śuddhādvaita and the Puṣṭimārga of grace)
   * Caitanya's Acintya Bhedābheda: the Six Gosvāmīs, including Rūpa (Bhaktirasāmṛtasindhu, Ujjvalanīlamaṇi), Sanātana and Jīva (Ṣaṭ Sandarbha), and Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja's Caitanya Caritāmṛta
   * Śrīkaṇṭha's Śaiva Vedānta
   * The Vīraśaiva qualified non-dualism of Śakti
* Their concepts: the five devotional moods (rasas: peace, service, friendship, parental love, lover's love); the stages of devotion; spontaneous devotion (rāgānugā bhakti); the practice of the divine name.

A7. The Śaiva lineages

* Pāśupata: Lakulīśa; the Pāśupata Sūtra with Kauṇḍinya's commentary; its five stages; its practices of courting dishonour, and of ash, laughter, song and dance.
* Lākula, Kāpālika and Kālāmukha, as known from inscriptions and from references in others' texts: the skull-bearer's great vow (mahāvrata).
* Śaiva Siddhānta.
   * Sanskrit: the Āgamas; Sadyojyoti, Rāmakaṇṭha, Aghoraśiva.
   * Tamil: Tirumūlar's Tirumantiram; the Tēvāram of Appar, Campantar and Cuntarar; Māṇikkavācakar's Tiruvācakam; the Periya Purāṇam (the sixty-three Nāyanārs); Meykaṇṭār's Civañāṉa Pōtam and the fourteen Meykaṇṭa Śāstras, including Aruṇanti's Civañāṉa Cittiyār and Umāpati.
   * Concepts: the Lord, the bound soul and its bonds (pati, paśu, pāśa); the three bonds (āṇava, karma, māyā); the four stages (conduct, ritual, yoga, knowledge); initiation (dīkṣā); the descent of grace (śaktipāta); Śiva's five acts.
* Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra).
   * Scriptural base: the Mālinīvijayottara, Svacchanda, Netra, Vijñāna Bhairava, Parātrīśikā and Siddhayogeśvarīmata tantras.
   * Spanda: Vasugupta's Śiva Sūtras; the Spanda Kārikā, with Kṣemarāja's Spanda Nirṇaya and Spanda Sandoha.
   * Pratyabhijñā: Somānanda's Śivadṛṣṭi; Utpaladeva's Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā and Śivastotrāvalī; Abhinavagupta's two commentaries on them; Kṣemarāja's Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam.
   * Trika: Abhinavagupta's Tantrāloka (thirty-seven chapters), Tantrasāra, Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa, Paramārthasāra and Mālinīślokavārttika; Jayaratha's commentary.
   * Krama (Mahārtha): the twelve Kālīs; Maheśvarānanda's Mahārthamañjarī.
   * Later: Lal Ded's sayings (vākhs); Rūpa Bhavānī.
   * Concepts:
      * the thirty-six principles, listed in full, including the five "sheaths of limitation" (kañcukas)
      * light and self-reflection (prakāśa, vimarśa)
      * vibration (spanda)
      * manifestation (ābhāsa)
      * Śiva's five acts
      * the three impurities
      * the four means (no means, the divine, the empowered, the individual)
      * the three powers (will, knowledge, action)
      * the letters (mātṛkā and mālinī)
      * recognition (pratyabhijñā)
      * the heart (hṛdaya)
      * the centre or gap (madhya)
      * the 112 contemplations of the Vijñāna Bhairava
* Vīraśaivism (Liṅgāyata).
   * Teachers and texts: Basava, Allama Prabhu, Akka Mahādevī, Cennabasava; the vacanas; the Śūnyasampādane.
   * Concepts: the six stages (ṣaṭ-sthala); the three pillars (teacher, liṅga, wandering devotee); work as worship (kāyaka); the personal liṅga worn on the body.
* The Nāth tradition.
   * Teachers and texts: Matsyendranātha (Kaulajñānanirṇaya); Gorakṣanātha (Gorakṣa Śataka, Siddha Siddhānta Paddhati, Amaraugha Prabodha); Jālandharanātha; the nine Nāths and eighty-four Siddhas; the Gorakhbānī.
* Aghora: Kīna Rām's Vivekasāra and his successors (flag the recent ones).
* The Tamil Siddhars.
   * Teachers: the eighteen, including Agastya, Bogar, Tirumūlar, Sivavākkiyar, Pāmbāṭṭi, Iḍaikkāṭar, Karuvūrar, Konganar and Sattaimuni. Confirm the full list in the hallucination sweep.
   * Concepts: the ninety-six principles; body-perfecting practices (kāya kalpa); breath practice (vāsi yoga); the Siddha critique of caste and ritual.
   * Siddha medicine: summary only; metal preparations with warnings only.

A8. The Śākta lineages

* Goddess texts: the Devī Māhātmya; the Devī Bhāgavata and its Devī Gītā; the Lalitā Sahasranāma with Bhāskararāya's Saubhāgyabhāskara; the Lalitā Triśatī; the Saundarya Laharī.
* Śrī Vidyā.
   * Texts: the Vāmakeśvara Tantra (Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava), the Yoginīhṛdaya, the Tantrarāja Tantra; Bhāskararāya's Varivasyārahasya and Setubandha; Puṇyānanda's Kāmakalāvilāsa.
   * Concepts: the Śrī Yantra and its nine enclosures; the fifteen- and sixteen-syllable mantras (summary level); kāmakalā; the three cities (tripura); inner worship (antaryāga); the "conventional" and "Kaula" modes of worship.
* The Kālī lineages and the ten Mahāvidyās: Kālī, Tārā, Tripura Sundarī, Bhuvaneśvarī, Bhairavī, Chinnamastā, Dhūmāvatī, Bagalāmukhī, Mātaṅgī, Kamalā; the Kālī tantras and the Toḍala Tantra.
* Kaula: the Kaulajñānanirṇaya, the Kulārṇava Tantra, the Kubjikāmata; the lineage families (āmnāyas); Kaula conduct; the five Ms (summary only).
* Sacred sites and rites: the Śākta seats (pīṭhas); Kāmākhyā and the Ambubachi Mela; the sixty-four yoginīs and their temples.
* Śākta poets: Rāmprasād Sen, Kamalākānta.
* Concepts: Śakti as consciousness-power; kuṇḍalinī; the Goddess's gentle and fierce forms; the yoni as sacred; menstruation rites.

A9. Vaiṣṇava, bhakti and Sant lineages

* Pāñcarātra and Vaikhānasa (see A4).
* The Āḻvārs: the twelve saints; Nammāḻvār's Tiruvāymoḻi; Āṇṭāḷ's Tiruppāvai; the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham.
* Devotional theory: the Nārada and Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtras; the nine forms of devotion (hearing, singing, remembering, serving the feet, worship, bowing, servanthood, friendship, self-offering); Jayadeva's Gīta Govinda.
* The Vārkarī: Jñāneśvar's Jñāneśvarī and Amṛtānubhava; Nāmdev, Eknāth, Tukārām.
* Other lineages:
   * the Rāmānandī (Rāmānanda; Tulsīdās's Rāmcaritmānas)
   * the Puṣṭimārga (Vallabha, Sūrdās)
   * the Gauḍīya (Caitanya)
   * the Rādhāvallabha
   * Mīrābāī
* The Sant tradition: Kabīr's Bījak, Ravidās, Dādū; Sikh scripture is outside scope and noted as context only. The later Sant Mat of the inner sound current is flagged as recent.
* The Bāuls of Bengal (Lalon Fakir) and the Vaiṣṇava Sahajiyā.
* Concepts: the divine name; grace; surrender (śaraṇāgati); the devotional moods; the longing of separation (viraha); devotion to the teacher; the inner beloved (maner mānuṣ).

   * the Śiva Saṃhitā

A11. The body and healing sciences

* Āyurveda.
   * Texts: Caraka Saṃhitā (eight sections), Suśruta Saṃhitā, Vāgbhaṭa's Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya and Aṣṭāṅga Saṅgraha, Mādhava Nidāna, Śārṅgadhara Saṃhitā, Bhāvaprakāśa.
   * Concepts:
      * the three doṣas and their subtypes (including Āyurveda's five vāyus)
      * the seven tissues, the waste products, the digestive fires, undigested residue (āma)
      * ojas, tejas and prāṇa
      * the body's channels (srotas)
      * constitution and imbalance (prakṛti, vikṛti)
      * the three supports of life (food, sleep, continence)
      * daily and seasonal routine, and good conduct (sadvṛtta)
      * rejuvenation (rasāyana)
      * Caraka's psychology of the three guṇas
      * the eight branches of medicine, including the branch on possession and spirits (summary only)
* Siddha medicine: summary only.
* Rasa Śāstra (Rasārṇava, Rasaratnākara and others): summary and warnings only.

A12. Sound, language and the arts as paths

* Philosophy of language: Bhartṛhari's Vākyapadīya: sound as Brahman (śabda-brahman), the burst of meaning (sphoṭa), and the levels of speech (spoken, mental, seeing, and the supreme level added by the Kashmir Śaivas).
* Nāda: in the tantras and the Yoga Upanishads, including the Haṃsa Upanishad's ten inner sounds and the Nādabindu.
* Music: Śārṅgadeva's Saṅgīta Ratnākara on nāda and the body.
* Aesthetics: Bharata's Nāṭya Śāstra and Abhinavagupta's theory of aesthetic rapture (rasa), including peace (śānta) as a rasa akin to realization.
* Mantra theory: the fifty letters (mātṛkā); seed syllables; Oṃ (the Māṇḍūkya's four parts; Yoga Sūtras 1.27–28).

A13. Jyotiṣa and sacred timing

* Texts: the Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa, Sūrya Siddhānta, Āryabhaṭīya, Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra, Varāhamihira's Bṛhat Jātaka and Bṛhat Saṃhitā, the Jaimini Sūtras.
* Concepts: the planets (grahas), signs (rāśis), houses (bhāvas), lunar mansions (nakṣatras), planetary periods (daśās), combinations (yogas), auspicious timing (muhūrta), and remedial measures.

B3. Early Buddhism and Theravāda

* The Pali Canon:
   * the Vinaya

* Concepts:
   * the four noble truths; the three marks; the five aggregates; the six sense bases; the twelve links of dependent origination
   * the thirty-seven wings to awakening, in their seven groups

* Later lineages (flag as recent): Ledi Sayadaw; Mahāsi Sayadaw (noting); U Ba Khin and Goenka (body scanning); Sunlun; Pa-Auk; the Thai Forest tradition (Ajahn Mun, Ajahn Chah); Buddhadāsa; and the older "esoteric" Theravāda meditation manuals (borān kammaṭṭhāna).

B4. Mahāyāna

* Sūtras: the Perfection of Wisdom sūtras (in 8,000, 25,000 and 100,000 lines, the Diamond and the Heart); the Lotus; the Avataṃsaka (with the Gaṇḍavyūha and the Ten Grounds); the Laṅkāvatāra; the Vimalakīrti; the Saṃdhinirmocana; the Śrīmālādevī; the Tathāgatagarbha Sūtra; the Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa; the two Śūraṅgama sūtras; the Samādhirāja; the Pure Land sūtras.
* Madhyamaka.
   * Texts and teachers: Nāgārjuna (Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, Vigrahavyāvartanī, Śūnyatāsaptati, Yuktiṣaṣṭikā, Ratnāvalī, the Letter to a Friend); Āryadeva's Four Hundred; Buddhapālita; Bhāviveka; Candrakīrti (Prasannapadā, Madhyamakāvatāra); Śāntideva (Bodhicaryāvatāra, Śikṣāsamuccaya); Śāntarakṣita; Kamalaśīla's Stages of Meditation.
   * Concepts: emptiness; the two truths; the four-cornered negation; the Prāsaṅgika and Svātantrika approaches; dependent designation; the emptiness of emptiness; the awakening mind (bodhicitta), relative and ultimate; the six and ten perfections; exchanging self and other.
* Yogācāra and Buddhist logic.
   * Texts and teachers: the five Maitreya texts; Asaṅga (Yogācārabhūmi, Mahāyānasaṃgraha, Abhidharmasamuccaya); Vasubandhu (Abhidharmakośa, Twenty Verses, Thirty Verses, Three Natures); Sthiramati; Dignāga's Pramāṇasamuccaya; Dharmakīrti's Pramāṇavārttika.
   * Concepts: the eight consciousnesses; the storehouse and its seeds; the three natures; mind-only; the transformation of the basis; the four wisdoms; the five paths and ten grounds; valid cognition.
* Buddha-nature: the Tathāgatagarbha sūtras and the Ratnagotravibhāga.
* Pure Land: Amitābha and the recitation of his name, through Hōnen and Shinran.
* Chan, Seon and Zen.
   * Texts and teachers:
      * Bodhidharma's Two Entrances and Four Practices; Sengcan's Faith in Mind
      * Huineng's Platform Sūtra; Shenhui on sudden awakening
      * Mazu; Huangbo; Linji's record; Dongshan; Shitou's Sandōkai
      * Yunmen; Dahui's koan-phrase practice; Hongzhi's silent illumination
      * the five houses; the koan collections (Blue Cliff Record, Gateless Gate, Book of Equanimity); the Ten Oxherding Pictures
      * Korea's Jinul (sudden awakening, gradual cultivation)
      * Japan's Eisai; Dōgen (Shōbōgenzō, Fukanzazengi, Genjōkōan); Hakuin, including his koan system and his account of "Zen sickness" and its cures in the Yasenkanna; Bankei on the Unborn
   * Concepts: sudden and gradual; no-mind; the original face; great doubt; silent illumination; just sitting; awakening experiences (kenshō, satori); meditation sickness.

B5. Vajrayāna in India

* Tantras: Guhyasamāja, Hevajra, Cakrasaṃvara, Kālacakra, the Vajrayoginī cycles, the Mañjuśrīnāmasaṃgīti, the Mahāvairocana, the Vajraśekhara.

B6. The Tibetan schools

* Nyingma.
   * Texts and teachers: Padmasambhava; the nine vehicles; Dzogchen's three series (mind, space, instruction); the seventeen tantras; the Kunjed Gyalpo; Longchenpa (the Seven Treasuries and the Trilogy of Natural Ease); Jigme Lingpa (the Longchen Nyingthig and the Yeshe Lama); Patrul Rinpoche's Words of My Perfect Teacher; the treasure texts and their revealers; Karma Lingpa's Bardo Thödol.
   * Concepts: rigpa; the ground as primordial purity and spontaneous presence; the practices of cutting through and direct crossing (trekchö, tögal: summary only); the four visions; the rainbow body; Garab Dorje's three statements; the preliminaries (ngöndro).
* Kagyu.
   * Texts and teachers: Tilopa; Nāropa and his Six Yogas; Marpa; Milarepa's songs; Gampopa's Jewel Ornament of Liberation; the Karmapas; Wangchuk Dorje; Dakpo Tashi Namgyal's Moonbeams of Mahāmudrā; the Shangpa (Niguma's six yogas); the Drikung and Drukpa.
   * Concepts: the four yogas of Mahāmudrā (one-pointedness, simplicity, one taste, non-meditation); sūtra, tantra and essence Mahāmudrā; the co-emergent; the pointing-out instruction.
* Sakya: Virūpa; the Path and Result (Lamdre) and its three visions; Parting from the Four Attachments; Sakya Paṇḍita.
* Kadam and Gelug.
   * Texts and teachers: Atiśa's Lamp for the Path; Chekawa's Seven-Point Mind Training; Langri Tangpa's Eight Verses; Tsongkhapa (Lamrim Chenmo, Ngagrim Chenmo, the Three Principal Aspects); the monastic debate curriculum of the five great texts.
   * Concepts: the three scopes; the nine stages of calm abiding; the union of calm and insight; analytical and placement meditation; the fifty-nine mind-training slogans; tonglen.
* Jonang: Dolpopa's Mountain Doctrine; Tāranātha; the debate between "empty of self" and "empty of other".
* Chöd and Shije: Machig Labdrön and Padampa Sangye.
* Tibetan medicine: the Four Tantras; the three humours and the medicine of mind and body.
* The Rimé (non-sectarian) movement: Jamgön Kongtrul, Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo (flag as recent).

G. The debates (record each side, then reconcile or queue)

* Is there a self? Nyāya, Vedānta and the Jains against the Buddhists.
* Causation: the pre-existent effect (Sāṃkhya), the new effect (Nyāya), apparent transformation (Advaita), real manifestation (Kashmir Śaivism), dependent origination (Buddhism).
* Is the world real or appearance? Advaita against Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita; Advaita against Kashmir Śaivism.
* Are souls one, or eternally distinct from God? Dvaita against Advaita.
* Was Advaita "Buddhism in disguise"? The Vaiṣṇava critics of Śaṅkara.
* Sudden or gradual awakening? The Samye debate (Kamalaśīla against Moheyan); Shenhui against the Northern school; Jinul's synthesis.
* Empty of self, or empty of other? Rangtong against shentong.
* Prāsaṅgika against Svātantrika.
* Liberation by works, knowledge or grace? Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta and bhakti; the "cat" and "monkey" theories of grace.
* Is Īśvara real, needed, or a special puruṣa? Nyāya, the Buddhists, Sāṃkhya and Yoga.
* The status of the Veda: authorless, God-authored, or rejected? Mīmāṃsā, Nyāya and the śramaṇas.
* Can women and people of every caste be liberated? Digambara against Śvetāmbara on women; the bhakti and Siddha critiques of caste.
* Is the ultimate personal or impersonal (saguṇa or nirguṇa)?
* Is kuṇḍalinī woken by effort or by grace?
* How many means of knowledge are valid?

## I. Additional lineages and sources

I1. Buddhist schools and sources
- Early Indian schools other than Theravāda: Sarvāstivāda and Vaibhāṣika (the Jñānaprasthāna, the Mahāvibhāṣā), Sautrāntika, Mahāsāṃghika and Lokottaravāda (the Mahāvastu), the Pudgalavāda (the "personalists" and their debate on the person), and the Dharmaguptaka and Mūlasarvāstivāda monastic codes.
- The Chinese Āgamas (Dīrgha, Madhyama, Saṃyukta, Ekottarika) as parallels to the Pali Nikāyas; the Gāndhārī manuscripts.

- Japanese schools: Tendai (Saichō); Shingon (Kūkai; becoming a buddha in this body; the syllable-A meditation; the two maṇḍalas); Nichiren (chanting the Lotus Sūtra's title); Shugendō (mountain asceticism); Jōdo and Jōdo Shinshū; Rinzai, Sōtō and Ōbaku Zen.
- Korea: Wonhyo (the One Mind); Uisang (Hwaeom).
- Vietnam: the Trúc Lâm school.
- Nepal: Newar Vajrayāna and its Vajrācārya priestly lineage.
- Tamil Buddhist literature: the Maṇimēkalai.
- Recent (flag): B. R. Ambedkar's Navayāna.
