## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama / upasaṃhāra)
- **Frame opening (1.1):** Dhṛtarāṣṭra's question calls the battlefield *dharmakṣetra*, the "field of dharma".
- **The problem the teaching answers:**
  - Arjuna's grief: he sits down "his mind overwhelmed by sorrow" (śoka, 1.47), and no kingdom could dispel his śoka (2.8).
  - His bewilderment about dharma (*dharmasaṃmūḍhacetāḥ*, 2.7).
  - His request for what is decisively better (*śreyas*, 2.7), repeated at 3.2 (the one thing by which he may reach śreyas).
  - His refusal: *na yotsya* (2.9).
- **First words of the teaching (2.11):** "you have grieved for those not to be grieved for… the wise do not grieve."
- **How each chapter in the chunk closes:**
  - Ch. 1 ends in śoka (1.47).
  - Ch. 2 ends with the *brāhmī sthiti*: no more delusion, and *brahmanirvāṇa* at death (2.72).
  - Ch. 3 ends with "knowing what is beyond the understanding, steadying the self by the self, slay desire" (3.42–43).
- **For the thesis merger:** the closing mark lies outside this chunk. Compare 18.73 (*naṣṭo mohaḥ*…) with the opening *moha/śoka* of 1.47 and 2.7.

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
- **The teaching on the self is not presented as new.** It is given in Upaniṣadic wording: 2.19–20 ≈ Kaṭha 1.2.19 and 1.2.18; 2.29 ≈ Kaṭha 1.2.7; 3.42 ≈ Kaṭha 1.3.10; *ātmarati* (3.17) as in ChU 7.25.2 and MuU 3.1.4. It is called a "wonder" that no one knows even after hearing (2.29).
- **The chunk's own marked step is 2.39,** "now hear it according to yoga". That step is:
  - the yoga of understanding (*buddhiyoga*) applied to action;
  - the right being to the action, never to its fruit (2.47);
  - yoga defined as equanimity (2.48) and as skill in actions (2.50).

  It is set explicitly against those who delight in the words of the Veda: "there is nothing else", the Vedas' domain is the three guṇas, the well in a flood (2.42–46).
- **The two commitments (3.3) are marked as ancient, not new** (*purā proktā mayā*). The novelty and secrecy marks for this yoga fall in the next chunk: 4.1–3, the lineage from Vivasvat, *yogo naṣṭaḥ*, and *rahasyaṃ hy etad uttamam*.
- **New in the chunk relative to its Upaniṣadic parallels:** Kṛṣṇa as the object the practitioner is oriented to, in *matparaḥ* (2.61) and *mayi sarvāṇi karmāṇi saṃnyasya* (3.30). Also his own example of acting with nothing to gain (3.22–24).

## 4. Stated result (phala)
- **Liberation and inner results:**
  - Fitness for immortality (*amṛtatva*, 2.15).
  - Casting off the bondage of action (*karmabandha*, 2.39).
  - No effort lost; protection from great fear (2.40).
  - Casting off good and evil deeds (2.50).
  - Freedom from the bondage of birth and reaching "the state free from ill" (*padam anāmayam*, 2.51).
  - Attaining yoga (2.53).
  - Serenity (*prasāda*): all sorrows end and understanding steadies (2.64–65).
  - Peace (*śānti*, 2.66, 2.70–71).
  - The *brāhmī sthiti*, no more delusion, and *brahmanirvāṇa* at death (2.72).
  - Reaching the highest (3.19).
  - Perfection (*saṃsiddhi*, 3.20).
  - Freedom from actions for those who follow the teaching with faith (3.31).
- **Ritual results:** the highest good through mutual nourishment of gods and humans (3.11); freedom from all faults for those who eat the remainder of the sacrifice (3.13).
- **Worldly and martial results, inside the svadharma argument:** the open door to heaven (2.32); heaven if slain, the earth if victorious (2.37); no sin (2.38).

## 5. Praise and blame (arthavāda)
- **Praise:**
  - Happy the kṣatriyas who meet such a battle (2.32).
  - Even a little of this dharma saves from great fear (2.40).
  - The sage awake in the night of beings (2.69).
  - The ocean filled yet unmoved (2.70).
- **Blame:**
  - Disgrace worse than death, and the scorn of enemies (2.34–36).
  - The "flowery speech" of the unwise who delight in Vedic words (2.42–44).
  - The well in a flood (2.46). Commentators divide on whether this blames the Veda or praises the knower.
  - "Pitiable are those whose motive is the fruit" (2.49).
  - The hypocrite (3.6).
  - One who enjoys the gods' gifts without offering to them is a thief (3.12).
  - Those who cook for themselves "eat sin" (3.13).
  - One who does not turn the wheel of sacrifice lives in vain (3.16).
  - Those who carp at the teaching are lost (3.32).
- **Candidates for the arthavāda principle (P7), for the interpretation layer:**
  - The appeals to heaven and honour in 2.32–37. They are immediately reframed by 2.38 and 2.47, where the fruit is not to be the motive.
  - Arjuna's appeals to hell and to the ancestors' fall (1.42–44). These belong to the view Kṛṣṇa is answering, not to Kṛṣṇa's teaching.

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- **From the nature of the embodied one:**
  - Eternal existence of "I, you and these kings" (2.12).
  - The analogy of the ages of life (2.13).
  - The principle of *sat* and *asat* (2.16).
  - That by which all is pervaded (2.17).
  - It neither slays nor is slain, and does not act as killer (2.19–21).
  - The garments simile (2.22).
  - The elements cannot affect it (2.23–24).
- **Argument on the opposing assumption:** even if the self were born and died again and again, death is certain for the born, beings are unmanifest at beginning and end, so grief is unfit (2.26–28).
- **Argument from svadharma and honour:** 2.31–37.
- **Causal chains in the mind:** the descent from dwelling on objects to ruin (2.62–63); the ascent from mastered senses to peace (2.64–66); the ship-and-wind image (2.67).
- **Inaction is impossible:** the guṇas make everyone act (3.5); even bodily life needs action (3.8).
- **The cosmological cycle of sacrifice:** food, rain, sacrifice, action, *brahman*, the imperishable (3.14–15).
- **Argument from example:** Janaka (3.20); the best person as the standard (3.21); Kṛṣṇa's own action (3.22–24).
- **Analysis:**
  - The guṇas are the doers; *ahaṃkāra* says "I am the doer" (3.27–28).
  - The three images of covering: smoke, dirt on a mirror, the womb-membrane (3.38).
  - The hierarchy senses < mind < understanding < "he" (3.42).

## Chunk-level observation for the thesis
The chunk moves from Arjuna's grief and confusion about dharma to two answers:
- knowledge of the imperishable embodied one ("according to sāṃkhya", 2.11–30);
- action without attachment to its fruit: done as one's own dharma, as sacrifice and for holding the world together, and finally renounced in Kṛṣṇa ("according to yoga", 2.39 onward; 3.30).

The tension between knowledge and action is stated by Arjuna himself (3.1–2) and answered by the two commitments (3.3–4) and the impossibility of not acting (3.5). The goals named are *brahmanirvāṇa*, freedom from the bondage of action and of birth, and reaching the highest.
