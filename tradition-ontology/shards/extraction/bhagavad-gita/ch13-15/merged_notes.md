## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **Ch. 13:**
  - Opens with Arjuna's six topics (13.1: prakṛti and puruṣa, field and knower, knowledge and the knowable).
  - Kṛṣṇa then defines his terms: the body is the field, and whoever knows it is the knower of the field. He is "also" the knower in all fields, and "knowledge of field and knower is knowledge" (13.2–3).
  - Closes on the same pair: those who know "the difference (antara) of field and knower" with the eye of knowledge, and the release from the prakṛti of beings, "go to the supreme" (13.35).
- **Ch. 14:**
  - Opens with "the highest of knowledges", declared "again", by which all the sages reached the highest perfection and came to sādharmya with the Lord (14.1–2).
  - Closes with the means: unswerving bhakti-yoga takes one beyond the guṇas to fitness for brahmabhūya, "for I am the foundation (pratiṣṭhā) of brahman …" (14.26–27).
- **Ch. 15:**
  - Opens with the aśvattha that is to be cut: "one who knows it knows the Veda" (15.1–3).
  - Closes with knowing the Lord as puruṣottama. This makes one sarvavid and a worshipper "with one's whole being" (15.19).
  - The chapter then calls itself "the most secret teaching", by which one becomes buddhimat and kṛtakṛtya (15.20).
- **The chunk as a whole:**
  - It moves in three steps:
    - discriminating field and knower (13);
    - the guṇas that bind the embodied one, and the way beyond them (14);
    - the puruṣottama beyond kṣara and akṣara (15).
  - Each chapter ends on the Lord's own position: param (13.35), pratiṣṭhā of brahman (14.27), puruṣottama (15.18–20).
  - Each has a devotional clause near its close (13.19, 14.26, 15.19).
  - 13.1 hinges back to the unmanifest/Lord question of 12.1–5.

## 2. Repetition (abhyāsa)
- **Field and knower:** kṣetra / kṣetrajña / kṣetrin at 13.1–4, 13.7, 13.19, 13.27, 13.34, 13.35.
- **Knowledge and the knowable:**
  - jñāna / jñeya at 13.1, 13.3, 13.12–13, 13.17–19.
  - jñāna again at 14.1–2, 14.6, 14.9, 14.11, 14.17 and 15.15.
- **True seeing:**
  - "yaḥ paśyati sa paśyati" (13.28, 13.30).
  - Other verbs of seeing: 13.25, 13.29, 13.31 (anupaśyati), 14.19 (anupaśyati), 15.10–11 (paśyanti / na paśyanti).
  - jñāna-cakṣus (13.35, 15.10).
- **Non-agency:**
  - Prakṛti is the cause of agency (13.21).
  - Actions are done by prakṛti alone (13.30).
  - The supreme self neither acts nor is stained (13.32–33).
  - There is no doer but the guṇas (14.19); "guṇā vartante" (14.23, echoing 3.28).
- **The guṇas:**
  - They are born of prakṛti (13.20, 13.22, 14.5).
  - They bind (13.22, 14.5–9).
  - They are to be gone beyond (14.20–26), and they swell the tree (15.2).
- **Immortality and no return:**
  - amṛtam aśnute (13.13, 14.20); na bhūyo 'bhijāyate (13.24).
  - atitaranti mṛtyum (13.26); sarge 'pi nopajāyante (14.2).
  - na nivartante (15.4, 15.6).
- **The Lord's state and seat:**
  - madbhāva (13.19, 14.19); sādharmya (14.2).
  - In the heart of all (13.18, 15.15).
  - Light beyond sun, moon and fire (13.18, 15.6, 15.12).
- **Unswerving devotion:**
  - bhakti avyabhicāriṇī by ananya-yoga (13.11).
  - avyabhicāreṇa bhakti-yogena (14.26).
  - bhajati sarvabhāvena (15.19); madbhakta (13.19).
- **Sameness:**
  - sama-cittatva (13.10); samam … tiṣṭhantam / samaṃ paśyan (13.28–29).
  - The lists of evenness at 14.24–25, echoing 12.13–19 and 6.7–9.
- **avyaya:** 13.32, 14.5, 14.27, 15.1, 15.5, 15.17.

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
- **New in the Gītā here:**
  - The kṣetra/kṣetrajña vocabulary with an enumerated field (13.2–7). Its items can be counted as twenty-four, but the text gives no number and names no school.
  - Twenty dispositions, including devotion and attendance on the teacher, called "knowledge" (13.8–12).
  - The knowable described by paired paradoxes, "neither sat nor asat" (13.13–18).
  - The guṇas set out as a full scheme: how each binds (14.6–9), their mutual overpowering (14.10), their signs (14.11–13), dying in each (14.14–15), their fruits and courses (14.16–18).
  - The marks of the guṇātīta (14.22–25).
  - The inverted aśvattha, whose roots also stretch downward and bind through action, cut with asaṅga (15.1–4). The tree itself is Kaṭha 2.3.1.
  - The Lord's eternal portion as the living being, carrying the senses from body to body (15.7–9).
  - Two puruṣas and a third, highest, with the name puruṣottama argued (15.16–18).
- **The Lord's new self-descriptions:**
  - Knower in all fields (13.3).
  - Seed-giving father, with "great brahman" as his womb (14.3–4).
  - Foundation of brahman (14.27).
  - Known by all the Vedas, maker of the vedānta (15.15).
- **The text's own claims are mixed:**
  - For ch. 13 it claims received authority rather than novelty ("sung by the seers … in the brahmasūtra words, reasoned and decisive", 13.5). The aśvattha and the puruṣottama are also presented as received ("prāhuḥ" 15.1; "loke vede ca prathitaḥ" 15.18).
  - The explicit claims of uniqueness are "the highest of knowledges" (14.1) and "the most secret teaching" (15.20).
  - Inaccessibility to ordinary means: "not to be known because of its subtlety" (13.16); the deluded do not see, those with the eye of knowledge do (15.10–11).

## 4. Stated result (phala)
- **Positive:**
  - Immortality (13.13, 14.20).
  - Fitness for the Lord's state (13.19, 14.19).
  - Not born again, however one lives (13.24).
  - Even those who only hear and worship cross death (13.26).
  - The highest goal (13.29); reaching brahman (13.31); going to the supreme (13.35).
  - The highest perfection (14.1); sādharmya, not born at creation nor troubled at dissolution (14.2).
  - Dying in sattva, the stainless worlds; the sattva-abiding go upward (14.14, 14.18).
  - Freedom from birth, death, old age and pain (14.20).
  - Fitness for brahmabhūya (14.26).
  - The imperishable place of no return, the Lord's supreme abode (15.4–6).
  - Sarvavid (15.19); buddhimat and kṛtakṛtya (15.20).
- **Negative:**
  - Births in good and bad wombs through attachment to the guṇas (13.22).
  - Birth among those attached to action, or in deluded wombs (14.15).
  - Pain and ignorance as the fruits of rajas and tamas (14.16); the tāmasa go downward (14.18).

## 5. Praise (arthavāda)
- **Clear praise:**
  - "jñānānāṃ jñānam uttamam", and all the sages reached perfection by it (14.1).
  - "guhyatamaṃ śāstram" (15.20); "yas taṃ veda sa vedavit" (15.1); "sa sarvavit" (15.19).
  - "sa paśyati" (13.28, 13.30).
  - "sung by the seers … reasoned and decisive" (13.5); "prathitaḥ puruṣottamaḥ" (15.18).
  - The Lord as the radiance of sun, moon and fire, sustainer of beings, soma and vaiśvānara (15.12–14).
- **Candidates for a praise-reading (P7), flagged by both extractors, not decided:**
  - "sarvathā vartamāno 'pi na sa bhūyo 'bhijāyate" (13.24; also 6.31). The commentators restrict its scope.
  - "those who, not knowing, only hear and worship also cross death" (13.26).

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- **Stated reasons:**
  - "anāditvān nirguṇatvāt": why the supreme self in the body neither acts nor is stained (13.32).
  - "saukṣmyāt": space is unstained (13.33); "sūkṣmatvāt tad avijñeyam" (13.16).
  - "yasmāt … ataḥ" for the name puruṣottama (15.18).
- **Causal analysis:**
  - Prakṛti is the cause in agency, puruṣa in experiencing (13.21).
  - Attachment to the guṇas is the cause of births (13.22).
  - Every being arises from the union of field and knower (13.27).
  - The guṇas prevail by overpowering one another (14.10).
- **Inference from signs:** "vidyāt" (14.11–13); Arjuna asks for the liṅgas of the guṇātīta (14.21).
- **Similes:**
  - Space (13.33) and the one sun lighting the world (13.34).
  - The wind carrying scents (15.8) and the tree (15.1–3).
- **The teaching's own claim to reasons:** "hetumadbhir viniścitaiḥ" (13.5).

## For the thesis entry
- **Purport on the six marks:**
  - Knowledge that discriminates the field (prakṛti and its guṇas) from its knower and sees the guṇas alone as agents (13.30, 14.19).
  - This knowledge is joined to unswerving devotion:
    - devotion is counted inside "knowledge" (13.11);
    - bhakti-yoga is the answer to "how does one pass beyond the guṇas" (14.26);
    - knowing the puruṣottama issues in worship with one's whole being (15.19).
  - It leads beyond the guṇas to immortality, the Lord's state or brahman, and the place of no return.
- **Means set side by side, unranked:** 13.25–26 names meditation, sāṃkhya, the yoga of action, and worship after hearing (bears on dsp:gita-primary-teaching).
- **Not settled by the six marks — how kṣetrajña, akṣara and puruṣottama relate:**
  - Identity: Śaṅkara.
  - The Lord as the self of the knowers, who are his body: Rāmānuja.
  - Eternal distinctness, with the akṣara as Lakṣmī: Madhva.
  - All three are recalled; see dsp:bhagavad-gita-ksetrajna-and-the-lord, dsp:bhagavad-gita-jiva-as-amsa and dsp:bhagavad-gita-aksara-purusa.
