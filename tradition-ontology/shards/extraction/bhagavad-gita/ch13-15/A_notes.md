# Bhagavad Gītā, chunk ch13-15 — extractor A: contribution to the six marks of purport (ṣaḍ-liṅga)
_(Returned as text by the subagent — subagents cannot write Markdown files in this harness — and saved verbatim by the orchestrator.)_


Refs follow this edition. In chapter 13 the vulgate number is one lower (13.2 = 13.1 vulgate); 13.1 is the extra verse.

## 1. Upakrama–upasaṃhāra (opening and closing)
- **Ch. 13** opens with Arjuna's question on prakṛti/puruṣa, kṣetra/kṣetrajña, jñāna/jñeya (13.1, extra verse). Kṛṣṇa then defines the body as the field and its knower as kṣetrajña, and says "know me also as the kṣetrajña in all fields; the knowledge of field and knower is knowledge" (13.2–3). The chapter closes by returning to the same pair: those who know "the difference (antara) between field and knower" with the eye of knowledge, and the release from the prakṛti of beings, "go to the supreme" (13.35). Opening and closing agree: knowledge of field, knower and their difference.
- **Ch. 14** opens with "the highest of knowledges", by which the sages reached the highest perfection and sādharmya with Kṛṣṇa (14.1–2). It closes with serving Kṛṣṇa by unswerving bhakti-yoga, which takes one beyond the guṇas to fitness for brahmabhūya, "for I am the foundation (pratiṣṭhā) of brahman …" (14.26–27).
- **Ch. 15** opens with the aśvattha: "one who knows it knows the Veda" (15.1). It closes with knowing Kṛṣṇa as puruṣottama, which makes one all-knowing and a worshipper "with one's whole being". It calls itself the "most secret teaching", by which one becomes buddhimat and kṛtakṛtya (15.19–20).
- **Across the chunk:** the movement runs from discriminating field and knower (13), through the guṇas that bind the embodied one (14), to the puruṣottama beyond kṣara and akṣara (15). Each chapter ends on Kṛṣṇa's own position (13.35 param; 14.27 pratiṣṭhā; 15.18–20 puruṣottama), with a devotional clause near each close (13.19, 14.26, 15.19).

## 3. Apūrvatā (novelty)
- Topics new in the Gītā at this point:
  - the kṣetra/kṣetrajña vocabulary (13.2–7, 13.27, 13.34–35);
  - twenty dispositions called "knowledge" (13.8–12);
  - the guṇas set out as a scheme — how each binds, its signs, dying in it, its fruit, its course (14.5–18);
  - the marks of the guṇātīta (14.22–25);
  - cutting the inverted aśvattha with asaṅga (15.3–4; the tree itself is Kaṭha 2.3.1);
  - two puruṣas plus a third, highest one, with the name puruṣottama argued (15.16–18).
- The text's own claims are mixed. For ch. 13 it claims received authority rather than novelty ("sung by the seers … in the brahmasūtra words", 13.5). The aśvattha and the puruṣottama are also presented as received ("prāhuḥ", 15.1; "loke vede ca prathitaḥ", 15.18). The explicit claims of uniqueness are "the highest of knowledges", declared "again" (bhūyaḥ, 14.1), and "the most secret teaching" (15.20).
- That the subject is not reachable by ordinary means is stated as "not to be known because of its subtlety" (13.16) and "the deluded do not see; those with the eye of knowledge see" (15.10–11).

## 4. Phala (stated result)
- **Positive:**
  - amṛta (13.13, 14.20);
  - madbhāva (13.19, 14.19);
  - not born again (13.24);
  - even those who only hear and worship cross death (13.26);
  - parā gati (13.29); brahma sampadyate (13.31); yānti param (13.35);
  - parā siddhi (14.1);
  - sādharmya, and not born at creation nor troubled at dissolution (14.2);
  - dying in sattva, the stainless worlds of the knowers of the highest (14.14); sattva-abiders go upward (14.18);
  - freedom from birth, death, old age and pain (14.20);
  - brahmabhūya (14.26);
  - the imperishable place of no return, Kṛṣṇa's supreme abode (15.4–6);
  - sarvavid (15.19); buddhimat and kṛtakṛtya (15.20).
- **Negative:**
  - births in good and bad wombs through guṇa-attachment (13.22);
  - dying in rajas, birth among those attached to action; in tamas, in deluded wombs (14.15);
  - pain and ignorance as the fruits of rajas and tamas (14.16);
  - the tāmasa go downward (14.18).

- Clear praise:

  - "guhyatamaṃ śāstram" (15.20);
  - "yas taṃ veda sa vedavit" (15.1);
  - "sa paśyati" (13.28, 13.30);
  - "sa sarvavit" (15.19);
  - "sung by the seers … reasoned and decisive" (13.5);
  - "prathitaḥ puruṣottamaḥ" (15.18).
- Candidates for a praise-reading (P7), not decided here:
  - "sarvathā vartamāno 'pi na sa bhūyo 'bhijāyate" (13.24), whose scope the commentators restrict;
  - "those who, not knowing, only hear and worship also cross death" (13.26).

## 6. Upapatti (reasoned demonstration)
- **Stated reasons in the ablative:**
  - "anāditvān nirguṇatvāt", why the supreme self in the body neither acts nor is tainted (13.32);
  - "saukṣmyāt", space is untainted (13.33);
  - "sūkṣmatvāt tad avijñeyam" (13.16).
- **Arguments from comparison:** space (13.33); the one sun illumining the world (13.34); wind carrying scents (15.8); the tree (15.1–3).
- **Causal analysis:** prakṛti as cause in agency, puruṣa in experiencing (13.21); guṇa-attachment as cause of birth (13.22); the union of field and knower as the origin of every being (13.27).
- **Inference from signs:** each guṇa prevails by overpowering the others (14.10); its increase "is to be known" (vidyāt) from marks (14.11–13); Arjuna asks for the liṅgas of the guṇātīta (14.21).
- **An explicit argument:** "yasmāt … ataḥ" for the name puruṣottama (15.18).
- **Appeal to reasoned authority:** "hetumadbhir viniścitaiḥ" (13.5).

**Input for the thesis entry.** On these marks the chunk's purport is knowledge that distinguishes the self (kṣetrajña/puruṣa) from prakṛti and its guṇas, joined to unswerving devotion, leading beyond the guṇas to Kṛṣṇa's state / brahman / the puruṣottama. The commentators divide on how kṣetrajña, akṣara and puruṣottama relate: identity (Śaṅkara), inner self and body (Rāmānuja), eternal distinctness (Madhva). The six marks here do not settle that question.
