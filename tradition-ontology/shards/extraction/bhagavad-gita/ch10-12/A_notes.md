# BhG ch. 10–12 — extractor A: the six marks of purport (ṣaḍ-liṅga) in this chunk

## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **Chunk opening:** 10.1, "hear again my supreme word (paramaṃ vacaḥ) … out of a wish for your welfare". It continues straight on from the end of ch. 9 (9.34, manmanā bhava madbhakto madyājī …).
- **Chunk closing:** 12.20. Those who with faith, holding the Lord supreme, attend to "this nectar of dharma" are "exceedingly dear to me". The next verse, 13.1 (the extra verse in this edition), turns to prakṛti and puruṣa, the field and its knower.
- **Chapter 10** opens by saying that the gods and seers do not know his origin, for he is their beginning (10.2). It closes with "I stand supporting this whole world with a single portion" (10.42).
- **Chapter 11** opens with Arjuna's request to see the sovereign form (11.3–4). It closes with "by undivided devotion alone can I be known, seen and entered … whoever does my work … comes to me" (11.54–55).
- **Chapter 12** opens with the question of which worshippers know yoga best (12.1). It closes with the refrain "dear to me" (12.13–20).
- **Movement of the whole chunk:**
  - Ch. 10: the Lord is the source of all, and this is known and contemplated through his manifestations.
  - Ch. 11: the same is seen directly in the universal form.
  - Ch. 12: how he is to be reached — undivided devotion, with graded fallbacks and the marks of the devotee.

## 2. Repetition (abhyāsa)
- **The "mat-" compounds:** maccittāḥ, madgataprāṇāḥ (10.9); matkarmakṛt, matparamaḥ, madbhaktaḥ (11.55); matparāḥ (12.6); matkarmaparamaḥ (12.10); madarthe (12.10); matparamāḥ (12.20).
- **Refrain in 12.13–20:** "(yo madbhaktaḥ) sa me priyaḥ" at 12.14, 15, 16, 17, 19, culminating in "atīva me priyāḥ" (12.20).
- **Undivided (ananya):** 11.54, 12.6.
- **Ever yoked:** satatayukta (10.10, 12.1), nityayukta (12.2).
- **Mind set on the Lord:** mayy āveśya / āveśita (12.2, 12.7); mind and understanding offered to him (mayy arpitamanobuddhiḥ, 12.14; cf. 12.8).
- **Faith (śraddhā):** 12.2, 12.20.
- **The manifestations:**

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
- The universal form has "never been seen before by anyone but you" (11.47). Arjuna sees "what was never seen before" (adṛṣṭapūrva, 11.6, 11.45).
- It cannot be seen with one's own eye, so a divine eye is given (11.8).
- It is not reached by Vedas, sacrifice, study, gifts, rites or austerity (11.48, 11.53), but by undivided devotion (11.54). The text marks this as a way of knowing that the ordinary Vedic means do not reach.
- Neither gods nor seers know the Lord's origin; he alone knows himself by himself (10.2, 10.14–15).
- New in the text's argument: the comparison of two kinds of worship — of the Lord, and of the imperishable unmanifest (12.1–5). The unmanifest path also reaches him, but with greater hardship.

## 4. Stated result (phala)
- Freed from all sins, undeluded (10.3).
- Unwavering yoga (10.7).
- The yoga of understanding given by the Lord, by which they come to him (10.10).
- The darkness born of ignorance destroyed (10.11).
- Arjuna's delusion gone (11.1).
- Victory and kingdom, the warriors being already slain (11.33–34).
- The Lord known, seen and entered in truth (11.54).
- "Comes to me" (11.55).
- "They too reach me" (12.4).
- Deliverance before long from the ocean of death and saṃsāra (12.7).
- Dwelling in the Lord (12.8).
- Perfection, siddhi (12.10).
- Peace immediately after relinquishing the fruit of actions (12.12).
- Being dear, and exceedingly dear, to the Lord (12.13–20).

## 5. Praise (arthavāda)
- Arjuna's praise of Kṛṣṇa as supreme brahman, backed by the seers' testimony (10.12–15).
- The whole list of manifestations as glorification (10.19–41).
- Arjuna's hymns (11.15–22, 11.36–44).
- "Even the gods ever long to see this form" (11.52).
- The ranking in 12.12 (relinquishing the fruit above meditation, knowledge and practice) runs opposite to its lowest place in 12.11. Commentators commonly read it as praise of relinquishment.
- "Most yoked" (yuktatamāḥ, 12.2) is a comparative commendation.
- The rhetorical 10.42 ("what use is this detailed knowledge?") puts the list beneath the single portion that supports the world.

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- 10.2 (hi): the seers cannot know his origin, because he is their beginning.
- 10.8 (iti matvā): knowing him as the origin from which all proceeds, the wise worship him.
- 10.39: nothing moving or unmoving could exist without him, because he is the seed of all.
- 10.41: whatever is glorious springs from a portion of his splendour, so every excellence points to him. This rule generalises the list.
- 11.8: the ordinary eye cannot see him; therefore a divine eye is given.
- 11.33 (tasmāt): because the warriors are already slain by Time, Arjuna is to rise and be merely the instrument.
- 11.37: why should they not bow, since he is greater (than Brahmā / the first maker even of Brahmā)?
- 11.40: "you pervade all, therefore you are all" (sarvaṃ samāpnoṣi tato 'si sarvaḥ).
- 12.5 (hi): the unmanifest goal is hard for the embodied — the reason given for the greater hardship.

## Contribution to the text-level thesis
Chapters 10–12 set out the Lord as the origin, seed and support of all:
- declared and illustrated through his manifestations (ch. 10);
- shown as the universal form and as Time (ch. 11);
- with undivided devotion named as the means by which he is known, seen and entered (11.54, 12.2–8).

Worship of the imperishable unmanifest is acknowledged as also reaching him, at greater cost (12.3–5). The practice is graded by capacity (12.8–12) and ends in ethical marks of the devotee (12.13–19). These marks repeat phrases from the portraits of the one of steady wisdom (2.57, 2.71) and the one beyond the guṇas (14.24–25).
