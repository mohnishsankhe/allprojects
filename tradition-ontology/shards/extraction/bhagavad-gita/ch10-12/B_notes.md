# BhG ch. 10–12, extractor B: what this chunk contributes to the six marks of purport (ṣaḍ-liṅga)
_(Returned as text by the subagent — subagents cannot write Markdown files in this harness — and saved verbatim by the orchestrator.)_


## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **Chapter 10** opens with "bhūya eva … śṛṇu me paramaṃ vacaḥ" (10.1): the supreme word spoken for Arjuna's good. The Lord first says that gods and seers do not know his origin, because he is their origin (10.2). The chapter closes with "viṣṭabhyāham idaṃ kṛtsnam ekāṃśena sthito jagat" (10.42): the whole world is held up by one portion of him.
- **Chapter 11** opens with "moho 'yaṃ vigato mama" (11.1) and Arjuna's request to see the Lord's sovereign form (11.3–4). It closes with 11.55: whoever does the Lord's work, holds him highest, is devoted, unattached and without enmity "comes to me" (sa mām eti).
- **Chapter 12** opens with the question which worshippers are the best knowers of yoga (12.1). It closes with "bhaktās te 'tīva me priyāḥ" (12.20).
- **The chunk as a whole** opens with the Lord as the source of all, to be known and worshipped (10.2–8). It ends with devotion as the way the Lord is known, seen, entered and reached, and with the marks of the devotee dear to him (11.54–55; 12.2, 12.13–20).
- **Link between chapters:** "evam" in 12.1 points back to 11.55.

## 2. Repetition (abhyāsa)
- **Refrain in ch. 12:** "sa me priyaḥ / yo madbhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ" (12.14, 12.15, 12.16, 12.17, 12.19), rising to "atīva me priyāḥ" (12.20).
- **"mat-" compounds for devotion to the Lord:**
  - maccitta, madgataprāṇa (10.9)
  - matkarmakṛt, matparama, madbhakta (11.55)
  - matparāḥ (12.6), matkarmaparama (12.10), madyogam (12.11)
  - madbhakta (12.14, 12.16), matparamāḥ (12.20)
- **"ever joined":** satatayukta (10.10, 12.1), nityayukta (12.2).
- **Devotion "to nothing else":** ananya (11.54, 12.6).
- **Negated means:** "not by Vedas, sacrifice, study, gifts, rites or austerities" (11.48), restated in 11.53.
- **Endlessness:** "no end to my extent / manifestations" (10.19, 10.40); "no end, no middle, no beginning" (11.16, 11.19).
- **Request for grace:** "prasīda" (11.25, 11.31, 11.45), with prasādaye (11.44) and the answering prasanna (11.47).
- **The Lord as time and death:** 10.30, 10.33, 10.34, 11.32.
- **Other repeated formulas:** the "of X I am Y" formula (10.20–38); "paśya" (11.5–8) answered by "paśyāmi" (11.15–19).
- **Verbatim echoes of earlier chapters:** "paśya me yogam aiśvaram" (11.8 = 9.5); "sarvabhūtahite ratāḥ" (12.4 = 5.25); "mayi sarvāṇi karmāṇi saṃnyasya" (12.6, cf. 3.30); "mayy arpitamanobuddhiḥ" (12.14, cf. 8.7).

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
- **The vision of the universal form**, possible only with a divine eye the Lord gives (11.8–13). The text itself stresses that it is new: "adṛṣṭapūrvāṇi" (11.6), "adṛṣṭapūrvam" (11.45), and "yan me tvadanyena na dṛṣṭapūrvam" (11.47).
- **The Lord's self-disclosure as Time**, who has already slain the warriors, so that Arjuna is "merely the instrument" (11.32–34).
- **In ch. 12:**
  - the explicit comparison of worshipping the Lord with worshipping the imperishable unmanifest (12.1–5);
  - the conditional ladder of means graded by capacity (12.8–11);
  - the ranking in 12.12, which puts relinquishing the fruit of actions above meditation, knowledge and practice.
- **The systematic vibhūti list** (10.20–38) and its rule that every excellence "springs from a portion of my splendour" (10.41).

## 4. Stated result (phala)
**Chapter 10**
- Knowing the Lord as unborn and beginningless frees one from delusion and from all sins (10.3).
- Knowing his vibhūti and yoga in truth joins one to unwavering yoga (10.7).
- The Lord gives buddhiyoga, "by which they come to me" (10.10).
- He destroys the darkness born of ignorance with the lamp of knowledge (10.11).

**Chapter 11**
- Glory and a prosperous kingdom for Arjuna (11.33); "you will conquer your rivals" (11.34).
- By undivided devotion the Lord is known, seen in truth and entered (11.54).
- "sa mām eti" (11.55).

**Chapter 12**
- The devotees are "most joined" (12.2).
- The worshippers of the imperishable also "reach me alone", though with greater hardship (12.4–5).
- The Lord soon lifts his devotees out of the ocean of death and saṃsāra (12.7).
- "You will dwell in me" (12.8); perfection (siddhi) through work for the Lord (12.10).
- Peace immediately after relinquishment (12.12).
- Being dear, and exceedingly dear, to the Lord (12.13–20).

## 5. Praise (arthavāda)
- **The vibhūti list as glorification** (10.20–41). It includes even gambling as the pre-eminent thing among deceivers (10.36).
- **Arjuna's hymns:**
  - 10.12–15, which cite Nārada, Asita Devala and Vyāsa (10.13);
  - 11.15–22 and 11.36–44: "namo namas te 'stu sahasrakṛtvaḥ" (11.39), "tato 'si sarvaḥ" (11.40), and "pitāsi lokasya … gurur garīyān" (11.43).
- **Praise of the vision's rarity:**
  - no other has seen it (11.47);
  - Vedas and rites cannot win it (11.48, 11.53);
  - the gods ever long to see it (11.52).
- **Praise of the teaching:** "paramaṃ vacaḥ" (10.1), "paramaṃ guhyam" (11.1), "dharmyāmṛtam" (12.20).
- **Praise of relinquishment above meditation (12.12).** Śaṅkara reads it explicitly as praise (stuti) meant to encourage the less capable. Whether it is praise or doctrine is exactly the interpretive question for this mark.

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- The gods and seers cannot know the Lord's origin because he is their origin: "aham ādir hi" (10.2).
- Worship follows from understanding: "aham sarvasya prabhavaḥ … iti matvā bhajante" (10.8).
- Nothing moving or unmoving exists without him (10.39); therefore every glorious being is from a portion of his splendour (10.41), and one portion is enough to support the world (10.42).
- "You pervade all; therefore you are all": sarvaṃ samāpnoṣi tato 'si sarvaḥ (11.40).
- The warriors are already slain by the Lord; therefore stand up and be merely the instrument: mayaivaite nihatāḥ pūrvam eva … tasmāt tvam uttiṣṭha (11.32–33).
- Therefore, bowing, I seek your grace: tasmāt praṇamya (11.44).
- Why the unmanifest path is harder: "avyaktā hi gatir duḥkhaṃ dehavadbhir avāpyate" (12.5).
- The means in 12.8–11 are graded by the hearer's capacity ("if you are unable …").
- Relinquishment is ranked highest because peace follows it immediately (12.12).

## Suggested contribution to the text-level thesis
- **What chapters 10–12 teach:** the Lord is the source and support of all, manifest pre-eminently in every class (ch. 10). He is seen in his universal form only by his grace, and is known, seen and entered by devotion to nothing else (11.47–54). His devotees are "most joined" (12.2), though the worshippers of the imperishable also reach him (12.4). For those who cannot fix manas and buddhi on him, graded means lead down to relinquishing the fruit of actions (12.8–12). The marks of the devotee dear to the Lord are set out in 12.13–20.
- **For the merger:** the commentarial dispute over 12.1–5 and 12.12 bears directly on whether these chapters' purport is devotion as the highest means or devotion as leading to knowledge (see dsp:gita-primary-teaching, dsp:bhakti-jnana-precedence).
