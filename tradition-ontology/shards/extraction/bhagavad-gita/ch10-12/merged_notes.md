## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **The chunk as a whole.**
  - It opens at 10.1: "bhūya eva mahābāho śṛṇu me paramaṃ vacaḥ" — hear again my supreme word, spoken out of a wish for your welfare. It continues straight on from 9.34 ("man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī …").
  - It closes at 12.20: those who, with faith and holding the Lord supreme, attend to "this nectar of dharma" (dharmyāmṛta) are "exceedingly dear to me" (atīva me priyāḥ).
  - The next verse, 13.1 (the extra verse of this edition), turns to prakṛti and puruṣa, the field and its knower.
  - At the start the Lord is the source of all, to be known and worshipped (10.2–8). At the end devotion is the way he is known, seen, entered and reached, and the devotee dear to him is described by his marks (11.54–55; 12.2; 12.13–20).
- **Chapter 10.**
  - Opens: neither gods nor great seers know his origin, for he is their beginning (10.2).
  - Closes: "viṣṭabhyāham idaṃ kṛtsnam ekāṃśena sthito jagat" — I stand supporting this whole world with a single portion (10.42).
- **Chapter 11.**
  - Opens: "moho 'yaṃ vigato mama" (11.1), and Arjuna's request to see the sovereign form (11.3–4).
  - Closes: "by undivided devotion alone can I be known, seen and entered … whoever does my work … comes to me" (sa mām eti) (11.54–55).
- **Chapter 12.**
  - Opens: which worshippers know yoga best? (12.1).
  - Closes: the refrain "dear to me" (12.13–19), rising to "atīva me priyāḥ" (12.20).
- **Hinges.** "evam" in 12.1 points back to 11.55. "bhūyaḥ" in 10.18 points back to the shorter lists of 7.8–12 and 9.16–19.
- **Movement of the chunk.**
  - Ch. 10: the Lord as source of all, known and contemplated through his manifestations.
  - Ch. 11: the same seen directly in the universal form and as Time.
  - Ch. 12: how he is reached — undivided devotion, with graded fallbacks, ending in the marks of the devotee.

## 2. Repetition (abhyāsa)
- **Refrain in 12.13–20:** "(yo madbhaktaḥ) sa me priyaḥ" at 12.14, 12.15, 12.16, 12.17, 12.19, rising to "atīva me priyāḥ" (12.20).
- **The "mat-" compounds of devotion:**

  - matkarmakṛt, matparama, madbhakta (11.55)
  - matparāḥ (12.6), matkarmaparama (12.10), madyogam (12.11)
  - madbhakta (12.14, 12.16), matparamāḥ (12.20)
- **Undivided (ananya):** 11.54, 12.6.
- **Ever yoked:** satatayukta (10.10, 12.1), nityayukta (12.2); yuktatama (12.2, echoing 6.47).
- **Mind set on the Lord:** mayy āveśya / āveśita (12.2, 12.7); mayi mana ādhatsva, mayi buddhiṃ niveśaya (12.8); mayy arpitamanobuddhiḥ (12.14, echoing 8.7).
- **Faith (śraddhā):** 12.2, 12.20.
- **The manifestations:**
  - "divyā hy ātmavibhūtayaḥ" (10.16, 10.19).
  - "No end to my extent / manifestations" (10.19, 10.40).
  - The formula "of X I am Y" (10.20–38; strictly from 10.21).
- **Vision vocabulary (ch. 11):**

  - "No end, no middle, no beginning" (11.16, 11.19).

- **The excluded means:** Vedas, sacrifice, study, gifts, rites, austerity — 11.48, restated at 11.53.

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
- **The vision of the universal form**, possible only with a divine eye the Lord gives, because the ordinary eye cannot see it (11.8). The text stresses its novelty itself:
  - "adṛṣṭapūrvāṇi" (11.6), "adṛṣṭapūrvam" (11.45);
  - "yan me tvadanyena na dṛṣṭapūrvam" (11.47);
  - not to be won by Vedas, sacrifice, study, gifts, rites or austerity (11.48, 11.53), but by undivided devotion (11.54).
- **The Lord's self-disclosure as Time**, who has already slain the warriors, so that Arjuna is to be "merely the instrument" (nimitta-mātra) (11.32–34).
- **No one knows the Lord's origin:** neither gods nor seers; he alone knows himself by himself (10.2, 10.14–15).
- **The systematic list of manifestations** (10.20–39) and its rule: whatever is glorious or mighty springs from a portion of his splendour (10.41).
- **In ch. 12:**
  - the explicit comparison of worshipping the Lord with worshipping the imperishable, the unmanifest; the second path also reaches him, at greater cost (12.1–5);
  - the conditional ladder of means graded by capacity (12.8–11);
  - the ranking of 12.12, which puts relinquishing the fruit of actions above meditation, knowledge and practice, the reverse of its place in 12.11.

## 4. Stated result (phala)
**Chapter 10**
- Knowing the Lord as unborn, beginningless, great lord of the world: undeluded, freed from all sins (10.3).
- Knowing his vibhūti and yoga in truth: joined with unwavering yoga (10.7).
- The yoga of understanding given by the Lord, "by which they come to me" (10.10).
- The darkness born of ignorance destroyed by the lamp of knowledge (10.11).

**Chapter 11**
- Arjuna's delusion gone (11.1).
- Glory and a prosperous kingdom; "you will conquer your rivals" — the warriors being already slain (11.33–34).
- The Lord known, seen in truth and entered by undivided devotion (11.54).
- "sa mām eti" (11.55).

**Chapter 12**
- The devotees are "the most yoked" (12.2).
- The worshippers of the imperishable "too reach me", with greater hardship (12.4–5).
- Deliverance before long from the ocean of death and saṃsāra (12.7).
- "You will dwell in me" (12.8); perfection (siddhi) through work for the Lord (12.10).
- Peace immediately after relinquishing the fruit (12.12).
- Being dear, and exceedingly dear, to the Lord (12.13–20).

## 5. Praise (arthavāda)
- **Arjuna's praise** of Kṛṣṇa as supreme brahman, supreme abode, eternal divine Person. It rests on the testimony of the seers Nārada, Asita Devala and Vyāsa and on Kṛṣṇa's own word (10.12–15).
- **The list of manifestations as glorification** (10.19–41). It includes even gambling as the chief means of deceivers (10.36).
- **Arjuna's hymns** (11.15–22, 11.36–44):
  - "namo namas te 'stu sahasrakṛtvaḥ" (11.39);
  - "tato 'si sarvaḥ" (11.40);
  - "pitāsi lokasya … gurur garīyān" (11.43).
- **Praise of the vision's rarity:** no other has seen it (11.47); Vedas and rites cannot win it (11.48, 11.53); the gods ever long to see it (11.52).
- **Praise of the teaching:** "paramaṃ vacaḥ" (10.1), "paramaṃ guhyam" (11.1), "dharmyāmṛtam" (12.20). "yuktatamāḥ" (12.2) is a comparative commendation.
- **The rhetorical question** of 10.42 ("what use is this detailed knowledge?") puts the whole list beneath the single portion that supports the world.
- **12.12, praise or doctrine?** Its ranking of relinquishment above meditation, knowledge and practice runs opposite to the place relinquishment has in 12.11. Śaṅkara reads it as praise (stuti) meant to encourage those unable to follow the higher means (recalled). Whether 12.12 is praise or doctrine is the interpretive question for this mark.

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- **10.2 (hi):** the gods and seers cannot know his origin, because he is their beginning.
- **10.8 (iti matvā):** knowing him as the origin from which all proceeds, the wise worship him. Worship follows from understanding.
- **10.39–42:**
  - nothing moving or unmoving could exist without him, since he is the seed of all (10.39);
  - therefore every excellence springs from a portion of his splendour (10.41) — a rule that generalises the list;
  - one portion suffices to support the world (10.42).
- **11.8:** the ordinary eye cannot see him; therefore a divine eye is given.
- **11.32–33 (tasmāt):** the warriors are already slain by the Lord as Time; therefore Arjuna is to rise and be merely the instrument.
- **11.37:** why should they not bow to him, who is greater, the first maker (even of Brahmā)?
- **11.40:** "sarvaṃ samāpnoṣi tato 'si sarvaḥ" — you pervade all, therefore you are all.
- **11.44 (tasmāt):** therefore, bowing, I seek your grace.
- **12.5 (hi):** the unmanifest goal is hard for the embodied — the reason for the greater hardship.
- **12.8–11:** the means are graded by the hearer's capacity ("if you are unable …").
- **12.12:** relinquishment is ranked highest because peace follows it immediately.

## Contribution to the text-level thesis
- **What chapters 10–12 set out:** the Lord as origin, seed and support of all.
  - Ch. 10 declares this and illustrates it by his manifestations, the chief member of every class.
  - Ch. 11 shows it as the universal form and as Time.
- **The means.** Undivided devotion is named as the means by which he is known, seen in truth and entered (11.54). Those who fix the mind on him with supreme faith are "the most yoked" (12.2).
- **The other path.** Worship of the imperishable, the unmanifest, is acknowledged as also reaching him, but at greater cost for the embodied (12.3–5).
- **Graded practice.** It runs by capacity down to relinquishing the fruit of actions (12.8–12), which 12.12 then ranks highest.
- **The marks of the devotee** (12.13–19) close the chunk. They repeat phrases from the portraits of the one of steady wisdom (2.57, 2.71) and anticipate the one beyond the guṇas (14.24–25).
- **For the thesis writer.** The commentarial dispute over 12.1–5 (`dsp:bhagavad-gita-lord-or-unmanifest`) and over 12.12 bears directly on the purport. The question is whether these chapters teach devotion as the highest means, or devotion leading to knowledge of the imperishable (see `dsp:gita-primary-teaching`, `dsp:bhakti-jnana-precedence`). Śaṅkara (Advaita) and Rāmānuja (Viśiṣṭādvaita) divide exactly here (recalled; to be checked against the bhāṣyas in Wave 2).
