## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **The chunk as a whole.**
  - It opens with "imaṃ … yogam avyayam", this imperishable yoga (4.1). Kṛṣṇa handed it down to Vivasvat, Vivasvat to Manu and Manu to Ikṣvāku; the royal sages knew it; it was lost and is now given again to Arjuna "because you are my devotee and friend", as "the highest secret" (4.1–3).
  - It closes with "tasmād yogī bhavārjuna" (6.46) and "śraddhāvān bhajate yo māṃ sa me yuktatamo mataḥ" (6.47): the faithful worshipper is the most yoked (yuktatama).
  - So yoga frames the chunk at both ends, and both ends tie yoga to devotion: bhakta in 4.3, bhajate in 6.47.
- **Chapter 4.**
  - Opens: the transmission and loss of the yoga (4.1–3).
  - Closes: cut your doubt with the sword of knowledge, resort to yoga, stand up ("yogam ātiṣṭhottiṣṭha", 4.42).
- **Chapter 5.**
  - Opens: Arjuna asks which is better, renunciation (saṃnyāsa) or yoga (5.1).
  - Closes: knowing the Lord as enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities, great lord of all worlds and friend of all beings, one attains peace (5.29).
- **Chapter 6.**
  - Opens: the renouncer and the yogin are the one who does the required action without depending on its fruit (6.1–2).
  - Closes: be a yogin; the one who worships me with faith is the most yoked (6.46–47).
- **Exhortations at each chapter's turning points:**
  - "kuru karmaiva" (4.15);
  - "yogam ātiṣṭhottiṣṭha" (4.42);
  - "tasmād yogī bhava" (6.46).
- **Neighbouring verses.**
  - 3.43 (conquer kāma) leads into the desire-free action of 4.19–23.
  - 7.1 ("mayy āsaktamanāḥ … yogaṃ yuñjan madāśrayaḥ") picks up directly from 6.47, which works as a hinge into chs. 7–12.

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
New to the text in these chapters:
- **The Lord's repeated coming into being.** It is by his own māyā, age after age, whenever dharma declines. Knowing that his birth and action are divine frees one from rebirth (4.5–9). The word avatāra does not occur; the verbs are saṃbhavāmi and sṛjāmi.
- **The divine succession (paramparā) of the yoga** and its loss (4.1–3).
- **The Lord's reciprocity:** "as people approach me, so I receive them" (4.11).
- **The triad of action, wrong action and inaction** (karma / vikarma / akarma), and the paradox of seeing inaction in action (4.16–18).
- **Sacrifice widened** to inner and cognitive offerings (4.24–33):
  - every element of the offering is Brahman (4.24);
  - the senses, breath, study and knowledge are offered as sacrifices;
  - the sacrifice of knowledge is the best.
- **Sāṃkhya and Yoga are one in result** (5.4–5), developing the twofold commitment of 3.3; renunciation is yoga (6.2).
- **The body as the "city of nine gates"** (5.13); cf. Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 3.18.
- **The stage distinction:** action is the means for the one wishing to ascend to yoga (ārurukṣu), calm (śama) for the one who has ascended (yogārūḍha) (6.3–4).
- **Concrete meditation instruction:** solitude, the seat, posture, gaze, and moderation in food and sleep (6.10–17).
- **Yoga defined** as "the unyoking from union with sorrow" (duḥkha-saṃyoga-viyoga) (6.20–23). Compare 2.48 and 2.50.
- **The fallen yogin is not lost.** He is reborn and carried on by former practice (6.37–45).
- **The devotee-yogin is the most yoked** of all (6.47).

## 5. Praise and blame (arthavāda)
**Praise:**
- the yoga is imperishable, ancient and "the highest secret" (4.1–3);
- knowledge is the unequalled purifier (4.38), a raft even for the worst evil-doer (4.36, framed as a hypothetical), and a fire that burns all actions to ashes (4.37);
- the sacrifice of knowledge is better than material sacrifice (4.33);
- karma-yoga is superior to renunciation (5.2);
- "whoever sees them as one truly sees" (5.5);
- the equal-seeing yogin is held supreme (6.32);
- a birth in a family of yogins is rarer (6.42);
- the yogin is greater than ascetics, knowers and performers of works (6.46).

**Blame:**
- no world, not even this one, for the one who does not sacrifice (4.31);
- the doubter perishes, with neither this world nor the next (4.40);
- only "children" call Sāṃkhya and Yoga separate (5.4);
- contact-born pleasures are wombs of sorrow (5.22).

**For the thesis merger:** 4.36, 5.2 and 6.46 are comparative praise and are the obvious candidates for P7 (praise passages). The commentators divide on how far the superiority stated in 5.2 reaches: Śaṅkara says only for one without knowledge; Rāmānuja says without that restriction. The merged teachings record this in their notes.

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
**Arguments:**
- **From the Lord's example:** he made the fourfold class order yet is a non-doer, and actions do not stain him; knowing this, the ancients acted, so Arjuna should act (4.13–15).
- **From the nature of sacrifice:** every sacrifice is born of action, and knowing this frees (4.32); all action culminates in knowledge (4.33).
- **From an analysis of agency:**
  - the senses move among their objects, so the knower thinks "I do nothing" (5.8–9);
  - the master (prabhu) creates no agency, and inherent nature (svabhāva) acts (5.14);
  - delusion comes from knowledge covered by ignorance (5.15).
- **Same goal:** Sāṃkhya and Yoga reach the same place, so they are one (5.4–5).
- **Sameness:** Brahman is flawless and the same, so the equal-minded are established in Brahman (5.19).
- **Pleasures:** contact-born pleasures have a beginning and an end, so the wise do not delight in them (5.22).
- **Renunciation is yoga:** no one is a yogin without renouncing intention (6.2).
- **Self-effort:** the self is its own friend or enemy (6.5–6).
- **Moderation** (6.16–17).

**Objection and answer (pūrvapakṣa–siddhānta):**
- 4.4–5, on Kṛṣṇa's birth;
- 5.1–6, renunciation versus yoga;
- 6.33–36, the restless mind; the answer is practice and dispassion;
- 6.37–45, the fallen yogin; the answer is that no doer of good comes to a bad end (6.40) and former practice carries over (6.43–44).

**Similes:**
- fire and fuel (4.37);
- the raft (4.36);
- the sword that cuts doubt (4.42);
- the lotus leaf untouched by water (5.10);
- the sun dispelling ignorance (5.16);
- the city of nine gates (5.13);
- the lamp in a windless place (6.19);
- restraining the mind like restraining the wind (6.34);
- the torn cloud (6.38).

## Tentative contribution to the text-level thesis
In chs. 4–6, yoga is the means to peace, brahma-nirvāṇa and the Lord. It is set out in three steps:
- action done without attachment, as sacrifice, culminating in knowledge (ch. 4);
- inner renunciation, which is one with Sāṃkhya in its result (ch. 5);
- the discipline of meditation, culminating in seeing the self in all and the Lord everywhere (ch. 6).

Both ends of the chunk tie yoga to devotion (4.3, 6.47). The chunk ends by placing the faithful worshipper of the Lord at the summit of yoga, which leads directly into ch. 7 (7.1).
