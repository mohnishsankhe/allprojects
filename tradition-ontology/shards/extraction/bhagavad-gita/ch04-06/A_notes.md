# Bhagavad Gītā ch. 4–6 — six marks of purport (extractor A)

## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **Chunk frame.** The chunk opens with "this imperishable yoga" taught to Vivasvat (4.1–3). It closes with the "most yoked" (yuktatama) yogin (6.47). So yoga frames the chunk from its first verse to its last.
- **Chapter 4** opens with the lineage and loss of the yoga (4.1–3). It closes with "cut doubt with the sword of knowledge, resort to yoga, stand up" (4.42).
- **Chapter 5** opens with Arjuna's question, renunciation or yoga (5.1). It closes with peace from knowing the Lord as enjoyer of sacrifices, great lord of all worlds and friend of all beings (5.29).
- **Chapter 6** opens with "the renunciant and yogin is the one who does the required action without depending on its fruit" (6.1). It closes with the faithful worshipper of the Lord as the most yoked (6.47).
- **Exhortations** frame each chapter: "kuru karma" (4.15), "yogam ātiṣṭha, uttiṣṭha" (4.42), "tasmād yogī bhava" (6.46).

## 2. Repetition (abhyāsa)
- **yukta / yoga-yukta, "the yoked one":** 4.18; 5.6–8, 5.12, 5.21, 5.23; 6.8, 6.14, 6.17–18, 6.29, 6.47.
- **Acting without attachment to the fruit:** 4.14, 4.20, 5.10–12, 6.1.
- **Not stained or bound though acting:** 4.14, 4.22, 4.41, 5.7, 5.10.
- **Knowledge as supreme:**
  - it is the goal of all action (4.33);
  - it is a raft, a fire and the supreme purifier (4.36–38);
  - it drives out ignorance like the sun (5.16).
- **Sameness / equal vision:** 4.22; 5.18–19; 6.7–9, 6.29–33.
- **Brahman, brahma-nirvāṇa, brahmabhūta:** 4.24, 4.31; 5.6, 5.10, 5.19–26; 6.15, 6.27–28.
- **Peace (śānti):** 4.39, 5.12, 5.29, 6.15.
- **Freedom from desire, anger and fear:** 4.10, 4.19; 5.23, 5.26, 5.28; 6.14, 6.18, 6.24.
- **The Lord as refuge and goal ("me"):** 4.9–11, 4.35, 5.29, 6.14–15, 6.30–31, 6.47.
- **Doubt cut by knowledge or by the teacher:** 4.40–42, 5.25, 6.39.

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
- **The Lord's coming into being** (4.5–9). He is unborn, yet comes into being by his own māyā whenever dharma declines, age after age. Knowing his divine birth and action frees one from rebirth.
- **Seeing inaction in action** and action in inaction (4.18).
- **The idea of sacrifice widened** to every discipline (4.24–33):
  - every element of the offering is Brahman;
  - the senses, the breath, study and knowledge are all offered as sacrifices;
  - the sacrifice of knowledge is the best.
- **The Lord's reciprocity:** "as people approach me, so I receive them" (4.11).
- **Sāṃkhya and Yoga are one;** renunciation is yoga (5.4–5, 6.2).
- **Two stages, each with its own means:** action for the one ascending, calm for the one who has ascended (6.3–4).
- **Yoga defined** as the disconnection from union with suffering (duḥkha-saṃyoga-viyoga, 6.23).
- **The fallen yogin is not lost.** He is reborn and carried on by former practice (6.40–45).
- **The devotee-yogin** is the most yoked of all (6.47).

## 4. Stated result (phala)
- **Freedom from rebirth and reaching the Lord:** not reborn, comes to me (4.9); came to my being (4.10); comes to the eternal Brahman (4.31); non-return (5.17).
- **Liberation:** freed from the inauspicious (4.16); freed (4.32); crosses all wrong (4.36); ever liberated (5.28).
- **Peace:** supreme peace (4.39); the peace of steadfastness (5.12); peace (5.29); peace culminating in nirvāṇa and abiding in the Lord (6.15).
- **Brahman and brahma-nirvāṇa:** reaches Brahman soon (5.6); brahma-nirvāṇa (5.24–26).
- **Happiness:** imperishable happiness (5.21); boundless happiness (6.21); the highest happiness and contact with Brahman (6.27–28).
- **End of suffering:** yoga destroys suffering (6.17, 6.23).
- **Not lost to the Lord:** "he is not lost to me" (6.30).
- **The highest goal** (6.45).
- **Not bound by action:** 4.14, 4.22, 4.41.

## 5. Praise (arthavāda)
**Praise:**
- the teaching is "the highest secret" (4.3);
- nothing purifies like knowledge (4.38);
- even the worst sinner crosses over by the raft of knowledge (4.36, framed as a hypothetical);
- the fire of knowledge burns all actions (4.37);
- the yogin is greater than ascetics, knowers and performers of works (6.46);
- a birth in a family of yogins is rarer (6.42);
- "whoever sees them as one truly sees" (5.5).

**Blame:**
- no world, not even this one, for the one who does not sacrifice (4.31);
- the doubter perishes, with neither this world nor the next (4.40);
- only "children" call Sāṃkhya and Yoga separate (5.4);
- contact-born pleasures are wombs of suffering (5.22).

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
**Similes:**
- fire and firewood (4.37);
- the raft (4.36);
- the sword that cuts doubt (4.42);
- the lotus leaf untouched by water (5.10);
- the sun dispelling ignorance (5.16);
- the city of nine gates (5.13);
- the lamp in a windless place (6.19);
- restraining the mind like restraining the wind (6.34);
- the scattered cloud (6.38).

**Arguments:**
- **Example to follow:** the Lord acts yet is unstained, and the ancients acted knowing this, so Arjuna should act (4.13–15).
- **Non-agency:** the senses move among their objects, so the knower does nothing (5.8–9); nature acts, not the master (5.14).
- **Same goal:** Sāṃkhya and Yoga reach the same place, so they are one (5.5).
- **Sameness:** Brahman is flawless and the same, so the equal-minded are established in Brahman (5.19).
- **Pleasure:** contact-born pleasures have a beginning and an end, so the wise do not delight in them (5.22).
- **Renunciation is yoga:** no one is a yogin without renouncing intention (6.2).
- **The fallen yogin:** no doer of good comes to a bad end, and former practice carries him on (6.40–44).

## Tentative contribution to the text-level thesis
In chs. 4–6, yoga is the means to peace, brahma-nirvāṇa and the Lord. It is set out as:
- action done without attachment, as sacrifice, and culminating in knowledge (ch. 4);
- inner renunciation that is one with Sāṃkhya (ch. 5);
- the discipline of meditation, culminating in seeing the self and the Lord in all (ch. 6).

The chunk ends by placing the faithful devotee of the Lord at the summit of yoga (6.47), and this leads directly into ch. 7 (7.1).
