# Six marks of purport (ṣaḍ-liṅga): Bhagavad Gītā chunk ch04-06 (extractor B)

## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **The chunk as a whole:**
  - It opens with "imaṃ … yogam avyayam" (4.1): the imperishable yoga, handed down from Kṛṣṇa through Vivasvat, Manu and Ikṣvāku to the royal sages. Kṛṣṇa gives it to Arjuna "because you are my devotee and friend" (4.3).
  - It closes with "tasmād yogī bhavārjuna" (6.46) and "śraddhāvān bhajate yo māṃ sa me yuktatamo mataḥ" (6.47).
  - Both ends are framed by yoga, and both tie yoga to devotion (bhakta in 4.3, bhajate in 6.47).
- **Chapter 4** opens with the transmission of the yoga (4.1). It closes by telling Arjuna to cut his doubt with the sword of knowledge, resort to yoga and stand up: "yogam ātiṣṭhottiṣṭha" (4.42).
- **Chapter 5** opens with Arjuna asking whether renunciation (saṃnyāsa) or yoga is better (5.1). It closes with the promise that knowing the Lord as enjoyer of sacrifices, great lord of the worlds and friend of all beings brings peace (5.29).
- **Chapter 6** opens by saying the true renouncer is the yogin (6.1–2). It closes with "be a yogin" and "the one who worships me with faith is the most yoked" (6.46–47).
- **Neighbouring verses:**
  - Chapter 3 ends by telling Arjuna to conquer kāma (3.43), which leads into the desire-free action of 4.19–23.
  - 7.1 ("mayy āsaktamanāḥ … yogaṃ yuñjan madāśrayaḥ") picks up directly from 6.47, so 6.47 works as a hinge into chapters 7–12.

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
New to the text in these chapters:
- The Lord's repeated births through his own māyā, age after age, for the sake of dharma. Knowing that his birth and action are divine frees one from rebirth (4.5–9). The word avatāra does not occur.
- The yoga's divine line of succession (paramparā) (4.1–3).
- The triad karma / vikarma / akarma, and the paradox of seeing inaction in action (4.16–18).
- Sacrifice (yajña) reread as inner and cognitive offerings, crowned by the sacrifice of knowledge (jñāna-yajña) (4.24–33).
- The claim that Sāṃkhya and Yoga are one in result (5.4–5), developing the two disciplines of 3.3.
- The body as the "city of nine gates" (5.13).
- The stage distinction: action is the means for one wishing to ascend to yoga (ārurukṣu), calm for one who has ascended (yogārūḍha) (6.3–4).
- Concrete meditation instructions: solitude, seat, posture, gaze, and moderation in food and sleep (6.10–17).
- A new definition of yoga as "the unyoking from union with sorrow" (duḥkha-saṃyoga-viyoga) (6.23). Compare the earlier definitions in 2.48 and 2.50.
- The fate of the yogin who falls away, and the carrying-over of practice across births (6.37–45).

## 5. Praise and blame (arthavāda)
- **Praise:**
  - The yoga is imperishable, ancient and "the highest secret" (4.1–3).
  - Knowledge is the unequalled purifier (4.38), a raft even for the worst sinner (4.36), and a fire that burns all actions to ashes (4.37).
  - The sacrifice of knowledge is better than the sacrifice of material things (4.33).
  - Karma-yoga is better than renunciation (5.2).
  - The equal-seeing yogin is "held supreme" (6.32).
  - The yogin is greater than ascetics, knowers and ritualists (6.46).
  - A birth among yogins is "harder to obtain" (6.42).
- **Blame:**
  - The doubter has neither this world nor the next (4.40).
  - The one who does not sacrifice does not have even this world (4.31).
  - Only "children" call Sāṃkhya and Yoga separate (5.4).
  - Pleasures born of contact are "wombs of sorrow" (5.22).
- **For the merger:** 4.36, 5.2 and 6.46 are comparative praise. They are the obvious candidates for handling under P7 (praise passages), and commentators already divide on how far 5.2's superiority reaches.

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- **From the Lord's example:** he made the four-class order yet is a non-doer, and actions do not stain him. The ancients acted knowing this, so Arjuna should act (4.13–15).
- **From the nature of sacrifice:** every sacrifice is born of action, and knowing this frees (4.32). All action culminates in knowledge (4.33).
- **From an analysis of agency:**
  - The senses move among their objects, so the knower thinks "I do nothing" (5.8–9).
  - The lord creates no agency, and it is svabhāva that acts. Delusion comes from knowledge being covered by ignorance (5.14–15).
- **Why the equal-minded are in Brahman:** Brahman is flawless and the same (5.19).
- **Why not to delight in pleasures:** they arise from contact and have a beginning and an end (5.22).
- **The basis for self-effort:** the self is its own friend or enemy (6.5–6).
- **The case for moderation:** 6.16–17.
- **Objection and answer (pūrvapakṣa–siddhānta):**
  - 4.4–5, on Kṛṣṇa's birth.
  - 5.1–6, on renunciation versus yoga.
  - 6.33–36, on the restless mind; the answer is practice and dispassion.
  - 6.37–45, on the fallen yogin; the answer is that no one who does good comes to a bad end (6.40), and former practice carries over (6.43–44).
- **Analogies:** fire and fuel (4.37), raft (4.36), sun (5.16), lotus leaf and water (5.10), city of nine gates (5.13), lamp in a windless place (6.19), wind (6.34), torn cloud (6.38).
