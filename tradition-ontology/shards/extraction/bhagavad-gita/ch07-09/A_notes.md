# BhG ch. 7–9 — extractor A: the six marks of purport (ṣaḍ-liṅga) for this chunk

## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **The chunk as a whole** has a clear frame.
  - It opens at 7.1: *mayy āsakta-manāḥ … mad-āśrayaḥ* (mind attached to me, refuge in me), with the promise to know the Lord *samagram*, "entire".
  - It closes at 9.34: *man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī māṃ namaskuru … mām evaiṣyasi … mat-parāyaṇaḥ*.
  - Both ends state the same thing: fixing the mind on the Lord and taking refuge in him.
  - 9.1 (*jñānaṃ vijñāna-sahitam*) repeats the 7.2 announcement (*jñānaṃ savijñānam*) word for word, so chs. 7 and 9 are one arc with ch. 8 inside it.
- **Ch. 7** opens with knowledge plus *vijñāna* "after which nothing remains to be known" (7.1–2). It closes with knowing Brahman, *adhyātma*, *karma*, *adhibhūta*, *adhidaiva* and *adhiyajña*, and knowing him even at death (7.29–30).
- **Ch. 8** opens with Arjuna's seven questions (8.1–2). It closes with "at all times be yoked in yoga" and "the yogin passes beyond all merit to the supreme primal place" (8.27–28).
- **Ch. 9** opens with the "most secret" royal knowledge (9.1–2). It closes with the 9.34 injunction, which recurs verbatim at 18.65.

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
**What the text claims for itself:**
- The teaching is "most secret", the "royal secret" (9.1–2).
- It is "known by direct apprehension" (9.2).
- After it "nothing remains to be known" (7.2).

**New in these chapters:**
- The Lord's two natures: the eightfold lower *prakṛti* and the higher one "become the living being" (7.4–5).
- Definitions of the six terms, with *adhiyajña* as "I, here in the body" (8.3–4).
- The "lordly yoga" paradox, left unresolved: beings in me, yet not in me (9.4–5).
- Worship of other deities is worship of the Lord "not according to the rule", and he himself steadies that faith and grants its fruits (7.21–22, 9.23).
- The simplest offering (leaf, flower, fruit, water) is accepted (9.26).
- Every act can be made an offering (9.27–28).
- The highest goal is open to women, vaiśyas and śūdras, and to those of sinful birth (9.32).

**Not new:** 8.11 = Kaṭha 1.2.15; 8.9 echoes Śvetāśvatara 3.8 and Kaṭha 1.2.20; 8.24–25 = the Upaniṣadic two paths (Chāndogya 5.10; Bṛhadāraṇyaka 6.2.15–16); 8.17 ≈ Manusmṛti 1.72–73. The new element is tying these to remembrance of the Lord (8.13 *mām anusmaran*).

## 4. Stated result (phala)
**For devotion and knowledge:**
- Nothing further to know (7.2).
- Crossing *māyā* (7.14).
- Devotees come to him (7.23).
- Knowing him at death (7.30).
- Coming to his state (8.5).
- Coming to him without doubt (8.7, 9.34).
- Reaching the supreme divine Person or the supreme goal (8.8, 8.10, 8.13).
- He is "easy to reach" (8.14).
- No rebirth or return (8.15–16, 8.21).
- Knowers of Brahman go to Brahman (8.24).
- Passing beyond all merit of Veda, sacrifice, austerity and gift (8.28).
- Release from the inauspicious (9.1).
- He carries the devotee's *yoga-kṣema* (9.22).
- Freedom from the bonds of action (9.28).
- Lasting peace; "my devotee does not perish" (9.31).
- The highest goal for all who take refuge (9.32).

**Contrasting results:**
- Finite fruit (7.23).
- Return from every world up to Brahmā's (8.16).
- The lunar light and return (8.25).
- Return to death and *saṃsāra* for the faithless (9.3).
- Return when merit is spent (9.21).
- "They fall" (9.24).

## 5. Praise and blame (arthavāda)
**Praise:**
- The teaching: *rāja-vidyā*, *rāja-guhya*, *pavitram uttamam*, *susukhaṃ kartum*, *avyayam* (9.2).
- The *jñānin*: dear, "my very self" (7.17–18).
- The *mahātman*: *sudurlabhaḥ*, very hard to find (7.19).
- The yogin's knowledge surpasses all merit (8.28).
- The devotee of very bad conduct is "to be counted good" (9.30–31).
- The a fortiori *kiṃ punaḥ* for brāhmaṇas and royal seers (9.33).

**Blame (nindā):**
- *duṣkṛtinaḥ … narādhamāḥ* (7.15).
- *hṛta-jñānāḥ*, *alpa-medhasaḥ* (7.20, 7.23).
- *abuddhayaḥ* (7.24).
- *mūḍhāḥ*, *mogha-āśāḥ …* (9.11–12).
- *aśraddadhānāḥ* (9.3).
- *kāma-kāmāḥ* (9.21).

These passages urge the reader toward refuge and devotion. The thesis writer should weigh them by what they urge rather than read them as separate doctrinal claims (P7).

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- **Illustrations:** thread and gems (7.7); wind abiding in space (9.6, for 9.4–5).
- **Inference:** one goes to what one remembers at death, and one remembers what one has always dwelt on (8.6); "therefore" (*tasmāt*) remember me at all times (8.7). The same *tasmāt* pattern appears at 8.27.
- **Cosmological argument:**
  - every world up to Brahmā's returns, within the cycle of Brahmā's day and night (8.16–19);
  - only the eternal unmanifest beyond the cosmic unmanifest does not perish (8.20);
  - so it alone is non-return (8.21).
- **Stated reasons (*hi*, *ataḥ*, *hetunā anena*):**
  - The world does not know him because the guṇas delude it (7.13); *māyā* is crossed only by refuge (7.14, *hi*).
  - The desires granted are "ordained by me" (7.22).
  - His acts do not bind him because he is "seated as one indifferent" (9.9).
  - The world revolves "for this reason": *prakṛti* produces under his oversight (9.10).
  - Other-deity worshippers fall because he is the enjoyer of all sacrifices and they do not know him in truth (9.24).
  - The ill-conducted devotee is good because "he has resolved rightly" (9.30).
  - How much more (*kiṃ punaḥ*) for brāhmaṇas and royal seers (9.33).

## Input for the thesis entry
**The chunk's purport by these marks:**
- The subject is knowledge of the Lord "entire": source, support, overseer, enjoyer of sacrifice and goal of all, above and beyond the guṇas and the cosmic unmanifest.
- The means is constant remembrance and undivided refuge and devotion, joined to one's duty (8.7) and to offering every act (9.27).
- These are presented as surpassing ritual merit and open to everyone.

**Tensions to carry forward, not resolved here:**

- 9.29 ("none dear") against 7.17 and 12.14–20.
- 7.23 (finite fruit of other-deity worship) against 9.23 (it is worship of him, not by the rule).
- The two "unmanifests" of 8.18–20 against the Lord's own *avyakta* nature (7.24, 9.4).
- The status of those named in 9.32.
