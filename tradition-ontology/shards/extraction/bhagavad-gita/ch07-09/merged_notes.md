## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **The chunk as a whole.**
  - It opens at 7.1, continuing directly from 6.47, where the one who worships the Lord with faith is called the most yoked.
    - "mayy āsakta-manāḥ … yogaṃ yuñjan mad-āśrayaḥ": with his mind attached to the Lord, practising yoga and taking refuge in him, Arjuna will know him "entire" (samagram), without doubt.
    - 7.2 offers "jñānaṃ savijñānam", after which nothing further remains to be known.
  - It closes at 9.34: "man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī māṃ namaskuru … mām evaiṣyasi … mat-parāyaṇaḥ". Its first line recurs at 18.65.
  - Both ends say the same thing: the mind is fixed on the Lord, and one takes refuge in him.
    - The unit opens with knowing the Lord entirely.
    - It closes with mind, devotion, worship and homage directed to him, and with reaching him.
  - 9.1 repeats the announcement of 7.2 ("jñānaṃ vijñāna-sahitam"), now calling it most secret (guhyatama). So chs. 7 and 9 bracket one unit on knowing the Lord, with ch. 8 inside it.
- **Chapter 7.**
  - Opens: knowledge with vijñāna of the Lord entire, after which nothing remains to be known (7.1–2); and the rarity of those who know him in truth (7.3).
  - Closes: those who take refuge and strive for release from old age and death know Brahman, the adhyātma and action completely (7.29).
  - Those who know him together with the adhibhūta, adhidaiva and adhiyajña know him even at the time of departure (7.30).
- **Chapter 8.**
  - Opens: Arjuna asks seven questions about the six terms left undefined at 7.29–30 and about how the Lord is known at departure (8.1–2).
  - Closes: "at all times be yoked in yoga" (8.27). Having known this, the yogin goes beyond all the merit of Vedas, sacrifices, austerities and gifts to the supreme, primal place (8.28).
- **Chapter 9.**
  - Opens: the most secret, royal knowledge (9.1–2).
  - Closes: the injunction of 9.34.
- **Hinges.**
  - 7.29–30 leads into 8.1–2: terms are left open, then asked about.
  - 9.1 resumes 7.2.

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
**What the text claims for itself:**
- The teaching is "most secret" and "the royal secret" (9.1–2).
- It is "directly apprehended" (pratyakṣāvagama, 9.2).
- After it, "nothing remains to be known" (7.2).

**New in these chapters:**
- **The Lord's two natures.** The lower is the eightfold prakṛti. The higher "has become the living being" and upholds the world (7.4–5).
- **The Lord as the essence in things and as every element of the rite.** The word vibhūti is not used in these verses.
  - He is the taste in water, the light of sun and moon, Oṃ in the Vedas, and so on (7.8–11).
  - He is every element of the rite; father, mother, sustainer; goal, witness, refuge and friend; immortality and death, being and non-being (9.16–19).
- **The four kinds of virtuous worshippers.**
  - Among them the knower is "my very self" (7.16–18).
  - The formula "vāsudevaḥ sarvam" appears at 7.19.
- **The Gītā's own definitions of six terms.** Brahman, adhyātma, karma, adhibhūta, adhidaivata and adhiyajña, the last being "I myself, here in the body" (8.3–4).
- **The last thought at death.** Remembrance "at all times" is joined to one's duty: "remember me and fight" (8.5–7).
- **Two "unmanifests".**
  - One is cosmic: beings dissolve into it.
  - The other is eternal and beyond it: the imperishable, the Lord's supreme abode (8.18–21).
- **The lordly yoga ("yogam aiśvaram").**
  - Beings are in him and he is not in them; and yet beings are not in him (9.4–5; cf. 7.12).
  - The text leaves this paradox unresolved.
- **Worship of other deities.**
  - It is worship of the Lord "not according to the rule" (9.23).
  - The Lord steadies that faith and ordains its fruits (7.21–22).
  - He is the enjoyer and lord of all sacrifices (9.24).
- **The simplest offering and the offering of every act.**
  - A leaf, flower, fruit or water is accepted when given with devotion (9.26).
  - Every act is to be done as an offering to him, called saṃnyāsa-yoga (9.27–28).
- **The highest goal is explicitly open to all who take refuge**, including those of sinful birth, women, vaiśyas and śūdras (9.32).

**Taken over and reframed:**
- 8.11 ≈ Kaṭha 1.2.15.
- 8.9 echoes Śvetāśvatara 3.8 and Kaṭha 1.2.20.
- Oṃ as "the one-syllabled Brahman" (8.13) ≈ Kaṭha 1.2.16.
- The two paths of 8.24–25 ≈ Chāndogya 5.10 and Bṛhadāraṇyaka 6.2.15–16. The Gītā does not use the names devayāna and pitṛyāna.
- Brahmā's day and night (8.17) ≈ Manusmṛti 1.72–73.

In each case the new element is that the goal is the Lord ("remembering me", 8.13; "my supreme abode", 8.21), reached by undivided devotion (8.22).

## 4. Stated result (phala)
**For knowledge, remembrance, refuge and devotion:**
- Nothing further to know (7.2).
- Crossing the Lord's māyā (7.14).
- His devotees come to him (7.23).
- Knowing Brahman, the adhyātma and action completely (7.29), and knowing him even at departure (7.30).
- Coming to his state (8.5); coming to him without doubt (8.7, 9.34).
- Reaching the supreme divine Person (8.8, 8.9–10) and the supreme goal (8.12–13; 9.32).
- He is "easy to reach" for the ever-yoked (8.14).
- No rebirth and no return (8.15–16, 8.21).
- Knowers of Brahman go to Brahman (8.24).
- The yogin who has known this passes beyond all the merit of Vedas, sacrifices, austerities and gifts (8.28).
- Release from the inauspicious (9.1) and from the bonds of action (9.28).
- He carries the gain and security (yoga-kṣema) of his undivided devotees (9.22).
- The ill-conducted devotee quickly becomes righteous and gains lasting peace: "my devotee does not perish" (9.31).

**Contrasting results:**
- Worshippers of other deities get finite fruit and go to the gods (7.23; 9.25).
- Every world up to Brahmā's is subject to return (8.16).
- The dark course leads to the lunar light and return (8.25).
- The faithless return to the path of death and saṃsāra (9.3).
- Heaven, then return when merit is spent: "going and coming" (9.20–21).
- Those who do not know him in truth "fall" (9.24).

## 5. Praise and blame (arthavāda)
**Praise:**
- **The teaching:** rāja-vidyā, rāja-guhya, the supreme purifier, directly apprehended, in accord with dharma, very easy to practise, imperishable (9.2); most secret (9.1).
- **The knower:** rare (7.3; "sudurlabhaḥ", 7.19); "my very self" (7.18); he and the Lord are mutually dear (7.17).
- **The yogin who has known this:** beyond all ritual merit (8.28).
- **The power of devotion:**
  - The very ill-conducted devotee "is to be considered good" (9.30).
  - The a fortiori "kiṃ punar" for brāhmaṇas and royal seers (9.33).

**Blame (nindā):**
- "duṣkṛtinaḥ … narādhamāḥ", evil-doers, the lowest of men (7.15).
- "hṛta-jñānāḥ", their knowledge carried off (7.15, 7.20).
- "alpa-medhasaḥ", of little understanding (7.23).
- "abuddhayaḥ", the unintelligent (7.24).
- "mūḍhāḥ … moghāśā moghakarmāṇo moghajñānāḥ", the deluded, of vain hopes, acts and knowledge (9.11–12).
- "aśraddadhānāḥ", the faithless (9.3).
- "kāma-kāmāḥ", desiring desires (9.21).

These passages urge refuge and devotion. For the interpretation layer they are candidates for P7-arthavāda: the thesis writer should weigh them by what they urge rather than read each as a separate doctrinal claim. The text layer records them as stated.

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- **From the last thought (8.6 → 8.7).** One goes to whatever state one remembers at the end, having always been shaped by it. *Therefore* (tasmāt) remember me at all times, and fight.
- **8.27.** Knowing the two paths, no yogin is deluded. *Therefore* be yoked at all times.
- **7.13–14.** The world does not know the Lord because it is deluded by the guṇa-made states. *For* (hi) his guṇa-made māyā is hard to cross, and refuge in him alone is the way across.
- **7.21–22.** Why worship of other deities bears fruit: the Lord makes the faith firm, and the desires obtained are "ordained by me".
- **9.9–10.** The acts of emission do not bind him, because he is seated as one indifferent and unattached. With him as overseer prakṛti brings forth, and *for this reason* (hetunā anena) the world revolves.
- **9.24.** He is the enjoyer and lord of all sacrifices, but they do not know him in truth; *hence* (ataḥ) they fall.
- **9.30.** The ill-conducted devotee is to be considered good, *for* "he has resolved rightly" (samyag vyavasito hi saḥ).
- **9.33, a fortiori (kiṃ punar).** If those of sinful birth, women, vaiśyas and śūdras reach the highest goal, how much more holy brāhmaṇas and royal seers.
- **Cosmological argument (8.16–21):**
  1. Every world up to Brahmā's lies within the cycle of Brahmā's day and night, in which beings arise from the unmanifest and dissolve into it again and again (8.17–19). So these worlds are subject to return (8.16).
  2. The other, eternal unmanifest does not perish when beings perish (8.20).
  3. So reaching it, the Lord's supreme abode, is non-return (8.21).
- **Illustrations (dṛṣṭānta):**
  - Gems strung on a thread (7.7).
  - The great wind that moves everywhere yet always abides in space (9.6, illustrating 9.4–5).

## Tentative contribution to the text-level thesis
By these marks, the purport of chs. 7–9 is knowledge of the Lord "entire" (samagra). He is:
- the source and dissolution of the world;
- its support and overseer;
- the enjoyer of every sacrifice and the goal of all;
- above the guṇas and beyond the cosmic unmanifest.

The means is constant remembrance and undivided refuge and devotion, joined to one's duty (8.7) and to the offering of every act (9.27–28). The text presents this means as surpassing the merit of Vedic rites (8.28, 9.20–21) and as open to all who take refuge (9.32).

The unit opens with knowledge (7.1–2) and closes with devotion (9.34). 9.1 ties the two ends together, and the first line of 9.34 returns at 18.65.

**Pairs not yet reconciled.** The text states both sides of each; they are carried forward, not resolved here.
- 9.4 ("all beings abide in me") beside 9.5 ("and yet beings do not abide in me"), which the text itself calls his lordly yoga.
- 9.29 ("none is dear to me") beside 7.17 and 12.14–20, where the knower and the devotee are dear to him.
- 7.23 (worship of other deities bears finite fruit; its worshippers go to the gods) beside 9.23 (it is worship of him, though not according to the rule).
- The two unmanifests of 8.18–21 beside the Lord's own unmanifest form (7.24, 9.4).
- The standing of those named in 9.32: whether pāpa-yonayaḥ names a further group or qualifies women, vaiśyas and śūdras.
- Knowledge (jñāna with vijñāna, 7.2, 9.1) and devotion (the knower as the foremost devotee, 7.17) as the chunk's two emphases. This bears on dsp:gita-primary-teaching and dsp:bhakti-jnana-precedence.
