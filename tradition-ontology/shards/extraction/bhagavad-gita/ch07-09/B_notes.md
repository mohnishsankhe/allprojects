# BhG ch. 7–9 — extractor B: contribution to the six marks of purport (ṣaḍ-liṅga)
_(Returned as text by the subagent — subagents cannot write Markdown files in this harness — and saved verbatim by the orchestrator.)_


## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **Opening.** 7.1–2 continues directly from 6.47 (the most-yoked yogin is the one who worships the Lord with faith). It promises that Arjuna, "mind attached to me, practising yoga, taking refuge in me", will know the Lord "entirely, without doubt" (samagraṃ mām). It offers "jñāna with vijñāna, knowing which nothing further remains to be known".
- **The bracket.** 9.1 reopens with the same formula, "jñānaṃ vijñāna-sahitam", now called most secret (guhyatama), so chapters 7–9 form a single bracketed unit on knowing the Lord.
- **Internal hinges.**
  - 7.29–30 leaves six terms undefined (brahman, adhyātma, karma, adhibhūta, adhidaiva, adhiyajña) and speaks of knowing the Lord "at the time of departure". Arjuna's questions in 8.1–2 then open chapter 8.
  - 8.28 closes chapter 8: the yogin surpasses all the merit of Vedas, sacrifices, austerities and gifts and reaches the supreme, primal abode.
- **Closing.** 9.34 ends the unit: "manmanā bhava madbhakto madyājī māṃ namaskuru … mām evaiṣyasi … matparāyaṇaḥ". It is repeated nearly verbatim at 18.65.
- **Arc.** The unit opens with *knowing the Lord entirely* and closes with *mind, devotion, worship and homage directed to him, and reaching him*.

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā) — what this chunk states that is not found earlier in the Gītā or in its Upaniṣadic parallels
- The Lord's twofold nature: the eightfold lower prakṛti and the higher one "that has become the jīva" (7.4–5).
- The four kinds of virtuous worshippers, with the knower as "my very self" (7.16–18).
- The formula "vāsudevaḥ sarvam" (7.19).
- The Gītā's own definitions of adhyātma, karma, adhibhūta, adhidaivata and adhiyajña ("I myself, here in the body", 8.3–4).
- The paradox named "yogam aiśvaram": beings in me, I not in them, and yet beings not in me (9.4–5).
- Worship of other deities as worship of the Lord "not according to rule" (9.23), with the Lord as enjoyer and lord of all sacrifices (9.24).
- A leaf, flower, fruit or water offered in devotion is accepted (9.26).
- Every act done as an offering to the Lord, called saṃnyāsa-yoga (9.27–28).
- The explicit extension of the highest goal to those of sinful birth, women, vaiśyas and śūdras who take refuge (9.32).
- Material taken over and reframed from other texts:
  - Brahmā's day and night (8.17 ≈ Manu 1.73).
  - The two paths (8.24–25 ≈ ChU 5.10, BĀU 6.2.15–16).
  - The imperishable declared "in brief" (8.11 ≈ Kaṭha 1.2.15).

  In each case the new element is that the goal is the Lord's "supreme abode" (8.21), reached by undivided devotion (8.22).

## 4. Stated result (phala)
- **Positive results:**
  - Nothing further remains to be known (7.2).
  - Crossing the Lord's māyā (7.14).
  - Knowing brahman, adhyātma and karma entirely (7.29).
  - Attaining the Lord's state (8.5).
  - Coming to him (8.7; 9.28; 9.34).
  - Reaching the supreme divine Person (8.8, 8.10) and the supreme goal (8.13; 9.32).
  - No rebirth (8.15–16, 8.21).
  - The yogin surpasses Vedic merit (8.28).
  - Release from the inauspicious (9.1) and from the bonds of action (9.28).
  - The Lord carries gain and security (yoga-kṣema) for his devotees (9.22).
  - Quickly becoming righteous and gaining lasting peace; "my devotee does not perish" (9.31).
- **Contrasting results:**
  - Finite fruit for worshippers of other deities (7.23; 9.25).
  - Heaven, then return when merit is spent (9.20–21).
  - "They fall" (9.24).
  - The faithless return to death-and-saṃsāra (9.3).

## 5. Praise and blame (arthavāda)
- **Praise of the teaching:** rāja-vidyā, rāja-guhya, supreme purifier, directly evident, in accord with dharma, very easy to practise, imperishable (9.2); most secret (9.1).
- **Praise of the knower:** rarity (7.3; 7.19 "sudurlabhaḥ"); the knower as the Lord's very self (7.18).
- **Praise of the yogin:** he surpasses the merit of Vedas, sacrifices, austerity and gifts (8.28).
- **Praise of the power of devotion:** even the very ill-conducted devotee is to be deemed good (9.30); the a-fortiori "how much more then brāhmaṇas and royal sages" (9.33).
- **Blame (nindā):**
  - "Evil-doers, deluded, lowest of men" (7.15).
  - "Of small understanding" (7.23).
  - "The unintelligent" (7.24).
  - "Vain hopes, vain acts, vain knowledge" (9.12).
  - "Desiring desires" (9.21).
- For the interpretation layer: these are candidates for P7-arthavāda. The text layer records them as stated.

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- **8.6 → 8.7:** one goes to whatever state one remembers at the end, having always been shaped by it; *therefore* (tasmāt) remember me at all times.
- **8.27:** knowing the two paths, the yogin is not deluded; *therefore* be yoked at all times.
- **7.13–14:** the world does not know the Lord because it is deluded by the guṇa-made states, *for* his guṇa-made māyā is hard to cross. Refuge in him is given as the way across.
- **7.21–22:** why worship of other deities bears fruit at all — the Lord steadies the faith and himself ordains the desires obtained.
- **9.24:** the Lord is the enjoyer and lord of all sacrifices, but they do not know him in truth; *hence* (ataḥ) they fall.
- **9.9–10:** the acts of emission do not bind him because he is unattached, "as if indifferent"; with him as overseer prakṛti brings forth, *for this reason* (hetunā anena) the world revolves.
- **Analogies (dṛṣṭānta):**
  - Gems strung on a thread (7.7).
  - Wind that moves everywhere yet always abides in space (9.6).
- **8.16–21, cosmological argument:**
  1. All worlds up to Brahmā's lie within the cycle of manifestation and dissolution of Brahmā's day and night (8.17–19), so they are subject to return.
  2. The other, eternal unmanifest does not perish when beings perish (8.20).
  3. So reaching it, the Lord's supreme abode, is non-return (8.21).
- **9.30:** the reason the ill-conducted devotee is to be deemed good is "samyag vyavasito hi saḥ", for he has resolved rightly.
