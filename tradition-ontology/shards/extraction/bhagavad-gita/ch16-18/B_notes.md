## Six marks of purport — ch. 16–18

**1. Upakrama–upasaṃhāra (opening and closing)**
- **Ch. 16** opens with fearlessness (abhaya, 16.1). It closes with "scripture is your authority … you should do action here" (16.24).
- **Ch. 17** opens with Arjuna asking about faith outside the ordinance of scripture (17.1). It closes with the rule that whatever is done without faith is asat, "nothing here or hereafter" (17.28).
- **Ch. 18** opens with the question of saṃnyāsa and tyāga (18.1). Its teaching closes with a relinquishment that is refuge: "sarvadharmān parityajya mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja … mā śucaḥ" (18.66). The dialogue closes with Arjuna's "naṣṭo mohaḥ smṛtir labdhā … kariṣye vacanaṃ tava" (18.73). The frame closes with Sañjaya's verdict (18.78).
- **Closing echoes of the text's opening, all inside this chunk:**
  - "mā śucaḥ" (16.5; 18.66) answers 2.11 "aśocyān anvaśocas tvam".
  - 18.59 quotes Arjuna's "na yotsya iti" from 2.9.
  - "śaraṇaṃ vraja" (18.66) answers "tvāṃ prapannam" (2.7).
  - "sarvadharmān" (18.66) answers "dharmasaṃmūḍhacetāḥ" (2.7).
  - "ajñānasaṃmohaḥ … naṣṭo mohaḥ smṛtir labdhā" (18.72–73) reverses the chain saṃmoha → smṛtivibhrama of 2.63.
  - 18.78 answers Dhṛtarāṣṭra's "kim akurvata" (1.1).

**2. Abhyāsa (repetition)**
- **The guṇa triad is applied twelve times:**
  - faith (17.2)
  - food (17.8–10)
  - sacrifice (17.11–13)
  - austerity (17.17–19)
  - giving (17.20–22)
  - relinquishment (18.7–9)
  - knowledge (18.20–22)
  - action (18.23–25)
  - doer (18.26–28)
  - understanding (18.30–32)
  - steadfastness (18.33–35)
  - happiness (18.37–39)

  All twelve are grounded in 18.40.
- **Acting without desire for fruit or attachment:** 17.11, 17.17, 17.25, 18.2, 18.6, 18.9, 18.11, 18.23, 18.26, 18.49.
- **Grace (prasāda):** 18.56, 18.58, 18.62, 18.73; Vyāsa's grace at 18.75.
- **Refuge:** mad-vyapāśraya (18.56), 18.62, 18.66.
- **"Secret":** 18.63, 18.64, 18.67–68, 18.75.
- **Nature-born own work:** 18.41–48, 18.59–60.
- **"Me alone" (mām eva / mām ekam):** 18.65, 18.66, 18.68.
- **Verbatim or near-verbatim repeats of earlier chapters:**
  - 18.47ab = 3.35ab.
  - 18.65ab = 9.34ab.
  - 18.53 repeats 16.18's list (ahaṅkāra, bala, darpa, kāma, krodha), now as what is cast off.
  - 18.55 echoes 11.54; 18.57 echoes 3.30.

**3. Apūrvatā (novelty)**
- **New in this chunk:**
  - the divine and demonic endowments (16.1–20)
  - the three gates of hell (16.21)
  - śāstra as pramāṇa (16.24)
  - "the person is made of faith" (17.3)
  - threefold food, sacrifice, austerity of body/speech/mind, and giving (17.7–22)
  - oṃ tat sat (17.23–28)
  - the separate definitions of saṃnyāsa and tyāga (18.2), and threefold tyāga (18.7–9)
  - the five causes of action (18.13–15) and karma-codanā / karma-saṃgraha (18.18)
  - the sixfold guṇa typology (18.19–39)
  - the varṇa duties by nature (18.42–44)
  - the sequence brahma-bhūta → parā bhakti → knowing and entering (18.54–55)
- **The text flags its own novelty in grades:** "guhyād guhyataram" (18.63), then "sarvaguhyatamaṃ bhūyaḥ śṛṇu … paramaṃ vacaḥ" (18.64) before 18.65–66. Compare 9.1–2 and 15.20.

**4. Phala (stated result)**
- divine endowment → release; demonic → bondage (16.5)
- freed from the three gates → highest goal (16.22)
- ignoring scripture → no perfection, happiness or highest goal (16.23)
- faithless acts → nothing (17.28)
- no fruit of action after death for renouncers (18.12)
- the one free of ego-sense is not bound (18.17)
- worship through one's own work → perfection (18.45–46)
- naiṣkarmya-siddhi (18.49)
- fitness for becoming brahman (18.53) → parā bhakti (18.54) → knowing and entering (18.55)
- the eternal abode by grace (18.56)
- crossing all difficulties (18.58)
- supreme peace and the eternal abode (18.62)
- "you will come to me — I promise" (18.65)
- "I will free you from all evils" (18.66)
- teaching it → reaching Kṛṣṇa (18.68); studying it = jñāna-yajña (18.70); hearing it → worlds of the meritorious (18.71)
- **Result in the narrative:** delusion destroyed, memory regained, doubt gone, resolve to act (18.73). Sañjaya adds śrī, vijaya, bhūti and nīti (18.78).

**5. Arthavāda (praise and censure; P7)**
- **Censure:** the long censure of the demonic (16.7–20) works as arthavāda for the divine endowment and for acting by scripture.
- **Praise:**
  - of sacrifice, giving and austerity as purifiers (18.5)
  - of whoever transmits the Gītā — "none dearer to me" (18.68–69)
  - of study (18.70) and of mere hearing (18.71)
  - Sañjaya's praise (18.74–78)
- **Caution for the merger:** 18.69–71 and 18.78 are the clearest praise passages. They should not be counted as doctrinal claims in their own right.

**6. Upapatti (reasoned demonstration)**
- 18.11: the embodied cannot give up action entirely, so the relinquisher is the one who gives up the fruit.
- 18.13–17: action has five causes, so seeing the self alone as the doer is not seeing; the one free of ego-sense is not bound.
- 18.40: no being is free of the guṇas, which grounds all the typologies.
- 18.48: every undertaking is covered by fault as fire by smoke, so innate work is not to be abandoned.
- 18.59–61: nature compels, and the Lord moves beings by māyā; therefore (18.62) take refuge in him.
- Explicit "tasmāt" steps: 16.23 → 16.24; 17.23 → 17.24.
- 17.3 → 17.4: faith shapes the person, and so the object of worship.

## Contribution to the text-level thesis (for `tea:bhagavad-gita:thesis`)

**Opening (upakrama)**
- **1.1:** Dhṛtarāṣṭra asks what his sons and the Pāṇḍavas did on the dharma-field.
- **2.7:** Arjuna speaks. His nature is struck by the fault of pitiable weakness (kārpaṇya), and his thought (cetas) is bewildered about dharma. He asks what is decisively better (śreyaḥ … niścitam) and declares himself Kṛṣṇa's disciple who has taken refuge (prapanna). Then 2.9: "I will not fight."
- **2.11:** Kṛṣṇa's first word: "You grieve for those not to be grieved for; the wise do not grieve." The teaching opens on grief (śoka) and delusion about dharma, and on the eternal embodied one.

**Closing (upasaṃhāra)**
- **18.63:** reflect fully and do as you wish.
- **18.64–66:** the "supreme word, most secret of all": be mind-on-me, devoted, sacrificing, bowing, "you will come to me" (18.65). Then: "relinquishing all dharmas, come to me alone for refuge; I will free you from all evils; do not grieve" (18.66).
- **18.72–73:** Kṛṣṇa checks the result, and Arjuna answers that his delusion is gone, his memory regained, his doubt gone, and that he will do Kṛṣṇa's word.
- **18.74–78:** Sañjaya closes the frame that 1.1 opened.

**Thesis as the six marks support it (the merger decides)**
- **What it answers:** a warrior's grief and bewilderment about dharma (2.7, 2.11).
- **First, act:** do one's own nature-born duty, giving up attachment and fruit rather than action (18.2–12, 18.45–48; cf. 2.47, 3.35). See that the self alone is not the doer (18.16–17; cf. 2.19).
- **Then, knowledge and discipline:** these make one fit to become brahman, and so to reach supreme devotion, by which one knows and enters the Lord (18.49–55).
- **Final word:** as its own marked "most secret" word, take refuge in Kṛṣṇa alone, relinquishing all dharmas, and be freed by his grace from all evils (18.62–66).
- **How the marks line up:**
  - upakrama–upasaṃhāra: 2.7 / 2.11 ↔ 18.66 / 18.73
  - phala: 18.73
  - apūrvatā: 18.64 flags 18.65–66
  - abhyāsa: relinquishing fruit, and grace or refuge, recurring
  - upapatti: non-agency (18.13–17) and the inevitability of action (18.11, 18.59–61)

**Readings to record in notes, not to decide (dsp:gita-primary-teaching)**
- **Advaita (Śaṅkara's bhāṣya):** reads 1.2–2.9 as showing that grief and delusion are the seed of saṃsāra. It takes the purport to be knowledge of the self, preceded by renunciation of all works. It reads 18.66 accordingly.
- **Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja) and other Vaiṣṇava schools:** read 18.64–66 (with 9.34) as the culmination in bhakti or prapatti. The Śrīvaiṣṇavas call 18.66 the carama-śloka.
- **Other readings:** some stress karma-yoga, or the combination of knowledge and works.

The thesis entry should state the text's own marks with verse references and keep these readings in notes.
