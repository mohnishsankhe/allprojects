# Bhagavad Gītā ch. 16–18: six marks of purport (ṣaḍ-liṅga), merged from extractors A and B, and the rationale of the thesis entry

_(Merger M, chunk ch16-18, the last chunk. Claims about the commentators are recalled, not checked against the bhāṣyas. Refs follow this edition; chs. 16–18 have the same numbers in the 700-verse text.)_

## 1. Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra)
- **Ch. 16** opens with the divine endowment, fearlessness first (16.1–3). It closes with scripture as authority: "you should do action here" (16.23–24). Its first word of comfort is "mā śucaḥ, you are born to the divine endowment" (16.5).
- **Ch. 17** opens with Arjuna's question about those who sacrifice with faith but outside scriptural injunction (17.1). It closes with: whatever is done without faith is asat, nothing here or hereafter (17.28).
- **Ch. 18** opens with the question of saṃnyāsa and tyāga (18.1).
  - The teaching closes with the word marked "most secret" (18.64–66): "abandoning all dharmas, go to me alone for refuge … do not grieve".
  - The dialogue closes with Arjuna's "naṣṭo mohaḥ smṛtir labdhā … kariṣye vacanaṃ tava" (18.73).
  - The frame closes with Sañjaya's verdict (18.74–78). (A and B agree.)
- **Closing echoes of the text's opening, all inside this chunk** (A and B):
  - "mā śucaḥ" (16.5, 18.66) answers "aśocyān anvaśocas tvam" (2.11).
  - "śaraṇaṃ vraja" (18.66) and "tam eva śaraṇaṃ gaccha" (18.62) answer "tvāṃ prapannam" (2.7).
  - "sarva-dharmān" (18.66) and "ajñāna-saṃmohaḥ … naṣṭo mohaḥ" (18.72–73) answer "dharma-saṃmūḍha-cetāḥ" (2.7).
  - "niścayaṃ śṛṇu" (18.4), "niścitaṃ matam uttamam" (18.6) and "hitaṃ vakṣyāmi" (18.64) answer "śreyaḥ … niścitaṃ brūhi" (2.7) [B].
  - Kṛṣṇa quotes "na yotsya iti" back (18.59; 2.9), and Arjuna answers "kariṣye vacanaṃ tava" (18.73).
  - Sañjaya's close (18.78) answers Dhṛtarāṣṭra's "kim akurvata" (1.1).
  - "naṣṭo mohaḥ smṛtir labdhā" reverses the chain saṃmoha → smṛti-vibhrama of 2.63 [B].

## 2. Repetition (abhyāsa)
- **The guṇa scheme** is applied twelve times:
  - faith (17.2), food (17.8–10), sacrifice (17.11–13), austerity (17.17–19), giving (17.20–22);
  - relinquishment (18.7–9);
  - knowledge (18.20–22), action (18.23–25), agent (18.26–28), understanding (18.30–32), steadiness (18.33–35), happiness (18.37–39).

  All are grounded in 18.40: no being is free of the guṇas. (A counts about 14 by including faith's objects and the varṇas at 18.41.)
- **Giving up attachment and fruit:** 17.11, 17.17, 17.25, 18.2, 18.6, 18.9, 18.11, 18.23, 18.26, 18.49.
- **Grace (prasāda):** 18.56, 18.58, 18.62, 18.73; Vyāsa's grace, 18.75.
- **Refuge:** mad-vyapāśraya 18.56; 18.62; 18.66.
- **"Me alone" (mām eva / mām ekam):** 18.65, 18.66, 18.68 [B].
- **Scriptural injunction:** 16.23–24, 17.1, 17.5, 17.11, 17.13, 17.24.
- **Faith:** 17.1–3, 17.13, 17.17, 17.28, 18.71.
- **Secret (guhya):** 18.63, 18.64, 18.67–68, 18.75.
- **Nature-born own work:** 18.41–48, 18.59–60 [B].
- **Verbatim echoes:**
  - 18.47a = 3.35a; 18.65a = 9.34a; "na yotsya iti" (18.59) = 2.9.
  - 16.18's list (ahaṅkāra, bala, darpa, kāma, krodha) returns at 18.53 as what is cast off.
  - 18.55 echoes 11.54; 18.57 echoes 3.30 [B].

## 3. Novelty (apūrvatā)
- **New in this chunk** (A and B):
  - The named divine and demonic endowments (26 and 6 marks, 16.1–4).
  - The demonic view of a world without truth, foundation or Lord (16.8).
  - The threefold gate of hell (16.21); scripture as pramāṇa (16.24).
  - "A person is made of faith" (17.3).
  - The typologies of food, sacrifice, austerity (of body, speech and mind) and giving (17.7–22).
  - Oṃ tat sat (17.23–27).
  - Saṃnyāsa and tyāga defined separately (18.2); threefold tyāga (18.7–9).
  - The five causes of action (18.13–15); karma-codanā and karma-saṃgraha (18.18).
  - The sixfold guṇa-typology (18.19–39); the varṇa duties by nature (18.41–44).
  - The sequence brahma-bhūta → parā bhakti → knowing and entering (18.54–55).
  - The rule on who may hear (18.67); the rewards of teaching, studying and hearing (18.68–71).
- **The text grades its own novelty:** "guhyād guhyataram" (18.63), then "sarva-guhyatamaṃ bhūyaḥ śṛṇu me paramaṃ vacaḥ" (18.64), set on 18.65–66. Compare 4.1–3, 9.1–2 and 15.20.

## 4. Stated result (phala)
- **The two endowments:** divine → release; demonic → bondage (16.5), hell and lower births without reaching the Lord (16.16, 16.19–20).
- **Scripture and faith:** freedom from the three gates → the highest goal (16.22). Casting scripture aside → no perfection, happiness or highest goal (16.23). Faithless acts → asat, nothing here or hereafter (17.28).
- **Action and its fruit:** the fruit of action does not come after death to those who relinquish (18.12). One not shaped by the sense of "I" is not bound (18.17). Perfection comes through one's own work (18.45–46); naiṣkarmya-siddhi through renunciation (18.49).
- **From discipline to the Lord:**
  - fitness for becoming brahman (18.53) → supreme devotion (18.54) → knowing and entering the Lord (18.55);
  - the eternal place by grace even while acting (18.56); crossing all difficulties (18.58); supreme peace and the eternal abode (18.62);
  - "you will come to me — I promise" (18.65); "I will release you from all sins" (18.66).
- **Handing on the text:** teaching it → reaching Kṛṣṇa, none dearer (18.68–69); studying it = jñāna-yajña (18.70); hearing it → the worlds of the meritorious (18.71).
- **Result shown in the story:** delusion destroyed, memory regained, doubt gone, resolve to act (18.73). Sañjaya adds fortune, victory, prosperity and firm right conduct (18.78).

## 5. Praise and blame (arthavāda; candidates for P7)
- **Praise:** of sacrifice, giving and austerity as purifiers (18.5); the chain of superlatives (18.63–68); of whoever transmits, studies or hears the text (18.68–71); Sañjaya's praise (18.74–78).
- **Blame:** the long censure of the demonic (16.7–20) and the tāmasic types serve the divine endowment and acting by scripture.
- **For the reconciliation auditor:** 18.69–71 and 18.78 are the clearest praise passages (A and B). They are not to be counted as doctrinal claims in their own right; that reading is interpretive.

## 6. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti)
- 18.11: the embodied cannot give up action entirely, so the relinquisher of fruit is the relinquisher.
- 18.13–17: action has five causes; "this being so", one who sees his self alone as agent does not see, and one not shaped by the sense of "I" is not bound.
- 18.40: nothing is free of the guṇas, which grounds all the typologies.
- 18.48: every undertaking is covered by fault as fire by smoke, so innate work is not to be abandoned.
- 18.59–61: Arjuna's nature will compel him, and the Lord in the heart moves all beings by māyā; therefore (18.62) take refuge in him.
- Explicit "tasmāt" steps: 16.21, 16.23 → 16.24, 17.23 → 17.24.
- 17.3 → 17.4: faith shapes the person and the object of worship.
- 18.64: "you are dearly loved, therefore I shall tell you".

## Rationale of the thesis entry (tea:bhagavad-gita:thesis)
- **Opening:**
  - Frame 1.1.
  - The problem: Arjuna's grief (1.47, 2.8), his nature struck by kārpaṇya and his thought bewildered about dharma, his request for what is decisively better as one who has taken refuge (2.7), and "na yotsye" (2.9).
  - The teaching's first word is 2.11.
- **Close:**
  - 18.63 (reflect and do as you wish).
  - 18.64–66: the word marked "most secret of all".
  - 18.72–73: the teacher checks, the disciple confirms delusion destroyed and resolves to act.
  - 18.74–78: the frame closes.
- **Chosen as decisive, following the orchestrator** (the text's own marks):
  - 2.7/2.11 ↔ 18.66/18.72–73;
  - 18.64's "most secret" flag on 18.65–66;
  - the phala shown in the story at 18.73.
- **Repetition across chs. 1–18** (from the merged notes):
  - action without attachment to fruit;
  - the imperishable embodied one that is not slain and not the agent;
  - refuge and devotion to "me alone" (18.65a = 9.34a);
  - sameness;
  - the guṇas.
- **Novelty the text itself marks:** its secrecy marks, 4.1–3, 9.1–2, 11.47–48, 15.20, 18.63–64. The ch01-03 note on novelty measured against the Upaniṣads is recorded in the entry's notes.
- **Upapatti:** 2.12–30, 3.5/18.11, 3.27–29/13.30/18.13–17, 18.59–62.
- **What the entry states:** the purport is removing Arjuna's grief and delusion about dharma and bringing him to act at Kṛṣṇa's word, through:
  - knowledge of the embodied one who is not slain and does not act;
  - own-dharma action without attachment to fruit, offered to the Lord;
  - knowing the Lord and taking refuge in him with devotion.

  It also records the text's own order (18.45–55) and 18.56 (refuge while acting), and the closing "most secret" word.
- **What it does not decide:** it chooses no tradition's thesis. It says explicitly that the marks do not settle what "abandoning all dharmas" means, or which means is final and direct.
- **The traditions' theses**, recorded in its notes as in dsp:gita-primary-teaching and marked recalled:
  - Advaita: Śaṅkara and Madhusūdana; knowledge preceded by renunciation of works; 18.66 as renouncing all works; the three hexads.
  - Viśiṣṭādvaita: Yāmuna and Rāmānuja; bhakti-yoga, with 18.66's two readings; the Śrīvaiṣṇava carama-śloka.
  - Dvaita: Madhva.
  - Abhinavagupta and Bhāskara (both low confidence).
  - Gauḍīya: pure devotion; 9.34 and 18.64–66.
  - Tilak (recent).
- **The dispute's partial reconciliation** (P3, P4, P8) claims only that the text addresses different temperaments and stages. Which means is final remains not yet reconciled.
