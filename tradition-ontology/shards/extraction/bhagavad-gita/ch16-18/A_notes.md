## Six marks of purport in this chunk
1. **Opening and closing (upakrama–upasaṃhāra).**
   - The chunk opens with the divine endowment. Its first word of consolation is 'mā śucaḥ, you are born to the divine endowment' (16.5).
   - It closes with 'mā śucaḥ' again after the call to take refuge (18.66). Then comes Arjuna's 'naṣṭo mohaḥ … kariṣye vacanaṃ tava' (18.73), and Sañjaya's verdict (18.78).
   - Each chapter is framed the same way:
     - Ch. 16 opens with the virtues (16.1–3) and ends with scripture as authority, 'you should act' (16.23–24).
     - Ch. 17 opens with faith outside scriptural injunction (17.1) and ends with 'without faith it is asat' (17.28).
     - Ch. 18 opens with the question about saṃnyāsa and tyāga (18.1) and ends with Arjuna's resolve (18.73) and Sañjaya's close (18.74–78).
2. **Repetition (abhyāsa).**
   - The three-guṇa scheme is applied about 14 times, to:
     - faith, food, sacrifice, austerity and giving (17.2–22);
     - relinquishment (18.7–9);
     - knowledge, action, agent, understanding, steadiness and happiness (18.19–39).
     - 18.40 then says no being is free of the guṇas.
   - Giving up attachment and fruit: 17.11, 17.17, 17.25, 18.2, 18.6, 18.9, 18.11, 18.23, 18.26, 18.49.
   - Grace (prasāda): 18.56, 18.58, 18.62, 18.73, 18.75.
   - Refuge: 18.56, 18.62, 18.66.
   - Scriptural injunction: 16.23–24, 17.1, 17.5, 17.11, 17.13, 17.24.
   - Faith: 17.1–3, 17.13, 17.17, 17.28, 18.71.
   - Secret (guhya): 18.63, 18.64, 18.68, 18.75.
   - The list ahaṅkāra–bala–darpa–kāma–krodha: 16.18, repeated at 18.53.
   - Verbatim echoes of earlier chapters:
     - 18.47a = 3.35a
     - 18.65a = 9.34a
     - 18.59 'na yotsya iti' = 2.9
3. **Novelty (apūrvatā).**
   - The named divine and demonic endowments, with 26 and 6 marks (16.1–4).
   - The demonic view of a world without truth, foundation or Lord (16.8).
   - The threefold gate of hell (16.21).
   - 'A person is made of faith' (17.3), and the guṇa-typologies of chapter 17.
   - Austerity of body, speech and mind (17.14–16).
   - Oṃ tat sat (17.23–27).
   - Saṃnyāsa and tyāga defined (18.2), and the three kinds of tyāga (18.7–9).
   - The five factors of action (18.13–15).
   - The guṇa-typology of chapter 18 (18.19–39).
   - The list of varṇa duties (18.41–44).
   - The sequence from becoming Brahman, to supreme devotion, to knowing, to entering (18.54–55).
   - 'Sarvadharmān parityajya', which the text itself calls 'the most secret of all' (18.64–66).
   - The rule on who may hear (18.67) and the rewards of teaching, studying and hearing (18.68–71).
4. **Stated result (phala).**
   - The two endowments:
     - Divine: release (16.5).
     - Demonic: bondage, hell, and lower births without reaching the Lord (16.5, 16.16, 16.19–20).
     - Freedom from the three gates: the highest course (16.22).
     - Casting aside scripture: no perfection, happiness or highest course (16.23).
   - Faith: without it, 'asat', nothing here or hereafter (17.28).
   - Action and its fruit:
     - The fruit of action comes after death only to those who do not relinquish (18.12).
     - One without the sense of doership is not bound (18.17).
     - Perfection comes through one's own work (18.45–46).
   - From discipline to the Lord:
     - Fitness for becoming Brahman (18.53).
     - Supreme devotion, then knowing and entering the Lord (18.54–55).
     - The eternal state by grace, even while acting (18.56).
     - Crossing every difficulty (18.58); supreme peace and the eternal abode (18.62).
     - 'You will come to me' (18.65); 'I will release you from all sins' (18.66).
   - Handing on the text:
     - Teaching it brings one to the Lord and makes one dearest to him (18.68–69).
     - Studying it is a jñāna-yajña (18.70).
     - Hearing it brings the worlds of the meritorious (18.71).
   - Results shown in the story: Arjuna's delusion destroyed (18.73), and fortune and victory (18.78).
5. **Praise and blame (arthavāda).**
   - Sacrifice, giving and austerity praised as purifiers (18.5).
   - The chain of superlatives (18.63–68).
   - Praise of whoever hands on the teaching (18.69–71).
   - Sañjaya's praise (18.74–78).
   - Blame by exaggeration: the demonic portrait (16.7–20) and the tāmasic types.
   - For the reconciliation auditor under P7: 18.71 and 18.78 are candidates for arthavāda. That reading is interpretive, not the text's own label.
6. **Reasoned demonstration (upapatti).**
   - 18.11: no embodied being can give up action entirely, so the relinquisher of fruit is the true relinquisher.
   - 18.13–16: action has five factors; 'this being so', one who sees the self alone as agent does not see.
   - 18.48: all undertakings are covered by fault as fire by smoke.
   - 18.40: nothing is free of the guṇas.
   - 18.59–60: Arjuna's nature will compel him.
   - 18.61, then 18.62: the Lord in the heart moves all beings, 'go to him alone for refuge'.
   - The 'tasmāt' ('therefore') steps: 16.21, 16.24, 17.24.
   - 17.3: a person is made of faith.
   - 18.64: 'you are dearly loved, therefore I shall tell you'.

## Contribution to the text-level thesis (for tea:bhagavad-gita:thesis)
- **Opening.**
  - 1.1: Dhṛtarāṣṭra asks what happened.
  - 1.28–2.9: Arjuna's grief and refusal.
  - 2.7: the request that sets the problem. Arjuna's nature is struck by weakness and his thought is confused about dharma. He asks what is decisively better (śreyas), 'your disciple, who has taken refuge in you (prapanna)'.
  - 2.9: 'na yotsya', I will not fight.
  - 2.11: the teaching begins. 'You grieve for those who should not be grieved for; the wise grieve neither for the dead nor for the living.'
- **Closing.** Each item answers one point of the opening.
  - 18.66 'mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja … mā śucaḥ' answers both the refuge of 2.7 and the grief of 2.11.
  - 18.72 asks whether 'the confusion born of ignorance' is destroyed. That picks up 'dharma-saṃmūḍha-cetāḥ' in 2.7.
  - 18.73 answers it: 'naṣṭo mohaḥ, smṛtir labdhā tvat-prasādāt … gata-sandehaḥ, kariṣye vacanaṃ tava'. 'Delusion destroyed' closes 2.7. 'I will do your word' reverses 'na yotsya' of 2.9, which Kṛṣṇa quotes back at 18.59.
  - 18.63, 'reflect fully, then do as you wish', shows the resolve is left to the disciple.
  - 18.74–78, Sañjaya's close, answers the frame question of 1.1. 18.78 gives a verdict of fortune and victory where Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are. It is Sañjaya's own judgment ('matir mama').
- **Reading supported by the frame.** A minimal reading, stated by the text's own opening and close, is this:
  - The teaching removes grief and the delusion about dharma and action. It does so through knowledge of the imperishable self (2.11 onward), action without attachment to fruit, and knowledge of and devotion and refuge in the Lord.
  - It ends in grace and resolved action.
  - The chunk supplies the last links in that sequence:
    - the inward relinquishment that makes action unbinding (18.2–12, 18.17);
    - the path from one's own work to becoming Brahman, supreme devotion and entering the Lord (18.45–56);
    - refuge as the 'most secret' word (18.64–66);
    - the result shown in the story (18.73).
- **Where the traditions divide.** The traditions draw different theses from these same marks. dsp:gita-primary-teaching records them: knowledge preceded by renouncing works (Śaṅkara; 18.66 read as giving up all action), bhakti or prapatti (Rāmānuja; 18.66 read as the carama-śloka), Madhva, a combination of knowledge and action, and pure devotion. So 18.66 should be cited as the chief closing mark, with its readings recorded, not decided. The text does not say which dharmas are to be given up. It does pair 'mā śucaḥ' with 'mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja'. It also places supreme devotion after becoming Brahman (18.54–55), and taking refuge while still acting (18.56–57).
