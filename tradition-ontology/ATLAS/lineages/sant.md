The Sants are the poet-saints of North India, from Kabīr, Ravidās and Dādū onward, who taught love of a formless God (nirguṇa bhakti) through the Name and the true guru, located the divine within the body, and rejected caste, idols, pilgrimage, scripture-learning and the rival formalisms of Hindus and Muslims. Their songs were gathered into anthologies and their followers formed panths (Kabīr Panth, Dādū Panth, Ravidāsī, Nirañjanī, Rāmsnehī, Caraṇdāsī, Satnāmī, Garībdāsī); the nineteenth-century Sant Mat (Radhasoami) is their recent heir.

## Distinctive positions
- The ultimate is nirguṇa (beyond attributes) and yet is loved: it is called Rām, Hari, Sāhib, Alakh, Nirañjan, Satnām — Rām not as the son of Daśaratha but as the one who pervades all.
- The Name (nām) and the Word (śabd), revealed by the true guru (satguru), are the means; outward repetition on beads without the mind is rejected.
- The true guru is praised as highly as God, sometimes higher ('guru and Govind both stand before me — whose feet shall I touch?').
- The divine is within the body (ghaṭ); temple and mosque, idols, pilgrimage, ritual bathing, fasting, sectarian garb, the Veda and the Qur'ān, Hindu and Turk forms are all rejected as outward.
- Caste has no standing before God; many Sants were of low or 'untouchable' birth (weaver, leatherworker, barber, cotton-carder) and spoke of it openly.
- Liberation is sought now, in this body: 'dying while living' (jīvan-mṛtak), the natural state (sahaj), in the company of sants (satsang), mostly as householders at honest work.
- Teaching is sung in the vernacular: sākhī couplets, pad/śabd songs and ramainī verses, gathered in anthologies arranged by topic (aṅg).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Rām (nirguṇa), Hari, Sāhib / Sāīṃ, Alakh, Nirañjan, Satnām, the Beloved (pīv)
**Descriptions:** in every body, 'in the breath of the breath'; the one of Hindu and Turk; the Beloved whose redness colours the lover; known in sahaj, beyond the three qualities
**Negations:** neither one nor two (Kabīr); not in temple, mosque, Kaaba or Kailāsa; not the son of Daśaratha; without form or sign
**Relation to self:** The Lord dwells within the self as musk in the deer; when the 'I' goes, Hari is; like gold and bracelet, water and wave (Ravidās).
**Relation to world:** The world is māyā's play and Kāl's grinding-mill, yet the Lord pervades it; Hindu and Turk, caste and sect are human constructions.
**Caveat:** The Sants would reject any reading that makes their Rām an avatāra or an image, or that reduces their Beloved to an impersonal abstraction; they also resist being enrolled in any scriptural system, Hindu or Muslim.
