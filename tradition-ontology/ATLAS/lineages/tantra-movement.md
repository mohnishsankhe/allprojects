An umbrella entry, owned by U49, for tantra as a movement that runs across families rather than a single lineage: the Śaiva Mantramārga (Siddhānta, Bhairava and Kaula scriptures), the Śākta traditions (Śrīvidyā, Kālīkula, Kubjikā), the Vaiṣṇava Pāñcarātra, the Buddhist Mantranaya/Vajrayāna and the Jain mantra-kalpas (Padmāvatī, Jvālāmālinī). They share a ritual and yogic grammar — initiation, mantra, maṇḍala and yantra, nyāsa and bhūtaśuddhi, deity identification, the subtle body, the guru as deity, secrecy, and the twin goals of power and liberation — while each keeps its own view of the ultimate. Its links are recorded as borrowings (brw:*) with typed evidence.

## Distinctive positions
- Liberation and power (mukti and bhukti/siddhi) are both legitimate aims of practice; the aspirant chooses between them or seeks both (Śaiva sādhaka and mumukṣu; Buddhist ordinary and supreme siddhi; the Kaula 'enjoyment becomes yoga').
- Practice begins with initiation (dīkṣā, abhiṣeka) by a qualified guru into a lineage and a mantra; without it the teaching is not to be practised.
- Mantra is the deity in sound: the mantra is the deity's body, and repetition with the correct form, seed syllables and nyāsa makes the deity present.
- One worships the deity by becoming the deity ('only a god may worship a god'; Buddhist divine pride; the Pāñcarātra sādhaka's identification with Viṣṇu).
- The body is a maṇḍala: channels, centres, seats (pīṭha) and letters are installed or discovered in it, and the outer sacred geography is mapped onto it.
- The guru is the deity present in human form; the teaching is secret and given only to the qualified.
- Purification of the elements (bhūtaśuddhi) and the placing of mantras (nyāsa) precede worship; the maṇḍala or yantra is the deity's palace and body.
- In the Kaula and Yoginī strata, transgressive substances and acts are used or — more commonly in the surviving traditions — reinterpreted as inner yoga; each family records its own warnings.
- Each family places its tantras above the Veda or the sūtras (as a higher or more rapid revelation) while usually denying that they contradict them.
- The movement is not one tradition: its Śaiva, Śākta, Vaiṣṇava, Buddhist and Jain forms keep their own ultimate, ethics and goal, and each rejects the others' final view.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Śiva / Paramaśiva / Bhairava (Śaiva tantra), Śakti as Tripurasundarī, Kālī, Kubjikā (Śākta tantra), Vāsudeva with Lakṣmī (Pāñcarātra), Vajradhara; the innate (sahaja); great bliss (Buddhist tantra), the Jina and the liberated soul (Jain mantraśāstra keeps the Jain goal)
**Descriptions:** In the non-dual Śaiva and Śākta tantras the ultimate is consciousness that is both light and self-aware power, which appears as the world and as the practitioner.; In the Buddhist tantras the ultimate is the union of emptiness and bliss (or of wisdom and means), the innate nature of mind, realized in the deity's form and the channels of the body.; In the Pāñcarātra the ultimate is the personal Lord Vāsudeva with his Śakti Lakṣmī, manifest in the vyūhas.; In Jain mantra practice the Jina and the five supreme beings are worshipped; liberation remains the Jain liberation of the soul, and the tantric rites serve protection and worldly ends.
**Relation to self:** Varies by family: identity of self and Śiva/Śakti (Trika, Kaula, Śrīvidyā); souls equal to Śiva but distinct (Śaiva Siddhānta); the self as the Lord's part (Pāñcarātra); no self but the innate mind (Buddhist); the plurality of souls (Jain).
**Relation to world:** Varies by family: the world as the deity's manifestation or play (non-dual Śaiva/Śākta); as real and produced by māyā under Śiva's agency (Siddhānta); as appearance empty of own nature, yet the maṇḍala of the deity (Buddhist); as real substances (Jain).
**Caveat:** The tantric movement is not one tradition and has no single view of the ultimate. The Buddhist and Jain tantric traditions reject a single creator or substrate; the Śaiva Siddhānta keeps souls distinct from Śiva; each family ranks the others' goal below its own (see dsp:ranking-of-other-revelations). A one-truth reading belongs only to the interpretation layer and each family would object to it.
