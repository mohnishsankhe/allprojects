**Regions:** Ensa (Tsang), Tashi Lhunpo
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Mahāmudrā received by Tsongkhapa from Mañjuśrī and passed orally until the First Panchen Lama wrote it down; Scholarly account: oral lineage claimed from the 15th c.; root text written c. early 17th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The oral 'hearing lineage' of Mahāmudrā and guru yoga that the Gelug traces from Tsongkhapa through Tokden Jampal Gyatso, Baso Chökyi Gyaltsen, Chökyi Dorje and Gyalwa Ensapa to the First Panchen Lama, who set it down in 'The Highway of the Conquerors'.

## Distinctive positions
- A Gelug Mahāmudrā practised as the union of the sūtra view of emptiness (as in Tsongkhapa) and the tantric clear light; the First Panchen Lama's root text says the Mahāmudrā, Dzogchen, Chöd and Madhyamaka instructions converge in one intent.


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** phyag rgya chen po — Mahāmudrā: sūtra Mahāmudrā as emptiness, mantra Mahāmudrā as the clear light of great bliss, sems kyi gnas lugs — the abiding nature of mind
**Descriptions:** The conventional nature of mind is clarity and knowing; its ultimate nature is its emptiness of inherent existence (Highway of the Conquerors, as the Gelug Mahāmudrā teaches).; Many names — co-emergent union, amulet box, fivefold, one taste, four letters, pacification, Chöd, Dzogchen, Madhyamaka view — converge in one intent (Highway of the Conquerors).
**Caveat:** The 'one intent' is the emptiness of mind as the Gelug understands it (a non-affirming negation); the convergence statement is not a claim that the schools' views are identical in every detail.
