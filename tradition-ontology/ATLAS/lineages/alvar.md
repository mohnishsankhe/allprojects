# The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition

**Status:** absorbed

The Āḻvārs — twelve in the tradition's standard list — are the Tamil saint-poets of Viṣṇu whose some four thousand verses, gathered by Nāthamuni as the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham, became the Tamil Veda of the Śrīvaiṣṇavas. Their hymns celebrate Tirumāl with Śrī in his temple images, teach surrender, service and the saving name, and voice love for God through the conventions of Tamil love poetry. Placed here also, as Tamil Vaiṣṇava devotional literature of the same milieu, are the precursor Paripāṭal hymns to Tirumāl and Kampaṉ's Rāmāyaṇa (neither is an Āḻvār work).

## Distinctive positions
- The Lord is Tirumāl/Nārāyaṇa with Śrī (Tiru), supreme beyond all (paratva) and yet easy of access to those who love him (saulabhya: 'eḷiyavaṉ', TVM 1.3.1).
- The Lord is fully present in the temple image (arcā) at the sites the Āḻvārs sang (the 108 divya-deśas); devotion is temple-centred and pilgrim.
- Knowledge-and-love (matinalam) is itself the Lord's gift, not the soul's achievement ('he graciously gave me knowledge-and-love free of delusion', TVM 1.1.1).
- Surrender at the Lord's feet, with Śrī as the one who never leaves his chest, is the refuge of those who have nothing (TVM 6.10.10; Kulaśekhara's Perumāḷ Tirumoḻi 5.1).
- The soul's nature is servitude (aṭimai) to the Lord at all times (TVM 3.3.1); the Lord's devotees, of whatever birth, are one's masters (TVM 3.7.9; Tirumālai; Amalaṉātipirāṉ 1).
- Love is sung in the voices of Tamil akam poetry: the poet becomes the girl in love (Parāṅkuśa-nāyakī, Parakāla-nāyakī), the mother, the friend; the Lord is sought as lover, child, master and friend.
- The divine name, above all 'Nārāyaṇa', is the refuge found after long searching (Tirumaṅkai, Periya Tirumoḻi 1.1).
- The Tamil hymns are Veda: Nammāḻvār's four works answer to the four Vedas and Tirumaṅkai's six to the six aṅgas (the tradition's account).
- Caste and learning do not measure a devotee: the tradition's twelve include a king, brāhmaṇas, a Vellāḷa (Nammāḻvār), a bard of a community held low (Tiruppāṇ), a Kaḷḷar chieftain (Tirumaṅkai) and a woman (Āṇṭāḷ).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Tirumāl (the Lord with Śrī), Nāraṇaṉ / Nārāyaṇa, Māyaṉ / Māyōṉ (the wondrous, dark one), Kaṇṇaṉ (Kṛṣṇa), Perumāḷ / Emperumāṉ (the Great One / our Lord), Tiruvaraṅkaṉ, Tiruvēṅkaṭamuṭaiyāṉ and the other names of the Lord at each sung site
**Descriptions:** He who has the highest good, beyond which there is nothing higher; lord of the unwearying immortals; whose radiant feet end sorrow (TVM 1.1.1).; When there was no god, no world and no life, he created the four-faced Brahmā with the gods, worlds and lives (TVM 4.10.1); Nārāyaṇa made Brahmā and Brahmā made Śaṅkara (Nāṉmukaṉ Tiruvantāti 1).; He pervades all things hidden within them as life within a body (TVM 1.1.7).; Easy of access to those who love him, hard to reach for others (TVM 1.3.1); he takes whatever form and name his devotees delight in (Mutal Tiruvantāti 44).; He dwells with Śrī on his chest, who says she cannot part from him even for a moment (TVM 6.10.10).; He is present in the temple image at Śrīraṅgam, Tiruvēṅkaṭam and the other sung sites, awakened at dawn, praised with benedictions for his own safety (Tiruppallāṇṭu; Tiruppaḷḷiyeḻucci).
**Negations:** Not one god among many: the Āḻvārs repeatedly deny that the other gods are refuges (Nāṉmukaṉ Tiruvantāti; Madhurakavi's 'I know no other god' is said of his teacher).
**Relation to self:** The self belongs to the Lord as servant to master: 'I thought I was my own and what I had was mine; I am yours and what I have is yours' (TVM 2.9.9); its fulfilment is faultless service at all times (TVM 3.3.1).
**Relation to world:** The world and lives are his creation and his body; he is hidden in them as life in a body (TVM 1.1.7) and swallowed and restored them (the 'world-swallower' image); the world is real and is the field of his play and avatāras.
**Caveat:** The Āḻvārs and the Śrīvaiṣṇava commentators would reject any one-truth reading that makes Nārāyaṇa one name among equals for an impersonal absolute or that treats Śiva, Brahmā or other gods as independent refuges; the Lord is one, but he is Nārāyaṇa with Śrī, personal and qualified.
