The Bön religion of Tibet in its Yungdrung ('eternal', swastika) form: the teaching attributed to Tönpa Shenrab Miwoche, transmitted from Tagzig through Zhang Zhung, persecuted under the Tibetan kings, preserved in treasures recovered from the 10th-11th c., and organized monastically from Menri (1405). It has its own canon, nine ways, deities, monastic discipline and Great Perfection teachings closely parallel to the Nyingma, while insisting on its separate origin.

## Distinctive positions
- Bön is the teaching of Tönpa Shenrab Miwoche, a fully enlightened teacher from Ölmo Lungring in Tagzig, independent of and prior to Śākyamuni's Buddhism.
- The teaching is ordered as nine ways (theg pa rim dgu), four causal ways and five fruitional ways, and in another scheme as four portals and a fifth, the treasury (sgo bzhi mdzod lnga).
- Its Dzogchen (the A-khrid, the Dzogchen Yangtse Longchen and the Zhang Zhung Nyengyü) teaches the base of all (kun gzhi) inseparable from awareness (rig pa).
- The swastika (g.yung drung) is the emblem of the indestructible and eternal; circumambulation is counter-clockwise.
- Tibetan Buddhist historians' portrait of Bön as a pre-Buddhist or derivative religion is rejected by the Bön tradition.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** the base of all (kun gzhi), awareness (rig pa), Kuntu Zangpo, the primordial buddha of Bön, bon nyid (the reality of Bön) and bon sku (the Bön-body)
**Descriptions:** The base (kun gzhi) is empty and clear, and awareness (rig pa) is inseparable from it; their union is called 'mother and son' (ma bu) in the Zhang Zhung Nyengyü (moderate confidence).; Kuntu Zangpo is the Bön-body; Shenlha Ökar the body of perfection; Tönpa Shenrab the emanation body.
**Negations:** the base is not a thing established by characteristics; awareness cannot be grasped as an object
**Relation to self:** The nature of one's own mind (sems nyid) is the base; recognizing it is Buddhahood.
**Relation to world:** Appearances arise from the base as light, sound and rays; not recognizing them as the base's own display is the origin of saṃsāra.
**Caveat:** Bön insists on its own origin (Tönpa Shenrab, Tagzig) and would reject being read as a variant of Buddhism; its 'kun gzhi' is the ultimate base, whereas in Nyingma usage 'kun gzhi' (all-base) usually names the ground of saṃsāric consciousness — the same word is not the same doctrine.
