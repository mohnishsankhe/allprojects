**Status:** living

The lineages that recite and ritually employ the four Vedic Saṃhitās — Ṛg (hymns), Sāma (chants), Yajus (sacrificial formulas; Black and White) and Atharva (hymns and spells) — each in hereditary branches (śākhās). Their teaching is carried in hymns to the gods (Agni, Indra, Soma, Varuṇa, Rudra, Viṣṇu, Vāc …), in the liturgy of the śrauta sacrifice, and in a set of speculative hymns on the One, creation, speech, the self and death that the later Upaniṣads and all Vedic schools take up.

## Distinctive positions
- The Veda is heard revelation (śruti), 'seen' by the ṛṣis rather than composed; the named seers are its first seers and transmitters (Nirukta 1.20).
- Ṛta, the true order, holds together the cosmos, the ritual and right conduct; satya (truth) is its spoken and lived form; its guardians are Varuṇa and Mitra.
- The sacrifice (yajña) sustains gods and world; the first sacrifice was the gods' offering of the cosmic Person (Puruṣa), and 'these were the first dharmas' (RV 10.90.16 = 1.164.50).
- Truth is one; the inspired call it by many names — Agni, Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Yama, Mātariśvan (RV 1.164.46; cf. 10.114.5, 8.58.2, 3.55).
- Speech (vāc) is a cosmic power: of her four quarters three are hidden, the fourth is human speech (RV 1.164.45); she chooses whom she makes a seer (RV 10.125.5).
- Before creation there was neither being nor non-being; That One breathed windless by its own power; whether even the overseer in the highest heaven knows its origin is left open (RV 10.129).
- Tapas (generative heat, austerity) is the source of ṛta and satya (RV 10.190.1) and the way the ancients reached heaven (RV 10.154.2).
- The dead go by the ancient paths to Yama and the fathers in the highest heaven, rejoined with their merit of sacrifice and gift (iṣṭāpūrta) and a radiant body (RV 10.14.8); the ideal on earth is a full life of a hundred autumns.
- The Veda is preserved by exact oral recitation in accent and letter within hereditary śākhās, guarded by the word-by-word and interwoven recitations (pada, krama, jaṭā, ghana).
- The Atharvaveda adds healing, protection, concord and royal rites, and hymns on Skambha, Prāṇa, Kāla, the Brahmacārin and the Vrātya that name one supporting principle (jyeṣṭha brahman).

**Transmissions given:** 
  - `lin:upanisadic` — what: the Upaniṣads as the closing portion (Vedānta) of each śākhā; prāṇa, brahman, ātman, the two birds (RV 1.164.20), the two paths (RV 10.88.15), the Puruṣa hymn (RV 10.90), the yoking of mind (RV 5.81.1; VS 11.1–5)
  - [Vedāṅga (the six limbs of the Veda)](vedanga.md) — what: the Saṃhitā texts that phonetics, metre, grammar, etymology, ritual and time-keeping exist to preserve and apply
  - `lin:mimamsa` — what: the mantra and injunction corpus whose ritual purport Mīmāṃsā interprets
  - [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](dharmasastra.md) — what: the Sāvitrī (Gāyatrī), the Puruṣa hymn's four varṇas, funeral and marriage mantras
  - `lin:saiva-siddhanta` — what: the Śatarudrīya (Śrī Rudram) and the five-syllable formula it contains (TS 4.5.8)
  - `lin:sakta` — what: the Vāk hymn (RV 10.125) recited as the Devī Sūkta; Śrī Sūkta (Ṛgveda Khila)
  - [Pāñcarātra](pancaratra.md) — what: the Puruṣa Sūkta and Viṣṇu's highest step as central liturgy
  - `lin:vyakarana` — what: verses on speech (RV 1.164.45, 4.58.3, 10.71) read as praise of grammar
  - `lin:sangita` — what: the melodies (sāman) and their notes, the root of music in the tradition's own account
  - `lin:ayurveda` — what: Atharvavedic healing (bhaiṣajya); Āyurveda counted as an upaveda / limb of the Atharvaveda
