**Status:** living

The lineages that recite and ritually employ the four Vedic Saṃhitās — Ṛg (hymns), Sāma (chants), Yajus (sacrificial formulas; Black and White) and Atharva (hymns and spells) — each in hereditary branches (śākhās). Their teaching is carried in hymns to the gods (Agni, Indra, Soma, Varuṇa, Rudra, Viṣṇu, Vāc …), in the liturgy of the śrauta sacrifice, and in a set of speculative hymns on the One, creation, speech, the self and death that the later Upaniṣads and all Vedic schools take up.

## Distinctive positions
- The Veda is heard revelation (śruti), 'seen' by the ṛṣis rather than composed; the named seers are its first seers and transmitters (Nirukta 1.20).
- Ṛta, the true order, holds together the cosmos, the ritual and right conduct; satya (truth) is its spoken and lived form; its guardians are Varuṇa and Mitra.
- The sacrifice (yajña) sustains gods and world; the first sacrifice was the gods' offering of the cosmic Person (Puruṣa), and 'these were the first dharmas' (RV 10.90.16 = 1.164.50).
- Truth is one; the inspired call it by many names — Agni, Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Yama, Mātariśvan (RV 1.164.46; cf. 10.114.5, 8.58.2, 3.55).
- Speech (vāc) is a cosmic power: of her four quarters three are hidden, the fourth is human speech (RV 1.164.45); she chooses whom she makes a seer (RV 10.125.5).
- Before creation there was neither being nor non-being; That One breathed windless by its own power; whether even the overseer in the highest heaven knows its origin is left open (RV 10.129).
- Tapas (generative heat, austerity) is the source of ṛta and satya (RV 10.190.1) and the way the ancients reached heaven (RV 10.154.2).
- The dead go by the ancient paths to Yama and the fathers in the highest heaven, rejoined with their merit of sacrifice and gift (iṣṭāpūrta) and a radiant body (RV 10.14.8); the ideal on earth is a full life of a hundred autumns.
- The Veda is preserved by exact oral recitation in accent and letter within hereditary śākhās, guarded by the word-by-word and interwoven recitations (pada, krama, jaṭā, ghana).
- The Atharvaveda adds healing, protection, concord and royal rites, and hymns on Skambha, Prāṇa, Kāla, the Brahmacārin and the Vrātya that name one supporting principle (jyeṣṭha brahman).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** ekaṃ sat (the One Being), tad ekam (That One), Puruṣa, Hiraṇyagarbha / Prajāpati, Viśvakarman, Skambha, jyeṣṭha brahman (the highest Brahman), brahman, Vāc (as self of all, RV 10.125), Prāṇa (AVŚ 11.4), Kāla (AVŚ 19.53)
**Descriptions:** The One Being that the inspired call by many names — Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Agni, Yama, Mātariśvan (RV 1.164.46); the one bird the poets shape into many (10.114.5); the One that has become all this (8.58.2).; That One which breathed windless by its own power before there was being or non-being, death or immortality, and was born by the power of tapas (RV 10.129.2–3).; The Person who is all this, what was and what will be, lord of immortality; one quarter of him is all beings, three quarters the immortal in heaven (RV 10.90.2–3).; The highest Brahman whose alone is the light; those who know Brahman in man know the Supreme (AVŚ 10.8.1, 10.7.17); the one-alone god in whom the gods become one (AVŚ 13.4.12–13); 'that is Agni, that the Sun … that is brahman' (VS 32.1).; The self, desireless, wise, immortal, self-existent, lacking nothing; knowing him one does not fear death (AVŚ 10.8.44).
**Negations:** 'Then there was neither non-being nor being' (RV 10.129.1); 'Who really knows? … perhaps he does not know' (RV 10.129.6–7); 'There is no likeness of him' (VS 32.3); 'You will not find him who produced these — something else has come between you' (RV 10.82.7); 'He is called neither second nor third …' (AVŚ 13.4.16–18)
**Relation to self:** Named in the Saṃhitās as the self of what moves and stands (Sūrya, RV 1.115.1), the self present in the golden city of the body (AVŚ 10.2.32–33), the one god who entered the mind (AVŚ 10.8.28) and the ageless self known by the wise (10.8.44); the seer who first 'does not know what he is' (RV 1.164.37) can also say 'I was Manu and the Sun' (4.26.1).
**Relation to world:** Both immanent and exceeding: the One has become all this (RV 8.58.2) and the Person covers the earth and exceeds it by ten fingers, three quarters of him beyond (10.90.1–3); the world is made by sacrifice of the Person, by the All-maker, from the Golden Germ, by tapas, or from Time — accounts the Saṃhitās place side by side.
**Caveat:** The Saṃhitās are a liturgy addressed to many gods, each praised in turn as supreme; the statements of one ultimate are a minority of speculative hymns, and they leave the origin itself in question (10.129.6–7). The ritual tradition's own hermeneutics (Mīmāṃsā) reads the corpus for injunctions and holds the deities to be what the mantras name, positing no single describable ultimate; Yāska records the view that there are three deities (Nirukta 7.5) beside the view that one Self is praised in many ways (7.4); Vaiṣṇava readers take the One as Viṣṇu. The one-truth reading (RV 1.164.46) is used by this ontology only in the interpretation layer.
