CE; Abhayadatta's collection of lives c. 11th–12th c.; (confidence low)

The Indian tantric adepts (siddhas) of c. 8th–12th c. — the eighty-four of Abhayadatta's lives and others — whose songs (dohās, caryāgīti) and instructions teach the innate (sahaja) and mahāmudrā beyond ritual and conceptual meditation, and whose lineages passed into the Tibetan Kagyu, Sakya and other schools and overlap with the Nāth siddhas.

## Distinctive positions
- The innate (sahaja), the nature of one's own mind, is the ultimate; it is found not by learning, ritual or contrived meditation but through the true guru's instruction (People Dohā v19, v38–39).
- Brahmanical, Jain and Buddhist monastic observances and even tantric ritual are criticized when taken as ends in themselves (People Dohā v2–15; Queen Dohā v32).
- Realization is lived in ordinary life — householder, courtesan, weaver, cobbler, fisherman, king — and each siddha's own trade becomes the image of the path (Caturaśītisiddhabodhihṛdaya).
- The songs (dohā, caryā) use intentional language (sandhyābhāṣā): outer images for inner yoga.
- Unconventional conduct (vratacaryā, unmattacaryā) is undertaken only after realization and with discernment (Caturaśītisiddhabodhihṛdaya: Nāgārjuna's song; Mekopa's song).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** sahaja (lhan cig skyes pa, the innate / co-emergent), cittatā, mind itself (sems nyid), mahāmudrā (phyag rgya chen po), mahāsukha (bde ba chen po), the natural state (gnyug ma, nija)
**Descriptions:** mind itself alone is the seed of everything, from which saṃsāra and nirvāṇa radiate; it grants what is wished like a wish-fulfilling jewel (People Dohā v42–43); the innate is the Buddha dwelling in the body (People Dohā v82); like space: beyond colour and shape, unstained by good and evil (Gaṅgā Mahāmudrā v11)
**Negations:** not thing and not no-thing (People Dohā v21–22); cannot be told in words (People Dohā v39)
**Relation to self:** The innate is realized when self and other are not distinguished (People Dohā v73, v98); Saraha's 'self' (bdag nyid) language is not an ātman doctrine but the nature of mind.
**Relation to world:** Whatever is seen in front, behind and in the ten directions is 'that itself' (People Dohā v29); saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are not two (People Dohā v122).
**Caveat:** The siddhas point to the nature of mind rather than to a being; their 'one' is the non-duality of appearance and emptiness, and they would reject both an eternal self and a mere nothingness.
