The Kaula or Kula tradition teaches the worship of the Goddess and her 'family' (kula) of powers — the yoginīs and mothers — together with Bhairava, as a path of both enjoyment (bhukti) and liberation (mukti). Its early scripture is the Kaulajñānanirṇaya ascribed to Matsyendra; its classic later exposition is the Kulārṇava Tantra (17 ullāsas) on the guru, initiation, the āmnāyas, the seven ullāsas and the Kula worship with the five tattvas; it branches into the four or five āmnāyas (Kubjikā's Paścimāmnāya, the Kālī Uttarāmnāya, Śrīvidyā's Dakṣiṇāmnāya, the Pūrva and Ūrdhva), shaped the Trika of Abhinavagupta and the eastern Kālī and Tārā cults, and insists on secrecy, the guru's authority and graded eligibility.

## Distinctive positions
- Kula is Śakti and Akula is Śiva; the Kaulas are skilled in joining kula and akula (Kulārṇava 17.27).
- Kaula is the highest of the seven modes of conduct: Veda < Vaiṣṇava < Śaiva < Dakṣiṇa < Vāma < Siddhānta < Kaula (Kulārṇava 2.7-8).
- In the Kula dharma enjoyment itself becomes yoga, sin becomes merit and saṃsāra becomes liberation (Kulārṇava 2.23-24).
- The Kula truth is beyond both duality and non-duality (Kulārṇava 1.110).
- The body is the temple and the jīva is Sadāśiva; bound it is jīva, freed it is Śiva (Kulārṇava 9.41-43).
- The teaching passes only from ear to ear from a tested guru to a tested disciple, never to the deceitful (Kaulajñānanirṇaya 6.9-14; Kulārṇava 14.19, 14.25).
- Worship with the kula substances (summary only) is held to reveal the bliss of Brahman latent in the body, and is condemned when done for pleasure or without initiation (Kulārṇava 2.117; 5.79-80; 5.99; 5.104).
- In the circle of worship (bhairavīcakra) all castes are twice-born; outside it they are separate (Kulārṇava 8.96-97).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Kula and Akula, Kuleśvarī and Kuleśvara (Bhairava), Śiva as the supreme Brahman
**Descriptions:** Śiva, the supreme Brahman, partless, all-knowing, the doer of all, pure, non-dual, self-luminous, without beginning or end, without qualities, being–consciousness–bliss (Kulārṇava 1.7-8); the bliss (ānanda) born of the union of Śakti and Śiva is called liberation (Kulārṇava 5.79); bliss is the form of Brahman, and it abides in the body (Kulārṇava 5.80)
**Negations:** beyond both duality and non-duality (Kulārṇava 1.110)
**Relation to self:** The individual souls are parts of Śiva like sparks of a fire, covered by beginningless ignorance (Kulārṇava 1.8-9); bound by fetters the self is jīva, freed it is Sadāśiva (Kulārṇava 9.42-43).
**Relation to world:** Kula is Śakti — the totality of powers and of the manifest — and Akula is Śiva; the Kaula joins both (Kulārṇava 17.27).
**Caveat:** The Kaula texts refuse to be classed as dualist or non-dualist (Kulārṇava 1.110) and insist that the truth is realised through the guru's transmission and Kula practice, not by learning (1.96-100); a purely doctrinal one-truth reading leaves out the ritual and lineage conditions they make essential.
