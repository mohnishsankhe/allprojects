**Dates:** Tradition's account: founded in 1175 (Angen 1) when Hōnen, reading Shandao, turned to the exclusive nenbutsu; Scholarly account: late 12th c.; institutionalised under the Chinzei line from the 14th c.; (confidence high)
**Status:** living

The Jōdo school founded by Hōnen (1133–1212), who after decades of Tendai study found in Shandao's commentary that calling Amitābha's name wholeheartedly at all times is 'the rightly determined act, because it accords with the Buddha's vow', and taught exclusive nenbutsu to all, monastic and lay, good and evil. Its classic is the Senchakushū; its creed the One-Sheet Document. After persecution (1207, 1227) Hōnen's disciples formed lines — Chinzei (Benchō), Seizan (Shōkū), and the extinct lines of Kōsai, Ryūkan and Chōsai — of which Chinzei became the main body of modern Jōdo-shū.

## Distinctive positions
- Of the two gates, set aside the holy path and enter the Pure Land gate; of the right and miscellaneous practices, set aside the miscellaneous and take the right; of the right, the calling of the name is the rightly determined act, because it accords with the Buddha's primal vow (Senchakushū).
- The eighteenth vow 'selected' (senchaku) the nenbutsu as the one practice for birth, above all other practices, being both superior (the name holds all virtues) and easy (open to all).
- The settled mind (anjin: the three minds of the Contemplation Sūtra), the practice undertaken (kigyō: the five right practices) and the manner of practice (sagyō: the four modes) — terms from Shandao.
- Believe that birth is attained by even one calling, yet keep calling all one's life (Hōnen's letters).
- At death Amitābha comes to welcome the practitioner (raigō); right mindfulness at death is sought.
- Monastic and lay alike; the bodhisattva precepts (Tendai line) kept alongside the nenbutsu; the fivefold transmission (gojū sōden) as the school's rite of entrusting the teaching.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Amida Butsu, Gokuraku jōdo (the Land of Utmost Bliss), the name Namu Amida Butsu, in which all virtues are gathered
**Descriptions:** The name contains all the virtues of the Buddha's inner realization and outer activity; hence it is superior and was selected in the primal vow (Senchakushū 3).; Amida is a reward-body buddha in a reward land to which ordinary beings are born by the power of the vow (following Shandao).
**Relation to self:** The practitioner is an ignorant ordinary being who should, like one who knows not a single letter, simply say the nenbutsu (One-Sheet Document).
**Relation to world:** This world in the last age is to be left; the Pure Land in the west is where awakening is completed; one lives so as best to say the nenbutsu.
**Caveat:** Jōdo-shū keeps 'pointing to a direction and establishing form' (shihō rissō) and rejects readings of the Pure Land as mind only; it would not accept reducing Amida to an impersonal absolute.
