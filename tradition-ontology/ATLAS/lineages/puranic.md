# The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)

`lin:puranic` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Purāṇa tradition, itihāsa-purāṇa tradition, the sūta-paurāṇika transmission, Paurāṇika
**Founders:** [Vyāsa (Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana)](../teachers/vyasa.md), [Romaharṣaṇa](../teachers/romaharsana.md)
**Key teachers:** [Vyāsa (Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana)](../teachers/vyasa.md), [Romaharṣaṇa](../teachers/romaharsana.md), [Ugraśravas Sauti](../teachers/ugrasravas.md), [Śuka](../teachers/suka.md), [Parāśara](../teachers/parasara.md), [Maitreya (sage)](../teachers/maitreya-puranic.md), [Mārkaṇḍeya](../teachers/markandeya.md), [Nārada](../teachers/narada.md), [Sanatkumāra](../teachers/sanatkumara.md), [Dattātreya](../teachers/dattatreya.md), [Upamanyu](../teachers/upamanyu.md), [Hayagrīva](../teachers/hayagriva.md), [Medhas](../teachers/medhas.md), [Śrīdhara Svāmin](../teachers/sridhara-svamin.md)
**Regions:** all of South Asia; recited at Naimiṣa (the tradition's frame), in temples, tīrthas and homes
**Dates:** Tradition's account: The Purāṇa is remembered by Brahmā before the Vedas (Matsya 53.3); in every Dvāpara age Vyāsa condenses it to four lakh verses in eighteen Purāṇas (Matsya 53.8-11); the present Vyāsa, Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana, taught it to Romaharṣaṇa at the junction of Dvāpara and Kali (Kali reckoned from 3102 BCE); Scholarly account: Purāṇa is named as a class of lore from the Atharvaveda and Brāhmaṇas on; the extant Purāṇas were composed and continually redacted from about the 3rd-4th c. CE to the second millennium CE; (confidence low)
**Status:** living

The tradition of the Purāṇas ('ancient lore'): Sanskrit compendia recited by sūtas and paurāṇikas, attributed to Vyāsa, teaching cosmology and time, genealogies of gods, sages and kings, the dharma of vows, pilgrimage and gifts, the rites of death and the ancestors, yoga, and above all devotion to Viṣṇu, Śiva or the Goddess. It carries the Vedic family's teaching to all classes and both sexes and supplies the scriptural base for most later devotional lineages.

## Distinctive positions
- The Purāṇa (with itihāsa) is the 'fifth Veda' and expands the meaning of the Veda (BhP 1.4.20; Matsya 53.3); it was taught out of compassion for those without access to the Veda (BhP 1.4.25).
- A Purāṇa has five marks - sarga, pratisarga, vaṃśa, manvantara, vaṃśānucarita (VP 3.6.24; KūP 1.1.12; Matsya 53.65); the Bhāgavata gives two lists of ten marks (BhP 2.10.1; 12.7.9-10).
- Eighteen Mahāpurāṇas and further Upapurāṇas, with lists that differ in detail (VP 3.6.21-23; BhP 12.7.23-24; KūP 1.1.13-20; Matsya 53).
- Cyclical cosmology: yugas, mahāyugas, manvantaras, kalpas and the lifetime of Brahmā; four kinds of dissolution (nitya, naimittika, prākṛtika, ātyantika).
- A layered cosmos of fourteen worlds, Bhū-maṇḍala of seven island-continents and seven oceans around Meru, and hells beneath the earth.
- Death is followed by Yama's messengers, a year-long road, judgment, and a preta state that the rites of the sons (piṇḍa, śrāddha, sapiṇḍīkaraṇa) end (Garuḍa Pretakalpa).
- Devotion (bhakti) to the chosen supreme deity - Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa, Śiva or the Goddess - is the highest dharma and the easy means in the Kali age; the divine name purifies (BhP 1.2.6; 6.2.9-10; 12.3.51-52; VP 6.2.17).
- Yoga is taught in the eightfold form, directed to the deity's form as its support (VP 6.7; BhP 3.28; LiP 1.8-9; ŚiP 7.2.37-39); powers are obstacles (BhP 11.15.33).
- Sectarian variety about the ultimate: each Purāṇa exalts its deity, while many passages identify Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva as functions of one reality (VP 1.2.66).

**Transmissions received:** 
  - [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](vedic-srauta.md) — what: itihāsa-purāṇa recitation as part of Vedic ritual (the pāriplava cycle of the Aśvamedha) and the name 'fifth Veda'; evidence: ChU 7.1.2; ŚB 13.4.3.13 (U01/U03 own the refs); BhP 1.4.20
  - [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](epic-teaching.md) — what: narrative material, genealogies and teaching dialogues shared with the Mahābhārata and Harivaṃśa; evidence: textual parallels (see brw:mahabharata-to-puranic)
  - [Sāṃkhya](samkhya.md) — what: the order of creation from pradhāna through mahat, ahaṃkāra, tanmātras, elements and senses; evidence: VP 1.2; BhP 2.10.3, 3.26 (see brw:samkhya-to-puranic)
  - [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](patanjala-yoga.md) — what: the eight limbs, the definition of yoga as restraint of the activities of citta, the list of obstacles; evidence: LiP 1.8.7-9, 1.9.1-16; VP 6.7.36-45; ŚiP 7.2.37-38 (see brw:patanjala-yoga-to-puranic)
  - [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](pasupata.md) — what: Pāśupata yoga and the Pāśupata vow in the Śaiva Purāṇas; evidence: LiP 1.7.54, 1.80, 1.88, 2.55; ŚiP 7.1.33; KūP (Īśvara Gītā)
  - [Pāñcarātra](pancaratra.md) — what: the Sātvata/Pāñcarātra doctrine of Vāsudeva and the vyūhas; evidence: BhP 1.3.8 (Nārada 'expounded the Sātvata tantra')
**Transmissions given:** 
  - `lin:gaudiya-vaisnava` — what: the Bhāgavata as the root scripture of rasa theology and of the practice of the name
  - `lin:pustimarga` — what: the Bhāgavata (Vallabha's Subodhinī) as scripture of the path of grace
  - `lin:srividya` — what: the Lalitopākhyāna's narrative of Lalitā; the Lalitā Sahasranāma's self-attribution to the Brahmāṇḍa
  - `lin:sakta` — what: the Devī Māhātmya as the root Śākta scripture; the Devī Bhāgavata
  - `lin:datta-sampradaya` — what: Dattātreya as avadhūta teacher of yoga (Mārkaṇḍeya 16-44; BhP 1.3.11, 11.7-9)
  - `lin:varkari` — what: the Bhāgavata's devotion to the name
  - `lin:ekasarana` — what: the Bhāgavata as the scripture retold by Śaṅkaradeva
