# Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata

**Alternate names:** Vīraśaivism, Liṅgāyata, Liṅgavanta, Vīramāheśvara, Śaraṇa tradition, Śivādvaita (self-designation in the Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi), Śakti-viśiṣṭādvaita (philosophical designation, after Śrīpati)

**Regions:** Karnataka (Kalyāṇa, Kūḍalasaṅgama, Uḷavi, Baḷḷigāve, Uḍutaḍi), Andhra Pradesh and Telangana (Śrīśaila, Kollipāki), southern Maharashtra (Sonnalige/Solapur), Tamil Nadu (minority), Kāśī and Kedāra (seats of two of the five pīṭhas)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: two accounts: (a) beginningless (anādi) dharma taught by Śiva in the Āgamas and renewed in every age by the five ācāryas; (b) begun by Basava and the śaraṇas in twelfth-century Kalyāṇa; Scholarly account: 12th-c. devotional movement in Karnataka with earlier Śaiva antecedents; Sanskrit systematization and anthologies 14th–16th c.; living community; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The Vīraśaiva or Liṅgāyata tradition of Karnataka and neighbouring regions worships Śiva as the liṅga, worn by every initiate on the body. Its śaraṇa strand looks to Basava, Allama Prabhu, Akka Mahādēvi, Cennabasava and the twelfth-century vacana saints of Kalyāṇa; its Pañcācārya strand looks to five ācāryas who emerged from liṅgas in every age and to the Sanskrit Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi. Both teach the six stages to union with Śiva, the unity of guru, liṅga and jaṅgama, work as worship and the equality of devotees.

## Distinctive positions
- Śiva is worshipped as the liṅga, and every initiate wears a personal liṅga (iṣṭaliṅga) on the body for life, worshipping it on the palm rather than in temples.
- The path runs through six stages (ṣaṭsthala: bhakta, māheśvara, prasādi, prāṇaliṅgi, śaraṇa, aikya) to the union of the soul (aṅga) with the liṅga (liṅgāṅga-sāmarasya); the Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi elaborates them as 101 sthalas.
- Guru, liṅga and jaṅgama (the moving devotee-teacher) are one; the eight shields (aṣṭāvaraṇa) and five codes (pañcācāra) govern life.
- Work is worship (kāyaka) and its fruit is to be shared (dāsōha).
- Initiation is open to all irrespective of caste and sex; the vacanas reject caste, the five pollutions (pañcasūtaka), Vedic sacrifice and temple ritualism.
- The vacanas — spontaneous utterances of experience (anubhava) — are scripture for the śaraṇa strand; the Āgamas, the Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi and Śrīpati's Śrīkarabhāṣya are scripture for the Pañcācārya strand.
- Death is union with the liṅga (liṅgaikya): the dead are buried, not cremated, and no ancestral rites are needed.
- The two strands hold different accounts of the tradition's origin and different views on its relation to the Veda and to 'Hinduism' (recorded as internal debates, not settled here).

**Transmissions received:**

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Śiva / Paraśiva, Liṅga, Mahāliṅga, brahman, Śūnya / Bayalu (Allama), Kūḍalasaṅgamadēva (Basava's name for the Lord), Guhēśvara (Allama), Cennamallikārjuna (Akka Mahādēvi), Rāmanātha (Dāsimayya)
**Descriptions:** the liṅga in which the world dissolves and from which it arises; being-consciousness-bliss (SSM 6.36–38); the pedestal is Śakti, the liṅga Paraśiva, the universe their union (SSM 11.32); the liṅga of light shining in all beings (SSM 6.35); the ocean of consciousness whose waves are the thirty-six tattvas (SSM 1.3); the one god among the many household gods (Basava); the husband who does not die or decay (Akka Mahādēvi)
**Negations:** beyond contemplation and unmanifest (SSM 6.51); where neither sun nor moon shines (SSM 12.38); not the avyakta with Maheśvara as its bearer (SSM 12.37); the void, bayalu (Allama)
**Relation to self:** The soul (aṅga) is a part of Śiva through beginningless ignorance and progresses through six stations to union (aikya, sāmarasya); in practice the relation is master and servant, and at union difference is seen to be imagined by māyā.
**Relation to world:** The world arises from Śiva and is not different from him, as his Śakti's manifestation and his body (aṣṭamūrti) — neither identical with nor separate from him; for the śaraṇas the moving devotee is his living temple.
**Caveat:** The tradition speaks with two voices: the Pañcācārya strand calls its doctrine Śivādvaita or Śakti-viśiṣṭādvaita and insists that difference is real at the level of practice; the vacana strand names Śiva as a personal lord and as the void and resists Vedāntic framing. Both would reject a reading of the one truth that makes the world, the liṅga or devotion illusory (SSM 10.37–43).
