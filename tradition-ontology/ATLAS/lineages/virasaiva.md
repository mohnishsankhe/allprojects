**Status:** living

The Vīraśaiva or Liṅgāyata tradition of Karnataka and neighbouring regions worships Śiva as the liṅga, worn by every initiate on the body. Its śaraṇa strand looks to Basava, Allama Prabhu, Akka Mahādēvi, Cennabasava and the twelfth-century vacana saints of Kalyāṇa; its Pañcācārya strand looks to five ācāryas who emerged from liṅgas in every age and to the Sanskrit Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi. Both teach the six stages to union with Śiva, the unity of guru, liṅga and jaṅgama, work as worship and the equality of devotees.

## Distinctive positions
- Śiva is worshipped as the liṅga, and every initiate wears a personal liṅga (iṣṭaliṅga) on the body for life, worshipping it on the palm rather than in temples.
- The path runs through six stages (ṣaṭsthala: bhakta, māheśvara, prasādi, prāṇaliṅgi, śaraṇa, aikya) to the union of the soul (aṅga) with the liṅga (liṅgāṅga-sāmarasya); the Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi elaborates them as 101 sthalas.
- Guru, liṅga and jaṅgama (the moving devotee-teacher) are one; the eight shields (aṣṭāvaraṇa) and five codes (pañcācāra) govern life.
- Work is worship (kāyaka) and its fruit is to be shared (dāsōha).
- Initiation is open to all irrespective of caste and sex; the vacanas reject caste, the five pollutions (pañcasūtaka), Vedic sacrifice and temple ritualism.
- The vacanas — spontaneous utterances of experience (anubhava) — are scripture for the śaraṇa strand; the Āgamas, the Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi and Śrīpati's Śrīkarabhāṣya are scripture for the Pañcācārya strand.
- Death is union with the liṅga (liṅgaikya): the dead are buried, not cremated, and no ancestral rites are needed.
- The two strands hold different accounts of the tradition's origin and different views on its relation to the Veda and to 'Hinduism' (recorded as internal debates, not settled here).
