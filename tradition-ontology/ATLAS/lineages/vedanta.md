The school of the 'later inquiry' (uttara-mīmāṃsā) into the Upaniṣads, systematized in Bādarāyaṇa's Brahma Sūtras. Its four chapters establish that the Upaniṣads teach Brahman, defend this against rival systems, set out the means, and describe the result. Every later Vedānta school — Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita, Dvaita, the Bhedābheda schools, Śuddhādvaita, Acintya-bhedābheda and Śaiva Vedānta — is constituted by a commentary on the sūtras.

## Distinctive positions
- The three foundations (prasthāna-traya): the Upaniṣads, the Bhagavad Gītā and the Brahma Sūtras; every Vedānta school establishes itself by commenting on all three.
- Brahman is that from which the origin, sustenance and dissolution of the world proceed (BS 1.1.2).
- Brahman is known only from scripture, not by independent inference (BS 1.1.3; 2.1.11).
- All Upaniṣadic passages converge (samanvaya) on Brahman (BS 1.1.4).
- The non-conscious pradhāna of Sāṃkhya is not the world-cause, because the cause 'thought' (īkṣati) (BS 1.1.5; 2.2.1).
- Brahman is both the material and the efficient cause of the world (BS 1.4.23).
- Liberation follows from knowledge of Brahman; the liberated do not return (BS 3.4.1; 4.4.22).
- The nature of the relation between the individual self and Brahman is the defining question on which the Vedānta schools divide (already debated by Āśmarathya, Auḍulomi and Kāśakṛtsna, BS 1.4.20–22).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Brahman, Ātman, Ānandamaya (contested reading, BS 1.1.12)
**Descriptions:** that from which the origin, sustenance and dissolution of this world proceed (BS 1.1.2); known from scripture (BS 1.1.3), the purport of all Upaniṣadic texts (BS 1.1.4); conscious: it 'thought' before creating (BS 1.1.5); material and efficient cause of the world (BS 1.4.23); creates as sport (līlā), without need (BS 2.1.33); the goal attained by the liberated, from which there is no return (BS 4.4.22)
**Negations:** the 'not so, not so' denies 'so much' (the two forms) of Brahman (BS 3.2.22); not distinguished by place/limiting adjuncts (BS 3.2.11)
**Relation to self:** Debated within the sūtras themselves: Āśmarathya, Auḍulomi and Kāśakṛtsna give three accounts (BS 1.4.20–22); every Vedānta school reads the sūtras on this point differently.
**Relation to world:** Brahman is the world's cause; the effect is non-different from the cause (BS 2.1.14); the manner (real or apparent transformation) is the chief point of divergence among commentators.
**Caveat:** This view records what the sūtras state on their face. Each Vedānta school's reading (Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita, Dvaita, Bhedābheda etc.) is its own view, and the schools deny that the others' readings are the sūtras' meaning.
