The Yoga darśana of Patañjali's Yoga Sūtra (four pādas, 195 sūtras) and its commentaries — Vyāsa's bhāṣya, Vācaspati Miśra's Tattvavaiśāradī, the Vivaraṇa ascribed to Śaṅkara, Bhoja's Rājamārtaṇḍa, Vijñānabhikṣu's Yogavārttika and later vṛttis. It teaches the stilling of the mind through practice, dispassion, devotion to Īśvara and the eight limbs, leading through the samādhis to discriminative discernment between sattva and puruṣa and to kaivalya. The tradition names Hiraṇyagarbha as the first teacher of yoga.

## Distinctive positions
- Yoga is the stilling (nirodha) of the activities (vṛtti) of the mind (YS 1.2); then the seer abides in its own form (1.3). Vyāsa: 'yoga is samādhi', a property of the mind at all its levels (YBh 1.1).
- Accepts the Sāṃkhya analysis of the seer (puruṣa, pure consciousness, many) and the seen (prakṛti, the three guṇas), and adds Īśvara, a special puruṣa untouched by afflictions, karma, fruition and residues (1.24), the teacher of the ancients (1.26), designated by Oṃ (1.27).
- Means: practice and dispassion (1.12); devotion to Īśvara (1.23); for the unconcentrated, kriyā-yoga (2.1); the eight limbs (2.29).
- The five afflictions (kleśa), rooted in ignorance (avidyā), produce the store of karma that ripens as birth, life-span and experience (2.3–14); the conjunction of seer and seen, caused by ignorance, is the cause of the suffering to be avoided (2.17, 2.24).
- The śāstra has four parts like medicine: what is to be avoided, its cause, avoidance, and the means of avoidance (YBh 2.15).
- Samādhi is with full awareness (samprajñāta: with reasoning, reflection, bliss, I-am-ness) or beyond it (asamprajñāta); with seed (sabīja) or seedless (nirbīja) (1.17–18, 1.46, 1.51).
- Three means of valid knowledge: perception, inference, testimony (1.7).
- Things transform in property, time-mark and state (3.13); past and future exist in their own form (4.12).
- The mind is not self-luminous; it is seen by the unchanging puruṣa, who always knows its activities (4.18–19); objects do not depend on a single mind (4.15–16).
- Powers (vibhūti, siddhi) arise from saṃyama but are obstacles in samādhi (3.37); dispassion even toward the highest of them leads to kaivalya (3.50); the yogin should not be attached or proud when the celestials invite him (3.51).
- Liberation is kaivalya, the aloneness of the seer, when the guṇas, their purpose fulfilled, return to their source (4.34); Vyāsa: when afflictions and karma cease, the wise one is liberated while living (YBh 4.30).
- Practitioners are graded by the intensity of their means and ardor (1.21–22).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** puruṣa / draṣṭṛ (the seer), citiśakti (the power of consciousness), kaivalya (aloneness) — the seer's own state, Īśvara, a special puruṣa (puruṣa-viśeṣa)
**Descriptions:** The seer is seeing alone; though pure, it sees by conforming to cognitions (YS 2.20).; Unchanging, it always knows the mind's activities (4.18); consciousness does not move into objects (4.22).; Vyāsa: the power of consciousness is unchanging, does not pass over, has objects shown to it, is pure and infinite (YBh 1.2).; Kaivalya: the guṇas, empty of purpose for the puruṣa, return to their source; or the power of consciousness established in its own form (4.34).; Īśvara: a special puruṣa untouched by afflictions, karma, fruition and residues (1.24), in whom the seed of omniscience is unsurpassed (1.25), teacher of the ancients (1.26), designated by Oṃ (1.27).
**Negations:** not an object (only the seen is object: 4.19); not the mind, not the buddhi-sattva: 'utterly unmixed' with it (3.35); not touched by afflictions, karma, fruition or residues (Īśvara, 1.24); neither the same as buddhi nor wholly different from it (YBh 2.20)
**Relation to self:** The puruṣa is one's true self (the seer); taking the instrument of seeing for the seer is I-am-ness (2.6) and taking the non-self for the self is ignorance (2.5).
**Relation to world:** The seen (prakṛti: elements and senses, three guṇas) is real, exists for the sake of the seer — for experience and liberation (2.18, 2.21) — and persists for other puruṣas when it has ceased for one who is liberated (2.22).
**Caveat:** Yoga posits many puruṣas (2.22; YBh 2.22), a real prakṛti, and Īśvara as a distinct special puruṣa who is not the material of the world. It does not teach one self of all; a one-truth reading would have to treat this plurality as a standpoint, which Vyāsa and Vācaspati would reject. Vijñānabhikṣu reads Yoga within his Vedānta of non-separation (avibhāga), where souls are non-separate from the Lord — itself a commentator's view. 'Personal or impersonal: both' = the puruṣa is impersonal pure consciousness, Īśvara is personal (a teacher, approached in devotion).
