**Status:** living

The Śrīvaiṣṇava school following Piḷḷai Lokācārya and Maṇavāḷa Māmuni, centred on Śrīraṅgam and on the Maṇipravāḷa commentaries on the Tamil Prabandham; it holds that the Lord's grace alone saves, with Śrī as mediator. It wears the Y-shaped mark with the line extending down the nose.

## Distinctive positions
- The Lord alone is the means (siddhopāya); prapatti is not a means but the soul's acceptance of, and awakening to, its dependence; to treat it as a means is a fault.
- Grace is without cause (nirhetuka-kṛpā); 'mārjāra-kiśora-nyāya': the mother cat carries the kitten, which does nothing.
- Śrī is the mediator (puruṣakāra) who turns the soul to the Lord and the Lord to the soul; she is not herself the means; she is commonly reported as atomic (aṇu) and of the rank of souls (jīva-koṭi), though ever inseparable from him.
- The Lord relishes the faults of his devotees as a cow licks its newborn calf (doṣa-bhogya).
- The surrendered keep scriptural duties as service (kainkarya) and for the sake of the world, not as a means or under threat.
- The teacher's gracious regard (ācārya-abhimāna) is itself sufficient for those who can do nothing else; the Tamil Prabandham has primacy of place.
- Kaivalya is an eternal state from which there is no return.


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Śrīman Nārāyaṇa, Emperumāṉ (our Lord), Brahman
**Descriptions:** as in Viśiṣṭādvaita generally; with the emphasis that the Lord himself is the already-accomplished means (siddhopāya) whose grace is causeless, who relishes even his devotees' faults, and to whom Śrī, ever inseparable, mediates
**Negations:** as for Viśiṣṭādvaita
**Relation to self:** The soul's essence is to belong wholly to the Lord (ananyārha-śeṣatva) and to be dependent (pāratantrya); it brings nothing of its own to its salvation.
**Relation to world:** As for Viśiṣṭādvaita: the world is the real body of the Lord.
**Caveat:** Same caveat as ult:visistadvaita; in addition the Teṅkalai reject any view that the soul's own act contributes to its salvation.
