The Ājīvikas were a naked ascetic order of Gosāla Maṅkhaliputta and his predecessors. No Ājīvika text survives; the doctrine is known only through hostile Buddhist, Jain and Tamil sources, which report a doctrine of total determinism (niyati) in which every being reaches the end of suffering after a fixed round of rebirths. The Jain Bhagavatī Sūtra (book 15) tells Gosāla's life, quarrel with Mahāvīra and death; the Aśokan and Daśaratha cave inscriptions record royal donations; the Tamil Maṇimēkalai and Nīlakēci attest a later South Indian community that has since disappeared.

## Distinctive positions
- (As reported) Niyati: all beings are without control, power or energy; they are 'transformed by destiny, circumstance and nature' (niyati-saṅgati-bhāva-pariṇatā) and experience pleasure and pain in six classes (DN 2).
- (As reported) Purification through saṃsāra (saṃsāra-suddhi): fool and wise alike, after transmigrating through 8,400,000 great aeons, make an end of suffering, like a ball of string that unwinds to its end; no vow or austerity ripens unripe karma (DN 2).
- (As reported) Six classes of birth (abhijāti) from black to supremely white, the supreme white being the three Ājīvika leaders (AN 6.57).
- (As reported) There is no exertion, action, strength, energy or human effort; all things are fixed (Uvāsagadasāo 7).
- (Jain account) Reanimation (pauṭṭa-parihāra): a soul may enter successive bodies; Gosāla claimed to be the seventh such re-entry.
- (Jain account) Mastery of the eightfold Mahānimitta and of the fiery power (tejoleśyā).
- (Tamil account) Five principles — life (uyir) and four kinds of indestructible atoms — and six births ending in release in the supreme white birth (Maṇimēkalai 27).
- Naked austerities (acelaka) and severe fasting rules (MN 36; Sthānāṅga 4).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** niyati (destiny, necessity), the end of suffering after the fixed round (dukkhass' anta), iṟai, 'the boundless knower' (Maṇimēkalai 27, Tamil Ājīvika)
**Descriptions:** (As reported) All beings are transformed by niyati, saṅgati and bhāva (DN 2).; (As reported) Pleasure and pain are measured out as with a measure; saṃsāra has fixed limits (DN 2).; (Tamil report) The lord is the boundless knower who pervades endless things everywhere and always; release is reached in the supreme white birth (Maṇimēkalai 27.108–165).
**Negations:** No cause or condition for the defilement or purification of beings (DN 2).; No exertion, strength, energy or human effort (Uvāsagadasāo 7).
**Relation to self:** (As reported) The jīva is real and transmigrates through the fixed round; in the Jain account a soul can re-enter successive bodies (reanimation).
**Relation to world:** (As reported) The world-course is fixed in every detail and runs through 8,400,000 great aeons for every being.
**Caveat:** No Ājīvika text survives; every statement comes from hostile Buddhist, Jain or Tamil sources. Niyati is a governing order, not a ground of being, and the sources never present it as 'the one truth'. The Tamil report of a 'lord who is the boundless knower' may refer to the omniscient teacher rather than a creator; it is recorded as the source gives it.
