The Kagyu ('lineage of the word') are the Tibetan Buddhist transmissions that descend from the Indian siddhas Tilopa, Nāropa and Maitrīpa through the translator Marpa, the yogin Milarepa and the monk-physician Gampopa, whose disciples founded the 'four great and eight lesser' Dakpo Kagyu schools (Karma, Tsalpa, Barom, Phagdru; Drikung, Drukpa, Taklung, Trophu, Martsang, Yelpa, Yazang, Shugseb). Their hallmark is Mahāmudrā — direct recognition of the mind's nature through the guru's blessing — together with the Six Yogas of Nāropa. The separate Shangpa Kagyu, from the ḍākinīs Niguma and Sukhasiddhi through Khyungpo Naljor, is grouped with them by name.

## Distinctive positions
- Mahāmudrā — the nature of one's own mind, empty and luminous, the co-emergent dharmakāya — is to be recognized directly through the guru's pointing-out instruction and blessing, then stabilized through the four yogas (one-pointedness, simplicity, one taste, non-meditation).
- Two paths: the path of method (the Six Yogas of Nāropa, on the basis of tantric empowerment) and the path of liberation (Mahāmudrā); in Gampopa's system Mahāmudrā may also be introduced on a sūtra basis ('sūtra Mahāmudrā'), a claim contested by Sakya Paṇḍita (dsp:sutra-mahamudra).
- Devotion to the guru is 'the head of meditation'; the blessing passes through an unbroken 'golden rosary' of masters each said to have realized the teaching before transmitting it (the 'practice lineage').
- Thoughts are not to be suppressed: their nature is dharmakāya (Dorje Chang Thungma); appearances are 'the light of the dharmakāya' (the co-emergent instruction).
- Gampopa united the Kadam graded path (the four thoughts, refuge, bodhicitta, the perfections) with Mahāmudrā: renunciation and compassion are the ground on which the view is pointed out.
- Renunciation of worldly concerns and long solitary practice in mountain retreats (Milarepa's model) make buddhahood possible in one life and one body.
- The recognized reincarnation (tulku) of teachers begins with the Karmapas (tradition's account).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Mahāmudrā (phyag rgya chen po, 'the great seal'), the nature of mind; mind-as-such (sems nyid), dharmakāya (chos sku), co-emergent wisdom (lhan cig skyes pa'i ye shes), ordinary mind (tha mal gyi shes pa), clear light ('od gsal), buddha-nature (bde gshegs snying po)
**Descriptions:** One's own mind, luminous (clear) and empty, unborn, beyond colour, shape and location, like space (Gaṅgā Mahāmudrā; Nāropa's Dṛṣṭisaṃkṣipta).; The union of clarity and emptiness, of appearance and emptiness, of bliss and emptiness — 'neither existent, for even the victors do not see it, nor non-existent, for it is the basis of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa' (3rd Karmapa's Aspiration Prayer).; Its emptiness is the dharmakāya, its unceasing radiance the nirmāṇakāya, their union great bliss the saṃbhogakāya (Karṇatantravajrapada).; The nature of thought is dharmakāya; appearances are the light of the dharmakāya.
**Negations:** not an existent thing: 'even the victors do not see it'; not nothing: it is the basis of all saṃsāra and nirvāṇa; beyond speech, thought and expression; cannot be shown (Gaṅgā Mahāmudrā v1); not a self (ātman) or a primordial nature (pradhāna) as the tīrthikas hold (Dṛṣṭisaṃkṣipta)
**Relation to self:** The ultimate is one's own mind's nature, not something to be sought elsewhere; the 'self' grasped by beings is the delusion that obscures it; buddha-nature is present in all beings as the cause of awakening.
**Relation to world:** Appearances are mind's own display, appearing yet empty like a moon in water; saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are that same mind obscured or freed of adventitious stains.
**Caveat:** The Kagyu would reject reading 'mind's nature' as a single substance, a creator, or an eternal self in the Vedāntic sense: it is empty of any intrinsic existence and is realized, not posited. Vajradhara, from whom the lineage descends, is the dharmakāya in personal form for the lineage, not a creator. 'One taste' is the yogin's realization that appearances and mind are not two, not a monist ontology.
