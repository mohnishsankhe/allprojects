The non-dual Śaiva exegetical tradition of Kashmir (9th-11th c. classical period) that interpreted the Bhairava and Kaula tantras (Mālinīvijayottara, Svacchanda, Netra, Vijñāna Bhairava, Parātrīśikā) through four interlocking systems - Spanda (Vasugupta, Kallaṭa), Pratyabhijñā (Somānanda, Utpaladeva), Trika (culminating in Abhinavagupta's Tantrāloka) and Krama (the Kālī-centred Mahānaya) - with the Kula and the little-known Mata alongside. It teaches that the self is Śiva, the world his free self-manifestation, bondage a contraction and liberation its recognition, attained by four graded means. Continued by Kṣemarāja, Jayaratha, the Kashmiri devotional poets (Lal Ded, Rūpa Bhavānī, Sāhib Kaul) and, recently, Swami Lakshmanjoo.

## Distinctive positions
- Reality is one consciousness (saṃvit, citi), Paramaśiva, whose nature is light (prakāśa) inseparably joined with reflexive awareness (vimarśa); an awareness without vimarśa would be insentient like a crystal (ĪPK 1.5.11).
- The world is a real manifestation (ābhāsa) of that consciousness by its own freedom (svātantrya), displayed on its own screen (Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya 2) - not an illusory appearance (Advaita's vivarta) and not a separate matter.
- Bondage is the contraction of consciousness through the three impurities; the root impurity is ignorance, not a substance (TĀ 1.22-23 citing the Mālinīvijayottara); liberation is recognition (pratyabhijñā) of one's identity with Śiva.
- Knowledge (jñāna) is the sole cause of liberation; ritual and yoga are means graded to the recipient's grace.
- The thirty-six tattvas (Śiva to earth), extending Sāṃkhya's twenty-five with the pure principles and the five kañcukas.
- Four means (upāya): anupāya, śāmbhava, śākta, āṇava - graded by the intensity of the descent of grace (śaktipāta).
- Liberation while living (jīvanmukti) with full activity in the world; the liberated sees the world as his own play.
- Śiva performs five acts (emission, maintenance, withdrawal, concealment, grace); the bound individual also performs them (Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya 10-11).
- Spanda: the ultimate is dynamic vibration, the opening and closing of Śaṅkara's eyes (Spandakārikā 1.1).
- Kaula ritual, including its antinomian elements, is interpreted by Abhinavagupta as an expression and means of non-dual consciousness (TĀ 29) - summary only.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Paramaśiva, Anuttara (the unsurpassed), Bhairava, Saṃvit / Citi (consciousness), Maheśvara, Hṛdaya (the Heart), Spanda
**Descriptions:** light (prakāśa) inseparable from self-reflexive awareness (vimarśa); perfect I-ness (pūrṇāhantā); absolutely free (svatantra); cause of the world's establishment (Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya 1); endowed with the powers cit, ānanda, icchā, jñāna, kriyā; performs the five acts; contains the universe as a mirror contains its images (Paramārthasāra 12-13)
**Negations:** not the ninefold, not the mass of sounds, not three-headed, not composed of nāda and bindu (Vijñāna Bhairava 11-12); free from the reckoning of space and time, indescribable in the supreme sense (VBT 14)
**Relation to self:** Identical: 'consciousness is the self' (Śiva Sūtra 1.1); the individual is Śiva contracted (Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya 4, 9), to be recognized as the Lord (ĪPK 1.1.1-3).
**Relation to world:** The world is Śiva's own free self-manifestation unfolded on his own screen (PH 2); real as his manifestation, not an illusion and not other than him.
**Caveat:** The tradition insists that the ultimate is active (vimarśa, svātantrya, the five acts) and that the world is real as its manifestation; it rejects readings of its absolute as an inactive witness or of the world as unreal appearance (Advaita), and it rejects the grammarians' paśyantī as the highest (Śivadṛṣṭi 2).
