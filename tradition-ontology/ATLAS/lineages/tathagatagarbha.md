The strand of Mahāyāna sūtras and treatises teaching that every sentient being contains the tathāgatagarbha — the buddha-nature, the pure dharmakāya obscured by adventitious defilements — which, once cleansed, is revealed as a buddha endowed with permanent, blissful, self-like and pure qualities. In India it was never a separate school; its sūtras and the Ratnagotravibhāga were read into Yogācāra and Madhyamaka, and it became central to East Asian Buddhism (Awakening of Faith, Chan, Huayan, Tiantai) and to Tibetan debates on buddha-nature (rangtong/shentong).

## Distinctive positions
- All sentient beings have (or are) the tathāgatagarbha: the embryo/womb of the Tathāgata, the buddha-element (dhātu) or buddha-nature, covered by adventitious defilements (Tathāgatagarbha Sūtra; RGV 1.27-28)
- The dharmakāya is permanent, blissful, self and pure — the four perfections of qualities (Śrīmālā; Mahāparinirvāṇa; RGV 1.35)
- The garbha is empty of the separable defilements and not empty of the inseparable buddha-qualities (Śrīmālā ch. 9; RGV 1.154-155)
- The realm of beings neither increases nor decreases; the sattvadhātu, the tathāgatagarbha and the dharmakāya are one (Anūnatvāpūrṇatvanirdeśa)
- Buddha-nature was taught to remove five faults: discouragement, contempt for others, grasping the unreal, denying the real, and excessive self-love (RGV 1.156-157)
- The one vehicle is final (Śrīmālā); even icchantikas have buddha-nature (later Mahāparinirvāṇa)

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** tathāgatagarbha (the embryo / matrix of the Tathāgata), buddha-element (buddhadhātu) / buddha-nature (foxing), the dharmakāya, the naturally luminous mind (prakṛti-prabhāsvara-citta), suchness with stains (samalā tathatā) and without stains (nirmalā tathatā), the true self (the 'great self', mahātman) of the Mahāparinirvāṇa, the one mind (yixin), in the Awakening of Faith
**Descriptions:** all beings are tathāgatagarbhas: the buddha's knowledge pervades them, suchness is undivided, and the gotra is present (RGV 1.27-28); the dharmakāya has the perfections of purity, self, bliss and permanence (RGV 1.35; Śrīmālā ch. 12); empty of the separable adventitious stains, not empty of the inseparable unsurpassed qualities (RGV 1.154-155; Śrīmālā ch. 9); 'self' means the tathāgatagarbha; all beings have buddha-nature (Mahāparinirvāṇa, T374 juan 7); buddha-nature is the emptiness of the highest meaning, which is wisdom and the middle way (Mahāparinirvāṇa, T374 juan 27)
**Negations:** nothing is to be removed from it and nothing added (RGV 1.154); not the self of the tīrthikas, but taught to remove the fear of no-self (Laṅkāvatāra, Vaidya p. 33)
**Relation to self:** The garbha is the being's own true nature; the Mahāparinirvāṇa and Śrīmālā call it 'self' (the perfection of self), in contrast to the false self imagined in the aggregates. Other readers (Laṅkāvatāra, Madhyamaka) hold that this 'self' language is a skillful means pointing to emptiness.
**Relation to world:** Saṃsāra depends on the tathāgatagarbha (Śrīmālā ch. 13), as does nirvāṇa; the defilements are adventitious, like clouds before the sun; beings and buddhas do not differ in the dhātu, only in its being obscured or revealed.
**Caveat:** The tathāgatagarbha texts insist the garbha is not an ātman of the tīrthikas, not a creator, and not separate from emptiness; the tradition divides on whether its permanence and 'self' are definitive (Jonang shentong, many East Asian readings) or provisional (Laṅkāvatāra's own gloss; Gelug). See dsp:buddha-nature-self-or-emptiness and dsp:rangtong-shentong.
