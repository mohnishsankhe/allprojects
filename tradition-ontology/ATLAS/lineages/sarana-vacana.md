**Status:** living

The strand of the tradition that looks to Basava and the twelfth-century śaraṇas of Kalyāṇa — Allama Prabhu, Akka Mahādēvi, Cennabasava, Siddharāma and some two hundred vacanakāras of every occupation — and holds their vacanas as scripture. It is carried by the Virakta maṭhas and by the modern vacana revival.

## Distinctive positions
- The vacanas of the śaraṇas, spoken from experience (anubhava), are the tradition's scripture.
- Basava (with Allama Prabhu presiding over the Anubhava Maṇṭapa) inaugurated the tradition in Kalyāṇa.
- Caste, the five pollutions, Vedic sacrifice and temple worship are rejected; all initiates, women included, are equal.
- Work as worship (kāyaka) and sharing its fruit (dāsōha) are the social form of devotion.
- Renunciant (virakta) maṭhas carry the teaching; many of its writers regard the Pañcācārya account of origins as later.


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Kūḍalasaṅgamadēva, Guhēśvara, Cennamallikārjuna, Śūnya / Bayalu, Liṅga
**Descriptions:** the one god, lord of the meeting rivers (Basava); the beautiful one without death, decay or form (Akka Mahādēvi); the void in which liṅga and aṅga are not two (Allama)
**Negations:** not the many gods of pot and winnowing fan (Basava); bayalu — no enclosure (Allama)
**Relation to self:** The devotee is servant (dāsōhaṃ), bride and finally one with the liṅga.
**Relation to world:** The world is to be lived in through work as worship and sharing; the moving devotee is Śiva's temple.
**Caveat:** Many in this strand reject framing the vacanas' Lord in Vedāntic or Sanskrit-philosophical terms.
