The classical science of music as recorded from the Nāradīya Śikṣā, Dattila and Mataṅga to Śārṅgadeva's Saṅgītaratnākara and the later rāga treatises. Beyond its technical content (śrutis, svaras, grāmas, mūrchanās, jātis, rāgas, tālas, instruments and dance), it teaches that the whole world depends on nāda, that nāda is Brahman, and that music is a path to both enjoyment and liberation — a view carried into practice by the singer-saints.

## Distinctive positions
- Song, instrumental music and dance together are called saṅgīta; it is twofold, mārga (divine, fixed, giving merit when performed before Śiva) and deśī (regional, pleasing the heart) (ŚR 1.1.21–23).
- Song is made of nāda, instrument manifests nāda, dance follows both: everything depends on nāda; from nāda come letters, words, sentences, all dealings (ŚR 1.2.1–2; Bṛhaddeśī 16–17).
- Nāda is twofold, struck (āhata) and unstruck (anāhata), and manifests in the body (piṇḍa) (ŚR 1.2.3).
- Nāda-brahman is the consciousness of all beings unfolded as the world; by worshipping nāda one worships Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheśvara (ŚR 1.3.1–2).
- Sages worship the unstruck nāda by the guru's path, but because it lacks charm (rakti) the struck nāda is taught, which delights people and breaks worldly existence (ŚR 1.2.163–167).
- Nāda is named from na (breath) and da (fire): it arises from their union (ŚR 1.3.6; Bṛhaddeśī 22).
- Twenty-two śrutis arise from twenty-two channels attached to the upward channel at the heart; seven svaras arise from the śrutis (ŚR 1.3.8–25).
- One who knows the vīṇā, the śrutis and tāla goes the way of liberation without effort (Yājñavalkya Smṛti 3.115).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Nāda-brahman, Brahman, consciousness-bliss, called Īśvara and liṅga (ŚR 1.2.4)
**Descriptions:** the consciousness of all beings, unfolded as the world; bliss, without a second (ŚR 1.3.1); Brahman is consciousness-bliss, self-luminous, stainless, called Īśvara and liṅga, unborn, all-pervading, changeless, formless, all-powerful and omniscient (ŚR 1.2.4–5); Brahmā, Janārdana, the supreme Śakti and Maheśvara are forms of nāda (Bṛhaddeśī 17–18)
**Negations:** changeless (nirvikāra) and formless (nirākāra) (ŚR 1.2.5)
**Relation to self:** The jīvas are parts of Brahman, like sparks of fire, conditioned by beginningless avidyā and bound by karma; they are not different from the self (ŚR 1.2.5–6, 1.2.11).
**Relation to world:** Brahman creates the world by its power as gold becomes earrings — or, 'others say', through avidyā as a rope appears as a snake; Śārṅgadeva reports both views (ŚR 1.2.11–12).
**Caveat:** The music treatises borrow their metaphysics (Vedāntic and Śaiva) and record rival views of creation without deciding them; 'nāda-brahman' names the ultimate as approached through sound, not a separate doctrine of the ultimate.
