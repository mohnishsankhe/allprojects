A classificatory grouping (not a tradition) for regional devotional poets in U26's scope — Narsinh Mehta (Gujarati), Annamācārya, Tyāgarāja and Bhadrācala Rāmadās (Telugu), Caṇḍīdās (Bengali) and Vidyāpati (Maithili) — each also linked to the traditions that claim them.

## Distinctive positions
- Not a sampradāya: a grouping made by this ontology for vernacular devotional poets who founded no order and are claimed by several traditions.
- Common ground: the name, the saints' company, love and longing for a personal God (Kṛṣṇa, Rāma, Veṅkaṭeśvara), often joined to a non-dual vision ('you alone appear in all forms').


## The ultimate in this lineage

**Names:** Śrī Hari (Narsinh), Veṅkaṭeśvara / Brahman (Annamācārya), Rāma (Tyāgarāja, Bhadrācala Rāmadās), Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa (Vidyāpati, Caṇḍīdās)
**Descriptions:** you alone, Śrī Hari, appear in endless forms (Narsinh); Brahman is one for king and servant (Annamācārya)
**Relation to self:** Varies by poet; Narsinh: until the Self is known all practice is false.
**Relation to world:** Varies by poet.
**Caveat:** Not a tradition: a classificatory grouping; each poet's view should be read with the tradition that claims him.
