**Regions:** Japan
**Dates:** Tradition's account: from Eisai's Huanglong-line transmission (1191); Scholarly account: Kamakura and Muromachi Five Mountains (gozan) and the rinka lines; all present-day Rinzai lines descend through Hakuin (18th c.) from the Ōtōkan line (Nanpo–Daitō–Kanzan); (confidence high)
**Status:** living

The Japanese form of the Linji house: introduced by Eisai and Chinese émigré masters in the Kamakura period, patronised through the Five Mountains, revived in the 18th century by Hakuin, whose kōan system all living Rinzai lines follow.

## Distinctive positions
- Kōan introspection under a teacher in private interview (sanzen/dokusan) as the path to kenshō.
- Great doubt (daigi), great faith and great determination as the three essentials of practice.
- Post-kenshō training (gogo no shugyō) through a graded kōan curriculum systematised in Hakuin's line.
- Hakuin's teaching that seeing the nature must issue in the 'four wisdoms' and compassionate activity.

**Transmissions received:** 
  - [Linji house](linji.md) — what: Linji house lineages (Huanglong via Eisai; Yangqi via Enni, Lanxi, Wuxue, Nanpo's master Xutang); evidence: tradition records

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** one's own nature (jishō 自性), original face, the true person of no rank
**Descriptions:** Seen directly in kenshō when the great doubt shatters (Hakuin).; 'Beings are originally buddhas, like water and ice' (Zazen wasan).
**Negations:** No-thought; beyond being and non-being (Wumenguan case 1 commentary).
**Relation to self:** The seeing nature is one's own; 'this very place is the Lotus Land, this very body the Buddha' (Zazen wasan closing).
**Relation to world:** The world seen after kenshō is the Pure Land here; compassion flows as the 'four wisdoms'.
**Caveat:** Rinzai insists the nature must be seen, not merely believed; it criticises the view that sitting in quiet is itself awakening ('dead sitting').
