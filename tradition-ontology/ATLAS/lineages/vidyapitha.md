# Vidyāpīṭha (the Seat of Vidyās)

`lin:vidyapitha` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** vidyāpīṭha, Yāmala and Śakti tantras
**Parent:** [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](mantramarga.md)
**Sub-lineages:** [Trika ('the Triad')](trika.md), [Kālīkula (the Kālī lineages)](kalikula.md)
**Key teachers:** [Abhinavagupta](../teachers/abhinavagupta.md), [Jayaratha](../teachers/jayaratha.md)
**Regions:** Kashmir, Nepal, North India
**Dates:** Scholarly account: Brahmayāmala/Picumata perhaps 7th–8th c.; Nepalese manuscript dated 1052 CE; Jayadrathayāmala c. 9th–10th c. (Kashmir); (confidence low)
**Status:** absorbed

Division of the Bhairava tantras centred on goddesses, yoginīs and feminine mantras. Its Yāmala tantras and Śakti tantras are the roots of the Trika and of the Kālīkula/Krama, and of much Kaula practice.

## Distinctive positions
- The deity of the Vidyāpīṭha is Bhairava with, and increasingly subordinate to, goddesses and yoginīs; mantras are feminine (vidyā).
- Its scriptures divide into Yāmala tantras (e.g. the Brahmayāmala/Picumata; the Jayadrathayāmala) and Śakti tantras (e.g. Siddhayogeśvarīmata, Tantrasadbhāva, Mālinīvijayottara).
- Its sādhaka follows Kāpālika-style observances in cremation grounds (summary only; see prc:vidyapitha-observance).

**Transmissions given:** 
  - [Kaula (the Kula tradition)](kaula.md) — what: yoginī cult and kula ritual reformulated in the Kaula traditions
  - [Trika ('the Triad')](trika.md) — what: the Śakti-tantras (Siddhayogeśvarīmata, Mālinīvijayottara) as scriptural base

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Bhairava with the goddess(es), Kapālīśabhairava and Caṇḍā Kāpālinī (Brahmayāmala), Kālasaṅkarṣiṇī (Jayadrathayāmala), Parā, Parāparā, Aparā (Trika Śakti tantras)
**Descriptions:** The supreme is Bhairava in union with his power(s); in later Vidyāpīṭha texts the goddess becomes primary.
**Caveat:** Catalogue-level summary; the doctrinal views of the Trika and Kālīkula are recorded by U19/U24.
