**Dates:** Scholarly account: the word bāula (a madman of God) appears in the Caitanya-caritāmṛta (Antya 19, Advaita's riddle); Bāuls as a distinct community are documented from the 18th–19th c.; Lalon Fakir d. 1890; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The Bāuls are wandering and householder singer-practitioners of Bengal, both Hindu (Vaiṣṇava-Sahajiyā-derived) and Muslim (Fakir), who seek the Man of the Heart within the human body under a living guru, reject caste, scripture and sectarian worship, and teach through songs; Lalon Fakir (d. 1890) is their most celebrated master.

## Distinctive positions
- The Man of the Heart (maner mānuṣ), the Unknown Bird (achin pākhi), the Uncatchable Man (adhar mānuṣ) dwells in this body; he is to be sought within, not in temple, mosque or scripture.
- Body-theology (deha-tattva): 'what is in the vessel (the body) is in the universe' (je āche bhāṇḍe tā āche brahmāṇḍe).
- What is present and verifiable in the body (bartamān) is trusted over inference, hearsay and scripture (anumān).
- Caste and religious labels are rejected: 'everyone asks what caste Lalon is; I have never seen the form of caste'.
- The guru (murśid) is the living human through whom the Man is found; worship of the human (mānuṣ bhajan).
- The song (gān) is teaching, practice and code: sung with the ektārā and dotārā at ākhṛās and melās.
- A secret practice of the couple and of the bodily substances, reversing the downward flow (ulṭā / ujān sādhanā) — recorded here only in summary (restricted).

**Transmissions received:**

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** the Man of the Heart (maner mānuṣ), the unknown bird (acin pākhi), the uncatchable Man (adhar mānuṣ), Sāṃi
**Descriptions:** dwells in this human body; near yet a hundred thousand leagues away; seen through the guru; worship of the human makes one golden
**Negations:** not in temple or mosque; not in scripture (anumān); without caste
**Relation to self:** The Man lives in one's own body, neighbour in the mirror-city, unseen until the guru reveals him.
**Relation to world:** What is in the body is in the universe; the world outside is known through the body.
**Caveat:** The Bāuls refuse the scriptural names and doctrinal frames (Vedāntic, Vaiṣṇava or Islamic) in which the 'one truth' might be stated; the ultimate is the living human, verifiable in the body.
