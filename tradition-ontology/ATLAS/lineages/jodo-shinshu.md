**Status:** living

The school descended from Shinran (1173–1263), Hōnen's disciple, who after exile (1207) lived as 'neither monk nor layman' among ordinary people in Echigo and the Kantō and in the Kyōgyōshinshō taught that teaching, practice, entrusting (shinjin) and realization are all Amida's transfer of merit to beings. Organised around Shinran's mausoleum (Honganji) by Kakunyo and spread by Rennyo's letters in the 15th c., it is today divided mainly into the Honganji (Nishi), Ōtani (Higashi) and Takada branches among the 'ten schools of Shinshū'.

## Distinctive positions
- Shinjin — the entrusting mind — is the true cause of birth; it is not produced by the practitioner but given (transferred) by Amida; the nenbutsu that follows is the expression of gratitude.
- Both going to the Pure Land (ōsō) and returning to this world to save others (gensō) are Amida's transfer of merit.
- At the moment shinjin is settled, one enters the rightly established state (shōjōju) in this life; birth in the true Pure Land at death is at once attaining great nirvāṇa; no deathbed welcome need be awaited.
- The evil person is the true object of the vow: 'even a good person attains birth, how much more an evil person' (Tannishō 3).
- Other practices and self-power nenbutsu lead only to the transformed land (19th and 20th vows); Shinran turned through the three vows to the eighteenth.
- Jinen hōni: things become so of themselves by the working of the vow, free of the practitioner's calculation (hakarai).
- Neither monk nor layman (hisō hizoku): married clergy, no precepts as condition of birth, no worship of other deities for worldly benefit, the name (myōgō) as object of reverence.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Amida Nyorai; the Tathāgata of Unhindered Light filling the ten directions (Jin jippō mugekō nyorai), the name (myōgō) Namu Amida Butsu, dharma-body as suchness (hosshō hosshin), formless, jinen (naturalness)
**Descriptions:** The supreme Buddha is formless; being formless it is called jinen; Amida is the means by which we are made to know jinen (the jinen hōni letter).; The true Buddha is the Tathāgata of inconceivable light; the true land is the land of immeasurable light (Kyōgyōshinshō, chapter on the true Buddha and land).; Shinjin is buddha-nature; buddha-nature is the Tathāgata (Jōdo wasan; Kyōgyōshinshō, Shin chapter).
**Negations:** Birth is not attained by the practitioner's calculation (hakarai); 'no working is true working'.
**Relation to self:** The self is a foolish being full of blind passions for whom no practice is possible; shinjin, given by Amida, is itself the Buddha's mind in the person, who becomes 'equal to the Tathāgatas' while remaining a foolish being.
**Relation to world:** The world is a burning house where 'all is empty and false; only the nenbutsu is true' (Tannishō postscript); one returns to it from the Pure Land to save others (gensō).
**Caveat:** Shinshū distinguishes sharply between the Buddha's mind and the foolish being's mind, and between the true and transformed lands; it would reject any reading that makes the practitioner already a buddha by his own realization or that treats Amida as one deity among many worthy of worship.
