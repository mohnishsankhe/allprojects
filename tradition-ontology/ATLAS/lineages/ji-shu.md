# Ji-shū (the Time school of Ippen)

The itinerant school of Ippen (1239–1289), who after the Kumano oracle of 1274 wandered through Japan distributing talismans inscribed 'Namu Amida Butsu — birth assured — six hundred thousand people' and leading dancing nenbutsu; he burned his writings at death. His successor Shinkyō organised the Ji-shū order, whose head bears the title Yugyō Shōnin.

## Distinctive positions
- The birth of all beings was settled when Amida attained awakening ten kalpas ago; the name 'Namu Amida Butsu' itself is birth; the practitioner neither causes nor secures it.
- Talismans (fusan) printed with the name are given to all, without choosing between those with and without faith, the pure and the impure (the Kumano oracle).
- Casting off (sutete koso) everything — home, learning, even the thought of self and of faith — in the nenbutsu; a life of wandering (yugyō).
- Dancing nenbutsu (odori nenbutsu) as joyful practice; recitation at the six times of day (whence 'Ji').

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** the name Namu Amida Butsu (as itself birth)
**Descriptions:** The birth of all beings was settled as 'Namu Amida Butsu' at Amida's perfect awakening ten kalpas ago (Kumano oracle, as reported in the Ippen Hijiri-e).; In the nenbutsu the self, the Buddha and the name are not separate; the name recites the name (reported sayings).
**Relation to self:** The self is to be cast off; not even faith is to be relied on as one's own.
**Relation to world:** Everything — mountains, rivers, grasses, the sound of wind and waves — is the nenbutsu (reported sayings, Goroku).
**Caveat:** Ippen's sayings survive mainly in later compilations; the view is reconstructed from them (low confidence on wording).
