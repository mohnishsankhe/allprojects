The Middle Way school founded by Nāgārjuna (c. 2nd c. CE) on the Perfection of Wisdom sūtras. It teaches that all things, including the Buddha, nirvāṇa and emptiness itself, lack own-nature because they arise dependently, and that this emptiness, rightly understood through the two truths, is the middle way between eternalism and annihilationism. Its Indian lines — the commentators on Nāgārjuna and Āryadeva (Buddhapālita, Bhāviveka, Candrakīrti), the bodhisattva manuals (Śāntideva) and the synthesis with Yogācāra and Buddhist logic (Śāntarakṣita, Kamalaśīla) — were classified by later Tibetan doxographers as Prāsaṅgika and Svātantrika; the school's view became the philosophical centre of Tibetan Buddhism and, as Sanlun, of East Asian Madhyamaka.

## Distinctive positions
- Every dharma is empty (śūnya) of own-nature (svabhāva) because it arises in dependence; emptiness = dependent origination = dependent designation = the middle way (MMK 24.18).
- Things do not arise from themselves, from another, from both, or without cause (MMK 1.1).
- The Buddhas teach by relying on two truths, the worldly-conventional and the ultimate; without relying on the conventional the ultimate cannot be taught, and without reaching the ultimate nirvāṇa is not attained (MMK 24.8–10).
- Emptiness is not a view: it is the relinquishing of all views, and those who make emptiness a view are called incurable (MMK 13.8).
- The Mādhyamika has no thesis of his own to defend (VV 29) and refutes the opponent's positions on their own terms.
- There is not the slightest difference between saṃsāra and nirvāṇa (MMK 25.19–20); nirvāṇa is neither existent nor nonexistent (25.4–10).
- Emptiness is not nihilism: for one for whom emptiness is possible, everything — the four truths, karma, the path — is possible (MMK 24.14; VV 70).
- The self is neither the same as nor different from the aggregates; it is a designation dependent on them (MMK 18.1; 27; MA 6).
- The bodhisattva path unites wisdom (emptiness) and method (compassion, bodhicitta, the perfections) (RĀ; BCA; BhK).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** śūnyatā (emptiness), paramārtha-satya (the ultimate truth), tattva (reality, 'thatness'), dharmatā (the nature of things), tathatā (suchness), nirvāṇa, pratītyasamutpāda (dependent origination) as prapañcopaśama
**Descriptions:** not dependent on another, peaceful, not elaborated by elaborations, free of conceptual construction, without multiplicity — the mark of reality (MMK 18.9); dependent origination, qualified by the eight negations, the pacification of elaborations, auspicious (MMK dedicatory verses); the pacification of all apprehending, the pacification of elaboration, auspicious (MMK 25.24); the emptiness which is dependent origination, which is a dependent designation and the middle way (MMK 24.18)
**Negations:** no cessation, no arising, no annihilation, no permanence, no unity, no plurality, no coming, no going (dedication); not arisen from self, other, both or no cause (MMK 1.1); nirvāṇa is neither an existent nor a non-existent (MMK 25.4–10); 'empty' should not be said, nor 'not empty', nor both, nor neither — they are said only for designation (MMK 22.11); emptiness is itself empty; it is not a view (MMK 13.8; 24.18)
**Relation to self:** The self is not identical with the aggregates nor different from them (MMK 18.1; 27.4–8); it is a designation made in dependence on them, like a chariot on its parts (MA 6.150ff). Knowing reality is the ceasing of 'I' and 'mine' (MMK 18.2–4).
**Relation to world:** Emptiness is not another thing behind the world: it is the world's being dependently arisen (MMK 24.18–19). The limit of nirvāṇa is the limit of saṃsāra (MMK 25.19–20). The world is accepted as conventional truth, and the ultimate is taught only by relying on it (MMK 24.10).
**Caveat:** Madhyamaka rejects any reading of emptiness as a positive ultimate entity, ground, substance or absolute — it is the mere absence of own-nature in each thing and is itself empty; to take it as a view or a thing is called incurable (MMK 13.8). Calling emptiness 'the one truth named variously' would be admitted only as a conventional designation, not as naming a real referent. The school also rejects identification with the Upaniṣadic brahman or ātman (Madhyamakahṛdaya ch.8; Tattvasaṅgraha).
