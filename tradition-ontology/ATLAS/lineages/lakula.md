The Lākulas form the second division of the Atimārga. They are known almost only through the Mantramārga scriptures that describe them (the Niśvāsamukha, the Svacchanda Tantra) and through the Kālāmukhas of Karnataka inscriptions, whom scholarship identifies with them. Their observance was the great vow of the skull, 'beyond the world'. No Lākula scripture survives complete.

## Distinctive positions
- Its observance is the great vow (mahāvrata), also called the skull observance (kapālavrata) or observance 'beyond the world' (lokātīta): the ascetic carries a human skull as his bowl and a skull-topped staff (khaṭvāṅga) and smears himself with ash (as described in Śaiva scriptures; details reconstructed).
- The Svacchanda Tantra names the skull-observers (kapālavratin) beside the Pāśupatas as those who stand 'in the Īśvara and in the Dhruva' and are not re-created (11.184).
- It is described by scholarship as a system intermediate between Pāñcārthika Pāśupatism and the Mantramārga, with its own hierarchy of worlds; its scriptures are lost.
