The 'Pacification' tradition brought to Tibet by the South Indian siddha Padampa Sangye (d. c. 1105/1117), who taught at Dingri. Grounded in the Perfection of Wisdom — whose mantra 'pacifies all suffering' — it combined sūtra and tantra instructions and was passed down in early, middle and later transmissions, the last through Dampa Kunga. Padampa's Hundred Verses to the people of Dingri remain widely read. As an independent line it faded; its instructions survive in other schools and in Kongtrul's Treasury of Instructions, where it is paired with Chöd.

## Distinctive positions
- Suffering is pacified by the realization of the emptiness taught in the Perfection of Wisdom; the name comes from the Heart Sūtra's description of the mantra of wisdom as 'pacifying all suffering'.
- Sūtra and mantra instructions are combined and given according to the disciple's capacity.
- Renunciation of worldly concerns, solitary practice and devotion to the guru are urged in the plain verses of the Dingri Hundred.
- The teaching was given in three transmissions (early, middle, later) on Padampa's successive visits.

**Transmissions received:** 
  - [The Mahāsiddhas (the eighty-four great adepts)](mahasiddha.md) — what: the siddha instructions Padampa is said to have received from many Indian masters; evidence: tradition-account
  - [Mahāyāna](mahayana.md) — what: the Perfection of Wisdom, above all the Heart Sūtra's 'mantra that pacifies all suffering', from which the name is taken; evidence: explicit-citation
**Transmissions given:** 
  - [Chöd (gcod), 'Cutting'](chod.md) — what: the father lineage of Chöd and Padampa's instructions to Machig Labdrön
  - [Rimé (ris med), the non-sectarian movement](rime.md) — what: counted with Chöd as one of the eight great practice lineages; its instructions gathered in the Treasury of Instructions

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** the perfection of wisdom (prajñāpāramitā), the nature of mind, empty and luminous
**Descriptions:** the emptiness whose realization pacifies all suffering; the mind as empty and clear, not to be sought elsewhere
**Negations:** suffering has no ground of its own once its nature is seen
**Relation to self:** The self is not found; recognizing the mind's nature ends suffering.
**Relation to world:** The world is impermanent and like a dream; attachment to it is to be abandoned.
**Caveat:** Shije is a practice lineage; it does not set out a distinct doctrine of the ultimate beyond the Perfection of Wisdom, and the details of its view survive only in later collections (low confidence).
