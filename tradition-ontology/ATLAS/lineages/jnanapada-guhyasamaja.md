The second Indian exegetical tradition of the Guhyasamāja, founded by Buddhajñānapāda (late 8th c.), a teacher of Vikramaśīla's early masters, centred on Mañjuvajra and the 'Oral Instruction' on the two stages; continued by Dīpaṅkarabhadra's maṇḍala ritual (Guhyasamājamaṇḍalavidhi).

## Distinctive positions
- The Guhyasamāja is practised with Mañjuvajra (a form of Mañjuśrī) at the centre of a 19-deity maṇḍala.
- The 'two stages' are presented in Buddhajñānapāda's 'Oral Instruction' (Dvikramatattvabhāvanā-mukhāgama), given, by the tradition's account, by Mañjuśrī himself.

**Relation to self:** The practitioner generates himself as Mañjuvajra; the deity is the purified nature.
