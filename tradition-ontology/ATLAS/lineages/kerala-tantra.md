# Kerala tantra (temple-tantra tradition of Kerala)

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Kerala Tāntrika tradition, the tantri tradition of Kerala, Tantrasamuccaya tradition
**Key teachers:** [Cēnnās Nārāyaṇan Nampūtirippāṭ](../teachers/cennas-narayanan.md), [Īśānaśiva Gurudeva](../teachers/isanasiva-gurudeva.md)
**Regions:** Kerala
**Dates:** Scholarly account: the Tantrasamuccaya is a 15th-c. work; the Īśānaśivagurudevapaddhati is older (c. 11th–12th c.); the Prapañcasāra and Śāradātilaka are earlier pan-Indian mantra manuals used in Kerala; (confidence low)
**Status:** living

Kerala's temple-tantra tradition is the body of ritual texts and hereditary tantri lineages that govern temple building, installation, daily worship, festivals and purification in Kerala's Hindu temples. Its central text is Cēnnās Nārāyaṇan's Tantrasamuccaya, supported by the Īśānaśivagurudevapaddhati, the Prapañcasāra, the Śāradātilaka and Malayalam manuals.

## Distinctive positions
- Temple worship is conducted according to tantra texts, above all the Tantrasamuccaya, by hereditary Nampūtiri brahmin tantri families who alone perform installations (pratiṣṭhā), festivals (utsava) and the major purifications.
- The Tantrasamuccaya treats the installation and worship of seven deities: Viṣṇu, Śiva, Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa, Durgā, Subrahmaṇya, Gaṇapati and Śāstā.
- Daily worship is performed by resident priests (śānti) under the tantri's authority; the day is structured by a series of pūjās and processional offerings (śīveli).
- Kerala tantra combines Vedic (smārta) Nampūtiri practice with Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava and Śākta tantric procedures.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Parameśvara (sakala), the seven installed deities as forms of the supreme
**Descriptions:** From the supreme Lord endowed with existence, consciousness and bliss, and with parts (sakala), arose Śakti; from her Nāda; from Nāda, Bindu (Śāradātilaka 1.7).
**Caveat:** Kerala tantra is a ritual tradition serving several deities; it has no single systematic theology. The Prapañcasāra (attributed to Śaṅkara) frames mantra practice non-dualistically, while the Īśānaśivagurudevapaddhati follows Śaiva Siddhānta.
