The line of post-Śaṅkara Advaita founded on Padmapāda's Pañcapādikā and Prakāśātman's Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa; it locates ignorance in Brahman, treats avidyā as a positive indescribable power, explains the jīva as a reflection, and makes hearing of the Upaniṣadic sentence the direct means of liberating knowledge. Madhusūdana Sarasvatī's Advaitasiddhi largely follows it.

## Distinctive positions
- Ignorance (avidyā) has pure consciousness (Brahman) as both its locus (āśraya) and its object (viṣaya).
- Avidyā is a positive entity (bhāva-rūpa), though indescribable, known through perception ('I am ignorant'), inference and presumption.
- Reflection theory (pratibimba-vāda): the jīva is the reflection of Brahman in avidyā/the inner organ; the reflection is real and identical with its prototype; Īśvara is the prototype (bimba) consciousness.
- Hearing (śravaṇa) of the Upaniṣads is the principal means (aṅgin), enjoined by a restrictive injunction (niyama-vidhi); reflection and contemplation are its auxiliaries.
- The mahāvākya itself generates immediate (aparokṣa) knowledge of Brahman.
- Vedic works, through purification, lead to knowledge itself (vedana), not merely to the desire to know.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Brahman, Bimba-caitanya (prototype consciousness, as Īśvara)
**Descriptions:** pure consciousness, the locus and object of the one beginningless ignorance; the prototype (bimba) of which the jīva is the reflection
**Negations:** as in Advaita generally
**Relation to self:** The jīva is Brahman's reflection (pratibimba) in avidyā/the inner organ; the reflection is not unreal but identical with the prototype, as a face in a mirror is the face itself.
**Relation to world:** The world is the transformation (pariṇāma) of avidyā and the apparent transformation (vivarta) of Brahman, which is the locus of avidyā.
**Caveat:** Shares Advaita's ultimate; differs from the Bhāmatī line only in the explanatory theory of how the one appears as many. Partisans of the Vivaraṇa hold their theory to be the correct one, not merely a method.
