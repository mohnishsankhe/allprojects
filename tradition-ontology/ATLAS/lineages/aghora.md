Aghora is the lineage of Śaiva ascetics centred on Bābā Kīnārām's seat at Krīm Kuṇḍ in Varanasi, who trace their teaching to Dattātreya through Bābā Kālūrām. Its ascetics practise in the cremation ground and cultivate non-discrimination (abheda), overcoming disgust, fear and the other fetters. Kīnārām's Hindi works (Vivekasār, Rāmgītā, Rāmrasāl, Gītāvalī) join this with devotion to Rām as the attributeless; in the twentieth century Aghoreśvar Bhagavān Rām founded the Śrī Sarveśvarī Samūh and turned the lineage toward service. Practice details are secret and only summarised here.

## Distinctive positions
- Non-discrimination (abheda): nothing is to be shunned as impure; the aghora (the 'not-terrible') Śiva is present in what is feared and loathed.
- Freedom from the eight fetters — disgust, doubt, fear, shame, loathing, family, conduct and caste (the Kaula list, Kulārṇava 13.90) — is the mark of the free one.
- The cremation ground (śmaśāna) is the place of practice; the skull bowl (khappar) is carried.
- The guru is Śiva; the lineage descends from Dattātreya through Kālūrām and Kīnārām.
- The ultimate is named also as Rām / nirguṇa (Kīnārām's works); the state sought is sahaja and unmanī.
- Recent teachers (Aghoreśvar Bhagavān Rām) direct Aghora toward service of the sick and outcaste and rejection of caste distinctions.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Aghora (Śiva, the not-terrible), Rām (the attributeless, in Kīnārām's works)
**Descriptions:** Śiva present equally in what is pure and impure, feared and loved; the state beyond the eight fetters, in which the bound paśu becomes Maheśvara (Kulārṇava 13.91)
**Relation to self:** The one freed from the fetters is himself Maheśvara; the guru is Śiva.
**Relation to world:** The world, including the cremation ground and what is loathed, is not other than Śiva.
**Caveat:** Aghora's own texts (Kīnārām's Vivekasār etc.) are not yet extracted; this view rests on the shared Kaula doctrine of the fetters and on the lineage's self-description, and should be revised from its own writings.
