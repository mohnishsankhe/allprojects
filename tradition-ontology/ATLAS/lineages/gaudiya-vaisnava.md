**Regions:** Bengal (Navadvīpa, Śāntipur, Khaḍadaha, Kheturi), Odisha (Purī), Braj (Vṛndāvana, Rādhākuṇḍa), Manipur, Assam, worldwide (ISKCON and Gauḍīya Maṭha, recent)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Caitanya appeared at Navadvīpa on Phālguna Pūrṇimā, Śaka 1407 (1486 CE), as Kṛṣṇa himself in the mood of Rādhā; Scholarly account: founded early 16th c. CE; theology systematised by the Vṛndāvana Gosvāmīs c. 1530–1600; (confidence high)
**Status:** living

The devotional tradition that grew from Caitanya (1486–1533) of Navadvīpa and Purī, his associates Nityānanda and Advaita, and the Six Gosvāmīs of Vṛndāvana, who gave it a theology (Jīva's Ṣaṭ Sandarbha: acintya-bhedābheda), an aesthetics of devotion (Rūpa's Bhaktirasāmṛtasindhu and Ujjvalanīlamaṇi), a ritual code (Haribhaktivilāsa) and a hagiography (Caitanya Caritāmṛta, Caitanya Bhāgavata). Its practice centres on chanting the divine name, deity service, hearing the Bhāgavata, residence in Vraja and meditative participation in Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa's eternal play.

## Distinctive positions
- Inconceivable difference-and-non-difference (acintya-bhedābheda) between the Lord and his powers (śakti), souls and world.
- Kṛṣṇa is the Lord himself (svayaṃ bhagavān, BhP 1.3.28); the one non-dual reality is called Brahman, Paramātman and Bhagavān (BhP 1.2.11), Bhagavān being fullest.
- Three powers: internal (svarūpa/cit: sandhinī, saṃvit, hlādinī), marginal (taṭasthā: the souls) and external (māyā).
- Rādhā is Kṛṣṇa's hlādinī-śakti and the peak of love (mahābhāva); Caitanya is Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in one form.
- Pure devotion (uttamā bhakti) is the means and prema is the supreme goal (the 'fifth aim' beyond mokṣa).
- Devotion is a rasa: five primary (śānta, dāsya, sakhya, vātsalya, mādhurya) and seven secondary rasas.
- Practice (sādhana-bhakti) is rule-based (vaidhī) or spontaneous, following the Vraja residents (rāgānugā); stages from faith to love (BRS 1.4.15–16).
- Congregational chanting of the name (nāma-saṅkīrtana), above all the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahāmantra, is the dharma of the Kali age; the name is non-different from Kṛṣṇa; ten offences against the name are to be avoided.
- Rūpa and Viśvanātha hold paramour love (parakīyā) of the gopīs to be the highest; Jīva holds it to be svakīyā in truth — a recorded internal debate.
- Caste and birth do not bar one from being a teacher of Kṛṣṇa (CC 2.8.128).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Kṛṣṇa, svayaṃ bhagavān, Bhagavān, Paramātman, Brahman, Govinda, Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa, Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya (Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa combined), advaya-jñāna (non-dual knowledge)
**Descriptions:** The non-dual reality is called Brahman, Paramātman and Bhagavān (BhP 1.2.11); Bhagavān is its full manifestation.; Kṛṣṇa is the supreme Lord, whose form is being, consciousness and bliss, beginningless, the origin and cause of all causes (Brahma Saṃhitā 5.1).; The possessor of inconceivable powers (acintya-śakti), related to them in inconceivable difference-and-non-difference.; Rasa itself; most fully manifest in Vraja with Rādhā, his hlādinī-śakti.
**Negations:** The impersonal Brahman is the effulgence of his body (Brahma Saṃhitā 5.40), an incomplete manifestation, not the highest.; Denies that his form is a product of māyā or that the world is vivarta.
**Relation to self:** The soul is the Lord's marginal power (taṭasthā-śakti), an eternal servant, one with and different from him inconceivably (CC 2.20.108).
**Relation to world:** The world is a real but temporary transformation of his external power (māyā-śakti); he himself remains unchanged, like the wish-fulfilling gem.
**Caveat:** Gauḍīyas accept the three names of BhP 1.2.11 but grade them (Bhagavān fullest); they reject any reading that makes the personal form a provisional appearance of an impersonal Absolute.
