**Dates:** Tradition's account: saints of all ages; Māṇikkavācakar placed before the Tēvāram poets by much of the tradition; Scholarly account: Kāraikkāl Ammaiyār c. 6th c.; Appar and Campantar 7th c.; Cuntarar 8th–9th c.; Māṇikkavācakar c. 9th c.; canon compiled c. 10th–12th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The Tamil Śaiva devotional movement of the sixty-three saints (Nāyaṉmārs) and nine groups of devotees celebrated in Cuntarar's Tiruttoṇṭattokai and Cēkkiḻār's Periya Purāṇam, whose hymns — the Tēvāram of Appar, Campantar and Cuntarar, Māṇikkavācakar's Tiruvācakam, Tirumūlar's Tirumantiram and others — form the twelve Tirumuṟai, the Tamil Śaiva canon still sung in temples by ōtuvārs.

## Distinctive positions
- Love (aṉpu) for Śiva, expressed as service (toṇṭu) to him and to his devotees, outweighs birth, caste, learning and rite.
- Śiva is present in named temples of the Tamil land; pilgrimage and song at each shrine are worship.
- The four samayācāryas exemplify four relationships: servant (Appar), son (Campantar), friend (Cuntarar), and the true path of knowledge (Māṇikkavācakar).
- Śiva's grace saves from all danger; the five syllables (namaccivāya) are the devotee's support.
- Polemic against the Jains and Buddhists of the Tamil country (the tenth verse of Campantar's hymns).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Civaṉ / Civam, Ammaiyappaṉ (Mother-Father, Śiva with Umā), Pittaṉ ('the Madman'), the Lord of each shrine (Tillaiyampalavāṇaṉ, Ārūraṉ …)
**Descriptions:** love itself (Tirumantiram 270); the one whom the Vedas call 'not this, not this', who came as guru and took the devotee as his own; the dancer in the cremation ground and in the golden hall; the thief who stole the heart
**Negations:** beyond the reach of Brahmā and Viṣṇu, who could not find his crown and feet
**Relation to self:** The devotee is his servant, son or friend; grace melts the heart; love of him is himself.
**Relation to world:** He dwells in the named temples of the Tamil land; all the universes are motes before him.
**Caveat:** Devotional, not scholastic; the hymns praise Śiva as supreme and reject Jain and Buddhist teaching.
