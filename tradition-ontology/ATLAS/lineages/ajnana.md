# Ajñāna (the sceptics)

`lin:ajnana` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Family:** ascetic
**Alternate names:** Ajñānavāda, Ajñānika, Annāṇiya (Prakrit), Amarāvikkhepika — 'eel-wrigglers' (Pali)
**Parent:** [The śramaṇa movement](sramana.md)
**Key teachers:** [Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta](../teachers/sanjaya-belatthiputta.md)
**Regions:** Rājagaha (Sañjaya's community)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 5th c. BCE (Sañjaya, contemporary of the Buddha); the Jain catalogue of 67 ajñānika views is given in the canonical commentaries (Niryukti, Śīlāṅka, 9th c.).; (confidence low)
**Status:** extinct

The Ajñānikas refused to affirm or deny any proposition about what lies beyond experience. The Pali texts call them 'eel-wrigglers' (amarāvikkhepika) and give Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta's formula as the type; the Jain canon counts ajñāna as one of the four 'assemblies' (samavasaraṇa) of rival doctrines, with 67 sub-kinds. No text of the school survives; all is reported by opponents.

## Distinctive positions
- (As reported) On whether there is another world, spontaneously born beings, fruit of deeds, or the Tathāgata after death, in each of the four forms, Sañjaya refuses every alternative: 'I do not say it is so, nor thus, nor otherwise, nor not so, nor not not so' (DN 2).
- (As reported) Four grounds of such evasion: fear of false speech, fear of grasping, fear of cross-examination, and dullness (DN 1).
- (Jain report) Ignorance is better (ajñānam eva śreyaḥ): 'who knows whether the soul exists, and what use is knowing it?'; 67 varieties (Śīlāṅka).

**Transmissions given:**

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** none affirmed (vikkhepa — the refusal)
**Descriptions:** (As reported) To every question about the other world, spontaneously born beings, fruit of deeds and the Tathāgata after death, in all four forms, the answer is refused (DN 2).
**Negations:** 'I do not say it is so, nor thus, nor otherwise, nor not so, nor not not so.'
**Relation to self:** Not asserted: (Jain report) 'who knows whether the soul exists, and what use is knowing it?'
**Relation to world:** Not asserted.
**Caveat:** The Ajñānikas refuse to affirm any ultimate; to read their refusal as pointing to an ineffable one truth would be an interpretation they are reported to reject as another assertion. Both Buddhist and Jain reporters insist their own undeclared points and 'inexpressible' (avaktavya) differ from this refusal.
