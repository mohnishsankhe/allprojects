**Status:** living

The movement begun by Lekhraj Kripalani (Brahmā Bābā, 1876–1969) in Sindh in the 1930s, teaching the soul as a point of light, a 5,000-year cycle of ages, and open-eyed Rāja Yoga meditation, led largely by women.

## Distinctive positions
- The soul is an eternal point of light seated in the forehead; the Supreme Soul Śiva is also an incorporeal point of light, not omnipresent.
- A 5,000-year world cycle (Golden, Silver, Copper, Iron ages and the present Confluence Age) repeats identically.
- Rāja Yoga as remembrance (yād) of the Supreme Soul, practised with open eyes; celibacy and pure vegetarian food.
- Knowledge given daily in the murlī, spoken by Śiva through Brahmā (tradition's account).
- Leadership mainly by women (the Dadis).


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** Śiva (Shiv Baba), the Supreme Soul
**Descriptions:** The Supreme Soul is an incorporeal point of light, the Ocean of knowledge, peace and love; he is not omnipresent.
**Relation to self:** Souls are eternal points of light, distinct from the Supreme Soul; they are his children.
**Relation to world:** The world drama is real and repeats every 5,000 years.
**Caveat:** Explicitly denies God's omnipresence and the identity of soul and God; rejects the Advaita reading.
