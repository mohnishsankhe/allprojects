**Status:** extinct

The Sautrāntikas were a critical current within the northern Sarvāstivāda ordination tradition that accepted only the sūtras as authoritative and reduced many of the Vaibhāṣika's real entities to designations. Their positions are known mainly through Vasubandhu's Abhidharmakośabhāṣya, Yaśomitra's commentary and Saṅghabhadra's refutations; they shaped Yogācāra (seeds) and the Buddhist logicians (momentariness, representationalism).

## Distinctive positions
- Only the sūtras are authoritative; the Abhidharma treatises are works of disciples, not the word of the Buddha (Yaśomitra: 'ye sūtraprāmāṇikā na śāstraprāmāṇikāḥ te sautrāntikāḥ').
- Only the present exists; past and future dharmas do not exist as entities: a conditioned thing 'arises having not been, and having been, ceases' (AKBh 5.27).
- Everything conditioned is momentary; destruction has no cause (AKBh 4.2–3).
- Karmic efficacy and latent defilements are carried as seeds (bīja) — capacities in the psycho-physical series that ripen through its specific transformation (santati-pariṇāma-viśeṣa) (AKBh 2.36, 5.2, ch. 9).
- Possession (prāpti), the characteristics of the conditioned, avijñapti-rūpa and the unconditioned dharmas are designations, not real entities (AKBh 2.36, 2.46, 2.55, 4.3).
- Cognition bears the form (ākāra) of its object; external objects are inferred, not directly grasped (later doxographic summary).
- An arhat does not fall back from arhatship (AKBh 6.58).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** nirvāṇa
**Descriptions:** Nirvāṇa is a mere absence: the non-arising of further defilements and births once the antidote has arisen (AKBh 2.55).
**Negations:** No unconditioned is a real entity; space is absence of resistance (AKBh 2.55).; No self as a separate entity; the 'person' is a designation of the series (AKBh 9).
**Relation to self:** There is no self; clinging to one is the root of all defilements (AKBh 9, p. 461).
**Relation to world:** Only momentary present dharmas exist; nirvāṇa is their cessation, not a further reality.
**Caveat:** The Sautrāntika denies that nirvāṇa is an entity at all; a 'one truth' reading that reifies it is exactly what it argues against.
