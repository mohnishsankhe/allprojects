The Sakya school, centred on the Khön family's monastery of Sakya (1073) and its 'five founding masters', transmits Virūpa's Path with its Result (Lamdre) with the Hevajra tantra as its core, the mind-training summary Parting from the Four Attachments, and a strong scholastic tradition (Sakya Paṇḍita's epistemology and Three Vows; Gorampa's and Shākya Chokden's Madhyamaka).

## Distinctive positions
- The Path with its Result (lam 'bras): the result is already present in the cause, so path and result are taught together; the whole path is laid out as the three visions (impure, experiential, pure) and the three continua (causal/all-base, method/body, result/Mahāmudrā).
- View of the inseparability of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa ('khor 'das dbyer med), the union of clarity and emptiness of the all-base causal continuum.
- Parting from the Four Attachments (zhen pa bzhi bral), received by Sachen Kunga Nyingpo from Mañjuśrī, as the summary of the whole common path.
- Sakya Paṇḍita's insistence that Mahāmudrā is the wisdom born of empowerment and the two stages; his critique of a self-sufficient 'white panacea' Mahāmudrā as Chinese-style (Hwashang) teaching.
- Three vows (prātimokṣa, bodhisattva, tantric) must be kept distinct and each by its own rules (Discrimination of the Three Vows).
- Madhyamaka (Gorampa): the final view is freedom from all four extremes (spros bral), not a mere negation of inherent existence; Tsongkhapa's reading rejected as 'nihilist Madhyamaka'.
- Epistemology (Sakya Paṇḍita): universals are conceptual constructions, not real entities, against the earlier Sangphu realists.
- Leadership of the school by the Khön family (Sakya Trizin), with the Ngor, Tshar and Dzong sub-schools.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** 'khor 'das dbyer med — the inseparability of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, kun gzhi rgyu rgyud — the all-base causal continuum, gsal stong zung 'jug — the union of clarity and emptiness, Mahāmudrā, the result continuum (phyag rgya chen po 'bras bu'i rgyud), spros bral — freedom from elaborations (Madhyamaka, as in Gorampa), śūnyatā
**Descriptions:** The all-base (ālaya) as causal continuum holds saṃsāra and nirvāṇa both (Vajra Verses, Derge 139b.1).; The result is Mahāmudrā, the result continuum, known through the four result-empowerments as omniscience (Vajra Verses 139b.2–3).; Pure vision: the inexhaustible ornamental wheel of the Sugatas' body, speech and mind (Vajra Verses 139a.7).; The ultimate is free from the four extremes of existence, non-existence, both and neither (Gorampa, Distinguishing the Views).
**Negations:** not merely the absence of inherent existence (Gorampa, against Tsongkhapa); not an other-empty permanent entity (Gorampa, against Dolpopa); 'If grasping arises, it is not the view' (Parting from the Four Attachments, line 4)
**Relation to self:** The individual mind-stream as ālaya is the causal continuum in which the result is present as potential; realizing its clarity-emptiness frees it from the self-grasping of the impure vision.
**Relation to world:** The three visions: the world as seen by beings with afflictions (impure), by yogins in meditation (experiential), and by buddhas (pure) — one reality appearing according to the purity of mind.
**Caveat:** Sakya teachers would reject any reading of 'inseparability of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa' as a substantial one-reality behind appearances; Gorampa's freedom from elaboration denies even that emptiness is an entity. The Lamdre view is realized through empowerment and the two stages, not by reasoning alone.
