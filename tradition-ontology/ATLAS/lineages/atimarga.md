The Atimārga ('path beyond') is the name the Śaiva scriptures give to the older of Śaivism's two great divisions, beside the Mantramārga ('path of mantras'). It comprises the Pāñcārthika Pāśupatas, who trace themselves to Lakulīśa and the Pāśupata Sūtra, the Lākulas (known in Karnataka as Kālāmukhas), and - in some sources and some modern accounts - the Kāpālikas or Somasiddhāntins. Its observances are ascetic and aim at liberation. Apart from the Pāśupata Sūtra with Kauṇḍinya's commentary and the Gaṇakārikā with its Ratnaṭīkā, it is known mainly from inscriptions, from the Mantramārga scriptures that describe it, and from opponents' reports.

## Distinctive positions
- The Śaiva scriptures divide Śaiva revelation into the Atimārga and the Mantramārga; the Niśvāsamukha counts the Atimārga as the fourth of five 'streams' (laukika, vaidika, ādhyātmika, atimārga, mantra).
- The Atimārga observances are for initiated ascetics and aim at liberation (duḥkhānta, the end of suffering); in the Pāśupata case initiation is restricted to brahmin men.
- Its observances are defined as beyond the āśramas (atyāśrama: the Pāśupata) or beyond the world (lokātīta: the skull observance of the Lākulas and Kāpālikas).
- The Svacchanda Tantra says the Atimārga lies 'beyond the states of the intellect' and that its skull-observers and Pāśupatas are not re-created, but it places their goals at levels below Śiva (Aiśvara pada, Dhruva).
- The Lord (Paśupati, Rudra) is the independent cause; souls are eternal paśus; liberation is attained by the Lord's grace.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Paśupati, Rudra, Maheśvara, Bhairava / Kapālin (Kāpālika)
**Descriptions:** the Lord (pati) as the one independent cause, acting by his own will; the Lord's knowledge and action are unsurpassed; liberation brings the soul into his proximity and union, sharing his powers (Pāśupata); for the skull-observers the Lord is Kapālin/Bhairava, whose penance they re-enact
**Negations:** the Lord is not dependent on karma (PABh on PS 2.6)
**Relation to self:** Souls are eternal paśus distinct from the Lord; liberation is the end of their suffering by the Lord's grace.
**Relation to world:** The Lord emits and withdraws the effects (the world and the embodied souls) at will; he is their cause.
**Caveat:** The Atimārga schools are theistic and keep the liberated soul distinct from the Lord; they would reject a reading that dissolves the soul into an impersonal absolute. The Mantramārga ranks their goal below its own (Svacchanda 11.71). For the Lākula, Kāpālika and Kālāmukha divisions almost no first-hand doctrinal text survives.
