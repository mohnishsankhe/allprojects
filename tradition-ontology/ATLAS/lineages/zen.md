Japanese Chan: the Rinzai, Sōtō and Ōbaku lineages established from the late 12th to the 17th century, teaching seated meditation (zazen), kōan introspection or just sitting, and monastic life as the direct realization of buddha-nature.

## Distinctive positions
- Zazen as the heart of practice, understood by Sōtō as just sitting (shikantaza) in which practice and realization are one, and by Rinzai as the field of kōan introspection leading to kenshō.
- Transmission certified face to face (menju) with documents of succession (shisho, inka).
- Monastic rule adapted from the Chinese qinggui; work, ritual and daily conduct as practice.

**Transmissions received:** 
  - [Linji house](linji.md) — what: Huanglong- and Yangqi-branch Linji lineages (Eisai; Enni; émigré Chinese masters); evidence: tradition records
  - [Caodong house](caodong.md) — what: Caodong lineage from Tiantong Rujing to Dōgen; evidence: Dōgen's Shisho, Hōkyōki

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** buddha-nature (busshō 仏性), the Unborn (fushō 不生), original face (honrai no menmoku)
**Descriptions:** 'All sentient beings are originally buddhas' (Hakuin, Zazen wasan, opening line).; The buddha-mind is unborn and marvellously illuminating (Bankei).; For Dōgen, 'whole being is buddha-nature' (shitsuu busshō) and impermanence itself is buddha-nature (Shōbōgenzō Busshō).
**Negations:** Not an object that can be grasped by thought; 'not-thinking' (hishiryō) (Fukanzazengi, after Yaoshan).
**Relation to self:** The self studied and forgotten: 'to study the buddha-way is to study the self; to study the self is to forget the self' (Genjōkōan).
**Relation to world:** The world is not other than buddha-nature: mountains and waters are the sūtras (Sansuikyō); being is time (Uji).
**Caveat:** Japanese Zen masters differ: Rinzai stresses seeing the nature (kenshō), Sōtō the enactment of buddha-nature in practice, Bankei the Unborn already here. None would accept an ultimate apart from practice and daily life.
