The umbrella of Goddess-centred traditions: Purāṇic devotion to the Devī of the Devī Māhātmya and Devī Bhāgavata, the tantric cults of the Śrīkula (Śrīvidyā) and Kālīkula, Kaula and Kubjikā lines, and regional goddess worship. They share the doctrine that the ultimate is Śakti, the Mother, identical with Brahman.

## Distinctive positions
- The supreme reality is the Goddess (Devī, Mahādevī, Parā Śakti), the Mother of all; the Śākta Upaniṣads and the Devī Gītā make her declare herself Brahman.
- Śakti is the power of the absolute; without her even Śiva cannot stir (Saundaryalaharī 1).
- The world is her form and her play; māyā is her power, by which she both binds and liberates (Devī Māhātmya 1).
- She has gentle and fierce forms — Lalitā, Umā, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī; Durgā, Caṇḍikā, Kālī, Cāmuṇḍā — all one Goddess (Devī Māhātmya 11: all knowledges and all women are her forms).
- Worship through name, mantra, yantra and image; recitation of her deeds (Saptaśatī) and names (Sahasranāma).
- Enjoyment and liberation (bhukti and mukti) are both granted by the Goddess.
- Kuṇḍalinī is the Goddess herself asleep in the body; her rise to Śiva in the crown is union.
- Esoteric branches require initiation (dīkṣā) from a qualified guru and keep their rites secret.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Devī / Mahādevī, Śakti / Parā Śakti, Mahāmāyā, Ambikā, the Mother, Caṇḍikā / Durgā, Brahman
**Descriptions:** Before creation the Goddess alone existed; she produced the cosmic egg and the gods (Bahvṛca Upaniṣad; Devī Gītā 7.32).; She is eternal and the world is her form; yet for the gods' sake she is born again and again (Devī Māhātmya 1).; She abides in all beings as consciousness, intelligence, sleep, hunger, power, faith, peace, compassion and mother (Devī Māhātmya 5).; She is Mahāmāyā, who drags even the minds of the wise into delusion, and when pleased grants liberation (Devī Māhātmya 1).; She declares herself Brahman, of the nature of consciousness (Devī Upaniṣad).
**Negations:** nirguṇā, niṣkalā, nirākārā — without qualities, parts or form (Lalitā Sahasranāma 43-45); beyond the three gods' consorts, 'a certain fourth', hard to reach, of boundless greatness (Saundaryalaharī 98)
**Relation to self:** She is the inner self of all; 'she is the self' (Bahvṛca); she abides in beings as consciousness.
**Relation to world:** The world is her manifestation and her body; she creates, sustains and withdraws it and deludes and liberates through her power of māyā.
**Caveat:** Śāktas name the ultimate as the feminine Śakti, the Mother, and many resist readings that make her a mere attribute of a male god or that make the world she manifests unreal; but Śākta texts themselves identify her with the Brahman of the Upaniṣads and declare all goddesses her forms, which fits the one-truth reading.
