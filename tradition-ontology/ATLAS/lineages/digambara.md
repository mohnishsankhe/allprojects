The 'sky-clad' branch of the Jain tradition, whose monks go naked and eat once a day from their hands. It holds that the Aṅgas were lost and bases itself on the Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama, the Kaṣāyapāhuḍa and the works of Kundakunda, Umāsvāti and later ācāryas; it denies liberation in a female body and eating by the omniscient. It includes the Bīsapantha and Terāpantha modes of worship and the aniconic Tāraṇapantha.

## Distinctive positions
- Complete nudity is required of the fully renounced monk; one who wears clothes cannot attain liberation
- Liberation is not possible in a female body; a woman must first be reborn as a man (nuns, āryikās, keep a white robe and are ranked with the most advanced laity)
- The omniscient does not take food (no kavalāhāra); hunger and thirst are among the eighteen faults absent in the Jina
- The original Aṅgas and Pūrvas were gradually lost; only portions survive in the Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama and the Kaṣāyapāhuḍa; the Śvetāmbara Āgamas are not accepted
- Scripture is arranged in four anuyogas (narrative, cosmological-karmic, conduct, substance)
- In Mahāvīra's life: no embryo transfer, sixteen dreams of the mother, and no marriage; Mallinātha was a man
