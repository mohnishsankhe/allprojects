# Digambara

`lin:digambara` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Family:** ascetic
**Alternate names:** the sky-clad, Nirgrantha (self-designation), Boṭika / Boḍiya (in Śvetāmbara sources), Mūla Saṅgha (its main historical lineage)
**Parent:** [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](jainism.md)
**Sub-lineages:** [Mūla Saṅgha (Digambara)](mula-sangha.md), [Kāṣṭhā Saṅgha](kastha-sangha.md), [Drāviḍa Saṅgha](dravida-sangha.md), [Māthura Saṅgha](mathura-sangha.md), [Bīsapantha (Digambara)](bisapantha.md), [Terāpantha (Digambara)](terapantha-digambara.md), [Tāraṇapantha (Samaiyā)](taranapantha.md), [Adhyātma movement (Adhyātma-mata)](adhyatma-jain.md), `lin:kanji-swami`, [Tamil Jain tradition](tamil-jain.md)
**Key teachers:** [Bhadrabāhu (the śrutakevalin)](../teachers/bhadrabahu.md), [Dharasena](../teachers/dharasena.md), [Puṣpadanta (ācārya)](../teachers/puspadanta-acarya.md), [Bhūtabali](../teachers/bhutabali.md), [Guṇadhara](../teachers/gunadhara.md), [Kundakunda](../teachers/kundakunda.md), [Samantabhadra](../teachers/samantabhadra.md), [Pūjyapāda Devanandin](../teachers/pujyapada.md), [Akalaṅka](../teachers/akalanka.md), [Vīrasena](../teachers/virasena.md), [Jinasena (author of the Ādipurāṇa)](../teachers/jinasena.md), [Guṇabhadra](../teachers/gunabhadra.md), [Nemicandra Siddhāntacakravartin](../teachers/nemicandra-siddhantacakravartin.md), [Amṛtacandra](../teachers/amrtacandra.md), [Ācārya Śāntisāgara](../teachers/santisagara.md)
**Regions:** Karnataka (Śravaṇabeḷagoḷa, Mūḍabidri), Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Bundelkhand, Delhi and Uttar Pradesh
**Dates:** Tradition's account: the Digambaras regard themselves as continuing the original naked practice of Mahāvīra; their account traces the division to Bhadrabāhu's migration south during a twelve-year famine (Maurya period); Scholarly account: a distinct Digambara identity is attested from roughly the early centuries CE; the Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama and Kundakunda's works are dated by scholars between the 2nd and 8th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The 'sky-clad' branch of the Jain tradition, whose monks go naked and eat once a day from their hands. It holds that the Aṅgas were lost and bases itself on the Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama, the Kaṣāyapāhuḍa and the works of Kundakunda, Umāsvāti and later ācāryas; it denies liberation in a female body and eating by the omniscient. It includes the Bīsapantha and Terāpantha modes of worship and the aniconic Tāraṇapantha.

## Distinctive positions
- Complete nudity is required of the fully renounced monk; one who wears clothes cannot attain liberation
- Liberation is not possible in a female body; a woman must first be reborn as a man (nuns, āryikās, keep a white robe and are ranked with the most advanced laity)
- The omniscient does not take food (no kavalāhāra); hunger and thirst are among the eighteen faults absent in the Jina
- The original Aṅgas and Pūrvas were gradually lost; only portions survive in the Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama and the Kaṣāyapāhuḍa; the Śvetāmbara Āgamas are not accepted
- Scripture is arranged in four anuyogas (narrative, cosmological-karmic, conduct, substance)
- In Mahāvīra's life: no embryo transfer, sixteen dreams of the mother, and no marriage; Mallinātha was a man


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** śuddhātman (the pure self), Paramātman, samaya (the self, in the Samayasāra), jñāyaka-bhāva (the knowing state), Siddha
**Descriptions:** from the standpoint of ultimate reality the self is unbound, untouched, not other, constant, undifferentiated and unconjoined (Samayasāra 14); one, pure, made of perception and knowledge, formless; not an atom of anything else is mine (Samayasāra 38); the knowing state beyond all stages of quality (Samayasāra 6); free of the eighteen faults, including hunger and thirst (Niyamasāra 6)
**Negations:** in truth there is no knowledge, conduct or faith as separate attributes of the knower (Samayasāra 7); not reached in a clothed or female body; not a creator
**Relation to self:** The pure self is the soul's own nature now, seen from the ultimate standpoint; the conventional standpoint describes the bound soul in order to lead it there.
**Relation to world:** Real and uncreated; karmic matter and the soul occasion each other's changes without either becoming the other.
**Caveat:** The Jain tradition denies a single all-encompassing reality and a creator: liberated souls are infinitely many and remain distinct, and the universe of six substances is uncreated. It can accept 'one truth, many names' only in its own way — as many-sidedness (anekānta) about a plural reality — not as the identity of all souls in one substance. Kundakunda's language of the pure self is sometimes compared with Advaita; the Digambara tradition insists the pure self is each individual soul's own, not a universal Brahman.
