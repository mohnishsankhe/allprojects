# Kāpālika (Somasiddhānta)

**Alternate names:** Kāpālika, Kāpāla, Kāpālin, Mahāvratin / Mahāvratadhara, Somasiddhāntin, Soma (Kūrma P. 2.37.146)
**Parent:** [Atimārga](atimarga.md)
**Regions:** Kāñcī (Mattavilāsa), Śrīparvata / Śrīśaila (Mālatīmādhava; Śaṅkaradigvijaya), Ujjayinī (Śaṅkaradigvijaya), Candella country (Prabodhacandrodaya)

**Status:** extinct

The Kāpālikas, 'skull-men', were Śaiva ascetics who kept the great vow of the skull in imitation of Bhairava's penance. Their own scriptures are not identified; they are known through Purāṇic myth, Vaiṣṇava and Advaita polemic (Yāmuna, Rāmānuja, the Śaṅkaradigvijaya), Jain and Buddhist reports, and satirical or hostile portraits in Sanskrit drama (Mattavilāsa, Mālatīmādhava, Prabodhacandrodaya). Some sources group them with the Atimārga; others connect them with the Bhairava cults of the Mantramārga.

## Distinctive positions
- The great vow (mahāvrata) of the skull, modelled on Bhairava's (Śiva's) penance for cutting off Brahmā's head (Kūrma P. 2.31): the ascetic lives as a Kapālin with a skull-bowl and skull-topped staff.
- As reported by Yāmuna (Āgamaprāmāṇya) and Rāmānuja: liberation comes from knowing and wearing the six insignia (necklace/ear-ornament, rucaka, earrings, crest-jewel, ash, sacred thread) with skull and khaṭvāṅga as sub-insignia, and from a meditation on the self seated in the yoni (opponents' report).
- As reported: by mere entry into initiation one becomes a brahmin at once, and by taking the Kāpāla vow a man becomes an ascetic (Yāmuna, opponent's report).
- Their doctrine is called Somasiddhānta; its content is not preserved.
- Opponents' dramas portray Kāpālikas worshipping Bhairava and Cāmuṇḍā with liquor and alleged human offerings (Mālatīmādhava act 5; Prabodhacandrodaya act 3).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Bhairava / Mahābhairava, Kapālin, Soma (Śiva 'with Umā'; name of the doctrine Somasiddhānta)
**Descriptions:** Worshipped as Mahābhairava (Prabodhacandrodaya act 3, an opponent's portrayal).; Kapālin: the skull-bearing form of Śiva whose penance for Brahmā's head the Kāpālika imitates (Kūrma P. 2.31).
**Relation to self:** Yāmuna reports that for the Kāpālikas liberation comes from knowing and wearing the six insignia and from a meditation on the self, not from knowledge of Brahman (opponent's report).
**Relation to world:** Not recorded in the tradition's own words.
**Caveat:** Known almost entirely through opponents and satirists; no Kāpālika scripture has been identified with certainty. Somasiddhānta, the name of their doctrine, is attested; its content is not.
