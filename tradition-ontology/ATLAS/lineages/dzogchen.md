The Great Perfection: the Nyingma (and Bön) teaching that the mind's nature, awareness (rig pa), is already perfect buddhahood — empty in essence, luminous in nature, all-pervading in responsiveness — and that liberation comes from recognizing it through a master's introduction and resting in it, rather than by constructing it. Transmitted through Garab Dorje, Mañjuśrīmitra, Śrī Siṃha, Jñānasūtra, Vimalamitra, Padmasambhava and Vairocana; organized in three series and systematized by Longchenpa.

## Distinctive positions
- The ground (gzhi) is primordial purity (ka dag) and spontaneous presence (lhun grub); its three aspects are essence (ngo bo: empty), nature (rang bzhin: luminous) and compassion (thugs rje: all-pervading).
- Awareness (rig pa) is distinguished from ordinary mind (sems): the path is recognizing awareness, not fabricating a meditative state.
- Three series: mind (sems sde), space (klong sde) and instruction (man ngag sde); the Heart-essence (snying thig) belongs to the innermost secret cycle of the instruction series.
- Cutting through (khregs chod) to primordial purity; direct crossing (thod rgal) of spontaneous presence through four visions; the fruit can include the rainbow body ('ja' lus).
- Thoughts self-liberate (rang grol) in awareness; effort and contrivance are called a sickness (Kunjed Gyalpo).
- Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa have one ground and two paths: recognition (Samantabhadra) and non-recognition (beings).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** awareness (rig pa), the ground (gzhi): primordial purity and spontaneous presence, bodhicitta, the all-creating king (byang chub kyi sems kun byed rgyal po), self-arising wisdom (rang byung ye shes), Samantabhadra (Kun tu bzang po), dharmakāya (chos sku)
**Descriptions:** Essence empty (ngo bo stong pa), nature luminous (rang bzhin gsal ba), compassion all-pervading (thugs rje kun khyab) — the three aspects of the ground.; Primordially pure (ka dag) and spontaneously present (lhun grub).; 'I am primordially self-arising wisdom, the essence of all phenomena' (Kunjed Gyalpo ch.4); the variety is non-dual, free of elaboration in its parts (Kunjed Gyalpo ch.31).
**Negations:** not made, not to be sought, not attained by meditation (Kunjed Gyalpo ch.45); beyond view, meditation, samaya, grounds and paths to traverse (Kunjed Gyalpo ch.44); neither existent nor non-existent, free of the extremes (Longchenpa)
**Relation to self:** Awareness is one's own nature; recognizing it is buddhahood, not recognizing it is the beginning of saṃsāra (one ground, two paths).
**Relation to world:** Appearances arise as the ground's own display (rtsal, rol pa); deluded beings take them as external objects; in realization they self-liberate.
**Caveat:** The 'all-creating king' of the Kunjed Gyalpo is awareness itself speaking, not a creator distinct from beings; Dzogchen also distinguishes the ground from the all-base (kun gzhi) and awareness from mind (sems), and would reject any identification of rig pa with a merely thoughtless state or with a Brahman-like self.
