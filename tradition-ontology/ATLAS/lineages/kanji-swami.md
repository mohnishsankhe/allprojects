The 20th-century lay Digambara movement of Kanji Swami (1890–1980) at Songadh, which revived Kundakunda's Samayasāra with an emphasis on the niścaya standpoint, right faith first, and the doctrines of ordered modifications and inefficacious instrumental causes.

## Distinctive positions
- Right faith (samyag-darśana) through discrimination of self from non-self (bheda-vijñāna) must come first; vows and rituals without it do not lead to liberation.
- Only the niścaya standpoint is real (bhūtārtha); the vyavahāra standpoint is unreal (Samayasāra 11), though it is to be known.
- Every modification of every substance occurs in its fixed sequence (kramabaddha-paryāya).
- The instrumental cause (nimitta) is present but does nothing to another substance; the substance itself is the material cause (upādāna).
- Auspicious dispositions (śubha-bhāva) are also bondage.
- A lay (non-monastic) teacher at the centre; devotion to Sīmandhara Svāmī, the living Tīrthaṅkara of Mahāvideha.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** the pure soul (śuddhātman), the siddha state
**Descriptions:** The soul in its niścaya nature is pure knowing, untouched by karma; there is no creator God.
**Relation to self:** Each soul is eternally distinct, and in its true nature already pure (niścaya).
**Relation to world:** Six real substances (dravya) with their own modifications; no substance acts on another.
**Caveat:** As a Jain school it denies a single creator or a single world-soul; the one-truth reading applies only to the common nature of all souls, and the tradition would reject any identity of souls.
