The Vedānta school of strict non-dualism: Brahman, pure consciousness without a second, is the self of all; the world of plurality is an appearance of Brahman through māyā/avidyā, real for empirical purposes but sublated by knowledge. Rooted in Gauḍapāda's Māṇḍūkya Kārikā and systematized by Śaṅkara's commentaries on the prasthāna-traya, it developed through Maṇḍana, Sureśvara, Padmapāda, Vācaspati and Prakāśātman into the Bhāmatī and Vivaraṇa lines, a dialectical literature (Śrīharṣa, Citsukha, Madhusūdana), popular manuals (Pañcadaśī, Vedāntasāra, Vedāntaparibhāṣā) and a living monastic order (Daśanāmī).

## Distinctive positions
- Brahman alone is real (satya); the world is not ultimately real (mithyā); the individual self is not other than Brahman (Brahmajñānāvalīmālā 20; VC 20).
- Bondage is superimposition (adhyāsa) of the not-self on the self and vice versa, which is called ignorance (avidyā) (BSBh introduction).
- The world is an apparent transformation (vivarta) of Brahman, not a real transformation (pariṇāma).
- Three levels of reality: ultimate (pāramārthika), empirical (vyāvahārika), illusory (prātibhāsika).
- Liberation is not produced; it is the self's own nature revealed by knowledge alone; action cannot remove ignorance (Ātmabodha 3).
- Liberation while living (jīvanmukti) is possible; the body continues until the karma that began it (prārabdha) is exhausted (BS 4.1.15, 4.1.19).
- The qualified seeker (with the four qualifications) hears (śravaṇa), reflects (manana) and contemplates (nididhyāsana) the Upaniṣadic teaching.
- Saguṇa Brahman (Īśvara) is real at the empirical level and the proper object of worship; nirguṇa Brahman is the ultimate.
- Scripture teaches by provisional superimposition and subsequent retraction (adhyāropa-apavāda).
- Post-Śaṅkara Advaita divides into the Vārttika (Sureśvara), Bhāmatī (Vācaspati) and Vivaraṇa (Prakāśātman) lines over the locus of ignorance and how the one appears as many.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Brahman, Ātman, Turīya (the fourth), Sat-cit-ānanda, Paramātman, Sākṣin (as witness of the three states), Īśvara (Brahman as associated with māyā, empirical level)
**Descriptions:** existence, consciousness, infinitude — satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ brahma (TU 2.1.1) as its own-nature definition (svarūpa-lakṣaṇa); being–consciousness–bliss (sat-cit-ānanda); one without a second (ekam evādvitīyam, ChU 6.2.1); self-luminous (svaprakāśa), never an object; the substratum (adhiṣṭhāna) of the world-appearance, as the rope is of the snake; the world's cause, defined incidentally (taṭastha-lakṣaṇa) as that from which the world arises (BS 1.1.2)
**Negations:** neti neti — not so, not so (BĀU 2.3.6; BS 3.2.22); no dissolution, no origination, none bound, no seeker, no liberated — this is the highest truth (GK 2.32); without parts, attributes, action or difference of any of the three kinds (sajātīya, vijātīya, svagata)
**Relation to self:** Identical: the inner self (pratyag-ātman) of every being is Brahman (tat tvam asi; ahaṃ brahmāsmi). The jīva is Brahman appearing as limited or reflected through adjuncts produced by ignorance.
**Relation to world:** The world is superimposed on Brahman and is its apparent transformation (vivarta); Brahman is its material and efficient cause through māyā. The world is real for empirical purposes (vyāvahārika) until knowledge, then sublated; ultimately it is not other than Brahman.
**Caveat:** Advaita accepts that truth is one (and reads RV 1.164.46 in that sense), but the one is non-dual consciousness without any internal difference: the personal Lord is Brahman as associated with māyā, real at the empirical level only. Advaita would reject any 'one-truth' reading that makes the world, or plural selves, or a personal God ultimately real, and rejects equating its Brahman with a sum or organism of parts.
