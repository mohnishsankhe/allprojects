The school of the elders, the one early Indian school that survives, carrying the Pali Tipiṭaka, its commentaries and a continuous monastic ordination lineage from Sri Lanka to Myanmar, Thailand, Laos and Cambodia. It holds its canon to be the word of the Buddha as recited at the First Council and reads it through Buddhaghosa's commentaries and the Abhidhamma; its practice is the Vinaya, the precepts and uposatha of the laity, merit-making, paritta chanting, and meditation (samatha and vipassanā).

## Distinctive positions
- The Pali Tipiṭaka is the word of the Buddha (buddhavacana) as recited at the First Council; its commentaries (aṭṭhakathā) transmit the old Sinhalese exegesis.
- Vibhajjavāda — 'the doctrine of analysis' — as the orthodoxy vindicated at the Third Council (Kathāvatthu).
- The arahant ideal for disciples; the bodhisatta path fulfilling the ten perfections (pāramī) for one who aspires to Buddhahood (Buddhavaṃsa, Cariyāpiṭaka, Jātaka nidāna).
- Lineage of valid higher ordination (upasampadā) and of the Pātimokkha as the life of the Sāsana; the Sāsana is held to decline and to be renewed by the future Buddha Metteyya (DN 26).
- The bhikkhunī order died out in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia; its revival is disputed within the tradition (recent).
- Commentarial two truths (sammuti- and paramattha-sacca) and the analysis of ultimate realities (paramattha-dhamma: citta, cetasika, rūpa, nibbāna) — see U37.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** nibbāna, asaṅkhata-dhātu (the unconditioned element), paramattha-dhamma nibbāna (the fourth ultimate reality, beside citta, cetasika and rūpa)
**Descriptions:** the one unconditioned dhamma, outside the five aggregates and the three periods of time; object of the path and fruition consciousness; one in nature (santi-lakkhaṇa, the characteristic of peace) but described as with residue (sa-upādisesa, the arahant's living) and without residue (anupādisesa, at the arahant's death) (Iti 44); described by the commentaries as existing (atthi) and not mere non-existence, against those who say it is only the absence of the defilements
**Negations:** not a self (sabbe dhammā anattā — Dhp 279, read by the commentaries to include nibbāna); not produced by the path but realised by it; without arising, change or passing away (AN 3.47)
**Relation to self:** Nibbāna is anattā; there is no self that attains it — 'suffering exists, but no sufferer; nibbāna exists, but no one who is extinguished' (Visuddhimagga 16.90, a commentarial verse; see U37).
**Relation to world:** The unconditioned is distinct from the conditioned world (saṅkhata) and is not its cause; the world is dependently arisen.
**Caveat:** Theravāda affirms nibbāna as a real, single unconditioned dhamma but explicitly rejects that it is a self, a soul-substance, a creator or a cosmic ground; it would reject the identification of nibbāna with Brahman or ātman made in some reconciliations (the Kathāvatthu and the commentaries argue against any permanent self).
