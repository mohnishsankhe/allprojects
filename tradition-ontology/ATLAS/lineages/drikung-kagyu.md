One of the 'eight lesser' Kagyu schools founded by disciples of Phagmodrupa; established by Jigten Sumgön at Drikung Thil, known for the Gongchig ('Single Intent') and the fivefold Mahāmudrā, for sending retreatants to Kailash, Lapchi and Tsari, and for its phowa.

## Distinctive positions
- The 'single intent' (dgongs gcig): all the Buddha's teachings share one intent; the three vows (prātimokṣa, bodhisattva, mantra) are one in essence (dsp:three-vows-one-essence).
- The fivefold Mahāmudrā (phyag chen lnga ldan): bodhicitta, the deity body, devotion to the guru, the Mahāmudrā view, and dedication — the five together make the practice complete.
- The Drikung transference (phowa) is famous; great public phowa gatherings are held (recent practice, earlier roots).

**Transmissions received:** 
  - [Phagdru Kagyu](phagdru-kagyu.md) — what: Phagmodrupa's Mahāmudrā and Six Yogas; evidence: tradition's account: Jigten Sumgön was Phagmodrupa's disciple

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Mahāmudrā, the single intent (dgongs gcig) of the Buddha's word
**Descriptions:** The whole of the Buddha's teaching has one intent, whose heart is Mahāmudrā; realized through the fivefold practice (bodhicitta, deity, guru, view, dedication).
**Negations:** not reached by view alone without bodhicitta and devotion (the fivefold teaching)
**Relation to self:** As in the Kagyu generally: the mind's own nature.
**Relation to world:** The vows and practices of all vehicles are aspects of one intent rather than rival systems.
**Caveat:** 'Single intent' concerns the unity of the Buddha's teaching, not a metaphysical monism.
