# Pañcācārya (Pañcapīṭha) tradition

**Alternate names:** Pañcapīṭha, Pañcācārya-sampradāya, Guru-paramparā of the five ācāryas, Guru-sthala maṭhas
**Parent:** [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](virasaiva.md)

**Regions:** Rambhāpurī (Bāḷehonnūr, Karnataka), Ujjayinī (Ujjaini, Kūḍligi, Karnataka), Kedāra (Kedārnāth), Śrīśaila (Andhra Pradesh), Kāśī (Jaṅgamavāḍī maṭha, Vārāṇasī)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: the five ācāryas appear in every yuga from liṅgas; in the Kali age as Revaṇa (Reṇuka), Maruḷa, Ēkōrāma, Paṇḍitārādhya and Viśvārādhya; Scholarly account: the five-pīṭha organization and its Sanskrit literature are attested from the late medieval period; dates debated; (confidence low)
**Status:** living

The strand of Vīraśaivism organized around five ācāryas — Revaṇa (Reṇuka), Maruḷa, Ēkōrāma, Paṇḍitārādhya and Viśvārādhya — who, in the tradition's account, emerged from liṅgas at five sacred sites and founded the five pīṭhas. Its scripture is the Śaiva Āgamas read in their Vīraśaiva portion, the Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi and Śrīpati's Śrīkarabhāṣya.

## Distinctive positions
- Vīraśaiva dharma is beginningless (anādi), taught by Śiva in the later portion of the Śaiva Āgamas (SSM 5.14) and consistent with the Veda (SSM 5.5–13).
- Basava is honoured as a great devotee and reviver within this older stream, not as its founder.
- The Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi's 101 sthalas are the full map of the path; doctrine is Śivādvaita (SSM 3.86) — in Śrīpati's formulation, Śakti-viśiṣṭādvaita.
- Five pīṭhas (seats), each founded by one ācārya, transmit initiation through the guru lineage.


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Paraśiva (Śiva qualified by Śakti), Mahāliṅga / parabrahman
**Descriptions:** Śivādvaita: the non-duality of Śiva taught in agreement with Veda and Vedānta (SSM 3.83–87); the great liṅga is supreme brahman, the breath the jīva (SSM 12.21–24)
**Negations:** beyond speech and mind, self-luminous, indescribable (SSM 12.21–24)
**Relation to self:** Difference of Lord and servant governs worship; non-duality is realized at aikya.
**Relation to world:** The world is Śiva-made, arising from and dissolving into the liṅga.
**Caveat:** Would reject any reading that dispenses with worship of the liṅga before realization (SSM 10.37–43).
