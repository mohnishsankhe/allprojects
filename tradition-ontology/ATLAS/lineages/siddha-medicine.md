# Siddha medicine (cittā maruttuvam)

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Cittā vaittiyam, Siddha vaidyam, Tamil medicine (tamiḻ maruttuvam)
**Parent:** [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](tamil-siddha.md)

**Dates:** Tradition's account: revealed by Śiva to Pārvatī, passed through Nandi to the Siddhars, above all Agastya; Scholarly account: the attributed Tamil medical verse-treatises are mostly placed in the 15th–19th c.; the humoral vocabulary is attested in the Tirukkuṟaḷ (ch. 95); (confidence low)
**Status:** living

The Tamil medical system ascribed to the Siddhars, above all Agastya, with Bogar, Tēraiyar, Yūkimuṉi, Tirumūlar and others as authorities. It explains health and disease by the three humours on a base of the five elements and ninety-six principles, diagnoses by pulse and eight examinations, and uses herbal, mineral-metal and animal medicines, including calcined metal and mineral preparations. Recorded here in summary only; no preparations, doses or procedures.

## Distinctive positions
- Health is the balance of the three vital humours (uyir tātu) — vaḷi (vātam), aḻal (pittam), aiyam (kapam) — whose excess or deficiency causes disease (Tirukkuṟaḷ 941).
- The body is built of the five elements and the 96 principles; the same anatomy serves medicine and yoga.
- Diagnosis by eight examinations (eṇvakait tērvu), above all the pulse (nāṭi) and the urine (nīrkkuṟi, neykkuṟi).
- Medicines are drawn from plants (mūlikai), minerals and metals (tātu) and animal products (jīvam); mineral and metal preparations (parpam, centūram) are held to be most potent and most dangerous if wrongly made.
- The tradition counts 4,448 diseases.
- Rejuvenation (kāya kaṟpam) aims at long life as a support for yoga and liberation.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Civam (as the source of the medical knowledge)
**Descriptions:** Siddha medicine places itself within the Siddhars' Śaiva frame: Śiva is the first teacher of medicine; body and cosmos share the five elements and the ninety-six principles.
**Relation to self:** The soul inhabits a body of 96 principles whose health is the balance of the three humours.
**Relation to world:** The body is built of the five elements, like the world; macrocosm and microcosm correspond.
**Caveat:** A medical system: it does not itself argue a doctrine of the ultimate, but borrows the Siddha/Śaiva frame; practitioners hold a range of religious views.
