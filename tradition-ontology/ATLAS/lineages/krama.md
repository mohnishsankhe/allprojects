The Kālī-centred Kaula tradition of the northern transmission as practised in Kashmir (Mahānaya, Mahārtha), rooted in the Jayadrathayāmala and related Kālīkula scriptures and said to have been revealed to Jñānanetra. Abhinavagupta absorbed its doctrine of the twelve Kālīs into the Trika (TĀ 4; Kramastotra); Maheśvarānanda's Mahārthamañjarī and Śitikaṇṭha's Mahānayaprakāśa are later statements. Much of its scripture survives only in manuscript or citation.

## Distinctive positions
- Worship and contemplation of Kālī as the power that 'counts', emits and devours (kalana) all things; the five senses of the root kal: projecting, knowing, counting, moving, sounding (TĀ 4.173).
- The twelve Kālīs as the phases of every act of cognition - emission, persistence, withdrawal and the nameless (anākhya) - across object, means of knowledge and knowing subject (TĀ 4.148-172; Abhinavagupta's Kramastotra).
- The five flows (pañcavāha) of goddesses: Vyomavāmeśvarī, Khecarī, Dikcarī, Gocarī, Bhūcarī (low confidence).
- Kālasaṅkarṣiṇī, 'she who draws in time', is supreme.
- The sequence (krama) of cognition leads into the sequenceless (akrama).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Kālī, Kālasaṅkarṣiṇī, Anākhya (the nameless), Svasaṃvid (one's own consciousness)
**Descriptions:** one's own consciousness, whose free and independent variety is the supreme Goddess (TĀ 4.172); the power that projects, knows, counts, moves and sounds (TĀ 4.173); the final Kālī in whom full consciousness dissolves into the Akula abode (TĀ 4.170)
**Negations:** the nameless (anākhya)
**Relation to self:** The goddess is one's own consciousness (svasaṃvid).
**Relation to world:** The Kālīs emit, hold and devour the world as phases of cognition across object, means and subject.
**Caveat:** Known chiefly through Abhinavagupta, Jayaratha and Maheśvarānanda; the Krama stresses the sequence of the goddess's phases as the way into the sequenceless and would resist a reading that erases the sequence.
