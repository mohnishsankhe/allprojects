# Trika ('the Triad')

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Trikaśāsana, Trikaśāstra, Ṣaḍardha(śāstra) ('half of six'), Anuttara-trika, Trika-Kaula
**Parent:** [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](kashmir-saivism.md)
**Key teachers:** [Abhinavagupta](../teachers/abhinavagupta.md), [Śambhunātha](../teachers/sambhunatha.md), [Somānanda](../teachers/somananda.md), [Kṣemarāja](../teachers/ksemaraja.md), [Jayaratha](../teachers/jayaratha.md), [Yogarāja](../teachers/yogaraja.md), [Śivopādhyāya](../teachers/sivopadhyaya.md), [Ānandabhaṭṭa (Bhaṭṭānanda)](../teachers/anandabhatta.md), [Rājānaka Lakṣmīrāma](../teachers/rajanaka-laksmirama.md)
**Regions:** Kashmir, Jālandhara
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Revealed by Bhairava; the Mālinīvijayottara presents itself as taught by Kārttikeya to the sages (MVT 1.1-7).; Scholarly account: Trika scriptures of the Vidyāpīṭha (Siddhayogeśvarīmata, Mālinīvijayottara) c. 6th-9th c.; exegetical Trika of Abhinavagupta c. 990-1020; Jayaratha 13th c.; (confidence low)
**Status:** absorbed

The 'Triad' tradition: a Vidyāpīṭha cult of the goddesses Parā, Parāparā and Aparā grounded in the Siddhayogeśvarīmata and Mālinīvijayottara, which Abhinavagupta made the frame of his synthesis of Kashmirian non-dual Śaivism in the Tantrāloka and Tantrasāra, incorporating Kaula, Krama, Spanda and Pratyabhijñā. It ranks itself highest among Śaiva revelations.

## Distinctive positions
- The triad of goddesses Parā, Parāparā and Aparā with Bhairava; in Abhinavagupta's Trika Kālasaṅkarṣiṇī stands above them as the fourth.
- The triads Śiva-Śakti-Nara (aṇu), icchā-jñāna-kriyā, and non-difference, difference-in-non-difference, difference.
- The Trika is the essence of all Śaiva scripture and the Mālinīvijayottara is the essence of the Trika (TĀ 1.18).
- Scriptural hierarchy: Veda < Śaiva (Siddhānta) < Vāma < Dakṣiṇa < Mata < Kula < Kaula < Trika (verse quoted by Jayaratha on TĀ 13.301; TĀ 13.300-301 grades śaktipāta in the same order).
- Four means of immersion (samāveśa): anupāya (TĀ 2), śāmbhava, śākta, āṇava (MVT 2.21-23, TĀ 1.168-170).
- Phonematic emanation: the letters as mātṛkā (A to KṢA) and in the scrambled order mālinī (NA to PHA); the Heart (hṛdaya) as the seed of the universe (Parātrīśikā 24).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Anuttara, Bhairava, Parā (the supreme Goddess), Kālasaṅkarṣiṇī, Hṛdaya (the Heart)
**Descriptions:** the Heart of Bhairava, in which the whole moving and unmoving world rests as the great tree rests in the banyan seed (Parātrīśikā 24); Bhairava's state, indescribable, full (bharita), identical with the supreme Goddess (Vijñāna Bhairava 14-18); the one consciousness whose powers are Parā, Parāparā and Aparā (will, knowledge, action; TĀ 1.271-272)
**Negations:** the forms of Bhairava with parts (sakala) are for meditation of the confused; in truth he is not these (VBT 8-13)
**Relation to self:** The limited individual (nara/aṇu) is Śiva through Śakti; śakti is the door into Śiva (VBT 20-21).
**Relation to world:** The world is the unfolding of the Heart through the letters (mātṛkā) and the tattvas; it is within Bhairava as his power.
**Caveat:** The Trika insists on the triadic self-unfolding of the one and on its own superiority to other revelations.
