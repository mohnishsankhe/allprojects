# Tājika (Indo-Persian annual and interrogational astrology)

Tājika is the branch of jyotiṣa that took over Perso-Arabic methods — annual revolutions, the sixteen Tājika yogas, sahamas and muntha — written in Sanskrit from the 13th century by Brahmin and Jain authors and standardized in Nīlakaṇṭha's Tājikanīlakaṇṭhī (1587). The name itself (tājika, 'Persian/Arab') marks the tradition's awareness of its foreign source.

## Distinctive positions
- The annual chart (varṣaphala) cast for the sun's return to its birth position.
- Sixteen aspect-combinations with Perso-Arabic names (itthaśāla, īsarāpha, etc.).
- Sensitive points called sahama and the progressed point muntha.
