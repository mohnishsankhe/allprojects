Jyotiṣa, the Vedāṅga of the heavenly lights, began as the reckoning of times for the Vedic sacrifice (Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa) and grew into three branches: mathematical astronomy (siddhānta), mundane astrology and omens (saṃhitā), and horoscopy (horā/jātaka), later also interrogation (praśna), electional timing (muhūrta) and the Indo-Persian Tājika. It teaches that the planets, signs and lunar mansions reveal — and as divine powers dispense — the fruits of past karma, and prescribes timing and pacificatory rites. It is a limb of the Veda rather than a path to liberation, though it names signs of renunciation and liberation in the chart.

## Distinctive positions
- Jyotiṣa is the eye of the Veda and stands at the head of the Vedāṅgas, because rites depend on times (VJ; Pāṇinīya Śikṣā 41–42).
- The science has three branches (skandha): gaṇita/siddhānta (computation), saṃhitā (omens and mundane matters) and horā (nativities) (BS 1.9; BPHS 1.2).
- The birth chart reveals the ripening of good and bad karma earned in a former birth, as a lamp shows objects in darkness (BJ 1.3; LJ 1.3).
- The planets are forms of Janārdana that give beings the fruits of their karma (BPHS 2.3); their wrath can be pacified by worship, gifts, mantra and fire-offering (YājñSm 1.295–308; BPHS 84).
- Fate (daiva) is karma made in another birth; success needs fate and human effort together, like farming (Yogayātrā 1.3–4).
- Time is twofold: the time that ends the worlds, and measurable time (SS 1.10).
- Eclipses occur by the shadow of the earth and the body of the moon; Rāhu is present at the eclipse by Brahmā's boon and receives its offerings (BS 5.8–15).
- A king or householder should act at a time when the planets are well placed (BS 103.59–60; Muhūrta Cintāmaṇi).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Brahman, unthinkable and unmanifest, without and with qualities (Sūrya Siddhānta 1.1), Vāsudeva, the supreme Brahman; Aniruddha as the Sun-Hiraṇyagarbha (SS 12.12–21), Ka — one and many, the true deity, the supreme Brahman (Āryabhaṭīya 1.1), Viṣṇu, one and unmanifest, with the powers Śrī, Bhū and Nīlā; Janārdana in the form of the planets (BPHS 1.9–20; 2.3), The Sun as self of all embodied beings, self of Kālapuruṣa, maker of time and door of liberation (Yogayātrā 1.1; BJ 1.1; 2.1), Kāla, time — the destroyer of worlds (SS 1.10)
**Descriptions:** acintya-avyakta-rūpa, nirguṇa yet guṇātman, support of the world (SS 1.1); one quarter manifest, three quarters immortal (SS 12.20; BPHS 1.10–11); present in all beings as the supreme-Self portion beside the jīva-portion (BPHS 1.21–24); gives beings the fruits of karma in the form of the planets (BPHS 2.3)
**Negations:** unthinkable, unmanifest, without qualities (SS 1.1); time without beginning or end (Āryabhaṭīya 3.11)
**Relation to self:** The Sun is the self of Kālapuruṣa and of all embodied beings (BJ 2.1; Yogayātrā 1.1); each being has a portion of the individual soul and a portion of the supreme Self (BPHS 1.21–24); the ātmakāraka signifies the self in the chart.
**Relation to world:** The Lord divides himself into the twelve signs and twenty-seven asterisms (SS 12.25); time is his form; the planets are his forms and avatāras dispensing karma (BPHS 2.3–7); the earth-globe stands in space by Brahman's supporting power (SS 12.32).
**Caveat:** Jyotiṣa is a limb (aṅga) of the Veda — its eye — and a science of time and of the ripening of karma, not itself a soteriology. It names the ultimate only in invocations and in cosmogonies borrowed from Pāñcarātra, Purāṇic and Vedāntic frames, and different texts invoke different ultimates (Brahman, Vāsudeva, Viṣṇu, the Sun, Ka); it would not claim that reading or remedying the chart liberates, though horā names the configurations that indicate liberation (BJ 25.15; Jaimini 1.2.69). A one-truth reading should not be read back into it as its own doctrine.
