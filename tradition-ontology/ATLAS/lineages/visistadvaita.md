The Vedānta of Nāthamuni, Yāmuna and Rāmānuja, carried by the Śrīvaiṣṇava community of South India. It teaches one Brahman, Nārāyaṇa with Śrī, who has souls and matter as his body, reached by devotion or surrender and served eternally in Vaikuṇṭha. From the 13th–14th c. its teachers divided into a northern (Vaṭakalai) and a southern (Teṅkalai) school over grace, surrender and Śrī's status.

## Distinctive positions
- Brahman is one, but qualified (viśiṣṭa): the one Brahman has sentient souls (cit) and insentient matter (acit) as its inseparable modes (prakāra).
- The world and souls are the body (śarīra) of Brahman; Brahman is their self (śarīrin), inner controller and support; the relation is inseparable existence (apṛthak-siddhi).
- Three realities (tattvatraya): cit, acit and Īśvara, all real and eternal; the world is real, not an appearance.
- Brahman is Nārāyaṇa, Lord of Śrī, free of every defect and possessed of infinite auspicious qualities (ubhaya-liṅga); 'nirguṇa' texts deny only defective (prākṛta) qualities.
- Co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya): identity statements such as 'tat tvam asi' refer to one Brahman under two modes, never to an attributeless identity.
- Every cognition is of the real (satkhyāti); Advaita's avidyā is untenable in seven ways.
- The self is an atomic, eternal knower, agent and enjoyer, essentially the servant (śeṣa) of the Lord; consciousness is its attribute (dharmabhūta-jñāna).
- Liberation is attained by bhakti (loving meditation, dhruvānusmṛti) or by prapatti (surrender), through the Lord's grace; there is no liberation while living.
- The liberated soul reaches Vaikuṇṭha by the path of light, keeps its individuality, shares the Lord's bliss and serves him eternally (kainkarya).
- Ubhaya Vedānta: the Sanskrit Veda and the Tamil hymns of the Āḻvārs are of equal authority.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Brahman, Nārāyaṇa, Śrīman Nārāyaṇa / Śriyaḥpati (Lord of Śrī), Puruṣottama, Bhagavān, Īśvara, Vāsudeva (para form)
**Descriptions:** the one Brahman qualified by sentient and insentient beings as its body (cid-acid-viśiṣṭa); opposed to all that is evil (akhila-heya-pratyanīka) and the sole abode of infinite auspicious qualities (kalyāṇaika-tāna) — the twofold mark (ubhaya-liṅga); satyam jñānam anantam: real, knowledge, infinite — each word naming a quality of one Brahman, not an attributeless essence; material and efficient cause of the world: in pralaya Brahman has souls and matter as a subtle body; in creation, as a gross body; present in five forms: para, vyūha, vibhava, antaryāmin, arcā; never apart from Śrī (Lakṣmī)
**Negations:** 'neti neti' (BĀU 2.3.6) denies only the limit 'so much' of what was described, not Brahman's qualities; 'nirguṇa' denies the defective qualities born of prakṛti, not the auspicious qualities; Brahman is not touched by the changes and imperfections of its body
**Relation to self:** The individual self (jīva) is a real, eternal, atomic knower that is a mode (prakāra) and body of Brahman, its servant (śeṣa), wholly dependent (paratantra), never identical with it and never lost in it, even in liberation.
**Relation to world:** The world is real; it is the body of Brahman, transformed from subtle to gross in creation while Brahman as its self remains unchanged and unaffected.
**Caveat:** The tradition affirms one ultimate but rejects any reading of 'one truth' as an attributeless or merely impersonal reality: the one is the personal Nārāyaṇa with Śrī, and souls and world remain real and distinct as his body. It rejects Advaita's identity of self and Brahman and the unreality of the world, and would reject a reconciliation that makes Nārāyaṇa one name among equals for a nameless absolute.
