The Pure Land tradition teaches reliance on the buddha Amitābha and aspiration to be born in his land Sukhāvatī, where awakening is assured. Grounded in the Larger and Smaller Sukhāvatīvyūha and the Contemplation Sūtra, in Nāgārjuna's 'easy practice' and Vasubandhu's Rebirth Treatise, it became a self-conscious tradition in China through Tanluan (other-power), Daochuo (the last age; the two gates) and Shandao (the calling of the name as the rightly determined act), was later counted as a lineage of patriarchs beginning with Huiyuan of Lushan, merged with Chan and Tiantai practice in the Song–Ming, and in Japan passed through Genshin and the hijiri to the independent schools of Hōnen (Jōdo-shū), Shinran (Jōdo Shinshū), Ippen (Ji-shū) and Ryōnin (Yūzū Nenbutsu).

## Distinctive positions
- Amitābha (Immeasurable Light, Immeasurable Life), who as the monk Dharmākara made vows, presides now over Sukhāvatī in the west; beings born there never fall back and attain awakening.
- Birth (wangsheng / ōjō) in the Pure Land is the condition for completing the path: awakening is finished there, not here.
- Practice centres on recollecting (nian) Amitābha — contemplating his body and land, and above all calling his name (chengming) — with faith and the aspiration to be born.
- Nāgārjuna's easy path versus difficult path; Daochuo's two gates (the holy path and the Pure Land) matched to the last age of the Dharma.
- Other-power (tali/tariki): birth is accomplished by the power of Amitābha's vow, not by one's own power (Tanluan), with differing degrees of emphasis in the later schools.
- Even an evil-doer who calls the name ten times at death is born in the lowest grade (Contemplation Sūtra); the eighteenth vow excludes the five grave offenders and slanderers of the Dharma — reconciled by Shandao as restraint rather than rejection.
- Later Chinese Pure Land joins recitation with Chan (dual cultivation) and reads the Pure Land also as mind-only while insisting on real birth in the West (Yanshou, Zhuhong, Ouyi).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Amitābha / Amitāyus (Immeasurable Light, Immeasurable Life), Sukhāvatī, the Pure Land of Peace and Bliss (Anle, Anyang, Jile), the uncreated dharma-body of true wisdom (zhenshi zhihui wuwei fashen), dharma-nature (faxing)
**Descriptions:** The three adornments of the land, the Buddha and the bodhisattvas 'enter the one dharma-phrase': the pure phrase, which is the uncreated dharma-body of true wisdom (Rebirth Treatise, T1524 232b22-25).; The Pure Land surpasses the paths of the three realms, is ultimately like space, vast and boundless (T1524 230c21-22); it is not included in the three realms (Tanluan, Daochuo).; Amitābha now present is a reward-body buddha and his land a reward land (Daochuo, Anle ji 5c; Shandao).; The dharma-body of dharma-nature gives rise to the dharma-body of expedient means and the latter manifests the former; different but inseparable, one but not identical (Tanluan's Commentary).
**Negations:** Birth there is 'the birth of non-birth', not the deluded birth of the three existences (T1958 11c11-14).; The Buddha seen in samādhi neither comes nor goes; mind makes the buddha (Pratyutpanna; Contemplation Sūtra 8).
**Relation to self:** Beings are ordinary foolish beings bound by passions (fanfu) who cannot reach awakening by their own power in this age; they are embraced by the Buddha's light and vow; in the Chan-inflected reading, 'this mind is the Buddha' and Amitābha is the Buddha of one's own nature, but birth in the West is still to be sought.
**Relation to world:** This Sahā world of five turbidities is to be loathed and left; Sukhāvatī, created by Dharmākara's vows, lies west beyond ten trillion buddha-lands; for the mind-only reading the pure land is where the mind is pure, without denying the Western land.
**Caveat:** Pure Land teachers insist on the distinctness of Amitābha and his land from the practitioner ('pointing to a direction and establishing form') and would reject a reading that dissolves the Western land into a symbol of one truth; they accept the Mahāyāna identity of all buddhas in dharma-nature but hold that one must rely on this Buddha's vow. The equation of Amitābha with a single ultimate is therefore made only in the interpretation layer and only under P1-level.
