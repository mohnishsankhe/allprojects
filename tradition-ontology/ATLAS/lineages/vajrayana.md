The esoteric Mahāyāna of India that teaches the swift path to buddhahood by mantra, deity yoga in the maṇḍala, consecration by a vajra master and the yogas of the subtle body, codified in tantras revealed (by the tradition's account) by the Buddha as Vajradhara and, historically, composed c. 6th–11th c. and taught at Nālandā, Vikramaśīla and among the wandering siddhas; transmitted to Tibet, Nepal and East Asia.

## Distinctive positions
- Buddhahood can be attained in this very life and body (Pañcakrama 3.12; the 'swift path'), by the Mahāyāna view joined with special methods (deity yoga, mantra, the subtle body).
- The mantra method is superior to the perfection method in being unconfused, rich in means, not difficult and for those of sharp faculties (Tripiṭakamala's Nayatrayapradīpa, the classic Indian statement).
- Entry requires consecration (abhiṣeka) from a qualified vajra master and keeping the pledges (samaya); the teacher is to be regarded as inseparable from Vajradhara (Gurupañcāśikā 22).
- The path has two stages: generation (utpattikrama) — the practitioner arises as the deity in the maṇḍala — and completion (utpanna-/niṣpannakrama) — work with channels, winds and drops toward clear light and union.
- The passions are not only abandoned but used as the path ('poison as medicine'); desire in particular is transformed into great bliss (mahāsukha) joined with emptiness.
- The goal is the union (yuganaddha) of clear light and illusory body, or of immutable bliss and empty form (Kālacakra), the non-duality of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa.
- Tantric statements are coded ('intentional language', sandhyābhāṣā) and must be read with the explanatory tantras and the guru's instruction (the six alternatives, ṣaṭkoṭi).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Vajradhara / Vajrasattva (the sixth buddha, lord of all families), mahāsukha (great bliss), prabhāsvara (clear light), yuganaddha (union), advayajñāna (non-dual gnosis), bodhicitta (ultimate: the union of emptiness and compassion), mahāmudrā (the great seal), sahaja (the innate), Ādibuddha (the primordial buddha)
**Descriptions:** the non-duality of emptiness (prajñā) and compassion/means (upāya), 'the union of the two'; bodhicitta 'without beginning or end, beyond existence and non-existence' (Guhyasamāja ch. 2); clear light, the fourth or 'all-empty' (Pañcakrama 2.5); without beginning or end, the primordial buddha, gnosis as its sole eye (Nāmasaṃgīti 100)
**Negations:** neither being nor non-being; 'in the absence of being there is no meditation' (Guhyasamāja 2.3); unborn, not an object of thought or speech (the dohās)
**Relation to self:** The deity one arises as is the practitioner's own purified nature ('I am Vajrasattva', vajrātmako 'ham, Tattvasaṃgraha); this 'divine pride' rests on emptiness and is not an eternal self.
**Relation to world:** Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are not two (Pañcakrama 5.2); the world is seen as maṇḍala and deity (pure vision); ordinary appearances are the illusory display of the deity.
**Caveat:** The Vajrayāna speaks of one non-dual gnosis (and personifies it as Vajradhara or the Ādibuddha), but always as empty of own-being; it rejects any reading of this as a permanent self or creator (Hevajra and Kālacakra commentators explicitly deny that the Ādibuddha is an Īśvara).
