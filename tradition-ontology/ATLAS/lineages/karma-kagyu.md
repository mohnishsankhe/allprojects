The school founded by the first Karmapa, Düsum Khyenpa, a principal disciple of Gampopa, and headed by the successive Karmapas; the largest Kagyu school, centred on Tsurphu and on the great monasteries of Kham, and in exile on Rumtek. Its Mahāmudrā system is that of the 3rd, 8th and 9th Karmapas; its later renaissance came through the 8th Situ and Jamgön Kongtrul.

## Distinctive positions
- The Karmapa, recognized in successive rebirths since Karma Pakshi (the first recognized tulku line by the tradition's account), heads the school; the black crown is his emblem.
- Mahāmudrā is taught in the system of the 9th Karmapa's three manuals (Ocean of Definitive Meaning, Dispelling the Darkness of Ignorance, Pointing Out the Dharmakāya): preliminaries, śamatha, vipaśyanā, pointing-out, and the four yogas.
- Rangjung Dorje's teaching on the nature of mind: buddha-nature is mind's luminous clarity-emptiness; the eight consciousnesses are distinguished from the five wisdoms into which they are transformed.
- Mahāmudrā and Dzogchen are of one essence (Rangjung Dorje; Karma Chagmé's union of the two).
- Within the school the Madhyamaka of the 8th Karmapa (the Chariot of the Dakpo Kagyu Siddhas) and the shentong reading of the 3rd Karmapa's buddha-nature teaching as understood by the 8th Situ and Jamgön Kongtrul coexist (not yet reconciled; see dsp:rangtong-shentong).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Mahāmudrā, buddha-nature / the sugatagarbha, mind's nature, clarity-emptiness (gsal stong), dharmadhātu wisdom
**Descriptions:** The ground of purification is mind itself, clarity and emptiness inseparable; the purifier is the vajra yoga of Mahāmudrā; the purified are the adventitious stains; the fruit is the stainless dharmakāya (3rd Karmapa).; The eight consciousnesses, recognized, are the five wisdoms (Distinguishing Consciousness and Wisdom).
**Negations:** neither existent nor non-existent; free of the extremes
**Relation to self:** Buddha-nature is the beginningless element present in every being, obscured by adventitious stains.
**Relation to world:** Appearances are mind; mind is empty; emptiness is not a blank but luminous.
**Caveat:** The school holds two readings of how buddha-nature is empty — as empty of own-nature (the 8th Karmapa's Madhyamaka) or as empty only of what is other than it (the shentong reading of the 8th Situ and Jamgön Kongtrul) — not yet reconciled (dsp:rangtong-shentong).
