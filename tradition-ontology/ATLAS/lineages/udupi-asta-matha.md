# The eight maṭhas of Uḍupi (aṣṭa-maṭha)

`lin:udupi-asta-matha` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Aṣṭa-maṭha, the Uḍupi maṭhas
**Parent:** [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](dvaita.md)
**Founders:** `tch:madhva`
**Key teachers:** `tch:hrsikesa-tirtha`, `tch:narasimha-tirtha-adamaru`, `tch:janardana-tirtha`, `tch:upendra-tirtha`, `tch:vamana-tirtha`, `tch:visnu-tirtha`, `tch:rama-tirtha-kaniyur`, `tch:adhoksaja-tirtha`, `tch:vadiraja-tirtha`, `tch:vijayadhvaja-tirtha`, `tch:visvesa-tirtha`
**Regions:** coastal Karnataka (Uḍupi)
**Status:** living

Eight monasteries founded, in the tradition's account, by Madhva for eight of his ascetic disciples, who worship in turn the image of Kṛṣṇa that Madhva installed at Uḍupi: Palimaru (Hṛṣīkeśa Tīrtha), Adamaru (Narasiṃha Tīrtha), Krishnapura (Janārdana Tīrtha), Puttige (Upendra Tīrtha), Shirur (Vāmana Tīrtha), Sode (Viṣṇu Tīrtha, Madhva's brother), Kaniyooru (Rāma Tīrtha) and Pejavara (Adhokṣaja Tīrtha). The worship rotates among them (paryāya); the tradition credits Vādirāja Tīrtha of Sode with changing the turn from two months to two years (from 1522).

## Distinctive positions
- Shares the Dvaita doctrine of lin:dvaita without doctrinal difference; distinct as an institutional line of ascetic pontiffs (guru-paramparā), with its own images, brindāvanas and observances.
