The third Japanese Zen denomination, founded by the Chinese Linji master Yinyuan Longqi (Ingen, 1592–1673), which preserves Ming Chinese monastic forms and combines Zen with Pure Land recitation.

## Distinctive positions
- Ming-style monastic observance: Chinese-pronounced sūtra chanting, Ming precepts platform and rules (Ōbaku shingi).
- Combination of kōan meditation with recitation of Amitābha's name (nenbutsu Zen), which Hakuin's Rinzai line criticised.
- Printing of the complete canon (Tetsugen edition) as Dharma work.

**Transmissions received:** 
  - [Linji house](linji.md) — what: Ming Linji lineage of Miyun Yuanwu and Feiyin Tongrong at Wanfusi, Mount Huangbo (Fujian); evidence: tradition records

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** buddha-mind (busshin), Amitābha as one's own mind (yuishin no Mida)
**Descriptions:** The Pure Land is only mind and Amitābha is one's own nature (the Ming Chan–Pure Land formula followed by Ōbaku).
**Relation to self:** Amitābha recited and the reciter are not two.
**Relation to world:** The Pure Land is not apart from the purified mind.
**Caveat:** Ōbaku holds recitation and Zen to be one; Hakuin's Rinzai criticised this mixture. Low confidence on a specific Ōbaku text stating this.
