The image-worshipping majority of the Śvetāmbaras, who accept forty-five Āgamas with their commentaries, worship Jina images in temples and are organized in monastic lineages called gacchas; the Tapā Gaccha is today the largest.

## Distinctive positions
- Accepts a canon of forty-five Āgamas (11 Aṅgas, 12 Upāṅgas, 10 Prakīrṇakas, 6 Chedasūtras, 4 Mūlasūtras, 2 Cūlikāsūtras) together with the niryukti, bhāṣya, cūrṇi and ṭīkā layers
- Worship of consecrated images of the Jinas (Jina-pūjā) in temples as a support for the inner state; material worship (dravya-pūjā) is for householders, inner worship (bhāva-pūjā) for all
- Pilgrimage to the tīrthas (Śatruñjaya, Girnār, Ābu, Sammeda Śikhara) and consecration rites re-enacting the five kalyāṇakas
- Organized in gacchas (Tapā, Kharatara, Añcala and others) that differ in ritual details and calendars


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** the Jina (worshipped in his image, sthāpanā-jina), Siddha
**Descriptions:** the liberated Jina, present to the worshipper through the consecrated image as a support for recalling his qualities; the model of the worshipper's own purified self
**Negations:** the Jina does not receive offerings or grant favours; the image is not itself divine apart from what it represents
**Relation to self:** Worship of the Jina's qualities purifies the worshipper toward the same state.
**Relation to world:** Real and uncreated; the images and temples are supports within it.
**Caveat:** The Jain tradition denies a single all-encompassing reality and a creator: liberated souls are infinitely many and remain distinct, and the universe of six substances is uncreated. It can accept 'one truth, many names' only in its own way — as many-sidedness (anekānta) about a plural reality — not as the identity of all souls in one substance. Image worship here is devotion to the qualities of a liberated soul, not to a creator-god.
