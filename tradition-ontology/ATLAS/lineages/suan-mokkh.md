The reformist Thai tradition of Buddhadāsa Bhikkhu (1906–1993) and his forest monastery Suan Mokkh, teaching emptiness of self, present-moment dependent origination and the sixteen-step mindfulness with breathing.

## Distinctive positions
- Return to the Buddha's words (the suttas) over later commentarial layers; the essential teaching is the emptying of 'I' and 'mine' (tua-ku khong-ku).
- Dependent origination describes the arising of suffering and of the 'self' within a single sequence of sense-contact in present experience, not only across three lives.
- 'Birth' in its Dhamma-language sense is the birth of the ego-sense; nibbāna is the coolness of the extinction of defilements here and now.
- Everyday language and Dhamma language (phasa khon, phasa tham) must be distinguished.
- Mindfulness with breathing in its sixteen steps as a complete path; work done with a mind void of self (chit wang).


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** nibbāna (coolness), suññatā (voidness)
**Descriptions:** the coolness of the extinction of defilements, available here and now, momentarily (tadaṅga-nibbāna) and finally; the mind void of 'I' and 'mine' (chit wang)
**Relation to self:** nibbāna is the complete absence of the illusion of 'I' and 'mine'
**Relation to world:** not a place or a heaven after death; found in the midst of life
**Caveat:** Buddhadāsa described Dhamma, God and nature in parallel terms in dialogue with other religions, but denied a personal creator; the one-truth reading is a reconciliation he partly invited.
