# Śvetāmbara

`lin:svetambara` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Family:** ascetic
**Alternate names:** the white-clad (seyambara), Śvetapaṭa (in Digambara sources)
**Parent:** [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](jainism.md)
**Sub-lineages:** [Śvetāmbara Mūrtipūjaka](murtipujaka.md), [Sthānakavāsī](sthanakavasi.md), [Śvetāmbara Terāpanth](terapanthi.md)
**Key teachers:** `tch:sudharman`, `tch:jambu`, `tch:bhadrabahu`, `tch:sthulabhadra`, `tch:suhastin`, `tch:aryaraksita`, `tch:skandila`, `tch:devarddhigani`, `tch:jinabhadra`, `tch:haribhadra`, `tch:silanka`, `tch:abhayadeva`, `tch:malayagiri`, [Hemacandra](../teachers/hemacandra.md), `tch:yasovijaya`
**Regions:** Gujarat, Rajasthan, Mumbai and Maharashtra, Punjab (Sthānakavāsī), diaspora
**Dates:** Tradition's account: the continuous line of Sudharman; the canon redacted at the Valabhī council under Devarddhigaṇi (980 or 993 years after Mahāvīra's nirvāṇa); Scholarly account: a distinct Śvetāmbara identity is visible from roughly the early centuries CE (Mathurā inscriptions name gaṇas and kulas matching the Kalpa Sūtra's list); the canon was fixed in writing in the 5th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The 'white-clad' branch of the Jain tradition, which preserves the Ardhamāgadhī canon of Āgamas redacted at Valabhī and holds that women can reach liberation and that the omniscient eats. It comprises the image-worshipping Mūrtipūjakas (organized in gacchas) and the two non-image-worshipping orders, the Sthānakavāsīs and the Terāpanthīs.

## Distinctive positions
- Monks and nuns wear white garments and carry bowls; holding clothes and bowls without attachment is not 'possession' (parigraha is defined as attachment, mūrcchā)
- The naked, solitary Jinakalpa mode was followed by the Jinas and early monks but lapsed after Jambū; the Sthavirakalpa (living in groups with minimal equipment) is the valid mode now
- Women can attain liberation in a female body; the nineteenth Tīrthaṅkara Mallī was a woman
- The omniscient (kevalin) continues to take food while his body lasts
- The canon of Āgamas survives in large part (the twelfth Aṅga, the Dṛṣṭivāda with the fourteen Pūrvas, is lost)
- In Mahāvīra's life: the transfer of the embryo from the brāhmaṇī Devānandā to the kṣatriyāṇī Triśalā; fourteen dreams of the mother; Mahāvīra's marriage to Yaśodā
