The Haridāsī or Sakhī sampradāya of Vṛndāvana, founded by the singer-saint Svāmī Haridās (16th c.), whose songs brought forth the image of Bāṅke Bihārī. It contemplates the ever-united divine pair in the inner grove as their sakhī and treats music as worship.

## Distinctive positions
- Worship in the grove (nikuñja-upāsanā): the natural, eternal pair (sahaja jorī) Kuñjabihārī and Śyāmā play for ever in the inner grove, beyond birth, avatāra and separation.
- The devotee witnesses and serves as a sakhī; Svāmī Haridās is held to be the sakhī Lalitā.
- Singing (dhrupad, samāj-gāyan) is itself worship.
- Two branches: householder gosvāmīs serving Bāṅke Bihārī, and strict renunciants (virakta) of the Ṭaṭṭī Sthān.
- Its relation to the Nimbārka sampradāya is disputed (see dsp:haridasi-nimbarka-affiliation).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Kuñjabihārī and Śyāmā, the natural pair (sahaja jorī), Bāṅke Bihārī
**Descriptions:** the pair eternally together in the grove, never joined and never parted
**Negations:** not born, not separated, not newly united
**Relation to self:** The devotee is a sakhī who serves and witnesses.
**Relation to world:** The world's pursuits are empty; death is ever near; live as Hari keeps you.
**Caveat:** The pair is held to be one reality in two forms; the tradition would reject an impersonal reading.
