The line of Madhyamaka that, following Bhāviveka's criticism of Buddhapālita, holds that emptiness must be established by autonomous formal inferences in Dignāga's manner, qualifying its negations as 'ultimate', and that admits conventional arising as it appears. Tibetan doxographers divided it into the Sautrāntika-Svātantrika (Bhāviveka, Jñānagarbha, Śrīgupta) and the Yogācāra-Svātantrika (Śāntarakṣita, Kamalaśīla, Haribhadra). Its Indian authors did not use the name.

## Distinctive positions
- A Mādhyamika must be able to state an autonomous inference (svatantra-anumāna) with thesis, reason and example; Buddhapālita's bare consequences are faulty (Bhāviveka, Prajñāpradīpa ch.1).
- Negations of arising and the like are qualified 'ultimately' (paramārthataḥ); conventionally things arise as they appear.
- Conventional truth is divided into the correct conventional (tathya-saṃvṛti), capable of efficacy as it appears, and the incorrect (mithyā-saṃvṛti) (Jñānagarbha).
- The ultimate is divided into the nominal or concordant ultimate (paryāya-paramārtha), the object of reasoning, and the non-nominal ultimate beyond elaboration (Bhāviveka).
- Bhāviveka accepts external objects conventionally (hence 'Sautrāntika-Svātantrika' in Tibetan doxography) and criticizes Yogācāra, Sāṃkhya, Vaiśeṣika, Vedānta and Mīmāṃsā in the Madhyamakahṛdaya.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** paramārtha (the ultimate), as object of the highest wisdom, paryāya-paramārtha (the nominal / concordant ultimate), aparyāya-paramārtha (the non-nominal ultimate), śūnyatā
**Descriptions:** what is known by non-conceptual wisdom, beyond all elaboration (the non-nominal ultimate); the negation of arising etc. established by reasoning, which accords with the ultimate (the nominal ultimate) (Madhyamakahṛdaya 3; Tarkajvālā); that which is found by reasoning that does not deceive (Jñānagarbha, Satyadvayavibhaṅga)
**Negations:** the earth-element etc. are ultimately not of the nature of the elements, because they are produced, like cognition (Bhāviveka's autonomous inference, Prajñāpradīpa); no arising ultimately, from self or other (Prajñāpradīpa ch.1)
**Relation to self:** The person is a designation on the aggregates; Bhāviveka identifies the conventional referent of 'I' with consciousness (mental consciousness) — a view Candrakīrti rejects.
**Relation to world:** Conventionally things arise and function as they appear (the correct conventional); only ultimately are they unborn.
**Caveat:** Emptiness is a negation, not an entity; the Svātantrika authors (who did not call themselves so) would reject treating it as a substantial ground.
