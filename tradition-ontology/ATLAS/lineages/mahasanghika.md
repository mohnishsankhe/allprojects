The Mahāsāṃghika ('those of the great community') formed one side of the first schism of the Buddhist order, the other being the Sthaviras. It divided into several branches (Ekavyavahārika, Lokottaravāda, Kaukkuṭika/Gokulika, Bahuśrutīya, Prajñaptivāda, Caitika and the Andhra Śaila schools). Its Vinaya survives in Chinese and its Lokottaravāda branch's Mahāvastu in Sanskrit; its doctrines are known largely through Vasumitra's doxography.

## Distinctive positions
- The Buddhas are supramundane (lokottara); their bodies, powers and lifespans are without limit; the Buddha is always in concentration, never sleeps, and teaches all dharmas with a single sound (Vasumitra).
- The mind is by nature luminous; it is defiled by adventitious defilements (Vasumitra).
- Past and future do not exist as entities (Vasumitra).
- There is no intermediate existence between death and rebirth (Vasumitra; Kathāvatthu 8.2 attributes the contrary view to others).
- Stream-enterers can fall back; arhats cannot (Vasumitra).
- Nine unconditioned dharmas (Vasumitra: the two cessations, space, the four formless spheres, the nature of dependent origination and of the noble path) — per Vasumitra, low confidence on the list.
- The five points of Mahādeva on the imperfections of the arhat were accepted by the Mahāsāṃghika side according to the Sarvāstivāda accounts.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** nirvāṇa, the supramundane Tathāgata
**Descriptions:** The Buddhas are supramundane, without outflows, limitless in body, power and life, always in concentration (Vasumitra).; The mind is by nature luminous, defiled by adventitious defilements (Vasumitra).
**Negations:** Past and future are not real entities (Vasumitra).
**Relation to self:** No self is affirmed; the luminous nature of mind is not called a self.
**Relation to world:** The Buddha only appears to live in the world; nine unconditioned dharmas are counted (low confidence).
**Caveat:** Known almost only through opponents' and later doxographies; the Mahāsāṃghika does not identify the luminous mind or the Buddha with a cosmic single reality.
