The Śākta tradition of the Goddess Lalitā Tripurasundarī worshipped through the Śrīcakra and the Śrīvidyā mantra. Rooted in the Vāmakeśvara tantra (Nityāṣoḍaśikārṇava and Yoginīhṛdaya), the Tantrarāja and the Lalitā Sahasranāma, it was systematized in Kashmir and South India, and has Samaya (internal) and Kaula branches. Its metaphysics is the non-dual union of Śiva-light and Śakti-awareness; its aim is liberation while living together with worldly well-being (saubhāgya).

## Distinctive positions
- The supreme is Lalitā Mahātripurasundarī, the union (sāmarasya) of Śiva as light (prakāśa) and Śakti as self-awareness (vimarśa); she sits on Kāmeśvara's lap and is 'the form of the oneness of Śiva and Śakti' (Lalitā Sahasranāma 183).
- Worship centres on the Śrīcakra, the 'king of cakras', with nine enclosures (āvaraṇa) from the outer square to the central point (bindu), and on the fifteen-syllable Śrīvidyā mantra (with its sixteen-syllable form) in three sections (kūṭa) that are the Goddess's subtle body.
- The vidyā has twelve forms after its twelve great devotees; the Kādi and Hādi forms are the living ones.
- Knowledge of the unity of cakra, mantra, deity, guru and one's own self is liberation (Yoginīhṛdaya; Paraśurāma Kalpasūtra 1.11); self-awareness (svavimarśa) is the goal of life (PKS 1.6).
- Worship is threefold: outer worship of the cakra (aparā), meditative worship (parāparā) and abiding in non-duality (parā) (Yoginīhṛdaya 3.2-3).
- The body itself is the Śrīcakra; contemplating the non-difference of knower, knowing and known is the worship of the Śrīcakra and makes one liberated while living (Bhāvanā Upaniṣad).
- Two modes: the internal Samaya way and the Kaula way; the Goddess is called both 'intent on Samaya conduct' and 'Kaulinī' (Lalitā Sahasranāma 36-37).
- Strict initiation (three dīkṣās), guru-lineage (the three streams of gurus), initiatory names ending in '-ānandanātha', and secrecy.
- A Vedic self-understanding: the Gāyatrī is read as Śrīvidyā; the Tripurā, Tripurātāpinī, Bhāvanā and Bahvṛca Upaniṣads are its śruti.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Lalitā, Mahātripurasundarī, Tripurā, Kāmeśvarī, Rājarājeśvarī, Parābhaṭṭārikā, Śrīvidyā
**Descriptions:** Tripurā is the supreme, whose nature is the fusion (sāmarasya) of light (prakāśa) and self-awareness (vimarśa) (Amṛtānanda on Yoginīhṛdaya 1.6).; Maheśa, whose body is pure light, holds self-awareness within; the primal Śakti is the stainless mirror in which Śiva knows himself; their union is the great point (mahābindu) and the 'I' (Kāmakalāvilāsa 1-5).; She is consciousness-power (citśakti) and also the power of the insentient (Lalitā Sahasranāma 90).; She is 'the form of the oneness of Śiva and Śakti' and dwells in the king of cakras (Lalitā Sahasranāma 182-183).; When the supreme Śakti, by her own will, beholds her own vibrancy, the cakra (the universe) arises (Yoginīhṛdaya 1.9-10).
**Negations:** nirguṇā, niṣkalā, nirupādhi — beyond qualities, parts and limiting adjuncts (Lalitā Sahasranāma 43-45); beyond the reach of mind and speech (manovācām agocarā, Lalitā Sahasranāma 89)
**Relation to self:** The Goddess is one's own self: the jīva is Śiva clothed in coverings, Paraśiva is the uncovered (PKS 1.5); worship is the contemplation of the non-difference of knower, knowing and known (Bhāvanā Upaniṣad).
**Relation to world:** The universe of thirty-six principles is the Śrīcakra, her self-expansion; she herself becomes mind, space, air, fire, water and earth, and nothing lies beyond her (Saundaryalaharī 35).
**Caveat:** Śrīvidyā holds that the Goddess in union with Śiva is the one ultimate, and treats the one-truth principle as its own (she is worshipped by all the gods). It would reject a reading that makes Lalitā one symbol among equals or that denies the reality of her power. Within the tradition the Samaya school insists on the strict equality of Śiva and Śakti, while uttara-Kaula readings hold Śakti alone; Bhāskararāya's gloss 'substratum of the false world' (name 735) leans to Advaita, while the Kāmakalāvilāsa speaks of real manifestation — these differences are recorded, not erased.
