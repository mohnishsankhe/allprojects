The Rādhāvallabha tradition of Vṛndāvana, founded by Hita Harivaṃśa in the 16th c., worships Rādhā as supreme and Kṛṣṇa as her beloved, contemplating their eternal, unbroken love-play in the groves. Its scripture is Harivaṃśa's Braj Hita Caurāsī (and, by its account, the Sanskrit Rādhā-sudhānidhi); love (hita) is its first principle and sakhī-like service its practice.

## Distinctive positions
- Rādhā is supreme: Kṛṣṇa is 'Rādhā's beloved' (Rādhāvallabha) and her devotee; the Rādhāvallabha temple has no image of Rādhā, her presence being marked by a seat (gaddī) beside Kṛṣṇa.
- Hita — love — is the ultimate principle; the divine pair are its two forms.
- The eternal love-play (nitya-vihāra) in the groves has no separation, no birth-story and no departure to Mathurā or Dvārakā; separation is felt only within union.
- The devotee serves as a sakhī (companion), finding joy in the pair's joy (tatsukha), never seeking union for herself.
- Rules of injunction and prohibition (vidhi-niṣedha) are secondary to love; liberation is not sought.
- Hita Harivaṃśa received his mantra from Rādhā herself, so the sampradāya counts itself outside the four classical Vaiṣṇava sampradāyas.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Hita (love), Śrī Rādhā (Śyāmā), Rādhāvallabha (Kṛṣṇa, Rādhā's beloved)
**Descriptions:** love (hita) itself, appearing as the pair in eternal play; Rādhā, whose favour even Madhusūdana seeks
**Negations:** no separation, birth-story or departure in the eternal play
**Relation to self:** The soul's true identity is as a sakhī who delights in the pair's joy.
**Relation to world:** The world is not the focus; the eternal Vṛndāvana is the reality contemplated.
**Caveat:** The tradition rejects making Rādhā a power of Kṛṣṇa or reducing the pair's love to an impersonal absolute; love, not Brahman, is its first term.
