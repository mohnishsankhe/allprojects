The Pāñcarātra is the Vaiṣṇava tantric tradition of revealed saṃhitās (traditionally 108) teaching the theology of Bhagavān with six qualities and his five modes, Lakṣmī as his Śakti, the pure and impure creations, initiation, temple building, image installation, daily worship in five periods, mantra, yoga and surrender. It governs worship in major South Indian Viṣṇu temples (e.g. Śrīraṅgam, Melkote, Kāñcī) and was defended as scripture by Yāmuna, Rāmānuja and Vedānta Deśika.

## Distinctive positions
- The Pāñcarātra (Bhagavacchāstra) was spoken by Bhagavān himself and is therefore authoritative (Āgamaprāmāṇya).
- The supreme Brahman is the ṣāḍguṇya: knowledge, power (śakti), lordship, strength, virility and splendour; knowledge is its very form, the other five are its aspects (Ahirbudhnya 2.53–62).
- God exists as para, vyūha (Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha), vibhava (39 descents in the Ahirbudhnya), antaryāmin and arcā (the consecrated image).
- Saṅkarṣaṇa manifests knowledge and strength, Pradyumna lordship and virility, Aniruddha power and splendour; each vyūha still possesses all six (Ahirbudhnya 5.16–19).
- Śrī/Lakṣmī is Nārāyaṇa's eternal Śakti, performing the five acts: concealment, creation, maintenance, dissolution and grace (Lakṣmī Tantra 12.12–14).
- Creation is pure (śuddha: the vyūhas and their realm) and other-than-pure (śuddhetara, from prakṛti); the Jayākhya distinguishes Brahmā's, primordial-matter's and the pure creation (JS 2–4).
- Grace (anugraha), called śaktipāta, brings the soul's karmas into equilibrium (karmasāmya) and lifts it from saṃsāra (Ahirbudhnya 14.28–34).
- Surrender (śaraṇāgati/nyāsa) with six limbs attains what other means cannot (Ahirbudhnya 37.25–29; Lakṣmī Tantra 17.56–62).
- The Pāñcarātrin's day has five periods (pañcakāla): approach (abhigamana), gathering (upādāna), worship (ijyā), study (svādhyāya) and meditation (yoga).
- The Pāñcarātra is fourfold by siddhānta: Mantra-, Āgama-, Tantra- and Tantrāntara-siddhānta (Pādma jñānapāda 1.76–82).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Bhagavān, Vāsudeva, Nārāyaṇa, Para Brahman as ṣāḍguṇya, Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa (Lakṣmī Tantra)
**Descriptions:** The ṣāḍguṇya — possessor of the six qualities — is the supreme Brahman, cause of all causes, free of all pairs of opposites and all limiting adjuncts (Ahirbudhnya 2.53).; Knowledge is the very form of Brahman; power, lordship, strength, virility and splendour are aspects of that knowledge (Ahirbudhnya 2.56–62).; Śrī/Lakṣmī is his eternal Śakti, inseparable from him, who performs his five acts (Lakṣmī Tantra 12.12–14).
**Negations:** He is called 'without qualities' (nirguṇa) because he is untouched by the qualities of prakṛti (Ahirbudhnya 2.55).
**Relation to self:** Individual souls depend on Bhagavān; by his grace (śaktipāta) their karmas are equalised and they are lifted from saṃsāra to his presence.
**Relation to world:** The world issues from his Śakti in a pure creation (the vyūhas) and an other-than-pure creation from prakṛti.
**Caveat:** Saṃhitās differ in emphasis (e.g. the Lakṣmī Tantra's Śakti-centred account); Śrīvaiṣṇava readers interpret the Pāñcarātra within qualified non-dualism. The tradition insists on a personal Lord and would reject reading him as an impersonal absolute.
