# Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)

`lin:mantramarga` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** mantramārga, Path of Mantras, Śaiva Mantramārga, mantra-tantra (Kāmika's name for the fifth stream)

**Regions:** Kashmir, Nepal, North and Central India, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Cambodia (Khmer inscriptions record Śaiva tantric texts)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: revealed by Śiva/Sadāśiva through his five faces at the beginning of creation and handed down through chains of divine and semi-divine transmitters (tantrāvatāra; Kāmika pūrva 1; Kiraṇa vidyāpāda 10); Scholarly account: earliest surviving scripture (core of the Niśvāsatattvasaṃhitā) c. 450–550 CE; most major scriptures c. 6th–10th c.; systematic exegesis 7th–12th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The Mantramārga is the initiatory, mantra-based division of Śaivism, distinguished in its own scriptures (Niśvāsa, Kāmika) from the ascetic Atimārga. It comprises the dualist Śaiva Siddhānta with its 28 scriptures and their upāgamas, and the non-Saiddhāntika tantras: the Bhairava tantras (Mantrapīṭha and Vidyāpīṭha), the Vāma tantras of Tumburu, and the Gāruḍa and Bhūta tantras. It teaches initiation, daily and temple worship, mantra-practice for powers, and yoga, as means to both enjoyment and liberation.

## Distinctive positions
- Scripture is revealed by Sadāśiva through five faces as five streams: Siddhānta (upper face), Gāruḍa (east), Vāma (north), Bhūta (west) and Bhairava (south) (Kāmika pūrva 1.20–27).
- Knowledge is twofold by eligibility: Śiva's knowledge (śivajñāna), revealing the Lord, is the higher; the Veda and the rest, revealing the bound soul and its bonds, are the lower (Kāmika pūrva 1.14–17).
- Religion is graded as worldly (laukika), Vedic (vaidika), spiritual (ādhyātmika: Sāṃkhya-Yoga), the Atimārga and the Mantramārga, the last being highest (Niśvāsamukha; Kāmika pūrva 1.17–19).
- Initiation (dīkṣā) is a rite on the soul, not on caste or body; liberation comes through it (Kiraṇa 6.3–9).
- The Mantramārga offers both supernatural powers/enjoyments (bhukti) and liberation (mukti), to householders as well as ascetics; initiates are graded samayin, putraka, sādhaka and ācārya.
- The Siddhānta's 28 scriptures (10 Śivabheda, 18 Rudrabheda) and the 64 Bhairava tantras (eight groups of eight) are all Śiva's teaching; the Kashmirian exegetes rank them as teaching difference, difference-in-identity and non-difference respectively (Tantrāloka 1.18 with Jayaratha).
- Worship is performed with mantras through which the deity is present; the officiant first purifies himself, the place, the substances, the mantras and the liṅga (Kāmika pūrva 4.37–38).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Śiva, Sadāśiva (the five-faced revealer of scripture), Parameśvara, Bhairava (in the Bhairava tantras)
**Descriptions:** The Lord who reveals all scripture through five faces, as five streams (Kāmika pūrva 1.20–27).; Śiva's knowledge (śivajñāna) is the higher knowledge revealing the Lord; lower knowledges reveal the bound soul and its bonds (Kāmika pūrva 1.14–17).; In the Kashmirian reading, the Lord's teaching is one, graded as difference (Śiva tantras), difference-in-identity (Rudra tantras) and non-difference (Bhairava tantras) (Jayaratha on TĀ 1.18).
**Relation to self:** Disputed within the Mantramārga: the Siddhānta holds the soul eternally distinct, becoming equal to Śiva in liberation; the Bhairava-tantra exegetes of Kashmir hold identity.
**Relation to world:** The Lord acts on the world through his power(s) and mantras; the Siddhānta treats māyā as a real material cause distinct from Śiva, the non-dual exegetes as Śiva's self-manifestation.
**Caveat:** The Mantramārga is not doctrinally uniform. The Saiddhāntikas reject the non-dual reading of their scriptures and the identity of soul and Śiva; a one-truth reading must record this denial.
