The 20th-century continuation of Kashmir Śaivism in Swami Lakshmanjoo (1907–1991), who taught the texts of Abhinavagupta's school orally at Srinagar and whose recorded lectures and scholarly students carried it abroad.

## Distinctive positions
- The whole of Abhinavagupta's system taught as living practice: the thirty-six tattvas, the three means and the means-less (anupāya), the seven perceivers, the states of turya.
- Oral explanation of the Tantrāloka, Śiva Sūtras and Vijñāna Bhairava recorded in English and Hindi.
- Recognition of one's own nature as Śiva as the goal, with grace (śaktipāta) as its cause.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** Paramaśiva, caitanya
**Descriptions:** Consciousness is the Self (Śiva Sūtra 1.1 as he taught it); Śiva with his energy of freedom (svātantrya) manifests the universe.
**Relation to self:** The individual is Śiva who has forgotten himself; recognition restores it.
**Relation to world:** The universe is real as the manifestation of Śiva's own energy.
**Caveat:** Follows Kashmir Śaivism's rejection of the world's unreality (cf. U19's view).
