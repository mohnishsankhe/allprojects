The yoga school founded by Swami Satyananda Saraswati at Munger in 1963–64, which systematized āsana, prāṇāyāma, tantric meditation and the modern practice of yoga nidrā in widely used manuals.

## Distinctive positions
- Yoga nidrā: a systematic relaxation with a resolve (saṅkalpa), rotation of awareness through the body and visualizations, presented by Satyananda as drawn from tantric nyāsa — a modern practice distinct from the yoganidrā of the haṭha texts and Purāṇas.
- Integrated yoga of the head, heart and hands after Sivananda; kuṇḍalinī and kriyā yoga taught as a graded system.
- Awaken ājñā cakra early so that later awakenings are witnessed safely (Kundalini Tantra; recalled).

## The ultimate in this lineage

**Names:** Śiva–Śakti; consciousness and energy, the Self
**Descriptions:** Consciousness (Śiva) and energy (Śakti) unite when kuṇḍalinī reaches sahasrāra.
**Relation to self:** The awakened consciousness is one's own Self.
**Relation to world:** The world is manifest energy (Śakti).
**Caveat:** The school presents much of its teaching as practical method; its metaphysics follows tantra and Vedānta without a single fixed doctrine.
