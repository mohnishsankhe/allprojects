**Status:** absorbed

The śramaṇas (Pali/Prakrit samaṇa) were the renunciant wanderers and ascetics of the Ganges plain who stood outside the brāhmaṇa sacrificial order. Besides the Buddha and Mahāvīra, the Pali canon names six rival leaders — Pūraṇa Kassapa, Makkhali Gosāla, Ajita Kesakambalī, Pakudha Kaccāyana, Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta (Mahāvīra) and Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta — whose doctrines are known only through Buddhist and Jain reports. This lineage entry groups the movement as a whole and the lost teachers who do not belong to a surviving order.

## Distinctive positions
- Leaving the household (pabbajjā) for a homeless, mendicant life outside the Vedic sacrificial order.
- Teachers lead their own orders (saṅghī gaṇī gaṇācariyo) and are called 'ford-makers' (titthakara) in the Pali texts.
- The recurring question debated between orders: is there fruit of good and bad deeds, another world, spontaneously born beings, and does the perfected one exist after death?
- The Buddhist and Jain canons sort the rival teachers into doctrines of non-action (akiriya), no-cause (ahetu), annihilation (uccheda), eternal bodies, fixed destiny (niyati) and suspension of judgment (amarāvikkhepa / ajñāna).
- Public debate in the 'halls of curiosity' (kutūhalasālā) and wanderers' parks is a common practice.

**Transmissions given:** 
  - [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](early-buddhism.md) — what: Sāriputta, Moggallāna and some 250 wanderers who left Sañjaya (Vinaya Mahāvagga 1.23–24); the shared ascetic vocabulary of going forth, alms, homelessness and vows
  - [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](jainism.md) — what: Jain canon lists the rival 'pravādins' and adopts the frame of kriyāvāda vs akriyāvāda
  - [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](carvaka.md) — what: the natthika / uccheda doctrine reported of Ajita Kesakambalī is the earliest statement of the view later called Lokāyata (the texts do not link them by lineage)

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** the end of suffering (dukkhass' anta), the fruit of recluseship (sāmaññaphala)
**Descriptions:** The movement shares a question rather than an ultimate: what is the visible fruit of the homeless life, and is there fruit of deeds and another world? Each teacher answers differently (DN 2).
**Negations:** Several of its teachers deny any fruit of deeds or any other world (DN 2: Pūraṇa, Ajita).
**Relation to self:** Contested within the movement: an eternal jīva among seven bodies (Pakudha), a person made of four elements that is annihilated (Ajita), no statement at all (Sañjaya).
**Relation to world:** Contested: fixed by niyati (Gosāla), eternal and barren (Pakudha), simply ends at death (Ajita).
**Caveat:** The śramaṇa movement is not one school: it includes Jains and Buddhists (who have their own views, U34–U36) and teachers who deny any ultimate. A one-truth reading would have to override the explicit denials of Pūraṇa and Ajita and the refusal of Sañjaya.
