# Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)

`lin:upanisadic` · `sourced` · confidence moderate

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Aupanisada, Vedānta in its original sense ('end of the Veda'), jnana-kanda of the Veda, the Upaniṣadic sages
**Parent:** [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](vedic-srauta.md)
**Key teachers:** [Yājñavalkya](../teachers/yajnavalkya.md), [Uddālaka Āruṇi](../teachers/uddalaka-aruni.md), [Śāṇḍilya](../teachers/sandilya.md), [Sanatkumāra](../teachers/sanatkumara.md), [Pippalāda](../teachers/pippalada.md), [Aṅgiras (teacher of Śaunaka)](../teachers/angiras.md), [Yama (Mṛtyu)](../teachers/yama.md), [Prajāpati (as teacher)](../teachers/prajapati.md), [Varuṇa (as teacher)](../teachers/varuna.md), [Ajātaśatru of Kāśī](../teachers/ajatasatru-kasi.md), [Pravāhaṇa Jaivali](../teachers/pravahana-jaivali.md), [Aśvapati Kaikeya](../teachers/asvapati-kaikeya.md), [Janaka of Videha](../teachers/janaka.md), [Raikva 'with the cart' (sayugvan)](../teachers/raikva.md), [Satyakāma Jābāla](../teachers/satyakama-jabala.md), [Śvetāśvatara](../teachers/svetasvatara.md), [Śākāyanya](../teachers/sakayanya.md), [Citra Gāṅgyāyani](../teachers/citra-gangyayani.md)
**Regions:** Videha (court of Janaka), Kuru-Pañcāla, Kāśī (Ajātaśatru), Pañcāla (Pravāhaṇa Jaivali), Kekaya (Aśvapati), Madra (BAU 3.3, 3.7), Kosala (PrU 6.1)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Revealed scripture (śruti): in the Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta view without human author and beginningless; the texts themselves trace their teaching through lineages of teachers back to Brahma/Prajāpati and brahman, the self-existent; Scholarly account: earliest prose Upaniṣads (BAU, ChU) c. 7th-6th c. BCE; the verse Upaniṣads (Katha, Isa, Śvetāśvatara, Muṇḍaka) in the last centuries BCE; Māṇḍūkya and Maitrī around the turn of the era (rough consensus estimates, disputed); (confidence low)
**Status:** living

The teaching of the early Upaniṣads, the concluding portions of the Vedic śākhās (hence 'Vedānta'). Through dialogues of named sages and kings (Yājñavalkya, Uddālaka Āruṇi, Śāṇḍilya, Sanatkumāra, Pippalāda, Yama) they teach that the self (ātman) is brahman, the ground of the world; that it is known by negation and by inquiry into waking, dream, sleep and the fourth; that action and desire bind to rebirth while knowledge frees; and, in the later texts, a personal Lord, grace, devotion and a method of yoga. The later Vedānta schools read these same texts in very different ways.

## Distinctive positions
- The self (ātman) is to be known; knowing it one knows all (ChU 6.1.3; BAU 2.4.5; MuU 1.1.3).
- The self is brahman: ayam ātmā brahma, ahaṃ brahmāsmi, tat tvam asi, prajñānaṃ brahma.
- The ultimate is described through negation: neti neti (BAU 2.3.6), asthulam ananu (BAU 3.8.8), the fourth (MaU 7).
- Consciousness is analysed through waking, dream, deep sleep and a fourth (BAU 4.3; ChU 8.7-12; MaU 3-7).
- Action (karma) and desire determine rebirth; the desireless knower does not depart but, being brahman, goes to brahman (BAU 4.4.5-7).
- Two paths after death: the path of the gods (no return) and the path of the fathers (return), with a third place for small creatures (ChU 5.10; BAU 6.2).
- Knowledge of the self is higher than sacrifice; rites are 'unsafe boats' and yield finite worlds (MuU 1.2.7-10; BAU 3.8.10).
- Breath (prāṇa) is the eldest and best of the vital powers (BAU 6.1; ChU 5.1; PrU 2; KauU 2.14).
- The teaching is secret (upanisad) and passed from teacher to qualified pupil who approaches with fuel in hand (MuU 1.2.12; SU 6.22).
- In the later texts: a personal Lord (Rudra-Śiva, the deva) who is the māyā-wielder and gives grace; devotion to God and teacher (SU 4.10, 6.18, 6.23).
- Yoga as a method: firm holding of the senses (KU 2.3.11), posture and breath (SU 2.8-10), six limbs (MaiU 6.18).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`sourced` · confidence high

**Names:** brahman, ātman, sat (being), akṣara (the imperishable), bhūman (plenitude), puruṣa, prajñāna, the fourth (turīya), deva / īśa / Rudra-Śiva (Śvetāśvatara)
**Descriptions:** satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ brahma — truth, knowledge, infinite (TU 2.1.1); sad eva ... ekam evādvitīyam — being alone, one without a second (ChU 6.2.1); vijñānam ānandaṃ brahma — understanding, bliss (BAU 3.9.28); raso vai saḥ (TU 2.7.1); that from which beings are born, by which they live, into which they enter (TU 3.1.1); sarvaṃ khalv idaṃ brahma — all this is brahman (ChU 3.14.1); brahmaivedaṃ viśvam (MuU 2.2.11); pūrṇam adaḥ pūrṇam idam — that is full, this is full (BAU 5.1.1); the Lord who wields māyā, whose power is innate knowledge and strength (ŚU 4.10, 6.8)
**Negations:** neti neti — not so, not so (BAU 2.3.6, 3.9.26, 4.2.4, 4.4.22, 4.5.15); asthūlam anaṇu ... — not gross, not fine ... without inside or outside (BAU 3.8.8); aśabdam asparśam arūpam avyayam (KU 1.3.15); adreśyam agrāhyam (MuU 1.1.6); yato vāco nivartante — from which words turn back with the mind (TU 2.4.1); nāntaḥprajñaṃ na bahiṣprajñaṃ ... the fourth (MāU 7); known by the one to whom it is unknown (Kena 2.3)
**Relation to self:** The self is declared to be brahman (ahaṃ brahmāsmi, tat tvam asi, ayam ātmā brahma, prajñānaṃ brahma); it is also the inner controller of every being (BAU 3.7) and, in the two-birds image, the Lord beside the grieving self (MuU 3.1.1-2; ŚU 4.6-7).
**Relation to world:** The source, support and end of all beings (TU 3.1.1); the world is woven on the imperishable (BAU 3.8) and pervaded by the Lord (Īśa 1); it emanates like sparks from fire (BAU 2.1.20; MuU 2.1.1), becomes manifest by name and form (BAU 1.4.7; ChU 6.3.2), and its modifications are 'a verbal handle, a name' (ChU 6.1.4); in the ŚU the māyin projects it by māyā-prakṛti (4.9-10).
**Caveat:** The Upaniṣads do not systematize; they state identity (the mahāvākyas), difference (the two birds; the inner controller 'other than' what it controls) and a personal Lord (Śvetāśvatara). The later Vedānta schools read these same texts in very different ways — see dsp:tat-tvam-asi, dsp:souls-one-or-distinct, dsp:world-real-or-appearance, dsp:saguna-nirguna and dsp:being-or-non-being-first; this view records the statements without deciding between those readings.
