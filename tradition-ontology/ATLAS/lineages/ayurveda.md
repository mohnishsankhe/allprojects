The science of life (āyus), taught in the tradition's account by Brahmā and brought to earth through Indra — to Bharadvāja and Ātreya Punarvasu (the physicians' school) and to Dhanvantari as King Divodāsa of Kāśī (the surgeons' school). Its classical compendia (Caraka, Suśruta, Vāgbhaṭa) teach the three doṣas, seven dhātus and malas, the digestive fires, ojas and the channels; constitution; diet, daily and seasonal routine and good conduct; the eight branches; rejuvenation; and, in Caraka, a doctrine of the self, rebirth, yoga and liberation. It regards itself as eternal and as a limb of the Atharvaveda, and was practised across Vedic, Buddhist and Jain communities.

## Distinctive positions
- Āyus (life) is the union of body, senses, mind and self; Āyurveda is the knowledge of what is wholesome and unwholesome, happy and unhappy life and its measure (Ca Sū 1.41-42).
- Its purpose is twofold: protecting the health of the healthy and allaying the disorder of the sick (Ca Sū 30.26; Su Sū 1.14).
- Health is the equilibrium (sāmya) of the dhātus, disease their disequilibrium (Ca Sū 9.4; AHS Sū 1.20).
- The body has three doṣas (vāta, pitta, kapha); the mind has two (rajas and tamas) (Ca Sū 1.57; AHS Sū 1.21).
- Three causes of all disease: wrong contact of senses and objects, failure of discernment (prajñāparādha), and time/transformation (pariṇāma) (Ca Sū 11.43).
- Three kinds of therapy: resting on the divine (daiva-vyapāśraya), on reasoned application (yukti-vyapāśraya), and conquest of the mind (sattvāvajaya) (Ca Sū 11.54).
- Four means of valid knowledge: authoritative testimony, perception, inference and yukti (Ca Sū 11.17).
- Rebirth is established by all four means (Ca Sū 11.6-33); liberation (mokṣa) is the complete cessation of all feeling, attained through yoga and the recollection of truth (Ca Śā 1.137-155).
- Āyurveda is beginningless and eternal; only its understanding and teaching have a beginning (Ca Sū 30.27).
- It is a science of life, not itself a path of liberation: health is the root of dharma, artha, kāma and mokṣa (Ca Sū 1.15).
- Like cures unlike: increase by the similar (sāmānya), decrease by the dissimilar (viśeṣa) (Ca Sū 1.44).
