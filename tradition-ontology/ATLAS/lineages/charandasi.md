**Status:** living

The Delhi sampradāya of Caraṇdās (1703–1782), which joined nirguṇa Sant teaching with Kṛṣṇa devotion and yoga, and is remembered for its women poet-saints Sahajobāī and Dayābāī.

## Distinctive positions
- A synthesis of Sant nirguṇa devotion with Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa devotion and aṣṭāṅga yoga.
- The guru is exalted above Hari (Sahajobāī).
- Notable women teachers: Sahajobāī and Dayābāī.


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Hari / Kṛṣṇa, Brahman, the guru
**Descriptions:** both the nirguṇa and Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa
**Relation to self:** The guru is above Hari (Sahajobāī).
**Relation to world:** Created by Hari; transcended through the guru.
**Caveat:** The sampradāya holds nirguṇa and saguṇa together; it would reject a purely impersonal reading.
