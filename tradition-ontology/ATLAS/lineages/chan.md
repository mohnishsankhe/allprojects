The Chinese school of the 'meditation' (chan 禪, from dhyāna) lineage, which traces itself through 28 Indian patriarchs to Bodhidharma and six Chinese patriarchs ending with Huineng, divides into the Northern and Southern schools and later the Five Houses, and teaches seeing one's own nature as buddha through direct pointing, sitting meditation, encounter dialogue and kōan practice.

## Distinctive positions
- A 'separate transmission outside the teachings, not dependent on words and letters, pointing directly at the human mind: see the nature and become a buddha' (the tradition's four-line self-description, assembled by the Song).
- The mind (xin 心) or self-nature (zixing 自性) is originally pure and complete; delusion and awakening differ, the nature does not (Platform Sūtra).
- Transmission from mind to mind (yi xin chuan xin 以心傳心) through a line of patriarchs, authenticated by a living teacher.
- Samādhi and prajñā are one in substance (lamp and light); no-thought is the tenet, no-form the substance, non-abiding the root (Platform Sūtra).
- Sudden awakening (dunwu 頓悟) is upheld against 'gradual' views; the relation of awakening to cultivation is debated within Chan itself.
- Encounter dialogue, shouts and blows, and later public cases (gong'an) and the critical phrase (huatou) as means of awakening.
- Monastic life organised by the 'pure rules' (qinggui) with communal work (puqing) as practice.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** self-nature (zixing 自性), buddha-nature (foxing 佛性), original mind (benxin 本心), the one mind (yixin 一心), original face (benlai mianmu 本來面目), suchness (zhenru 真如)
**Descriptions:** Originally pure, originally unborn and undying, originally complete, originally unmoving, able to produce all dharmas (Huineng's words on awakening, Platform Sūtra 4.13).; The one mind of buddhas and beings, without beginning, neither arisen nor ceased, without form, beyond all measure and names (Huangbo, Chuanxin fayao).; Samādhi is its substance and prajñā its function, like lamp and light (Platform Sūtra 7.1).
**Negations:** 'Originally there is not a single thing' (Platform Sūtra 4.11).; 'Neither mind nor buddha' (Mazu).; 'Neither cold nor hot, neither blue nor yellow, not belonging to existence or non-existence' (Huangbo).
**Relation to self:** Not something other than one's own mind: 'if deluded, the buddha is a sentient being; if awakened, the sentient being is a buddha' (Dunhuang Platform Sūtra 52.2).
**Relation to world:** All dharmas are not apart from the self-nature and are produced by it; mountains, rivers and ordinary activity are its function; the Pure Land is the purified mind.
**Caveat:** Chan does not posit an ultimate as an object or a being; names such as buddha-nature and mind are 'expedient names' to be dropped ('if you meet the Buddha, kill the Buddha'). The tradition would reject reading its buddha-nature as a Vedāntic self; Chan critics of the 'Śreṇika heresy' (Nanyang Huizhong) explicitly reject an eternal mind-nature that leaves the body.
