The early devotional religion of Nārāyaṇa-Vāsudeva (Kṛṣṇa) as found in the Nārāyaṇīya of the Mahābhārata, the Bhagavad Gītā's teaching of devotion and surrender, the Harivaṃśa and the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma: exclusive devotion (ekānta-bhakti) to one Lord in a fourfold form, worship without animal sacrifice, and the Lord's repeated appearances to protect dharma. It flows into the Pāñcarātra, the Purāṇic Vaiṣṇavism and the later Vaiṣṇava sampradāyas.

## Distinctive positions
- Nārāyaṇa-Vāsudeva is the one supreme Lord, the knower of the field whose self is without guṇas (12.326.38).
- The fourfold form (caturvyūha): from Vāsudeva comes Saṃkarṣaṇa (the jīva), from him Pradyumna (the mind), from him Aniruddha (the ego), from whom Brahmā arises (12.326.68–69; 12.327.26).
- The liberated ascend through the sun into Aniruddha, Pradyumna and Saṃkarṣaṇa and finally Vāsudeva; the one-pointed devotees (ekāntins) enter Vāsudeva directly (12.332.13–18; 12.336.3).
- The Lord appears again and again (prādurbhāva) as Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana, Rāma Bhārgava, Rāma Dāśarathi and at Mathurā, and earlier as Haṃsa and Hayaśiras (12.326.71–94); cf. BhG 4.7–8.
- Exclusive devotion (ekānta-bhakti), worship according to rule without desire, is the highest dharma, equal to Sāṃkhya-yoga (12.336.4, 12.336.69).
- Worship by sacrifice without slaughter (Uparicara Vasu's sacrifice; the dispute over 'aja' as goat or grain, 12.323–324).
- Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Pañcarātra, the Vedas and Pāśupata are distinct systems, yet Nārāyaṇa is the culmination seen in all (12.337.59–67).
- Rudra and Nārāyaṇa are one being made twofold (12.328.24).
- The teaching Kṛṣṇa gave Arjuna before the battle is this dharma (12.336.8); taking refuge in the Lord alone frees from all sins (BhG 18.66), even for women, vaiśyas and śūdras (BhG 9.32).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Nārāyaṇa, Vāsudeva, Hari, Puruṣottama, Bhagavat (the Lord), Kṛṣṇa (his manifestation at Mathurā)
**Descriptions:** Vāsudeva the Lord is the knower of the field, whose self is without guṇas (MBh 12.326.38); the fourfold form (caturmūrti): Vāsudeva, Saṃkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha (12.326.68–69, 12.326.93); the dwelling of all (sarvāvāsa), the supreme self entered by the liberated (12.332.17); the culmination (niṣṭhā) of every system of knowledge (12.337.63–64); the one who appears again and again to protect the worlds (12.326.71–94)
**Negations:** without guṇas (nirguṇātmaka) (12.326.38; 12.332.17); not seen by those without one-pointed devotion; even Brahmā has not had such a sight (12.326.96)
**Relation to self:** The individual self is Saṃkarṣaṇa, arising from Vāsudeva (12.326.38, 12.326.68); the liberated enter Vāsudeva through the sun and the vyūhas, the ekāntins directly (12.332.13–18; 12.336.3).
**Relation to world:** From Aniruddha comes Brahmā, from Brahmā all moving and unmoving beings, at the beginning of each kalpa (12.326.69–70); the Lord withdraws the world at the end of a thousand ages and dwells alone with knowledge (12.326.66–67).
**Caveat:** Described both as the Lord with a form and as without guṇas; the later schools that inherit this devotion (Pāñcarātra, Viśiṣṭādvaita, Dvaita, Gauḍīya) reject an impersonal reading of it, while Advaita readers take the personal Lord as Brahman with attributes. The Nārāyaṇīya itself records the Pāśupata system as a distinct knowledge (12.337.59, 62) and states that Rudra and Nārāyaṇa are one being made twofold (12.328.24).
