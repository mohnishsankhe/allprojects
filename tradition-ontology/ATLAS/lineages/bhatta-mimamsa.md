The school of Kumārila Bhaṭṭa, built on his Ślokavārttika, Tantravārttika and Ṭupṭīkā and developed by Maṇḍana Miśra, Umbeka, Sucarita, Pārthasārathi Miśra and, later, Khaṇḍadeva, Āpadeva and Laugākṣi Bhāskara. It defends six means of knowledge, the abhihitānvaya theory of sentence meaning, the bhāvanā theory of injunction, and a realist ontology; its epistemology was adopted by Advaita Vedānta for worldly matters.

## Distinctive positions
- Six means of knowledge: perception, inference, verbal testimony, comparison, postulation (arthāpatti) and non-apprehension (anupalabdhi / abhāva-pramāṇa).
- Sentence meaning by 'connection of the expressed' (abhihitānvaya): words first convey their own meanings, which then combine into the sentence meaning (Śabara on MS 1.1.25; ŚV vākya 342–343).
- Error as apprehension of a thing as other than it is (viparītakhyāti): the related items are real, only their relation is unreal (Śāstradīpikā).
- Cognition is not itself perceived; it is inferred from the 'knownness' (jñātatā / prākaṭya) it produces in the object.
- The injunctive suffix expresses a verbal efficient force (śābdī bhāvanā) that prompts the person's own effort (ārthī bhāvanā) with its three expectancies: what, by what, how.
- Obligatory and occasional rites destroy past sin and avert the demerit of omission (Tantravārttika).
- Liberation: the one seeking release avoids desire-prompted and prohibited acts, performs obligatory and occasional rites, knows the self, and when past karma is exhausted by experience no new body arises (ŚV sambandhākṣepaparihāra 102–111).
- Pārthasārathi: liberation is the absolute dissolution of the threefold bond (body, senses, objects) and is without bliss; some Bhāṭṭas held that the liberated self enjoys its innate bliss.
- Words are the eternal letters; the grammarians' sphoṭa is superfluous (ŚV sphoṭavāda).
- Kumārila rejects a creator Prajāpati and a total dissolution of the world (ŚV sambandhākṣepaparihāra 42–116).

**Transmissions given:**

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** dharma, the Veda, the self in its own nature after release
**Descriptions:** Kumārila opens the Ślokavārttika by saluting the one whose body is pure knowledge, whose divine eye is the three Vedas, the cause of attaining the good, the bearer of the half-moon (ŚV pratijñā 1).; Liberation is the absolute dissolution of the self's threefold bond — body, senses and objects (Śāstradīpikā on MS 1.1.5).; Dharma performed with its declared aim produces that aim; performed with the thought of offering it to Govinda / Īśvara it becomes a cause of the highest good (Mīmāṃsānyāyaprakāśa, Arthasaṅgraha, conclusions).
**Negations:** No creator Prajāpati and no total dissolution (ŚV sambandhākṣepaparihāra 42–116).; Liberation is not an enjoyment of pleasure, which would be only another name for a perishable heaven (ŚV sambandhākṣepaparihāra 105).
**Relation to self:** Each self is eternal and all-pervading; release leaves it in its own nature, without body, senses or objects (for Pārthasārathi, without bliss; for other Bhāṭṭas he reports, with innate bliss).
**Relation to world:** The world is real; creation and dissolution are to be understood 'as today', i.e. as the ongoing births and deaths of beings, not as a total destruction and re-creation (ŚV sambandhākṣepaparihāra 113).
**Caveat:** The Bhāṭṭa school does not posit one ultimate reality; its ontology is pluralist and realist. The salutation to the moon-crested one in ŚV pratijñā 1 and the late dedication of dharma to Govinda/Īśvara (Āpadeva, Laugākṣi Bhāskara) show devotion, not a doctrine that the Lord is the one ground of being. Advaitins adopt Bhāṭṭa epistemology for empirical matters only, and Bhāṭṭas do not accept the Advaitic reading of their system.
