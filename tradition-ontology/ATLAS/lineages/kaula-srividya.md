The line of Śrīvidyā that follows the Vāmakeśvara tantra and Yoginīhṛdaya within a Kaula frame: Puṇyānanda's Kāmakalāvilāsa, Amṛtānanda's Dīpikā, the Paraśurāma Kalpasūtra with Umānandanātha's Nityotsava, and Bhāskararāya's commentaries. It combines outer Śrīcakra worship, Kaula conduct kept secret, and non-dual knowledge.

## Distinctive positions
- Outer worship of the cakra is performed even by the omniscient Śiva because it leads to the knowledge of non-difference (Yoginīhṛdaya 3.3 with the Dīpikā); inner and outer worship together.
- Bliss is the form of Brahman, present in the body; the five 'M's manifest it and are used in secret; exposure leads to hell; when they are unavailable the daily worship continues without them (PKS 1.12, 1.24).
- The Goddess is at once Kaula and Samaya (Bhāskararāya on Lalitā Sahasranāma 36-37).
- Initiation in stages (Bālā first, then the higher vidyās) with initiatory names ending in '-ānandanātha' (PKS 1.39-41).
- The Vedic standing of Śrīvidyā is defended by Mīmāṃsā reasoning (Bhāskararāya).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Tripurā / Mahātripurasundarī, Kāmeśvarī on Kāmeśvara's lap, Parā Saṃvid
**Descriptions:** Tripurā is the fusion of prakāśa and vimarśa (Dīpikā on YH 1.6).; The kāmakalā — two bindus, white and red, and their union — is the Goddess's own form; knowing it one becomes Mahātripurasundarī (Kāmakalāvilāsa 6-8).
**Relation to self:** Knowledge of the cakra-saṅketa makes one liberated while living, abiding in the I-sense of Śiva (YH 1.86 with the Dīpikā).
**Relation to world:** The cakra of thirty-six principles unfolds when Śakti beholds her own vibrancy (YH 1.9-10).
**Caveat:** This line holds the Goddess is both Kaula and Samaya and that outer worship is part of the path even for the knower; it would not accept a Samaya reconciliation that declares Kaula worship outside the Veda.
