**Dates:** Tradition's account: founded by Tsangpa Gyare Yeshe Dorje (1161–1211), who at Namdruk saw thunder-dragons (druk) and so named the school; Scholarly account: late 12th c. onward; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

One of the 'eight lesser' Kagyu schools, founded by Tsangpa Gyare at Ralung and Druk; divided into Upper, Middle and Lower branches; its Southern branch became the state tradition of Bhutan under Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal. Known for renunciate retreat practice, Pema Karpo's writings and the divine madmen.

## Distinctive positions
- The 'six cycles of equal taste' (ro snyoms skor drug) and the 'seven auspicious coincidences' (rten 'brel rab bdun), rediscovered by Tsangpa Gyare: adversity, illness and death are carried onto the path.
- The renunciate mountain-retreat ideal of Milarepa (the Upper Drukpa of Götsangpa); the tradition's saying that half the people are Drukpa, half the Drukpa are beggars, and half the beggars are siddhas.
- Pema Karpo's systematization of Mahāmudrā and the Six Yogas; the 'divine madmen' (Tsangnyön Heruka, Drukpa Kunley) whose unconventional conduct is read as teaching.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Mahāmudrā, co-emergent union (lhan cig skyes sbyor), dharmakāya
**Descriptions:** The co-emergent: mind-as-such is dharmakāya, appearances its light, thoughts its waves; adversity, illness and death, carried onto the path, are of one taste with it (the six equal tastes).
**Negations:** not found by rejecting thoughts and afflictions
**Relation to self:** The mind's own nature.
**Relation to world:** Circumstances, including obstacles and death, are carried onto the path as one taste.
**Caveat:** As for ult:kagyu.
