The movement of Sree Narayana Guru (1856–1928) of Kerala, a non-dual Vedāntin and poet who worked against caste through temple consecrations, education and organization, teaching 'one caste, one religion, one God'.

## Distinctive positions
- One caste, one religion, one God for humankind; humanity itself is the only caste (Jāti Mīmāṃsā).
- Non-dual knowledge (aṟivu) is the one reality; what one does for one's own happiness should also bring happiness to others (Ātmopadeśa Śatakam 24).
- Temples, schools and consecrations opened to those excluded by caste; the mirror installed as object of worship (tradition's account).
- Whatever the religion, it suffices if a person becomes good.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** Aṟivu (knowledge-consciousness), the Karu (core), God (Daivam)
**Descriptions:** Knowledge alone is: knower, knowing and known are one aṟivu (Ātmopadeśa Śatakam).
**Relation to self:** The Self is that knowledge; the one God of all humans.
**Relation to world:** The world is superimposed on the one reality (Darśana Mālā, adhyāropa and apavāda).
**Caveat:** His social teaching (one caste, one religion) is an ethical corollary of non-duality, not a separate doctrine.
