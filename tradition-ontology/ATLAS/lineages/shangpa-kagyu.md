A Tibetan transmission distinct from the Marpa Kagyu, founded by Khyungpo Naljor on the teachings of the Indian ḍākinīs Niguma and Sukhasiddhi; its 'five golden dharmas' parallel the Six Yogas and Mahāmudrā. It survives within the Karma Kagyu, Jonang and Rimé and was revived by Kalu Rinpoche.

## Distinctive positions
- Its source is two women: the ḍākinīs Niguma (by the Tibetan account Nāropa's sister or consort) and Sukhasiddhi, received by Khyungpo Naljor in India.
- The 'five golden dharmas' pictured as a tree: the root, Niguma's six dharmas; the trunk, the Amulet Mahāmudrā; the branches, the three integrations; the flowers, the white and red Khecarī; the fruit, the deathless body and mind.
- By Niguma's command (tradition) the teachings passed to a single disciple for the first seven generations (the 'seven jewels'), then spread widely.
- The Shangpa has had no large institutional school of its own after the 14th c.; it lives inside other schools.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Mahāmudrā of the amulet box (phyag chen ga'u ma), the four kāyas as self-liberated mind
**Descriptions:** The mind's nature, left uncontrived, is self-liberated; its empty, clear and blissful aspects are the kāyas, 'enclosed together like the two halves of an amulet box' (the tradition's image for the inseparability of ground, path and fruit).
**Negations:** not produced by fabrication
**Relation to self:** The mind's own nature.
**Relation to world:** Appearances are carried as the guru, the deity and illusion (the three integrations).
**Caveat:** The amulet-box image and the 'three self-liberations' are Shangpa formulations known here only from memory; low confidence.
