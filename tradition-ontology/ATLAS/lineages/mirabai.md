# Mīrābāī's devotion (the Mīrā song-tradition)

**Regions:** Rajasthan (Meṛtā, Chittor), Braj, Gujarat (Dvārakā, Ḍākor), all north India through song
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Mīrā's life c. 1498–1547; Scholarly account: 16th c. onward; (confidence low)
**Status:** living

The devotion of Mīrābāī, a 16th-c. Rajput princess of Meṛtā married into Mewar, who took Kṛṣṇa as her husband and defied her in-laws. Her songs (mostly attributed) of love, separation and defiance are sung everywhere; the Bhaktamāl praises her for casting off family honour to worship Giridhar.

## Distinctive positions
- Kṛṣṇa as Giridhar Gopāl / Giridhar Nāgar is her only husband; she refuses the conduct expected of a royal widow and the family's honour (lok-lāj, kul-kī-maryādā).
- Love-in-separation (viraha) and madness of love (prem-dīvānī) are the texture of devotion.
- The company of sādhus regardless of caste; the name as the jewel-treasure.
- No formal sampradāya: the tradition lives in song, in shrines (Meṛtā, Chittor, Vṛndāvana) and in the hagiographies; several traditions claim her.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Giridhar Gopāl / Giridhar Nāgar
**Descriptions:** the dark one wearing the peacock crown, her husband; the physician of love's wound
**Relation to self:** The soul is his bride and servant.
**Relation to world:** Family honour and the world's shame are to be cast off for him.
**Caveat:** A song-tradition without a formal theology; songs using the name ('Rām ratan') are read by some as nirguṇa, by most as devotion to Kṛṣṇa.
