The Vaiṣṇava sampradāya founded in Gujarat by Sahajānanda Svāmī, 'Svāminārāyaṇa' (1781–1830), teaching devotion to God as Puruṣottama with a Viśiṣṭādvaita-type ontology, strict conduct and temple-centred community.

## Distinctive positions
- Five eternal realities: jīva, īśvara, māyā, brahman (Akṣara) and Parabrahman (Puruṣottama).
- God always has a divine human-like form and is manifest on earth; followers hold Svāminārāyaṇa to be Puruṣottama himself (tradition's account).
- Ekāntika dharma: dharma, knowledge of the self (ātma-jñāna), detachment (vairāgya) and devotion together.
- Strict conduct: non-violence, vegetarianism, abstention from alcohol and theft; renunciants avoid wealth and women; separation of men and women in worship.
- The authority of the two ācārya seats (Ahmedabad, Vadtal) established by Svāminārāyaṇa's Lekh (1826).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** Parabrahman Puruṣottama, Svāminārāyaṇa (for followers)
**Descriptions:** God is Puruṣottama, eternally possessing a divine form, residing in Akṣaradhāma, the all-doer and source of the other four eternal realities.
**Relation to self:** Jīvas are eternally distinct from God, to be liberated by devotion and dwell in his service.
**Relation to world:** The world (from māyā) is real and God's creation.
**Caveat:** Denies that God is formless or that the soul becomes God; the one-truth reading would be accepted only as God's sovereignty over all.
