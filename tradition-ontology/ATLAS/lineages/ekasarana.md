The Ekaśaraṇa ('single refuge') Dharma of Assam, founded by Śaṅkaradeva (1449–1568 by tradition) and continued by Mādhavadeva, teaches exclusive servant-devotion to Kṛṣṇa through his name, the guru and the congregation, without images, other gods or Vedic rites. Its scriptures — the Kīrtana-ghoṣā, the Nām-ghoṣā, the bargīts and one-act plays — are sung and performed in village nāmghars and in the sattras (monasteries), which after Mādhavadeva divided into four saṃhatis.

## Distinctive positions
- Single refuge (eka-śaraṇa) in Kṛṣṇa alone: no other deity is worshipped, and Vedic ritual and animal sacrifice are rejected.
- The name (nāma) is the chief means; of the nine forms of devotion, hearing (śravaṇa) and singing (kīrtana) are foremost.
- The four real things (cāri vastu): the name, God (deva), the guru and the community of devotees (bhakta).
- Devotion is that of the servant (dāsya); Rādhā is not worshipped and the erotic mood is not cultivated.
- The true devotee does not desire even liberation.
- In the nāmghar and sattra a sacred book — the Bhāgavata (or the Kīrtana-ghoṣā) — sits on the guru-āsana in place of an image.
- Initiation (śaraṇ) is open to all castes, tribes and to Muslims; the sattras house celibate (kevaliyā) and married devotees.
- Drama (bhāonā), song (bargīt) and dance are forms of worship.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Kṛṣṇa / Nārāyaṇa, Hari's name
**Descriptions:** the eternal one whose form is Brahman, Nārāyaṇa, cause of all avatāras (Kīrtana-ghoṣā); the root whose watering nourishes all the gods
**Relation to self:** The soul is Kṛṣṇa's servant; the devotee seeks devotion, not liberation.
**Relation to world:** Other gods and rites belong to the world; Kṛṣṇa alone is refuge.
**Caveat:** The tradition itself refuses worship of other deities; it would reject a reconciliation that makes all deities equal objects of refuge, even while holding that worship of Kṛṣṇa satisfies all.
