# Rasa Śāstra (the science of mercury) and the Raseśvara-darśana

**Regions:** north and west India, the Deccan, Nepal, Tamil country (via the Siddhars)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: revealed by Bhairava (Śiva) to the Goddess (Rasārṇava); mercury is Śiva's own seed; Scholarly account: alchemical tantras c. 10th–13th c. CE; medical rasa compendia 13th–17th c.; still used in Āyurvedic pharmacy; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

A Śaiva tantric science, presented as revealed by Bhairava to the Goddess, that seeks a stable, undecaying 'divine body' through mercury processed with sulphur, mica and other minerals and joined with breath-control, mantra and worship. Its goal is liberation while living (jīvanmukti) in that body; the metal-transmuting work (lohavedha) serves as a test before the body is treated (dehavedha). Its medical side supplied the mercurial and metallic medicines of later Āyurveda. RESTRICTED: recorded here as summary and the texts' own warnings only.

## Distinctive positions
- Liberation in life (jīvanmukti) requires a stable, undecaying body; liberation promised only after the body falls cannot be seen and is 'meaningless' (Rasārṇava 1.8-13; SDS Raseśvara 4-5).
- Mercury (pārada, 'that which carries across to the far shore of saṃsāra') is Śiva's seed; mica (abhraka) the Goddess's; their union yields the divine body (SDS Raseśvara 1-10).
- The discipline of action (karmayoga) that stabilizes the body is twofold: mercury and breath (Rasārṇava 1.18-19).
- Knowledge liberates; knowledge comes from practice; practice needs a stable body (Rasahṛdaya 1.10).
- The goal of mercury is the body (dehavedha) and through it liberation, not the making of gold (SDS Raseśvara 23-25).
- Mercury with its impurities is poison; only purified mercury is nectar (Rasaratnākara, Rasa-khaṇḍa 1.30).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

**Names:** Parameśvara / Śiva (Bhairava), the conscious light (cinmaya jyotis), supreme bliss, brahman; rasa ('raso vai saḥ')
**Descriptions:** Liberation in life is knowledge of identity with Śiva in an ageless, deathless body (Rasārṇava 1.8).; The conscious light between the brows: supreme bliss, of the nature of light, free of conception, peaceful, self-known; seeing the whole world as consciousness one attains brahmanhood here (Rasahṛdaya 1.21-23; SDS 46-48).; Mercury is Śiva's own seed and is praised as equal to the supreme brahman (Rasārṇava 1.36; SDS 49-51).
**Negations:** Free of conception (avikalpa), free of all afflictions (Rasahṛdaya 1.22).
**Relation to self:** The Raseśvaras are Māheśvaras who hold identity (tādātmya) with the Supreme Lord; the self realizes it only in a stable body.
**Relation to world:** The world is to be seen as consciousness; mercury and mica, seeds of Śiva and the Goddess, make the divine body.
**Caveat:** The tradition would reject any one-truth reading that allows liberation without the stable, undecaying body ('liberation after the body falls is meaningless'). Its practices are restricted and are recorded here only in summary.
