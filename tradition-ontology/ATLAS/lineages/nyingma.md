**Status:** living

The oldest school of Tibetan Buddhism, tracing itself to the 8th-century imperial translations and to Padmasambhava, Śāntarakṣita, Vimalamitra and the translator Vairocana. It arranges the whole Dharma as nine vehicles, keeps its own canon of Old Tantras (the Nyingma Gyubum), transmits teachings both by unbroken spoken lineage (kama) and by revealed treasures (terma), and holds the Great Perfection (Dzogchen, Atiyoga) as the summit of the path, systematized by Longchenpa and Jigme Lingpa.

## Distinctive positions
- The teaching is ordered as nine vehicles (theg pa rim pa dgu), culminating in the three inner tantras Mahāyoga, Anuyoga and Atiyoga (Dzogchen).
- Three streams of transmission: the long lineage of the spoken word (bka' ma), the short lineage of treasures (gter ma) and profound pure visions (dag snang).
- The primordial buddha Samantabhadra (Kuntuzangpo) is the dharmakāya teacher; Garab Dorje the first human teacher of Dzogchen; Padmasambhava the 'second Buddha' for Tibet.
- The Guhyagarbha is the root Mahāyoga tantra: all phenomena are primordially the maṇḍala of deities — 'great purity and equality' (dag mnyam chen po).
- Atiyoga distinguishes awareness (rig pa) from ordinary mind (sems) and teaches the ground as primordial purity and spontaneous presence, realized by cutting through (khregs chod) and direct crossing (thod rgal).
- Its tantras (the rnying rgyud) were translated in the first diffusion; their Indian authenticity was disputed by later critics and defended by the school (see dsp:authenticity-of-nyingma-tantras).
- A double sangha: celibate monastics and lay tantric householders (sngags pa, the 'white-robed, long-haired' community).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Samantabhadra (Kun tu bzang po), the dharmakāya primordial buddha, the ground (gzhi), the nature of the sugatas (bde gshegs snying po), great purity and equality (dag mnyam chen po), dharmadhātu (chos kyi dbyings)
**Descriptions:** All phenomena are primordially buddha: 'from the unborn all is born; in being born there is no birth' (Guhyagarbha ch.2).; Appearances are primordially pure as the maṇḍala of deities (the aggregates are the five buddhas, Guhyagarbha ch.1) and equal in emptiness.; In Mipham's Nyingma reading, buddha-nature is the union of luminosity and emptiness, with the qualities spontaneously present.
**Negations:** no arising, abiding, ceasing, coming or going (Guhyagarbha ch.2); no bondage and no liberation: 'no one binds, there is no bondage' (Guhyagarbha ch.2)
**Relation to self:** Beings are primordially buddhas whose own conceptual grasping at 'I' and 'mine' ties 'knots in space'; the 'self' is not established, but the nature of mind is the sugatagarbha.
**Relation to world:** The world as it appears through karmic delusion is the five realms; as it is, it is the spontaneously present maṇḍala (pure appearance).
**Caveat:** The Nyingma deny a creator god and a permanent self: Samantabhadra is the dharmakāya — the ground recognized — not a person who made the world, and the sugatagarbha is not the self of the tīrthikas (Mipham). The one-truth reading is acceptable only as 'one ground, two paths' (recognition and non-recognition), not as the identity of all doctrines.
