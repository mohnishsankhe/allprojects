The tradition of learned manuals that collect the mantras of many deities with their seers, metres, seeds, rules of initiation, purification, placement and repetition — above all the Śāradātilaka (with Rāghavabhaṭṭa's Padārthādarśa), the Prapañcasāra and the Mantramahodadhi. Their theory: the supreme becomes sound (nāda, bindu), sound becomes the fifty letters, the letters become mantras, and the mantra, given by a guru and repeated with its purifications, gives enjoyment and liberation.

## Distinctive positions
- From the supreme Lord, being-consciousness-bliss, arose Śakti; from her nāda; from nāda bindu (ŚT 1.7); the supreme bindu divides into bindu, nāda and bīja — Śiva, Śakti and their union (ŚT 1.8–9).
- From the dividing bindu arose unmanifest sound, called śabda-brahman; śabda-brahman is not the word or its meaning (both insentient) but the consciousness of all beings (ŚT 1.11–13).
- Śabda-brahman dwells in the body of beings as kuṇḍalinī and manifests as the letters, in prose and verse (ŚT 1.14).
- The fifty letters (mātṛkā) are the body of the Goddess and of every mantra; mantras are placed on the body (nyāsa).
- Every mantra has a seer, metre and deity, a seed (bīja), power (śakti) and pin (kīlaka); mantras are male, female or neuter by their endings.
- A mantra may be defective in some fifty ways (ŚT 2.64–70) and is purified by ten rites (ŚT 2.112–122); secrecy (gopana) is itself one of the ten.
- Only a qualified guru may give a mantra, to a qualified disciple tested for one to four years (ŚT 2.141–153); a mantra is 'mastered' by puraścaraṇa before it is used.
- Japa is spoken, whispered or mental, each far superior to the previous.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Parameśvara, being-consciousness-bliss (saccidānanda), Śabda-brahman = the consciousness of all beings, Kuṇḍalinī (śabda-brahman in the body)
**Descriptions:** the supreme Lord, splendid with being, consciousness and bliss, with parts (sakala), from whom Śakti, nāda and bindu arise (ŚT 1.7); śabda-brahman is the consciousness of all beings (ŚT 1.13)
**Negations:** śabda-brahman is neither the word nor its meaning, both of which are insentient (ŚT 1.12–13)
**Relation to self:** The same consciousness dwells in each body as kuṇḍalinī and becomes the letters (ŚT 1.14).
**Relation to world:** The world of words and of things unfolds from the supreme through Śakti, nāda, bindu and the fifty letters (ŚT 1.7–16).
**Caveat:** The mantra manuals serve worshippers of many deities (Śiva, Viṣṇu, the Goddess, Gaṇeśa, Sūrya) and adopt a Śaiva–Śākta cosmogony of sound; they are not a separate darśana and would defer to the metaphysics of the worshipper's own school.
