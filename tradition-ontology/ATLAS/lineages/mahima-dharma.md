The Odia renunciant movement of Mahimā Svāmī (d. 1876) and the blind Kondh poet Bhīma Bhoi, worshipping the formless Alekha and rejecting caste and image worship; centred at Joranda.

## Distinctive positions
- God is Alekha ('the unwritable'), the formless Śūnya Brahman or Mahimā (Glory), not to be worshipped in images.
- Rejection of caste, of brāhmaṇa priesthood and of image and temple worship.
- Ascetics wear tree bark, eat once a day before sunset and do not stay two nights in one place (recalled; low confidence).
- Bhīma Bhoi: 'let my life be cast in hell, but let the world be saved'.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** Alekha (the unwritable), Śūnya Brahman, Mahimā (Glory)
**Descriptions:** God is formless, beyond writing and description, the void (śūnya) that is full.
**Relation to self:** All beings belong to Alekha; devotion and surrender to him.
**Relation to world:** The world is his creation; images are false.
**Caveat:** The tradition rejects image worship and would reject being assimilated to Jagannātha worship.
