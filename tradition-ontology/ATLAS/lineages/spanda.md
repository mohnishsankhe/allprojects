**Dates:** Tradition's account: The Śiva Sūtras were revealed to Vasugupta on Mahādeva mountain - in a dream by Paramaśiva who showed him a rock that turned over at his touch (Kṣemarāja, Śivasūtravimarśinī intro.), or 'by a siddha's command' (Bhāskara, Śivasūtravārttika 1.3).; Scholarly account: c. 850-900 CE (Vasugupta and Kallaṭa; Kallaṭa is placed under King Avantivarman, 855-883, by the Rājataraṅgiṇī); commentaries to Kṣemarāja (c. 1000-1050) and later.; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** absorbed

The earliest systematic current of Kashmirian non-dual Śaivism, founded on the Śiva Sūtras revealed to Vasugupta and the Spandakārikā (ascribed to Vasugupta by Kṣemarāja, to Kallaṭa by Rāmakaṇṭha, Bhagavadutpala and Bhāskara). It presents the ultimate as vibrant, self-aware power (spanda), identical with one's own agency, discoverable in every moment of intense experience, and absorbed later into the Trika synthesis.

## Distinctive positions
- The ultimate is Śaṅkara, from whose opening (unmeṣa) and closing (nimeṣa) of the eyes the world arises and dissolves; this dynamic pulsation is spanda (Spandakārikā 1.1).
- The knowing and acting subject never ceases through waking, dream and deep sleep; what is lost in sleep is only the effort turned to objects, not the inner agent (SK 1.3, 1.14-16).
- Spanda can be grasped in intense states - great anger, joy, perplexity, running - and at the gap where one thought ends and another arises (SK 1.22, 3.9).
- Bondage comes from the powers arising from the mass of sounds (śabdarāśi) when they are not recognized; recognized, the same power grants perfection (SK 3.13-16).
- 'Consciousness is the self; knowledge [limited] is bondage' (Śiva Sūtra 1.1-2); 'effort is Bhairava' (1.5).
- The Śiva Sūtras are read (by Kṣemarāja) as teaching the three means in three sections: śāmbhava, śākta, āṇava.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Spanda (vibration), Śaṅkara, Svabhāva (one's own nature), sāmānya-spanda (the universal vibration)
**Descriptions:** that in which all this rests and from which it has come forth; its nature is unobstructed (Spandakārikā 1.2); the inner (antarmukha) agency, seat of omniscience, never lost (SK 1.14-16); the opening and closing of Śaṅkara's eyes (SK 1.1)
**Negations:** where there is neither pain nor pleasure, neither object nor subject, nor even insentience - that exists in the supreme sense (SK 1.5)
**Relation to self:** It is one's own nature (svabhāva), the knower-agent persisting through all states (SK 1.3, 1.10).
**Relation to world:** The world emerges and dissolves with the unmeṣa and nimeṣa of the one Śaṅkara (SK 1.1); all particular vibrations rest on the universal vibration (SK 1.19).
**Caveat:** Spanda insists that the ultimate is dynamic; it would reject any account of a static, inactive absolute.
