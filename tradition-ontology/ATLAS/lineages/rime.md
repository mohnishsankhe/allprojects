The 19th-century non-sectarian (ris med) movement of eastern Tibet led by Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo (1820–1892) and Jamgön Kongtrul Lodrö Thayé (1813–1899), with Chokgyur Lingpa, Mipham and Patrul Rinpoche. It gathered, practised and printed the teachings of all Tibetan lineages — above all those in danger of loss — in collections such as Kongtrul's Five Treasuries, organizing them as the eight great practice lineages, and urged respect for every tradition in its own terms. Flagged as recent (post-1800).

## Distinctive positions
- All authentic lineages are to be respected and preserved; disparaging another tradition is the grave fault of abandoning the Dharma.
- The whole of Tibetan practice is mapped as eight great chariots of practice lineages: Nyingma, Kadam, Sakya Lamdre, Marpa Kagyu, Shangpa Kagyu, Shije-and-Chöd, the Kālacakra six-branch yoga, and Orgyenpa's approach-and-accomplishment of the three vajras.
- Each lineage is to be received, practised and taught in its own terms, without mixing its methods or views with another's.
- Rare and endangered transmissions are to be gathered and printed so that they survive (the Five Treasuries; the Compendium of Sādhanas; the Compendium of Tantras).
- In Kongtrul's presentation, the other-emptiness Great Madhyamaka and the self-emptiness Madhyamaka each have their place; Mipham defends a Madhyamaka of the unity of appearance and emptiness; the movement does not impose one view.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** the Great Madhyamaka (dbu ma chen po), in Kongtrul's presentation, the unity of appearance and emptiness; primordial purity (Mipham), the single intent (dgongs pa gcig) of the lineages' final views — Dzogchen, Mahāmudrā, Lamdre, the Great Madhyamaka
**Descriptions:** each of the eight practice lineages is a complete path to the same buddhahood; buddha-nature as the union of luminosity and emptiness (Mipham); the other-empty Great Madhyamaka as the view for meditation, the self-empty Madhyamaka for cutting elaborations in study (Kongtrul, as recalled)
**Negations:** no single school's formulation is the only valid one; the ultimate is neither a mere absence nor a permanent entity (Mipham)
**Relation to self:** As in the lineages it preserves: buddha-nature present in all beings; the imputed self does not exist.
**Relation to world:** As in the lineages it preserves.
**Caveat:** Rimé is an approach, not a doctrine: its masters insisted that each lineage keep its own view and methods unmixed, and they themselves differed (Kongtrul's shentong-leaning Great Madhyamaka; Mipham's Madhyamaka of unity). Reading it as a single 'one-truth' doctrine would erase the distinctions it insists on. Gelug critics rejected parts of the Rimé masters' views (see dsp:mipham-gelug-madhyamaka).
