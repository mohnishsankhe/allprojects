The guru tradition of the avadhūta Bhagavan Nityananda of Ganeshpuri (d. 1961) and his disciple Swami Muktananda (1908–1982), centred on śaktipāta initiation and kuṇḍalinī awakening by grace, with Kashmir Śaiva philosophy; continued by Gurumayi Chidvilasananda.

## Distinctive positions
- Śaktipāta: the guru awakens the disciple's kuṇḍalinī by grace (look, touch, word or thought); the awakened energy then performs the yoga by itself (claims of the tradition).
- 'Honor your Self, worship your Self, meditate on your Self; God dwells within you as you.'
- Chanting (the Guru Gītā, Om Namaḥ Śivāya), meditation on So'ham, and service in the ashram.
- Visions of lights culminating in the Blue Pearl (nīla bindu), in Muktananda's own account.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** Śiva / Consciousness, the Self
**Descriptions:** God dwells within you as you; the universe is the play of Consciousness (Citi).
**Relation to self:** The Self is God; the guru's grace awakens this recognition.
**Relation to world:** The world is the play of Consciousness.
**Caveat:** Draws on Kashmir Śaivism; the guru is venerated as the embodiment of grace.
