**Dates:** Tradition's account: Atiśa invited to Tibet by the Ngari kings Yeshe Ö and Jangchub Ö; Reting founded by Dromtönpa after Atiśa's death; Scholarly account: Atiśa in Tibet 1042–1054 CE; Reting 1056/57; Kadam as a separate order absorbed into the Gelug from the 15th c. and its lojong and lamrim into all schools; (confidence high)
**Status:** absorbed

The Kadam, founded on Atiśa's teaching in Tibet (1042–1054) and organised by his lay disciple Dromtönpa at Reting, stressed renunciation, bodhicitta and the graded path for three persons (Lamp for the Path), and produced the mind-training (lojong) literature — Langri Tangpa's Eight Verses, Chekawa's Seven Points — that all later schools adopted; its lines were absorbed into the Gelug ('New Kadam').

## Distinctive positions
- All the Buddha's words are to be taken as personal instruction for practice; none is to be discarded as irrelevant.
- The path is graded for three kinds of person (small, middling, great) — Bodhipathapradīpa 2–5.
- Mind training (blo sbyong): relative bodhicitta by equalizing and exchanging self and other, sending and taking; turning adversity into the path.
- The seven divine dharmas (lha chos bdun ldan): the four deities (Śākyamuni, Avalokiteśvara, Tārā, Acala) and the three baskets.
- The six Kadam treatises (bka' gdams gzhung drug) as core texts.
- Celibate monastics are not to take the secret and wisdom initiations literally (Bodhipathapradīpa 66–68).
- Madhyamaka in Candrakīrti's line: the ultimate is one, not realized by the two means of valid cognition taken as conceptual (Satyadvayāvatāra 4, 10).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** don dam bden pa (ultimate truth), one only, chos nyid (dharmatā), stong pa nyid (emptiness), ultimate bodhicitta
**Descriptions:** The ultimate is one; others hold it to be two; dharmatā, established as nothing at all, cannot be two or three (Satyadvayāvatāra 4).; Without arising or ceasing; free of thing and non-thing; inexpressible, unconditioned; realizing it the yogin abandons the afflictive and cognitive obscurations (Satyadvayāvatāra 5–9).; Wisdom is knowing the emptiness of own-nature of aggregates, elements and sense-bases as unborn (Bodhipathapradīpa v48).
**Negations:** no arising from self, other, both or without cause (Bodhipathapradīpa v50); not found when analysed as one or many (Bodhipathapradīpa v51); seeing it is 'not seeing'; no seer and nothing seen (Satyadvayāvatāra 7)
**Relation to self:** Selflessness of persons and phenomena; the practitioner meditates on selflessness without conceptualization (Bodhipathapradīpa v54–55).
**Relation to world:** Conventional truth is of two kinds, false and correct; correct conventional truth is what, unexamined, arises and ceases and can perform functions; it is to be respected — cause and effect, virtue and vice — and is the ladder to the ultimate (Satyadvayāvatāra 2–3, 18–22).
**Caveat:** Atiśa's 'the ultimate is one' denies that the ultimate is divisible, not that it is a single substance: it is dharmatā 'not established as anything'. A reading of it as a positive one-reality would be rejected.
