The school of Prabhākara Miśra ('the Guru'), based on his Bṛhatī and Laghvī on Śabara's commentary and systematized by Śālikanātha's Prakaraṇapañcikā, then by Bhavanātha and later Prābhākara authors. It holds five means of knowledge, the self-luminosity of cognition, the akhyāti account of error, and the doctrine that words mean in connection with what is to be done (kārya).

## Distinctive positions
- Five means of knowledge: perception, inference, verbal testimony (śāstra), comparison and postulation; non-apprehension is not a separate means (Prakaraṇapañcikā, Pramāṇapārāyaṇa).
- Words convey their meanings only as already connected with other meanings, ultimately with what is to be done (anvitābhidhāna / kāryānvitābhidhāna).
- Error is non-apprehension (akhyāti): 'this is silver' is two cognitions — a perception ('this') and a memory ('silver') — whose difference is not grasped (Prakaraṇapañcikā, Nayavīthī and Pramāṇapārāyaṇa).
- Every cognition is self-luminous and manifests knower, known and knowing together (tripuṭī-pratyakṣa); the self appears only as the subject of cognition.
- The meaning of an injunction is the command or 'what is to be done' (niyoga / kārya), identified with the apūrva known only from the Veda.
- The Veda does not convey accomplished (siddha) things for their own sake; hence descriptions of a blissful liberation have no independent authority (Prakaraṇapañcikā ch. 8).
- Eight categories, adding power (śakti), similarity (sādṛśya), number (saṅkhyā) and dependence (paratantratā) to substance, quality, action and universal.
- Liberation is the absolute ending of embodiment caused by the complete exhaustion of dharma and adharma; the liberated self remains in mere being, without cognition (Prakaraṇapañcikā ch. 8).
- No Īśvara directs dharma and adharma or creates the world; things arise and perish gradually, and the usage of elders from which word-meaning is learned is beginningless (Prakaraṇapañcikā ch. 7).


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** kārya / niyoga (what is to be done, known only from the Veda), dharma
**Descriptions:** The Veda's authority lies in making known what is to be done; statements about accomplished things are not independently authoritative (Prakaraṇapañcikā ch. 8).; Released, the self remains in mere being (sanmātra), all-pervading, beyond all apprehension (Prakaraṇapañcikā ch. 8).
**Negations:** No Īśvara supervises dharma and adharma or creates the world (Prakaraṇapañcikā ch. 7, Īśvaranirākaraṇa).; Liberation is not the manifestation of a supreme bliss (Prakaraṇapañcikā ch. 8).; Release is not the 'setting' of ignorance (avidyā-astamaya) held by Maṇḍana (Prakaraṇapañcikā ch. 8).
**Relation to self:** Selves are many, eternal and all-pervading; in themselves non-luminous, they appear as subjects in every cognition.
**Relation to world:** The world is real, arising and perishing in parts, without total creation or dissolution.
**Caveat:** The Prābhākaras explicitly deny a supervising Lord and deny that the Veda teaches an existent absolute; any reading of their 'what is to be done' (kārya) as a name of one truth would be foreign to them.
