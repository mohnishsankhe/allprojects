The line of Madhyamaka exegesis that follows Buddhapālita's commentary on the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā as defended by Candrakīrti against Bhāviveka's criticism: emptiness is shown by reducing the opponent's positions to absurd consequences, without autonomous proofs, and no own-nature is admitted at any level. Śāntideva, Prajñākaramati, Jayānanda and Atiśa are counted in it by Tibetan doxographers, for whom (from Patsab Nyima Drak onward) it became the highest Buddhist view.

## Distinctive positions
- The Mādhyamika need only draw out the unwanted consequences (prasaṅga) of the opponent's own theses; he does not use autonomous inferences (svatantra-anumāna) whose subject and reasons would have to be established for both parties by their own nature (Candrakīrti, Prasannapadā ch.1).
- Nothing has own-nature even conventionally; the conventional is accepted only as the world accepts it (lokaprasiddhi).
- Each thing bears two natures, the one found by correct seeing (the ultimate) and the one found by false seeing (the conventional) (MA 6.23).
- Reflexive awareness (svasaṃvedana) and a storehouse consciousness are rejected even conventionally (MA 6; BCA 9.17–25).
- Śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas also realize the selflessness of phenomena (Candrakīrti, MA bhāṣya on 1.8).
- The ultimate is not within the domain of the intellect; the intellect is conventional (BCA 9.2).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** paramārtha-satya (ultimate truth), śūnyatā, niḥsvabhāvatā (lack of own-nature), tattva
**Descriptions:** the nature found by correct seeing, as against the nature found by false seeing, which is conventional truth (MA 6.23); the reality which is not in the domain of the intellect; the intellect is said to be conventional (BCA 9.2); the mere negation of own-nature, which implies nothing else in its place (non-implicative negation, prasajya-pratiṣedha)
**Negations:** things arise neither from themselves, nor from another, nor from both, nor without cause (Buddhapālita and Candrakīrti on MMK 1.1; MA 6.8–104); the self is not found in any of the seven ways the chariot is sought (MA 6.151ff); no own-nature even conventionally
**Relation to self:** Both the selflessness of persons and the selflessness of dharmas are emptiness; the view of a real self (satkāyadṛṣṭi) is the root of saṃsāra and is cut by seeing its object as unfindable (MA 6.120).
**Relation to world:** The world is not negated as it is known by worldly people; it is accepted by worldly consensus (lokaprasiddhi), without analysis, as conventional truth, while under analysis nothing is found.
**Caveat:** The name 'Prāsaṅgika' is a Tibetan classification. Candrakīrti and Śāntideva deny that emptiness is an entity or a thesis to be proved by autonomous inference; any 'one truth' reading as a positive absolute is rejected.
