# Mata (the 'Doctrine'; the Mata scriptures)

`lin:mata` · `skeleton` · confidence low

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Mataśāstra, Mata tradition
**Parent:** [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](kashmir-saivism.md)
**Regions:** Kashmir (as known to Abhinavagupta and Jayaratha)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: Known only through references by Abhinavagupta (c. 1000) and Jayaratha (13th c.); origin and dates unknown.; (confidence low)
**Status:** extinct

A Śaiva/Kaula scriptural tradition, now lost, that the Kashmirian exegetes count as one of the systems alongside Kula, Krama and Trika. What is known comes almost entirely from the Tantrāloka and Jayaratha's Viveka: its rejection of the samaya rules and of lineage-based exclusions, and its rank in the scriptural hierarchy. Its own texts, deities and teachers are not securely identified in this skeleton.

## Distinctive positions
- Its scriptures (mataśāstra) reject the samaya observances and similar rules as based on difference, teaching instead that one's own awareness, unbounded and complete, is to be realized (TĀ 4.262-263 with Jayaratha, who quotes the Mata line 'nirmaryādaṃ svasambodhaṃ sampūrṇaṃ budhyatām').
- They forbid restricting eating and the worship of the cakra to members of one's own lineage (santāna), because such division fragments the undivided reality (TĀ 4.268-270).
- In the Kashmirian scriptural hierarchy the Mata stands between the Dakṣiṇa (right-hand Bhairava) scriptures and the Kula (TĀ 13.300-301 and the verse quoted there by Jayaratha); a variant verse quoted in the Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa places it after the Kula and just below the Trika.
