# Mahāyāna

**Alternate names:** the Great Vehicle, Bodhisattvayāna, Buddhayāna, Agrayāna (the foremost vehicle), Vaipulya (the extensive sūtras), theg pa chen po (Tibetan), Dasheng 大乘 (Chinese), Daijō (Japanese)

**Dates:** Tradition's account: Taught by the Buddha Śākyamuni himself; some sūtras were withheld (e.g. kept by the nāgas) and brought forth later when there were beings able to receive them; Scholarly account: Mahāyāna sūtras appear from c. 1st c. BCE / 1st c. CE (first Chinese translations by Lokakṣema, c. 179 CE; Gāndhārī Prajñāpāramitā fragment c. 1st-2nd c. CE); composition continued to c. 5th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The 'Great Vehicle': the body of sūtras and the movement, within the Buddhist monastic and lay communities of India from around the turn of the common era, that takes the bodhisattva's path to full buddhahood for all beings as the highest aim, teaches the emptiness of all dharmas, skillful means, innumerable buddhas and buddha-fields, and (in some sūtras) the buddha-nature in all beings. It became the Buddhism of East Asia and Tibet. This umbrella entry covers the sūtra layer; the scholastic schools (Madhyamaka, Yogācāra), Pure Land, Chan/Zen, the Chinese schools and Vajrayāna have their own entries.

## Distinctive positions
- The goal is the full awakening of a buddha (anuttarā samyaksaṃbodhi) for the sake of all beings, not the personal nirvāṇa of the arhat; the practitioner is a bodhisattva who vows to liberate all beings
- All dharmas are empty (śūnya) of own-being (svabhāva), unborn and like an illusion — including the aggregates, the path and nirvāṇa itself (Prajñāpāramitā)
- The six (or ten) perfections, grounded in the perfection of wisdom and in skillful means, are the path
- The three vehicles are the Buddha's skillful means; there is ultimately one buddha-vehicle (Lotus, Śrīmālā) — or, per other sūtras, beings have fixed lineages (gotra) (Laṅkāvatāra, Saṃdhinirmocana)
- The Buddha is not only the historical teacher: his dharmakāya is suchness; his lifespan is immeasurable and his parinirvāṇa a skillful display (Lotus 16, Mahāparinirvāṇa); countless buddhas teach in buddha-fields in the ten directions
- All beings possess the tathāgatagarbha / buddha-nature (Tathāgatagarbha sūtras, Mahāparinirvāṇa)
- The sūtras themselves are objects of worship: where a sūtra is kept or taught is a shrine (caitya); copying, reciting and teaching them is supreme merit
- Saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, defilement and purity, are not two (Vimalakīrti); the bodhisattva does not abandon saṃsāra (non-abiding nirvāṇa)

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** suchness (tathatā), emptiness (śūnyatā), the reality-limit (bhūtakoṭi), the dharma-element / realm of dharmas (dharmadhātu), the nature of dharmas (dharmatā), the dharma-body (dharmakāya), non-arising (anutpāda), non-duality (advaya), unsurpassed perfect awakening (anuttarā samyaksaṃbodhi), nirvāṇa
**Descriptions:** the suchness of the Tathāgata and the suchness of all dharmas are one suchness, undivided (Aṣṭasāhasrikā 16); the Tathāgata is non-arising, the reality-limit, emptiness, suchness — which neither come nor go (Aṣṭa 31); all dharmas have the mark of emptiness: unborn, unceasing, not defiled, not pure, not deficient, not complete (Heart, s6); the Tathāgata is to be seen from the dharma; the guides have the dharma for body; dharmatā cannot be cognized (Vajracchedikā 26); the one buddha-vehicle (Lotus 2); the Tathāgata's lifespan is immeasurable (Lotus 16); entry into non-duality is Vimalakīrti's silence, where there is no movement of letters, sounds or ideas (Vimalakīrti 8.33)
**Negations:** not the least dharma is found in anuttarā samyaksaṃbodhi — therefore it is called so (Vajracchedikā 22); even nirvāṇa is like an illusion, like a dream; if there were anything higher than nirvāṇa that too would be like an illusion (Aṣṭa 2); in emptiness there is no form ... no suffering, origin, cessation, path; no knowing and no attaining (Heart s6-7); not existent, not non-existent; the middle between 'is' and 'is not' (Kāśyapaparivarta §60)
**Relation to self:** Persons and dharmas are both without self (pudgala- and dharma-nairātmya); the bodhisattva who has the notion of a self, a being, a soul or a person is not a bodhisattva (Vajracchedikā 3, 14). Some sūtras nonetheless name the buddha-nature 'self' (see ult:tathagatagarbha).
**Relation to world:** The world is empty, dependently arisen, like an illusion, dream or reflection; it is not annihilated either. Form is emptiness and emptiness is form (Heart s4); saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are not two (Vimalakīrti 8); the purity of a buddha-field follows the purity of the mind (Vimalakīrti 1.14).
**Caveat:** The Prajñāpāramitā denies that emptiness, suchness or nirvāṇa is an entity or a substance: they are 'names only', to be neither grasped nor rejected, and 'the view of emptiness' is itself declared incurable (Kāśyapaparivarta §64-65). The sūtras also speak of the Buddha's eternal dharmakāya and of compassionate buddhas and bodhisattvas, which is why 'both' is recorded; they reject any creator god and any reading of suchness as a Brahman-like self (Laṅkāvatāra p. 33, on the tīrthikas' ātman).
