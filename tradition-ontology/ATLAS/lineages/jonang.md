A Tibetan school centred on the Kālacakra tantra and on the view of 'other-emptiness' (gzhan stong). Its Kālacakra lineage runs from the 11th-century master Yumo Mikyö Dorje; the Jonang hermitage was founded by Künpang Thukje Tsöndrü (c. 1294). Dolpopa Sherab Gyaltsen (1292–1361) taught openly that the ultimate — buddha-nature, the dharmakāya — is permanent and empty only of adventitious phenomena other than itself, in his Mountain Doctrine and Fourth Council, and oversaw a new translation of the Kālacakra. Tāranātha (1575–1634) revived the school, wrote on other-emptiness, on Kālacakra practice and on the history of Buddhism in India. In the mid-17th century its Tsang seats were converted to Gelug and its writings restricted under the Fifth Dalai Lama's government; the tradition survived in Amdo (Dzamthang), and its view was revived more widely by the 19th-century Rimé masters.

## Distinctive positions
- Two truths: conventional phenomena are empty of their own nature (rang stong); the ultimate — the dharmatā, dharmakāya, buddha-nature — is empty only of what is other than it (gzhan stong), i.e. of the adventitious conventional phenomena, and is not empty of its own nature and qualities.
- The ultimate is permanent, stable, peaceful and eternal (rtag brtan ther zug g.yung drung) and is not a dependent arising.
- Buddha-nature is present in all beings complete with the buddha-qualities, which are uncovered, not newly produced (Ratnagotravibhāga 1.154-155 read literally).
- The third turning (the tathāgatagarbha sūtras and the Maitreya texts), the Kālacakra and the 'bodhisattva commentaries', and Nāgārjuna's hymns (the Dharmadhātustava) teach the definitive meaning; together they are the Great Madhyamaka (dbu ma chen po).
- The ālaya is twofold: the ālaya-consciousness (kun gzhi rnam shes), which is conventional, and the ālaya-wisdom (kun gzhi ye shes), which is ultimate.
- Dolpopa presents his teaching as the doctrine of the age of perfection (rdzogs ldan, kṛtayuga), a 'fourth council' restoring the Buddha's final intent.
- The Kālacakra six-branch vajra-yoga is the principal practice through which the other-empty ultimate — 'the emptiness endowed with all supreme aspects' — is directly realized.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** ultimate truth (don dam bden pa), buddha-nature, the essence of the sugatas (bde gshegs snying po), dharmakāya (chos sku), dharmatā / dharmadhātu (chos nyid / chos dbyings), other-emptiness (gzhan stong), the emptiness endowed with all supreme aspects (rnam kun mchog ldan gyi stong nyid), ālaya-wisdom (kun gzhi ye shes), Kālacakra, the primordial buddha (dus kyi 'khor lo, dang po'i sangs rgyas)
**Descriptions:** permanent, stable, peaceful and eternal (rtag brtan ther zug g.yung drung); self-arisen primordial wisdom, luminous, non-dual; endowed from the beginning with the inseparable buddha-qualities, which are uncovered, not produced; pervading all beings as space pervades; not a dependent arising; beyond the three times; possessing the perfections of purity, bliss, self and permanence (RGV 1.35 read as definitive)
**Negations:** empty of the adventitious stains — every conventional phenomenon — which are other than it; not empty of its own nature; not the mere non-affirming negation, the 'emptiness of annihilation' (chad stong) that is nothing whatsoever
**Relation to self:** The dharmakāya is the true self in the sense of the perfection of self; the imputed self of persons and phenomena does not exist. Buddha-nature is complete in every being.
**Relation to world:** The world of conventional phenomena is empty of its own nature, false and adventitious; it is 'other' than the ultimate, which it obscures but never taints.
**Caveat:** The Jonang affirm one ultimate, but insist that it is other than conventional phenomena and deny that the rangtong emptiness (mere absence of inherent existence) is the final ultimate. The rangtong schools — Gelug (Tsongkhapa, Khedrup), the Sakya scholar Gorampa and many others — reject a non-empty, permanent ultimate as the extreme of eternalism, close to the tīrthikas' self; the Jonang reject the equation of their dharmakāya with a tīrthika ātman. Not yet reconciled: see dsp:rangtong-shentong.
