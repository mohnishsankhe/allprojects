One of the Five Houses of Chan, named from Dongshan Liangjie and Caoshan Benji in the line of Qingyuan and Shitou, known for the five ranks and later for Hongzhi's silent illumination; source of Japanese Sōtō.

## Distinctive positions
- The five ranks of the real and the apparent (zheng-pian wuwei) and the five ranks of merit.
- Mutual interfusion and non-interfusion (huihu 回互) of principle and phenomena (Cantongqi).
- Silent illumination (mozhao) as the Song Caodong practice (Hongzhi).
- 'Precious Mirror Samādhi' (Baojing sanmei): the reflection and the one reflected.

**Transmissions given:** 
  - [Sōtō Zen](soto.md) — what: Rujing's line to Dōgen (and Dongming Huiri's line to Tōmyō Enichi)

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** the real (zheng 正), the numinous source (lingyuan 靈源)
**Descriptions:** 'The numinous source shines bright and pure; the branch streams flow in the dark' (Cantongqi).; The real and the apparent interpenetrate in five ranks (Dongshan).
**Negations:** 'Holding to phenomena is delusion; merging with principle is not yet awakening' (Cantongqi).
**Relation to self:** 'You are not it; it is just you' (Baojing sanmei).
**Relation to world:** Principle and phenomena interfuse and yet each abides in its place (huihu / bu huihu).
**Caveat:** Caodong's 'real' is not a separate substance: 'it' and 'you' are neither identical nor two.
