The fraternity of the Great Monastery at Anurādhapura, whose commentarial tradition Buddhaghosa rendered into Pali in the 5th century and which, after the 12th-century unification of the Sri Lankan saṅgha, became the normative Theravāda.

## Distinctive positions
- The Pali Tipiṭaka including the seven Abhidhamma books is the Buddha's word; the Sinhala commentaries (rendered into Pali by Buddhaghosa and others) are the authoritative explanation.
- Rejection of the Vetulya (Vaitulya) texts accepted at the Abhayagiri as not the Buddha's word.
- The path as the seven purifications and the forty meditation subjects as systematized in the Visuddhimagga.

**Transmissions given:** 
  - [Theravāda](theravada.md) — what: the Pali commentaries (aṭṭhakathā) and the Visuddhimagga as the orthodox exegesis of the canon; Sri Lankan ordination lineages sent to Burma and Thailand

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** nibbāna, asaṅkhata-dhātu (the unconditioned element), amata (the deathless)
**Descriptions:** the one unconditioned dhamma among the four ultimate realities (citta, cetasika, rūpa, nibbāna); single in its own nature (sabhāva) but described as with residue (sa-upādisesa) and without residue (anupādisesa), and as empty, signless and desireless; an object (ārammaṇa) of the path and fruition consciousness, not a mere absence
**Negations:** not arisen, not ageing, not dying (asaṅkhata); not a self: 'there is extinction, but no one extinguished' (Vism XVI, PTS p. 513)
**Relation to self:** no self is found in or behind it; all dhammas including nibbāna are not-self
**Relation to world:** the cessation of the conditioned; entirely transcending the world of the five aggregates
**Caveat:** The commentarial tradition would reject any reading of nibbāna as a cosmic ground, a creator or a self; the 'one truth' reading applies only in the sense that nibbāna is the single unconditioned element.
