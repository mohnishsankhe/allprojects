# Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)

`lin:hatha-yoga` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Haṭhayoga, Haṭhavidyā ('the science of haṭha'), Ghaṭastha-yoga (Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā: 'yoga of the pot/body'), the yoga of the Siddhas (siddhamata)
**Founders:** [Ādinātha](../teachers/adinatha.md)
**Key teachers:** [Ādinātha](../teachers/adinatha.md), [Matsyendranātha](../teachers/matsyendranatha.md), [Gorakṣanātha](../teachers/goraksanatha.md), [Dattātreya](../teachers/dattatreya.md), [Svātmārāma](../teachers/svatmarama.md), [Gheraṇḍa](../teachers/gheranda.md), [Brahmānanda (author of the Jyotsnā)](../teachers/brahmananda-jyotsna.md), [Śrīnivāsa (author of the Haṭharatnāvalī)](../teachers/srinivasa-hatharatnavali.md), [Sundaradeva](../teachers/sundaradeva.md), [Jayatarāma](../teachers/jayatarama.md), [Yājñavalkya](../teachers/yajnavalkya.md), [Vasiṣṭha](../teachers/vasistha.md), [Pūrṇānanda](../teachers/purnananda.md), [Virūpa](../teachers/virupa.md)
**Regions:** pan-Indian, western and central India (early texts), Vārāṇasī and the Gangetic plain, the Himalaya and Nepal, Bengal (Ṣaṭcakranirūpaṇa), Mysore (Śrītattvanidhi)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: taught by Ādinātha (Śiva) and handed down by Matsyendra, Gorakṣa and the Mahāsiddhas (HYP 1.1–9); Scholarly account: earliest text c. 11th c. CE (Amṛtasiddhi); classical compilation c. 1450 (Haṭhapradīpikā); large compilations 17th–18th c.; living in ascetic orders; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The textual tradition of haṭha ('force') yoga, from the Amṛtasiddhi (c. 11th c.) through the Haṭhapradīpikā (c. 1450) to the great 17th–18th-c. compilations. It teaches postures, breath-retention, seals and locks, cleansing acts and absorption in the inner sound as means to rājayoga, the mindless state of samādhi, and aims at liberation while living and the conquest of death. Its lineage is traced to Ādinātha (Śiva), Matsyendra and Gorakṣa; its texts circulated among Nāth, Daśanāmī, Rāmānandī and householder practitioners, and borrowed their metaphysics from Advaita, Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava and (at its origin) Buddhist tantric sources.

## Distinctive positions
- Haṭha is taught solely for the sake of rājayoga (samādhi); rājayoga and haṭha each need the other (HYP 1.2, 2.76).
- Mind and breath are bound together: stilling the breath stills the mind; when breath enters the central channel the mind dissolves (HYP 2.2, 4.21–25).
- Death comes from the fall of bindu, life from its retention; the moon's nectar dripping from the palate is consumed by the sun at the navel (HYP 3.77, 3.88).
- Kuṇḍalinī, the goddess asleep at the mouth of the central channel, must be awakened; then the knots are pierced and the mind becomes supportless (HYP 3.1–5).
- Practice alone, not scriptural learning, sectarian garb or talk, is the cause of success; anyone may succeed — young, old, sick (HYP 1.64–66; DYŚ 37–42).
- The four yogas — mantra, laya, haṭha, rāja — and the four stages — ārambha, ghaṭa, paricaya, niṣpatti (DYŚ 9–10; HYP 4.69).
- Samādhi has many names that denote one state: rājayoga, unmanī, manonmanī, amaratva, laya, śūnyāśūnya, amanaska, sahaja, turyā (HYP 4.3–4).
- Secrecy: the teaching is potent when kept secret and powerless when divulged; it must come from a guru (HYP 1.11, 3.129).
- The body can be perfected and made to cheat time (kālavañcana) — a goal the Amanaska and some Vedāntins subordinate or reject.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Śiva / Ādinātha, the guru whose nature is nāda, bindu and kalā, nirañjana, the stainless; paraṃ padam, the supreme state, paraṃ brahma, the soundless; paramātman, parameśvara, the formless end of the tattvas, the one eternal knowledge without beginning or end (Śiva Saṃhitā 1.1), Brahman as being, consciousness and bliss; the world full of Viṣṇu (Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā 7.4, 7.18)
**Descriptions:** Whatever is heard as nāda is śakti; the formless end of the tattvas is the supreme Lord; the soundless is the supreme Brahman (HYP 4.101–102).; Stainless, unmoving, eternal, actionless, qualityless, great, consciousness of space, bliss (GŚ 99).; The supreme abode of Viṣṇu, where the mind dissolves within the resonance of the unstruck sound (HYP 4.100).
**Negations:** niḥśabdam — soundless (HYP 4.101); nirātaṅka, nirālamba, niṣprapañca, nirāśraya, nirāmaya, nirākāra (GŚ 92); not smell, taste, form, touch or sound; neither self nor other (HYP 4.109)
**Relation to self:** In samādhi the individual self and the supreme self become one, as salt dissolves in water (HYP 4.5–7); 'I am Brahman and no other' (GS 7.4).
**Relation to world:** Duality is only what the mind sees; through unmanī it is not perceived (HYP 4.61); the world is the play of māyā (SS 1.64–67) — for the Vedāntic haṭha texts. The body is a microcosm of the world (SS 2.1–5).
**Caveat:** The haṭha texts are practice manuals whose metaphysics is borrowed: Advaita in HYP 4 and Śiva Saṃhitā 1, a Vaiṣṇava theism joined to Advaita in the Gheraṇḍa, Śaiva in the Khecarīvidyā, Buddhist tantric in the Amṛtasiddhi. The HYP itself says of laya 'let this be called liberation or not' (4.30, 4.78), subordinating doctrine to attained state. The Amṛtasiddhi's Buddhist setting would not accept a Brahman–ātman reading of the one truth; the Amanaska subordinates all methods to the mindless state rather than to a named ultimate.
