# The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Cittar, Siddhar, the eighteen Siddhars (patiṉeṇ cittarkaḷ), Tamil Siddha tradition

**Regions:** Tamil Nadu (Potikai/Potiyil hills, Palani, Kolli hills, Caturakiri, Tiruvaṇṇāmalai, Chidambaram), Kerala (southern Travancore), northern Sri Lanka (Jaffna)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: the Siddhars are held to be ancient and, in their jīva-samādhis and hill retreats, still living; Scholarly account: named authors and texts c. 10th–18th c.; most of the song corpus is usually placed after c. 1400, the medical-alchemical corpus later still; (confidence low)
**Status:** living

A loose, non-institutional stream of Tamil perfected ones (cittar) — yogins, poets, physicians and alchemists — counted conventionally as eighteen, with lists that vary. They trace their knowledge to Śiva through Nandi, Agastya and Tirumūlar, teach an inner Śaiva-Śākta yoga of breath, the root centre and the goddess Vālai aimed at a perfected, deathless body and union with Civam, and in their songs attack caste, idols, temples, pilgrimage and Brahmanical ritual. Their songs (Sivavākkiyar, Pāmbāṭṭi, Iṭaikkāṭar, Kuṭampai, Aḻukaṇṇi, Kaṭuveḷi, Pattinattār, Pattirakiriyār) are anthologized as the Siddhar songs (Cittar pāṭalkaḷ); Tāyumāṉavar (18th c.) joins Siddha, Vedānta and Siddhānta. Siddha medicine is their medical branch.

## Distinctive positions
- The body is the temple and the instrument of liberation: it is to be preserved and perfected (kāya-siddhi, kāya kaṟpam), not despised — 'when the body perishes the life perishes and true knowledge is not reached' (TM 724).
- God (Civam) is within; love itself is Śiva (TM 270); the stone image, temple, pilgrimage, Vedic recitation and ritual without inner knowledge are mocked (Sivavākkiyar; Kaṭuveḷi 15; Pattirakiriyār 16; Kuṭampai).
- Caste is rejected: 'one family, one God' (TM 2104); the castes said to be unequal are one (Kaṭuveḷi 32).
- Breath (vāci), the root centre (mūlam), the six supports (ātāram), the goddess Vālai/Maṉōṉmaṇi and the open space above the crown (veṭṭa veḷi) frame an inner yoga that culminates in 'dying without dying' (cākāmal cātal).
- Teaching in veiled, coded language (paripāṣai, maṟaipporuḷ), with secrecy around alchemy and medicine.
- Mastery of the powers (citti) is claimed, yet stillness of mind is held harder and higher (Tāyumāṉavar; Kuṭampai 2).
- The 96 principles (toṇṇūṟṟāṟu tattuvam) map body, mind and bondage; medicine and liberation share one anatomy.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Civam, Parāparam, cōti / parañcōti (the Light), veṭṭa veḷi (the open space), Vālai / Maṉōṉmaṇi (as Śakti)
**Descriptions:** self-luminous everywhere, dwelling in the heart of those who love (Kaṭuveḷi 34); love itself is Civam (TM 270); the undivided fullness beyond qualities (Rāmatēvar 1); light within like the inner light of a ruby (Aḻukaṇṇi 39); without here or there, everywhere as light, fullness of bliss, filled with grace (Tāyumāṉavar)
**Negations:** not the planted stone (Sivavākkiyar); not seen by the four Vedas (Pattirakiriyār 48); not reached by pilgrimage or holy bathing (Kaṭuveḷi 15); beyond elements, senses, qualities and instruments (Paṭṭiṉattār, Tiruvēkampamālai 31)
**Relation to self:** Within the body and the heart; the seeker longs to stand as 'I am He' (Pattirakiriyār 219) and asks 'who am I, who are you'; guru and Lord are one; devotional and non-dual voices coexist.
**Relation to world:** The world and body are real enough to be perfected (TM 724–725) yet impermanent, a bubble and a leaking pot; macrocosm and microcosm mirror each other.
**Caveat:** The Siddhars are not one school: some speak as Śaiva devotees, some as non-dualists of the void, some as alchemists of the deathless body. They would reject any reading that places the ultimate in a temple image or makes caste or scripture a condition of reaching it.
