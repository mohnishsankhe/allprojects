# Mūlasarvāstivāda

**Family:** ascetic
**Alternate names:** Mūlasarvāstivādin, Genben shuo yiqie you bu 根本說一切有部 (Chinese), gzhi thams cad yod par smra ba (Tibetan)
**Parent:** [Sarvāstivāda](sarvastivada.md)
**Regions:** Kashmir and Gilgit, Nālandā and eastern India (Yijing), Tibet, Mongolia, Bhutan, the Himalaya (through its Vinaya)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: The 'root' (mūla) Sarvāstivāda, whose Vinaya was recited in the Buddha's time (Tibetan tradition); Scholarly account: Vinaya compiled c. 1st–4th c. CE; named as a school from the 7th c. (Yijing); living through its Vinaya; (confidence low)
**Status:** living

The Mūlasarvāstivāda is known chiefly through its enormous Vinaya, preserved in Tibetan, partly in Chinese (Yijing) and in Sanskrit from the Gilgit manuscripts. Its relation to the Sarvāstivāda (identical, a later reorganisation, or a separate Mathurā-based group) is disputed. Its Vinaya is the living monastic code of Tibetan Buddhism.

## Distinctive positions
- A very large Vinaya in seventeen sections (vastu) with a Vibhaṅga, Kṣudrakavastu and Uttaragrantha, rich in narratives (avadāna).
- The only Indian Vinaya transmitted in Tibetan; the basis of all Tibetan monastic ordination.
- Doctrinally at one with the Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma (the Kośa is the Abhidharma of the Tibetan curriculum).
- The bhikṣuṇī ordination of this Vinaya was never established in Tibet; whether it can be supplied from another Vinaya lineage is debated (recent).
- Prātimokṣa: 258 rules in the Sanskrit text (the Tibetan tradition speaks of 253 vows of a gelong) — low confidence on both counts.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** nirvāṇa
**Descriptions:** Doctrinally follows the Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma of the Kośa: nirvāṇa as cessation through discernment.
**Negations:** No self (AK 3.18).
**Relation to self:** As in the Sarvāstivāda.
**Relation to world:** As in the Sarvāstivāda.
**Caveat:** The Mūlasarvāstivāda is known chiefly as a Vinaya lineage; in Tibet its monks hold Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna views of the ultimate, which belong to other lineages' entries.
