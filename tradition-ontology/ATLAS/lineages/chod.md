The practice tradition of 'cutting' ego-clinging founded by the Tibetan woman master Machig Labdrön (11th–12th c.), drawing on the Perfection of Wisdom and on instructions from the Indian siddha Padampa Sangye. It is known for its visualized offering of one's own body to guests of four kinds and for practice in charnel grounds and haunted places, accompanied by drum and thighbone trumpet. It never formed a separate monastic school but entered every Tibetan tradition; the Rimé masters counted Shije and Chöd as one of the eight great practice lineages.

## Distinctive positions
- What is cut (gcod) is ego-clinging (bdag 'dzin), the root of the mind's afflictions; cutting it cuts all four māras.
- The four māras of Chöd — the obstructive (thogs bcas), the unobstructive (thogs med), the māra of elation (dga' brod) and the māra of conceit (snyems byed) — are all contained in the last, the self-fixation.
- Gods and demons (lha 'dre) are the play of one's own mind; as long as there is ego-clinging there are demons, and when it is cut there are none.
- The body, the object of the strongest attachment, is offered in visualization as food to four kinds of guests (the Three Jewels, the protectors, the six classes of beings, and harmful spirits and karmic creditors) — generosity joined with the view of emptiness.
- Practice is done in frightening places — charnel grounds, haunted sites (gnyan sa) — so that fear itself exposes and cuts self-grasping.
- Its view is the Perfection of Wisdom; Chöd presents itself as the practical application of the Prajñāpāramitā, combining sūtra and mantra.
- The tradition's account holds that Chöd is the only Dharma that went from Tibet to India: Indian scholars who came to test Machig accepted her teaching and carried it back.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** the Great Mother, Prajñāpāramitā (yum chen mo), the meaning of the perfection of wisdom (shes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa), Mahāmudrā, the natural state of mind (phyag rgya chen po)
**Descriptions:** the unborn, empty nature of mind in which ego-clinging has been cut; the natural state resting without fixation once the four māras are cut
**Negations:** gods and demons (lha 'dre) have no existence apart from the mind; the self that is clung to has no root
**Relation to self:** The self to be cut is ego-clinging (bdag 'dzin); when it is cut, the māras are cut; the Great Mother is the nature of one's own mind.
**Relation to world:** Appearances, including terrifying ones, are the display of mind; offered without attachment they become the means of cutting clinging.
**Caveat:** Chöd presents itself as the practice of the Perfection of Wisdom, not as a separate philosophical system; practitioners explain the ultimate according to their own school (rangtong, shentong, Mahāmudrā or Dzogchen). The Great Mother is both the personified deity and emptiness itself.
