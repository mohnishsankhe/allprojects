**Dates:** Tradition's account: taught by the Buddha at Dhānyakaṭaka to King Sucandra of Śambhala; abridged by Mañjuśrīyaśas and commented on by Puṇḍarīka (the Vimalaprabhā); brought to India from Śambhala; Scholarly account: Laghukālacakra and Vimalaprabhā composed c. 1025–1040; epoch year 1027 (start of the sixty-year cycle); (confidence moderate)
**Status:** absorbed

The last major Indian tantric system (11th c.), the 'Wheel of Time', which correlates cosmos, body and deity, teaches the six-branch yoga culminating in empty form and immutable bliss, gives its own astronomy and calendar (epoch 1027) and the Śambhala narrative; taught by Kālacakrapāda, Nāropa and Abhayākaragupta and transmitted to Tibet (Jonang, Gelug, Sakya).

## Distinctive positions
- The outer (cosmos and time), inner (body, channels, winds) and other/alternative (the deity and maṇḍala) Kālacakras correspond; liberation reverses the flow of time within the body.
- The six-branch yoga (pratyāhāra, dhyāna, prāṇāyāma, dhāraṇā, anusmṛti, samādhi) leads through ten signs to empty form (śūnyatābimba) joined with immutable bliss (akṣarasukha) (Sekoddeśa 24–27, 146).
- The Ādibuddha, the primordial buddha, is the non-dual gnosis of immutable bliss.
- Śambhala's kings will in the future defeat the barbarian (mleccha) religion and restore the dharma (a prophecy in the first chapter).
- Its own astronomy and calendar, and a prophecy of the kalkin rulers of Śambhala.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Ādibuddha (the primordial buddha), akṣarasukha (immutable bliss), Kālacakra (time and wheel = means and wisdom), śūnyatābimba (empty form)
**Descriptions:** the image arisen from emptiness is the cause; the bliss born of the immutable is the result; each seals the other (Sekoddeśa 146); the fourth consecration is the ultimate, the other three are conventional (Sekoddeśa 8)
**Negations:** the stain of mind is neither adventitious nor long-lasting, neither born without mind nor abiding in it (Sekoddeśa 129)
**Relation to self:** Kālacakra is the non-dual union within the practitioner's body of the unchanging bliss and the empty form of the deity.
**Relation to world:** Outer (cosmos, time), inner (body) and alternative (deity) wheels correspond; the practitioner reverses the movement of time in the channels.
**Caveat:** The Vimalaprabhā insists that the Ādibuddha is not a creator god and not a self; it is emptiness endowed with all aspects.
