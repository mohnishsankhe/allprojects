The panth founded by Dādū Dayāl (1544–1603) in Rajasthan, with its chief seat at Naraina. It preserved Dādū's Vāṇī and, in its anthologies, the western recension of Kabīr and other Sants; its scholars (Rajjab, Sundardās) gave the Sant teaching systematic and learned form.

## Distinctive positions
- Remembrance of the nirguṇa Rām, the one God of Hindu and Turk, without idols or temples.
- Impartiality (nipakh) and the middle way (madh): neither Hindu nor Turk, neither indulgence nor harsh austerity.
- The guru met 'in the unseen' (gaib) and devotion to the guru; the Vāṇī itself is honoured in the panth's seats.
- Five divisions in the later panth: Khālsā (the Naraina seat), Virakt (wandering renunciants), Uttarādhī, Khākī and the armed Nāgā.
- Anthologies arranged by aṅg (topic), which preserved much of the Rajasthani Kabīr.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Rām, Nirañjan, Alakh / Allāh (as the one Lord of Hindu and Turk)
**Descriptions:** the stainless one saluted first in the Vāṇī; met in the unseen; Brahman one without a second (Sundardās)
**Negations:** neither Hindu nor Turk; not in temple or mosque
**Relation to self:** The Lord is within the body, which is the true mosque and temple; the self erased, Hari remains.
**Relation to world:** One Lord of all beings; hence no enmity toward any.
**Caveat:** Sundardās's Advaita and Dādū's devotional voice coexist; the panth would reject a reading that erases devotion to the guru and the Name.
