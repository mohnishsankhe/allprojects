# Tamil Jain tradition

`lin:tamil-jain` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Family:** ascetic
**Alternate names:** Tamil Jains (Nayiṉār), Tamiḻ Camaṇam
**Parent:** [Digambara](digambara.md)
**Key teachers:** [Kundakunda](../teachers/kundakunda.md), [Tiruttakkatēvar](../teachers/tiruttakkatevar.md), [Tōlāmoḻittēvar](../teachers/tolamolittevar.md), [Pavaṇanti](../teachers/pavanandi.md), [Vāmaṉa Muṉivar](../teachers/vamana-muni.md)
**Regions:** Tamil Nadu (Maturai hills, Kāñcīpuram and Tiruparuttikkuṉṟam, Ciṅkavaram, Mēlcittāmūr, Tirumalai)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Tamil Jains trace their teachers to Bhadrabāhu's migration and to Kundakunda (Elācārya); inscriptions in Tamil-Brāhmī in hill caves are claimed as the earliest Jain presence; Scholarly account: Tamil-Brāhmī cave inscriptions c. 2nd c. BCE onward; the Jain literary flowering c. 5th–13th c. CE; decline after the Śaiva revival (7th c. onward); (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The Digambara Jain community of the Tamil country, whose monks and poets produced much of classical Tamil didactic and narrative literature and whose memory of conflict with the Śaiva Nāyaṉmārs is recorded on both sides.

## Distinctive positions
- Digambara in doctrine and practice; the monastic seats (maṭhas) at Mēlcittāmūr and Arahantagiri (Tirumalai) with their bhaṭṭārakas
- a major literary contribution to Tamil: the Nālaṭiyār and other didactic works, the epics Cīvakacintāmaṇi and Nīlakēci, grammar (Naṉṉūl) and prosody (Yāpparuṅkalam)


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Arukaṉ (the Arhat), Jiṉaṉ / Iṟaivaṉ (the Lord, as the Jina), vīṭu (release)
**Descriptions:** the Arhat saluted at the opening of Tamil Jain works; release (vīṭu) won by virtue (aṟam) and renunciation (tuṟavu)
**Negations:** not a creator
**Relation to self:** Release is the soul's freedom from karma through virtue, austerity and renunciation.
**Relation to world:** Wealth, youth and the body are impermanent; the world is real but not to be clung to.
**Caveat:** The Jain tradition denies a single all-encompassing reality and a creator: liberated souls are infinitely many and remain distinct, and the universe of six substances is uncreated. It can accept 'one truth, many names' only in its own way — as many-sidedness (anekānta) about a plural reality — not as the identity of all souls in one substance.
