The yoga taught by T. Krishnamacharya (1888–1989) at Mysore and Chennai and carried worldwide by his students (Iyengar, Pattabhi Jois, Desikachar, Indra Devi); the principal source of modern postural yoga, combining Patañjali's framework with breath-linked āsana sequences.

## Distinctive positions
- Āsana practised with coordinated breath in sequenced movement (vinyāsa); prāṇāyāma and meditation in their place in the eight limbs.
- Practice adapted to the individual and the stage of life (viniyoga: śikṣaṇa, rakṣaṇa, adhyātmika krama; cikitsā for the sick) — as taught by Desikachar.
- Claimed textual sources (Nāthamuni's Yoga Rahasya, Vāmana Ṛṣi's Yoga Kuruṇṭa) that are not independently extant: the tradition's account only.
- Yoga taught to women and to non-brāhmaṇas, and in Western countries through his students.

## The ultimate in this lineage

**Names:** Īśvara / Nārāyaṇa
**Descriptions:** Krishnamacharya, a Śrīvaiṣṇava, taught devotion to the Lord (Īśvara-praṇidhāna) as the summit of yoga (recalled; low confidence).
**Relation to self:** The seer (puruṣa) distinct from the mind, devoted to the Lord.
**Relation to world:** The world real, the field of practice.
**Caveat:** Most of the lineage's students taught āsana without a fixed metaphysics; this view represents Krishnamacharya himself only.
