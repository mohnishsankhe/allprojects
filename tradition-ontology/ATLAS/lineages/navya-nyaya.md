**Dates:** Scholarly account: from Gaṅgeśa (first half of the 14th c.) to the present; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

Navya-Nyāya ('New Nyāya') begins with Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya's Tattvacintāmaṇi in Mithilā (14th c.) and flourished there and, from Raghunātha Śiromaṇi (c. 1500), in Navadvīpa in Bengal. It restates Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika doctrine in a precise technical language for analysing cognitions and their contents; this language became the common idiom of later Sanskrit learned discourse (Vedānta, Mīmāṃsā, grammar, law and poetics).

## Distinctive positions
- Organizes philosophy around the four means of knowledge (the four khaṇḍas of the Tattvacintāmaṇi).
- Analyses every cognition into qualificand (viśeṣya), qualifier (prakāra) and relation (saṃsarga), and specifies properties by their delimitors (avacchedaka), counterpositives (pratiyogin) and relations, yielding a precise technical language.
- Gives exact definitions of pervasion (vyāpti) — testing and rejecting five provisional ones before its own — and of consideration (parāmarśa), subjecthood (pakṣatā) and the adjunct (upādhi).
- Recognizes extraordinary sense-contacts (via the universal, via a prior cognition, and yogic) to explain the grasp of pervasion, some errors, and yogic perception.
- Validity of cognition is extrinsic (Gaṅgeśa's Prāmāṇyavāda).
- Liberation is the absolute cessation of pain, defined technically as a destruction of pain not co-temporal with any prior absence of pain in the same self (Muktivāda).
- The denotative power (śakti) of a word is the Lord's convention that 'this meaning is to be understood from this word'; verbal cognition requires expectancy, compatibility, proximity and intention.
- Raghunātha Śiromaṇi re-examines the traditional list of categories (Padārthatattvanirūpaṇa).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Īśvara, Paramātman, Kṛṣṇa (in Viśvanātha's invocation)
**Descriptions:** The one omniscient supreme self; His cognition, desire and effort are eternal (Tarkasaṅgraha).; His convention 'from this word this meaning is to be understood' is the denotative power of words; all Vedic statements are valid because He spoke them (Tarkasaṅgraha).; Proved as the maker of earth, sprouts and the like because they are effects (Gaṅgeśa; Viśvanātha).; Viśvanātha salutes Kṛṣṇa as the seed of the tree of saṃsāra (Bhāṣāpariccheda 1).
**Negations:** not the material cause of the world; no non-eternal cognition (His cognition is eternal)
**Relation to self:** The supreme self is one of the two kinds of self; individual selves are distinct in each body, all-pervading and eternal (Tarkasaṅgraha).
**Relation to world:** Maker (efficient cause) of all effects; the categories are real and exactly definable.
**Caveat:** As for Nyāya; in addition, Navya-Nyāya's analysis never admits a featureless or indeterminable ultimate, and it defends the exact definability of categories against Advaita's claim that they are indeterminable.
