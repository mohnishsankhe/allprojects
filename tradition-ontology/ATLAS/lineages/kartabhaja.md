The Kartābhajā is a Bengali sect founded in the late 18th c. around Āulcānd, held to be Caitanya returned, and organized by Rāmśaraṇ Pāl and his wife Satī Mā at Ghoshpara; it worships the Kartā (Master) in the living guru, admits all castes and Muslims, keeps its teaching secret behind outward conformity, and sings the Bhāber Gīt.

## Distinctive positions
- God is the Kartā, the Master, present as the living guru (Mahāśay); 'worship the human' (mānuṣ bhajan).
- Open to all castes and to Muslims; caste distinctions are void within the fellowship.
- A double life: 'in the world, worldly custom; with the true guru, oneness' — outward conformity, inner Kartābhajā practice.
- The Bhāber Gīt (Songs of Bhāva) as scripture, rich in the mercantile imagery of Calcutta (bazaar, company, trade).
- The Dol Pūrṇimā festival at Ghoshpara, with healing at the Himsāgar tank and the pomegranate tree of Satī Mā.
- Hereditary Kartā of the Pāl family; disciples (barātī) pay dues through the Mahāśays.

## The ultimate in this lineage

**Names:** the Kartā (Master), Āulcānd / Caitanya, sahaja mānuṣa
**Descriptions:** the one Master present in the living guru; hidden in the human form
**Negations:** not the many gods of outward custom
**Relation to self:** The Kartā is found in the human, above all the guru.
**Relation to world:** Outward custom is kept for the world's sake; the truth is secret.
**Caveat:** The tradition's double life means its outward Hindu observances should not be read as its view of the ultimate.
