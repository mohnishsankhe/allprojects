# Mahīśāsaka

`lin:mahisasaka` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Family:** ascetic
**Alternate names:** Mahiṃsāsaka (Pali), Huadi bu 化地部 (Chinese)
**Parent:** [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](early-buddhism.md)
**Regions:** North-west India, Sri Lanka (Faxian obtained its Vinaya there), Andhra (inscriptions)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Split from the Sarvāstivāda (Vasumitra) or from the Theravāda (Dīpavaṃsa); Scholarly account: c. 3rd c. BCE – c. 5th c. CE; (confidence low)
**Status:** extinct

The Mahīśāsaka was a Sthavira-derived school whose Five-Part Vinaya was brought from Sri Lanka by Faxian and translated into Chinese in 423–424. Vasumitra records a first group holding that only the present exists and a later group closer to Sarvāstivāda.

## Distinctive positions
- Past and future do not exist; the present and the unconditioned exist (Vasumitra).
- There is no intermediate existence (Vasumitra).
- Since the Buddha is included in the Saṅgha, a gift to the Saṅgha yields great fruit, not a separate gift to the Buddha (Vasumitra).
- Stream-enterers can fall back; arhats cannot (Vasumitra; low confidence).
- Nine unconditioned dharmas including the 'suchness' of good, bad and neutral dharmas, of the path and of dependent origination (Vasumitra; low confidence on the list).
- The Five-Part Vinaya (Wufen lü) with 251 rules for bhikṣus (low confidence on the count).

**Transmissions given:**
