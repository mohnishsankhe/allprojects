The initiatory lineage of kriyā yoga stemming from Lahiri Mahasaya of Varanasi (initiated, in the tradition's account, by Mahāvatār Bābājī in 1861), carried by Sri Yukteswar and spread worldwide by Paramahansa Yogananda's Autobiography of a Yogi and the Self-Realization Fellowship.

## Distinctive positions
- Kriyā: a breath-and-energy technique given only at initiation, by which the yogi directs the life current up and down the spine through six centres; the technique is kept secret (restricted here).
- Householder yoga: Lahiri Mahasaya lived as a family man and gave kriyā to people of all castes and creeds.
- The claimed antiquity of kriyā (BhG 4.29, 5.27–28; YS 2.1) and the deathless Bābājī are the tradition's account.
- Sri Yukteswar: the harmony of Hindu and Christian scripture; a 24,000-year cycle of ascending and descending yugas.
- Yogananda: Self-realization as knowing in body, mind and soul one's oneness with God; the SRF line of gurus includes Kṛṣṇa and Jesus.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** God (Parambrahma), the Eternal Father, Kūṭastha Caitanya (Christ consciousness), Aum, the Word
**Descriptions:** Sri Yukteswar: God is the one eternal substance, from whom the Word (Aum) and creation proceed.; Yogananda: Self-realization is knowing in body, mind and soul one's oneness with the omnipresence of God.
**Relation to self:** The soul is an individualized reflection of Spirit; Self-realization reveals oneness with God.
**Relation to world:** Creation is God's dream and light; the cycle of yugas shapes human understanding.
**Caveat:** The lineage harmonizes Hindu and Christian scripture; a one-truth reading suits it but it would keep God's personal aspect and the guru's role.
