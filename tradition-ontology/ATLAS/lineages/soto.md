The Japanese Caodong lineage founded by Dōgen (1200–1253) after training with Tiantong Rujing, centred on just sitting as the enactment of original awakening, expanded by Keizan (1268–1325) into Japan's largest Zen denomination.

## Distinctive positions
- Just sitting (shikantaza) as the whole of practice, not a means to a later awakening.
- Oneness of practice and realization (shushō ittō): sitting is itself the enactment of buddhahood.
- Dropping off body and mind (shinjin datsuraku) as Dōgen's account of his awakening under Rujing.
- Being-time (uji), impermanence as buddha-nature (mujō busshō), and the whole world as the 'kōan realized' (genjōkōan).
- Deep faith in cause and effect (jinshin inga) against readings that deny karma after awakening.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** buddha-nature (busshō), the realized kōan (genjōkōan), being-time (uji)
**Descriptions:** 'Whole being is buddha-nature' (Busshō).; Practice and realization are one: sitting is itself the realization of buddhahood (Bendōwa).
**Negations:** Buddha-nature is not a seed awaiting conditions nor a permanent self; impermanence is buddha-nature (Busshō).
**Relation to self:** The self forgotten is authenticated by the myriad dharmas (Genjōkōan).
**Relation to world:** Mountains, waters, time and all beings are the presencing of buddha-nature.
**Caveat:** Dōgen rejects the 'Śreṇika heresy' of an eternal mind-nature separate from a perishing body (Bendōwa, Sokushin zebutsu); Sōtō would reject reading buddha-nature as a hidden essence.
