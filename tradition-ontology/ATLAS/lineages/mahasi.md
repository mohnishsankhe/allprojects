The intensive insight method of Mahāsi Sayadaw (1904–1982), based on the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta and the Visuddhimagga's insight knowledges, taught at the Sāsana Yeiktha from 1949 and spread across Theravāda Asia and to the West.

## Distinctive positions
- Bare, moment-to-moment noting of whatever arises — beginning with the rising and falling of the abdomen and extending to all sensations, thoughts and actions — develops momentary concentration (khaṇika-samādhi) sufficient for insight without prior absorption (the vipassanā-yānika or dry-insight vehicle).
- Intensive retreat with alternating sitting and walking, continuous noting in daily activities and regular interviews with the teacher.
- The meditator passes through the insight knowledges in the order described in the Visuddhimagga and Paṭisambhidāmagga, which Mahāsi described from practice in The Progress of Insight.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** nibbāna
**Descriptions:** experienced at the path and fruition moments as the cessation of all formations noted up to that point; afterwards it can be re-entered as fruition attainment
**Relation to self:** noting reveals only arising and passing processes, no self who notes
**Relation to world:** the cessation of the noted object and the noting mind
**Caveat:** The tradition describes nibbāna by the knowledges leading to it, not as a metaphysical ground.
