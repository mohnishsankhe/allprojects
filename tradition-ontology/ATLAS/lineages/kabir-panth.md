The Kabīr Panth is the organized following of Kabīr, with monastic and householder members, sādhus and mahants, initiation and ritual. It venerates Kabīr as the incarnate True Being, which stands in some tension with Kabīr's own rejection of ritual and of worshipping persons; its Dharamdāsī branch developed an elaborate cosmogony of Kāl Nirañjan and the Sat Puruṣ.

## Distinctive positions
- Kabīr is the incarnation of the Sat Puruṣ (the True Being) who descends in every age to free souls (haṃs) from Kāl (in the Dharamdāsī literature: as Sat Sukṛt, Munīndra, Karuṇāmay and Kabīr in the four ages).
- A cosmogony (Anurāg Sāgar) in which Kāl Nirañjan, a fallen son of the Sat Puruṣ, creates and rules the three worlds and binds souls; Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheś are his sons.
- Initiation (dīkṣā) with a kaṇṭhī necklace; in the Dharamdāsī line also the pān-parvānā and the caukā rite.
- Two main branches: the celibate Kabīr Chaurā line in Varanasi, whose scripture is the Bījak, and the Dharamdāsī line of 42 generations of householder ācāryas in Chhattisgarh.
- Vegetarianism, abstention from intoxicants, and rejection of caste distinctions within the panth are enjoined.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Sat Puruṣ, Satguru Kabīr Sāhib, Satnām
**Descriptions:** the True Being of Satlok, beyond the three worlds; incarnate as Kabīr in every age
**Negations:** not Kāl Nirañjan, the creator of the three worlds; not Brahmā, Viṣṇu or Maheś
**Relation to self:** The soul (haṃs) is of the Sat Puruṣ's substance, fallen under Kāl, and returns to Satlok by the true Name.
**Relation to world:** The three worlds are Kāl's creation and prison; Satlok alone is true.
**Caveat:** The Kabīr Panth's cosmogony and its worship of Kabīr as God sit uneasily with the Bījak's apophatic critique; the Kabīr Chaurā and Pārakh readings stress discernment over mythology.
