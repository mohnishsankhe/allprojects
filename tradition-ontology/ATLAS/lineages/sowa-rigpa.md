The Tibetan science of healing (one of the five major sciences), based on the Four Tantras (rgyud bzhi) — in the tradition's account the word of the Medicine Buddha, in the scholarly account a 12th-century compilation by Yuthok Yönten Gönpo the Younger drawing heavily on the Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya. It explains health and disease through the three nyepa (rlung, mkhris pa, bad kan), which arise from the three poisons rooted in ignorance, and the seven bodily constituents; it diagnoses by pulse and urine and treats by diet, behaviour, medicines and external therapies. Desi Sangye Gyatso's Blue Beryl commentary and the medical paintings (late 17th c.) fixed the classical tradition, which is practised across the Himalaya, Mongolia and the diaspora.

## Distinctive positions
- The Four Tantras were taught by the Medicine Buddha, emanating as the sage Rigpé Yeshe, in answer to the sage Yilé Kyé (the tradition's account).
- The ultimate cause of all disease is ignorance (ma rig pa) that does not understand selflessness; from it arise desire, hatred and delusion, which give rise to the three nyepa — rlung, mkhris pa and bad kan.
- Health is the balance of the three nyepa, the seven bodily constituents and the three excretions; disease is their imbalance.
- Diagnosis is by seeing (above all tongue and urine), touching (above all the pulse) and questioning; treatment is by diet, behaviour, medicines and external therapies, in that order of gentleness.
- The whole science is summarized in the image of three trees with roots, trunks, branches and leaves, whose flowers are health and long life and whose fruits are Dharma, wealth and happiness.
- The physician must have compassion (bodhicitta) as well as learning; medicine is practised as a path of the bodhisattva.
- Some diseases are karmic or caused by spirits (gdon) and need Dharma practice or ritual rather than medicine alone.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** selflessness (bdag med), whose non-realization (ignorance) is the root of all disease, the Medicine Buddha (sangs rgyas sman bla), teacher of the Four Tantras
**Descriptions:** the Four Tantras are taught by the Medicine Buddha's emanation of awareness-wisdom (rig pa'i ye shes); freedom from ignorance is freedom from the root cause of illness
**Negations:** as long as the self is grasped, the causes of disease follow beings as a bird's shadow follows it
**Relation to self:** Grasping at a self is the root cause of the three poisons and, through them, of the three nyepa.
**Relation to world:** The body and its medicines are composed of the five elements; health belongs to the conventional world and serves Dharma, wealth and happiness.
**Caveat:** Sowa Rigpa is a science (rig gnas), not a philosophical school; it does not posit its own ultimate and defers to Buddhist teaching (Bön physicians to Bön). This entry records only its framing of health and disease within that teaching.
