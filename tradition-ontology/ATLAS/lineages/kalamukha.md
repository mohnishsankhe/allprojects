# Kālāmukha

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Kālāmukha, Kālamukha, Kāḷāmukha (Kannada inscriptions), Lākula (self-description in inscriptions)
**Parent:** [Lākula](lakula.md)
**Key teachers:** [Kedāraśakti](../teachers/kedarasakti.md), [Vāmaśakti](../teachers/vamasakti.md), [Kriyāśakti](../teachers/kriyasakti.md)
**Regions:** Karnataka (Balligāve/Belagāmi in Shimoga district and many other sites), Śrīśaila (Andhra)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 9th–14th c.; most inscriptions 11th–13th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** extinct

The Kālāmukhas are the Śaiva ascetics of numerous Karnataka inscriptions of the 11th–13th centuries, where they appear as learned heads of temples and monasteries, above all at Balligāve. Scholarship identifies them with the Lākula division of the Atimārga. Vaiṣṇava opponents describe skull and corpse-ash practices under their name. They disappear from the record by the 14th–15th century; how far their institutions passed to the Vīraśaivas is disputed.

## Distinctive positions
- A temple- and monastery-based Śaiva order: its ācāryas served as temple heads and royal preceptors (rājaguru), and their maṭhas were centres of learning and charity (inscriptions).
- Organised in 'assemblies' (pariṣad): the Śakti-pariṣad, whose chief seat was the Kōḍiya-maṭha of the Kedāreśvara temple at Balligāve, and the Siṃha-pariṣad.
- Inscriptions praise its ācāryas as learned in the Lakula-siddhānta, Pātañjala yoga, Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Mīmāṃsā, Sāṃkhya, Buddhist doctrine, grammar and poetry.
- Opponents (Yāmuna; Rāmānuja) report that they eat from a skull, bathe in and eat the ash of corpses, carry a club, and set up a pot of liquor and worship the deity placed in it.
