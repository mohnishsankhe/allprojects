The Sarvāstivāda ('those who say everything exists') was the most influential Abhidharma school of northern India, centred in Mathurā, Kashmir and Gandhāra. Its canonical Abhidharma of seven books (the Jñānaprasthāna and six 'feet') was systematized in the Mahāvibhāṣā and summarized — and partly criticized — in Vasubandhu's Abhidharmakośa, defended by Saṅghabhadra. Its categories and path structure became the base of later Indian, Tibetan and East Asian scholasticism.

## Distinctive positions
- Everything exists (sarvam asti): dharmas of the past, present and future all exist as real entities (dravya); only the activity (kāritra) of a dharma distinguishes the times (AK 5.25–26).
- Seventy-five dharmas in five groups (in the later scholastic count): matter, mind, mental factors, conditioned factors dissociated from mind, and three unconditioned dharmas.
- Real dissociated factors: possession (prāpti) and non-possession, the four characteristics of the conditioned (birth, duration, decay, impermanence), the life faculty, homogeneity (AK 2.35–48).
- Unmanifest matter (avijñapti-rūpa) carries vows and non-vows through time (AK 1.11, 4.13).
- Six causes, four conditions and five results (AK 2.49–64).
- Realization of the truths is gradual: sixteen moments, fifteen of which make up the path of seeing (AK 6.25–28).
- An arhat can fall back from arhatship (six kinds of arhat, AK 6.56–60); a stream-enterer cannot.
- An intermediate existence (antarābhava) links death and rebirth (AK 3.10–14).
- The Abhidharma is the word of the Buddha, arranged by his disciples (Vaibhāṣika view reported by Yaśomitra).
- The Buddha's physical body is impure (sāsrava); his dharmakāya is the pure qualities of the aśaikṣa.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** nirvāṇa, pratisaṃkhyānirodha
**Descriptions:** Cessation through discernment: disjunction from all sāsrava dharmas, a real (dravya), unconditioned, eternal dharma, obtained separately for each bond (AK 1.5–6).; One of the three objects of refuge, with the Buddha's and the Saṅgha's pure dharmas (AK 4.32).
**Negations:** There is no self; only aggregates conditioned by defilement and karma (AK 3.18).; Things do not arise from Īśvara or any single cause (AK 2.64).
**Relation to self:** No self exists; nirvāṇa is not the attainment of a self but the cessation of the defilements and of future aggregates.
**Relation to world:** The world of conditioned dharmas is real in all three times; nirvāṇa is a real unconditioned dharma alongside it, not its ground.
**Caveat:** The Sarvāstivāda recognizes many ultimately real dharmas (paramārthasat) and three unconditioned ones; it would reject reading nirvāṇa as a single substance underlying the world or as a self.
