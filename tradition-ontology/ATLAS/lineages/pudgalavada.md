The Pudgalavāda ('those who affirm the person') comprised the Vātsīputrīya and its offshoots, above all the Saṃmitīya, which Xuanzang and Yijing describe as one of the four great orders of 7th-century India. They taught an inexpressible person, neither identical with nor different from the aggregates, and were refuted by the Theravāda (Kathāvatthu 1.1), the Sarvāstivāda (Vijñānakāya, Mahāvibhāṣā) and Vasubandhu (Abhidharmakośabhāṣya ch. 9). Few of their own texts survive, in Chinese.

## Distinctive positions
- There is a person (pudgala) that is neither the same as nor different from the aggregates; it is designated in dependence on the present, appropriated aggregates, as fire is designated in dependence on fuel (AKBh 9, Pradhan p. 461; Vasumitra).
- The person is 'inexpressible' (avaktavya) — a fifth kind of knowable besides the past, the future, the present and the unconditioned (AKBh 9, p. 462–463).
- Dharmas do not pass from one life to the next apart from the person; the person is the bearer of the burden of the aggregates (Bhārahāra-sūtra, as they read it).
- Three designations of the person — by support, by transmigration and by cessation (Saṃmitīyanikāya-śāstra; low confidence on the terms).
- Some conditioned things are momentary, others (e.g. the body) endure for a time (Vasumitra; low confidence).
- An arhat can fall back (Kathāvatthu 1.2, per the commentary); an intermediate existence exists (Kathāvatthu 8.2, per the commentary: Sammitiyas).


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** nirvāṇa (the unconditioned)
**Descriptions:** The unconditioned is the fourth of five knowables; the person, the fifth, is inexpressible (AKBh 9, p. 462).
**Negations:** The person is neither the same as nor different from the aggregates.; The person is neither a real substance nor a mere designation (as the Vātsīputrīyas replied).
**Relation to self:** They affirm a person (pudgala) but not the self (ātman) of the outsiders; its relation to the aggregates and to nirvāṇa is inexpressible.
**Relation to world:** The person transmigrates, bearing the aggregates, until its cessation in nirvāṇa (low confidence).
**Caveat:** Known mainly through opponents; the Pudgalavādins insisted their person was not the ātman of other schools, and would reject assimilation to it.
