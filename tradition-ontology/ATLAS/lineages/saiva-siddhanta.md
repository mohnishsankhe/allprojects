The dualist (or 'realist') ritual Śaivism of the Mantramārga, grounded in the 28 Siddhānta Āgamas. Its Sanskrit exegesis runs from Sadyojyoti (c. 700) through the Kashmirian commentators Nārāyaṇakaṇṭha and Rāmakaṇṭha to the South Indian ritual authors Somaśambhu and Aghoraśiva; in the Tamil country it merged with the devotional corpus of the Nāyaṉmārs (the twelve Tirumuṟai) and was systematized in Tamil by Meykaṇṭār and the fourteen Meykaṇṭa Śāstras. It remains the theology of most Śiva temples of South India and Sri Lanka.

## Distinctive positions
- Three eternal, uncreated realities: the Lord (pati = Śiva), bound souls (paśu), and the bonds (pāśa: āṇava mala, karma, māyā).
- Souls are countless, eternal and distinct from Śiva; each has innate powers of knowing and acting that are covered, not created, by mala.
- Āṇava mala is a real, beginningless substance (dravya); knowledge alone cannot remove it — Śiva removes it by his power through initiation (dīkṣā).
- Śiva is the efficient cause only; māyā (and bindu, pure māyā) is the material cause; his single power (śakti) is the instrument.
- Śiva performs five acts — emission, maintenance, withdrawal, concealment and grace — all for the souls' release.
- Liberation (Sanskrit exegetes): the soul becomes equal to Śiva (śivasamatva), its omniscience and omnipotence manifested, yet it remains numerically distinct and does not perform the cosmic acts.
- The path is fourfold: caryā, kriyā, yoga and jñāna, whose fruits are sālokya, sāmīpya, sārūpya and sāyujya.
- The world is real, not an appearance; the Siddhānta rejects Advaita's unreality of the world and the non-dualists' identity of soul and Śiva.

**Transmissions received:** 
  - [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](mantramarga.md) — what: the Siddhānta scriptures (the ten Śiva and eighteen Rudra Āgamas) and their ritual; evidence: the Āgamas themselves (tradition's account: revelation)

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Śiva, Pati, Parameśvara, Hara, Sadāśiva (for worship)
**Descriptions:** one, eternally free (anādimukta), omniscient and omnipotent; a mass of consciousness, pervasive, ever-risen, tranquil, who favours all (Tattvaprakāśa 1); acts by a single conscious power (śakti) and has no body of māyā; for worship, a body of mantras; performs the five acts for the souls' release
**Negations:** not the material cause of the world; not identical with souls; unchanged by his acts
**Relation to self:** Souls are eternal and many, distinct from Śiva; freed, each becomes equal to Śiva (a 'Śiva') in knowledge and agency without becoming him.
**Relation to world:** The world is real, produced from māyā (and bindu) as material cause, with Śiva as efficient cause; it exists to ripen and release souls.
**Caveat:** Accepts one supreme Lord but denies that souls and māyā are his appearances; a 'one truth' reading that collapses souls or the world into Śiva is rejected.
