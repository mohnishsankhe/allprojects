**Dates:** Tradition's account: teaching descended from Śrīkaṇṭha Rudra through Nandi, Sanatkumāra, Satyajñānadarśin and Parañcōti to Meykaṇṭār; Scholarly account: 12th c. forerunners (Tiruvuntiyār 1147/48); Meykaṇṭār early 13th c.; Umāpati 1306–1313; monastic (ātīṉam) tradition continuing; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The Tamil systematization of Śaiva Siddhānta in the fourteen Meykaṇṭa Śāstras, above all Meykaṇṭār's twelve-sūtra Civañāṉa Pōtam, Aruṇanti's Civañāṉa Cittiyār and Umāpati Civācāriyār's eight works. It reads the Āgamic dualism as a 'pure non-dualism' of inseparability and is carried by the non-brahmin monastic houses (ātīṉams) of Tiruvāvaṭuturai, Dharmapuram and others, with Civañāṉa Muṉivar's Māpāṭiyam (18th c.) as its classical commentary.

## Distinctive positions
- Advaita understood as inseparability (ananya): God is one with souls, other than them, and together with them (oṉṟāy, vēṟāy, uṭaṉāy) — as the vowel 'a' pervades every letter.
- The malas are eternal; in liberation their power is quelled, not their existence destroyed.
- Grace (aruḷ) comes when the two deeds are balanced (iruviṉaiyoppu) and āṇava has ripened (malaparipākam); Śiva himself comes as the guru.
- The liberated soul abides at Śiva's feet in bliss, knowing through his grace; it is not a doer of the five acts.
- The freed soul should live among devotees and revere the devotees' garb and the temple as Śiva (Civañāṉa Pōtam 12).
- Vedas and Āgamas are both Śiva's word — the Veda general, the Āgama special.

**Transmissions received:**

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Civam / Civaṉ, Pati / Iṟaivaṉ, Cemporuḷ / Meypporuḷ (the Real), the Lord of the Dance (Naṭarājaṉ, Ampalavāṇaṉ)
**Descriptions:** the Real (cat), known neither as a sense-object nor as nothing, but by grace; pervades all as the vowel 'a' pervades letters (Tiruvaruṭpayaṉ 1.1); of eight qualities (eṇkuṇam); grace (aruḷ) is his power, and he comes as the guru; dances the five acts at Chidambaram
**Negations:** not the object of the senses (Civañāṉa Pōtam 6); not one with souls in substance; not two in separation
**Relation to self:** Advaita as inseparability: one with, other than, and together with souls; the freed soul abides in him enjoying his bliss.
**Relation to world:** Efficient cause of the real world produced from māyā; the world is 'acat' only as perishing, not as illusion.
**Caveat:** The Tamil school calls its view non-dual but insists on three eternal realities; it rejects Advaita's identity and unreality of the world.
