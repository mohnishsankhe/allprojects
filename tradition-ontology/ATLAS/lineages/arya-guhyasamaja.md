The exegetical school of the Guhyasamāja tantra associated with the tantric Nāgārjuna, Āryadeva, Nāgabodhi and Candrakīrti, which systematized the completion stage in five stages leading through the four empties to clear light, illusory body and union; its Akṣobhya-centred 32-deity maṇḍala was taken into Tibet (Marpa, Gö Khukpa Lhetse) and made central by Tsongkhapa.

## Distinctive positions
- The completion stage is five (or six) stages: speech isolation (vajra recitation), mind isolation, self-consecration (illusory body), clear light (abhisambodhi) and union (yuganaddha) (Pañcakrama).
- The four empties (śūnya, atiśūnya, mahāśūnya, sarvaśūnya) arise as winds dissolve; the fourth is clear light (Pañcakrama 2.4–5).
- The illusory body arisen from wind-and-mind is the conventional truth, clear light the ultimate; their union is buddhahood (Pañcakrama 3.13, 5.11).
- The root tantra is coded and must be read through the explanatory tantras (Vajramālā, Sandhivyākaraṇa, Caturdevīparipṛcchā) and the six alternatives (Pradīpoddyotana).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** prabhāsvara (clear light, 'all-empty'), yuganaddha (union), advayajñāna (non-dual gnosis), Mahāvajradhara
**Descriptions:** the fourth empty, clear light (Pañcakrama 2.4–5); the union where saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, the two truths, grasped and grasper become one (Pañcakrama 5.2–13); non-dual gnosis, non-abiding nirvāṇa, buddhahood (Pañcakrama 5.25)
**Negations:** 'unborn' and other such words only point to non-dual gnosis (Pañcakrama 5.27)
**Relation to self:** The self-consecration (svādhiṣṭhāna) body is the conventional truth, the illusory body of the yogin himself.
**Relation to world:** The world is not self-arisen; its cause is clear light (Pañcakrama 3.15).
**Caveat:** Clear light is 'all-empty' and non-dual gnosis — the tradition frames it within the two truths of Madhyamaka and would not accept it as a self-existent absolute.
