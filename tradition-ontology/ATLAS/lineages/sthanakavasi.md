The non-image-worshipping Śvetāmbara order descending from the 15th-century critic Loṅkā Śāh and the 17th-century reformers Lavjī, Dharmasiṃha and Dharmadāsa. It accepts thirty-two Āgamas, rejects temple worship, and its mendicants always wear the mouth-cloth; many groups united in the Śramaṇa Saṅgha in 1952.

## Distinctive positions
- Rejects the worship of images: the thirty-two Āgamas it accepts contain no injunction to image worship, and worship with water, flowers and fire harms living beings
- Accepts thirty-two Āgamas: 11 Aṅgas, 12 Upāṅgas, 4 Chedasūtras, 4 Mūlasūtras and the Āvaśyaka; rejects the Prakīrṇakas and later commentarial layers as authorities
- Monks and nuns wear the mouth-cloth (mukhavastrikā) at all times to protect beings of the air
- Religious life centres on the 'sthānaka' (hall for monks and for lay sāmāyika), not the temple


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** the Jina / Arihanta and the Siddhas
**Descriptions:** the qualities of the Jina and the liberated soul, approached through the scripture, the mendicants and one's own equanimity, not through images
**Negations:** not to be worshipped in images; worship that harms beings is not dharma
**Relation to self:** The soul's own nature, realized by restraint and equanimity (sāmāyika).
**Relation to world:** Real and uncreated; every being in it, down to the one-sensed, is to be protected.
**Caveat:** The Jain tradition denies a single all-encompassing reality and a creator: liberated souls are infinitely many and remain distinct, and the universe of six substances is uncreated. It can accept 'one truth, many names' only in its own way — as many-sidedness (anekānta) about a plural reality — not as the identity of all souls in one substance.
