Not an institutional order but the textual tradition of the Bhakti Sūtras: the Nārada Bhakti Sūtra (84 sūtras) and the Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra (100 sūtras, with Svapneśvara's commentary), which define devotion, rank it above works, knowledge and yoga, name its means and signs, and cite a line of 'bhakti teachers' (bhaktyācāryas: Kumāra, Vyāsa, Śuka, Śāṇḍilya, Garga, Uddhava, Bali, Hanumān, Vibhīṣaṇa and others, NBS 83). Later Sanskrit systematizations of Bhāgavata devotion (Vopadeva's Muktāphala with Hemādri's commentary; Viṣṇupurī's Bhaktiratnāvalī) are grouped here.

## Distinctive positions
- Bhakti is supreme love (parama-prema) for the Lord, of the nature of nectar/immortality (NBS 2–3); it is supreme attachment (parānurakti) to Īśvara (ŚBS 2).
- Bhakti is not a means but the fruit itself (phala-rūpa), higher than action, knowledge and yoga (NBS 25–26, 30).
- Bhakti is not knowledge: even one who hates the Lord may know him (ŚBS); knowledge of a king or of food does not please the king or appease hunger (NBS 31–32).
- Love must include knowledge of the Lord's greatness (māhātmya-jñāna) and seek his happiness, not one's own — the gopīs' love is the model, the paramour's love is not (NBS 21–24).
- Bhakti is attained chiefly by the grace of great souls or by a drop of the Lord's grace; the company of the great is itself won by his grace (NBS 38–40).
- Among devotees there is no distinction of caste, learning, beauty, family, wealth or occupation, 'because they are His' (NBS 72–73); the lowest births are eligible (ŚBS, on the tradition of eligibility).
- Secondary devotion (gauṇī) is graded by the guṇas or by the motive of the devotee; primary devotion is one, though it appears as eleven attachments (NBS 56, 82).
- The devotee should not engage in disputation (NBS 74–75) but reflect on the bhakti-śāstras and keep the virtues (NBS 76–78).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Bhagavān / Īśvara (the Lord), Hari, Kṛṣṇa (as the object of the gopīs' love, NBS 21)
**Descriptions:** The Lord who, when sung of, quickly manifests himself and makes his devotees experience him (NBS 80).; The Lord dislikes pride and loves humility (NBS 27).; Love for him (prema) is indescribable, like a mute's tasting; free of the guṇas and of desire, increasing every moment, unbroken, subtlest, of the nature of experience (NBS 51–54).; Three views of the object of devotion: the Lord's majesty as other (Kāśyapa), the one self (Bādarāyaṇa), both (Śāṇḍilya) (ŚBS, as recalled).
**Relation to self:** Devotees are 'His own' (tadīya, NBS 73); there is no difference between the Lord and his people (NBS 41). ŚBS: Śāṇḍilya holds devotion bears on both the Lord as other and the self as one (low confidence on the exact sūtras).
**Relation to world:** Worldly and Vedic activity is to be laid down in him (nirodha, NBS 8); until love is attained worldly activity is not abandoned but its fruit is given up (NBS 62).
**Caveat:** The bhakti-śāstra would reject a one-truth reading that makes devotion a preparatory stage to be surpassed by impersonal knowledge: for the Nārada Sūtra love is the fruit itself (NBS 26, 30).
