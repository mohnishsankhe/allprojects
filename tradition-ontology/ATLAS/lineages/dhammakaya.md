# Dhammakāya meditation tradition (Vijjā Dhammakāya; Wat Paknam)

**Status:** living

The Thai meditation tradition founded by Luang Pu Sodh Candasaro (1884–1959) of Wat Paknam, teaching concentration at the body's centre leading to the inner Dhammakāya; continued by Wat Paknam and by the Wat Phra Dhammakaya movement.

## Distinctive positions
- The mind is brought to rest at the centre of the body (the seventh base, two finger-breadths above the navel) while visualizing a bright crystal sphere and reciting 'sammā arahaṃ'.
- Successive refined inner bodies appear, culminating in the Dhammakāya, the Buddha-body within.
- Nibbāna is described as a true self (attā) — a teaching contested by other Thai Theravāda scholars.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** nibbāna, Dhammakāya (the Dhamma-body)
**Descriptions:** the Dhammakāya, a luminous Buddha-form, is found within at the body's centre; nibbāna described as a true self (attā) and as a realm (āyatana) where the Buddhas abide (the tradition's teaching)
**Relation to self:** teaches a true self in nibbāna, distinct from the non-self aggregates
**Relation to world:** the refined inner bodies lead beyond the world
**Caveat:** Mainstream Thai Theravāda scholars (notably P. A. Payutto) reject the teaching that nibbāna is a self; recorded as a dispute (dsp:nibbana-atta-or-anatta).
