The Vedānta school founded by Madhva (Ānandatīrtha, 13th c.), called by its adherents Tattvavāda, 'the doctrine of reality'. It teaches that Viṣṇu alone is the independent reality, that souls and matter are real, dependent and eternally distinct from him and from one another (the five differences), that souls are innately graded, and that liberation — the soul's enjoyment of its own bliss in Viṣṇu's presence — comes only by his grace, won through knowledge and devotion. Its classical literature runs from Madhva's thirty-seven works through Jayatīrtha's commentaries and Vyāsatīrtha's polemics to Rāghavendra; it is carried by ascetic maṭhas (the eight of Uḍupi with their biennial rotation of Kṛṣṇa worship, and the Uttarādi, Rāghavendra and Vyāsarāja maṭhas) and was sung in Kannada by the Haridāsas.

## Distinctive positions
- Viṣṇu (Nārāyaṇa, Hari) alone is independent (svatantra); Lakṣmī, all souls, matter, time and the Veda are dependent (paratantra) on him (Tattvasaṅkhyāna 1; Tattvaviveka 1).
- Five real and eternal differences (pañca-bheda) — God–soul, God–matter, soul–soul, soul–matter, matter–matter — hold in every state, including liberation (Mahābhārata-tātparya-nirṇaya 1.68–71).
- The world is real (satya); the Advaita doctrines of māyā, the world's falsity (mithyātva) and the indeterminable (anirvacanīya) are refuted (the ten prakaraṇas; Nyāyāmṛta).
- Souls are innately graded (tāratamya) and fall into three classes — fit for liberation (mukti-yogya), ever-transmigrating (nitya-saṃsārin), fit for blinding darkness (tamo-yogya); a soul's fitness is beginningless and unalterable (Tattvasaṅkhyāna 4–7; MBTN 1.86–95).
- Liberation is the soul's enjoyment of its own intrinsic bliss, graded even among the liberated (ānanda-tāratamya), in eternal difference from and dependence on Viṣṇu.
- Viṣṇu is full of infinite auspicious qualities and free of every defect; 'nirguṇa' means free of prakṛti's qualities; his qualities, acts, forms and avatāras are not different from him (Viṣṇutattvavinirṇaya 3).
- Without Nārāyaṇa's grace there is no liberation, and without knowledge there is no abundant grace (Brahmasūtrabhāṣya 1.1.1).
- Bhakti is firm love for Viṣṇu exceeding all other love, preceded by knowledge of his greatness (MBTN 1.85); it continues in liberation as eternal bliss (MBTN 1.104).
- Three means of valid knowledge — perception, inference, authoritative word; perception is sevenfold, the witness (sākṣin) plus six senses; what the sākṣin establishes is never sublated (Pramāṇalakṣaṇa; Anuvyākhyāna).
- Vāyu (Mukhyaprāṇa) is the highest of souls; grace descends through the higher beings; Madhva is Vāyu's third avatāra after Hanumān and Bhīma (MBTN 32.162–166).
- God is the efficient cause of the world, not its material cause; prakṛti is the material cause.
- All words, letters and scriptures in their primary sense designate Viṣṇu; the Veda is authorless; valid scripture (sadāgama) is the Vedas, the Mahābhārata, the Pañcarātra, the original Rāmāyaṇa and the Purāṇas that agree with them (Viṣṇutattvavinirṇaya 1).
- The soul is a reflection (pratibimba) of God — dependent on and similar to its original (bimba), and eternally distinct from it.
- Chāndogya 6.8.7 is read as 'sa ātmā atat tvam asi' ('you are not that'), taught nine times with examples to show difference (Viṣṇutattvavinirṇaya 1).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Viṣṇu, Nārāyaṇa, Hari, Brahman (in its primary sense only Viṣṇu), Puruṣottama, the Independent (svatantra), Vāsudeva, Paramātman
**Descriptions:** the one independent reality; everything else is dependent on him (Tattvasaṅkhyāna 1); free of all defects and full of all good qualities, each unlimited (Viṣṇutattvavinirṇaya, maṅgala); known through the true scriptures alone, not by bare reasoning or the senses (Viṣṇutattvavinirṇaya 1); source of the creation, maintenance, dissolution, control, knowledge, ignorance, bondage and release of the world (BSB 1.1.2); of the nature of full bliss, knowledge and power; the fourfold Viśva, Taijasa, Prājña, Turīya in all beings (Māṇḍūkya-bhāṣya); his qualities, acts, forms and avatāras are identical with him; there is no internal difference in him (MBTN 1.69; VTN 3); the primary meaning of every word and letter and the great purport of all scripture (BSB 1.1.1; VTN 1); beyond the perishable (all souls) and the imperishable (Lakṣmī) (VTN 2)
**Negations:** not without qualities — 'nirguṇa' means free of prakṛti's qualities (Karmanirṇaya); not identical with the soul or the world; not their material cause; not subject to any defect, even in his descents (VTN 3); not an attributeless consciousness — such a Brahman would be the Mādhyamika's void (Anuvyākhyāna 2.2.241–242)
**Relation to self:** Eternally different from every soul; the soul is his reflection (pratibimba), wholly dependent on him, moved by him within, and in liberation enjoys its own bliss in his presence, desiring only in accord with his will and still graded and distinct.
**Relation to world:** The world is real, eternally different from him and wholly dependent; he is its efficient cause and controller, prakṛti its material cause; he brings about its creation, maintenance, dissolution, bondage and release.
**Caveat:** Dvaita affirms one supreme, independent reality and can read Ṛgveda 1.164.46 as 'the wise call the one (Viṣṇu) by many names', since for Madhva all names primarily denote Viṣṇu. It rejects any reading of 'one truth' as an identity in which souls, world and God are ultimately one; it rejects treating difference as provisional, empirical (vyāvahārika) or merely a standpoint; and it rejects placing other deities (Brahmā, Śiva and the rest, who are dependent souls) on a par with Viṣṇu as equal names of the supreme. Any reconciliation that dissolves the five differences is one the tradition explicitly refuses.
