The Gelug school, founded by Tsongkhapa Lobsang Drakpa (1357–1419) at Ganden and continued by his disciples Gyaltsab Je and Khedrup Je and the great monasteries of Drepung, Sera and Tashi Lhunpo, unites the Kadam graded path with Prāsaṅgika Madhyamaka and the Highest Yoga Tantras under strict monastic discipline; its lines of Dalai and Panchen Lamas and its debate curriculum made it the largest Tibetan school.

## Distinctive positions
- The three principal aspects of the path — renunciation, bodhicitta and the correct view — frame the whole of sūtra and tantra.
- Prāsaṅgika Madhyamaka as the final view: the object of negation is inherent existence (existence from the object's own side), to be identified precisely — neither too broadly nor too narrowly.
- Emptiness is a non-affirming negation; it is fully compatible with conventionally valid cause and effect (dependent arising), and ultimate truth is an object of knowledge.
- Tsongkhapa's 'eight difficult points' distinguishing Prāsaṅgika (no ālaya distinct from the six consciousnesses; no reflexive awareness; no autonomous syllogisms; external objects conventionally accepted; śrāvakas realize the selflessness of phenomena; grasping at the self of phenomena is an affliction; disintegration is a functioning thing; the special account of the three times).
- Analytical meditation is indispensable, not an obstacle to non-conceptual realization; insight arises when analysis itself induces pliancy.
- Tantra is distinguished from sūtra by deity yoga (method), not by a higher view; strict monastic discipline is kept alongside Highest Yoga Tantra (Guhyasamāja, Cakrasaṃvara, Vajrabhairava).
- Monastic education through debate on the five great texts, leading to the geshe degree.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** stong pa nyid — emptiness of inherent existence (rang bzhin gyis stong pa), don dam bden pa — ultimate truth, med dgag — non-affirming negation, rten 'brel — dependent arising, the king of reasons, ngo bo nyid sku — the nature body (svābhāvikakāya), 'od gsal — the clear light (in Highest Yoga Tantra)
**Descriptions:** The mere absence of inherent existence — of existence from the object's own side — in every phenomenon (Lamrim Chenmo, insight section).; Emptiness and dependent arising are one intention: because things arise dependently they are empty, and because empty they can arise (Three Principal Aspects 10–13; Praise of Dependent Origination).; The ultimate truth is an object of knowledge, found by a reasoning consciousness analysing the final mode of being.
**Negations:** the object of negation is inherent existence, not conventional existence — negating too much falls into nihilism, too little into eternalism; emptiness is not an other-empty permanent absolute (against Dolpopa); no ālaya-vijñāna and no reflexive awareness even conventionally (eight difficult points)
**Relation to self:** The self exists merely as designated in dependence on the aggregates; the innate apprehension of an inherently existent self is the root of saṃsāra, cut only by the wisdom realizing its emptiness.
**Relation to world:** The world exists conventionally, validly established by conventional valid cognition; cause and effect are 'unfailing' and fully compatible with emptiness.
**Caveat:** Gelug holds that each phenomenon has its own emptiness (though all emptinesses are of one taste) and that emptiness is itself empty; it rejects any positive, self-existent absolute (shentong, Advaita's Brahman). It would accept 'one truth, many names' only for the single mode of being of all phenomena — their emptiness — not for a substance or a divine person.
