The six auxiliary sciences through which the Vedic schools preserved, pronounced, analysed, interpreted, performed and timed the Veda: śikṣā (Pāṇinīya Śikṣā and the Prātiśākhyas), chandas (Piṅgala), vyākaraṇa (Pāṇini, Kātyāyana, Patañjali), nirukta (the Nighaṇṭu and Yāska), kalpa (Śrauta, Gṛhya, Dharma and Śulba sūtras) and jyotiṣa (Lagadha). They treat the Veda as sound to be kept intact and as meaning to be understood, and they stand to the Veda as limbs to a body.

## Distinctive positions
- The Veda has six limbs — phonetics (śikṣā), ritual procedure (kalpa), grammar (vyākaraṇa), etymology (nirukta), metre (chandas) and time-reckoning (jyotiṣa); the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (1.1.5) counts them with the four Vedas as 'lower knowledge' (aparā vidyā), and the Pāṇinīya Śikṣā (41–42) pictures them as the Veda's feet, hands, eye, ear, nose and mouth.
- The exact sound, accent, quantity and sequence of the Vedic text must be preserved; a mantra defective in accent or sound turns into a verbal thunderbolt against the sacrificer, as Tvaṣṭṛ's mis-accented 'indraśatru' did (Pāṇinīya Śikṣā 52; ŚB 1.6.3.10).
- The meaning of the mantras must be understood: the one who recites without understanding is 'a post, a mere bearer of loads' (Nirukta 1.18); Kautsa's thesis that mantras are meaningless is answered by Yāska (Nirukta 1.15–16).
- All nouns derive from verbal roots, according to Śākaṭāyana and the Nairuktas; Gārgya and some grammarians deny that all do (Nirukta 1.12).
- Grammar is the chief of the six limbs; it is studied to protect the Veda, adapt mantras, fulfil the scriptural command that a brāhmaṇa study the Veda with its six limbs as a duty without motive, save effort and remove doubt (Mahābhāṣya, Paspaśā).
- Time-reckoning exists for the sacrifices, which are enjoined for fixed times; it stands at the head of the Vedāṅgas like a peacock's crest (Vedāṅga Jyotiṣa, Ṛk recension 3, 35).
- The first seers had direct vision of dharma (sākṣātkṛtadharmāṇaḥ) and 'saw' the hymns; later generations received the mantras by instruction (Nirukta 1.20; 2.11).
