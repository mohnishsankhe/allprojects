The Thai forest meditation lineage descending from Ajahn Sao Kantasīlo (1861–1941) and Ajahn Mun Bhūridatta (1870–1949), emphasizing Vinaya, dhutaṅga austerities, 'buddho' and body contemplation, and realization in the heart; spread internationally through Ajahn Chah's and Ajahn Lee's lines.

## Distinctive positions
- Strict observance of the Vinaya and the ascetic practices (dhutaṅga): forest dwelling, alms-round, one meal a day, wandering (tudong) in wilderness and charnel places.
- Meditation with the parikamma word 'buddho' and on the breath, and contemplation of the body (its thirty-two parts, foulness, elements) until the mind converges in calm and sees the body's true nature.
- Calm and insight developed together; direct experience in the heart (citta) is valued over textual learning, though the teachers used the Pali terms.
- The mind in itself is radiant; defilements are visitors (āgantuka) — with the later teaching (Ajahn Maha Boowa) that even the radiant citta must be seen through, since ignorance can dwell in it.
- The tradition's biographies record visions, signs and the teachers' attainments (the tradition's account).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** nibbāna, the heart / mind (citta) released, vimutti (release)
**Descriptions:** the heart released from all defilements, known directly by the one who has reached it; Ajahn Maha Boowa: the 'pure citta' after even the radiant point of the mind — the seat of ignorance — has been destroyed; it does not die; Ajahn Chah: the unconditioned is realized by letting go; 'peace' beyond happiness and suffering
**Relation to self:** the teachers deny that the released heart is a self, yet speak of it in positive terms (pure, deathless), a feature noted by other traditions
**Relation to world:** beyond the conventions (sammuti) of the world, while conventions are still used
**Caveat:** The forest teachers would reject a reading of the released citta as an ātman or a cosmic self; they speak from their practice, not as metaphysics.
