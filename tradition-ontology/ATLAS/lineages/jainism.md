# Jainism (Jaina dharma)

**Alternate names:** Jaina-śāsana / Jina-śāsana (the Jina's teaching), Nirgrantha / Niggaṇṭha dharma (the 'knotless' ones; the name in Pali and early Prakrit sources), Arhat-dharma / Ārhata-mata, Śramaṇa-saṅgha of the Jinas, Samaṇa (Prakrit), Amaṇar / Camaṇar (Tamil)

**Dates:** Tradition's account: beginningless: the dharma is re-proclaimed by twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras in every half of each time-cycle; in this descending half the first was Ṛṣabha and the last Mahāvīra (nirvāṇa traditionally 527 BCE); Scholarly account: Pārśva (probably historical) c. 8th–7th c. BCE; Mahāvīra 6th–5th c. BCE (nirvāṇa dated by scholars between c. 527 and c. 425 BCE); (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The Jain tradition of the Jinas ('conquerors'), re-proclaimed in this era by Ṛṣabha and, last, by Vardhamāna Mahāvīra, a contemporary of the Buddha. It teaches the eternal reality of innumerable souls and of matter, karma as subtle matter binding the soul through passion and activity, and liberation through the three jewels, austerity and non-harm. It divided into the Śvetāmbara ('white-clad') and Digambara ('sky-clad') traditions, with the Yāpanīya between them, and within the Śvetāmbaras into image-worshipping and non-image-worshipping orders.

## Distinctive positions
- Innumerable individual souls (jīva), each by nature endowed with infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and energy, obscured by karma conceived as subtle matter that binds to the soul
- No creator or controlling God; the universe (loka) is uncreated and eternal, running through endless ascending and descending time-cycles
- Non-harm (ahiṃsā) toward all six classes of living beings, including earth-, water-, fire-, wind- and plant-bodied beings, as the pure and eternal law proclaimed by all Arhats
- Liberation by right faith, right knowledge and right conduct (with austerity), by one's own effort; the liberated soul (siddha) rises to the summit of the universe and remains individual
- Twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras ('ford-makers') in each half-cycle, who found the fourfold community (monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen)
- Many-sidedness (anekānta), standpoints (naya) and conditional predication (syādvāda) as the way of stating truth
- Voluntary, peaceful death by gradual fasting (sallekhanā) as the culmination of a disciplined life — under strict conditions (restricted; summary only)

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Paramātman (the supreme self, the soul fully manifest), Siddha (the perfected one), Arhat / Jina / Kevalin (the embodied omniscient), mokṣa / nirvāṇa (the state), paramātman (the supreme self), siddha (the perfected), kevala-jñāna (omniscience), mokṣa / nirvāṇa, the soul's own nature (svabhāva) with its infinite fourfold
**Descriptions:** the soul's own nature fully unveiled: infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and energy; who knows one knows all; who knows all knows one (Ācārāṅga 1.3.4); formless being that perceives and knows, beyond comparison (Ācārāṅga 1.5.6); abiding at the summit of the universe in unobstructed happiness (Aupapātika; Uttarādhyayana 36); the pure, eternal law is non-harm, proclaimed by all Arhats (Ācārāṅga 1.4.1); Each soul's own pure nature: infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and energy, manifest when all karma is shed (TS 10.2–4).; The liberated soul abides at the summit of the universe, bodiless, knowing all substances and all modes (TS 1.29, 10.5).; Called by others Sadāśiva, the supreme Brahman, the perfected self, suchness — one goal under many names (Haribhadra, YDS 129–130).; Non-violence is called the supreme Brahman known in the world (Svayambhūstotra, recalled).
**Negations:** not long or short, of no colour, smell, taste or touch; not female, male or neuter; beyond sound, reasoning and mind (Ācārāṅga 1.5.6); not a creator or ruler of the world; not a single substance of which souls are parts; No creator or controller of the world (Ādipurāṇa 4; Anyayogavyavacchedikā 6).; Not one all-pervading self: souls are innumerable, distinct and body-sized (TS 5.16).; Not merely permanent nor merely momentary (TS 5.30).
**Relation to self:** Every soul is by nature what the Siddha is; bondage is karmic matter covering that nature, and liberation is its uncovering by one's own effort through the three jewels.
**Relation to world:** The world is real, uncreated and eternal, made of souls and non-souls; the liberated know it entirely but neither make nor govern it.
**Caveat:** The Jain tradition denies a single all-encompassing reality and a creator: liberated souls are infinitely many and remain distinct, and the universe of six substances is uncreated. It can accept 'one truth, many names' only in its own way — as many-sidedness (anekānta) about a plural reality — not as the identity of all souls in one substance.
