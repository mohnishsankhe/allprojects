# The White Lotus school of Mao Ziyuan (Bailian zong)

`lin:bailian-zong` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Family:** ascetic
**Alternate names:** 白蓮宗, Bailian chantang 白蓮懺堂, Lianzong (as Pudu uses it), Bailian jiao 白蓮教 (later sects)
**Parent:** [Pure Land (Jingtu / Jōdo): birth in Amitābha's land](pure-land.md)
**Founders:** [Mao Ziyuan](../teachers/mao-ziyuan.md)
**Key teachers:** [Mao Ziyuan](../teachers/mao-ziyuan.md), [Pudu of Lushan](../teachers/pudu.md)
**Regions:** China (Jiangnan: Dianshan lake, Pingjiang)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: 12th c. (Mao Ziyuan d. 1166) to Yuan; later 'White Lotus' sects of the Ming–Qing are distinct and partly millenarian; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** absorbed

Lay Pure Land repentance societies founded by the monk Mao Ziyuan (Cizhao zongzhu, d. 1166) on the model of Huiyuan's Lotus society; repeatedly banned, defended by Pudu's Lushan lianzong baojian (1305). The later 'White Lotus teachings' of Ming–Qing sectarian religion took the name but are outside this entry.

## Distinctive positions
- Lay societies keeping the five precepts, confirmed by five recitations of Amitābha's name; repentance halls; Tiantai-style 'four lands and three contemplations'.


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Amitābha and the Western Land, read through the Tiantai 'four lands'
**Descriptions:** Mao Ziyuan taught the 'perfect interfusion of the four lands and three contemplations' (Lushan lianzong baojian).
**Relation to self:** The lay practitioner keeps the five precepts and recites to purify the five roots.
**Relation to world:** The five turbidities of this world are to be left through the five precepts and recitation.
**Caveat:** Known only through its defender Pudu's compilation; views of the later White Lotus sects are not represented here.
