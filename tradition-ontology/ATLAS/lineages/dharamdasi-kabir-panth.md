**Dates:** Tradition's account: Kabīr promised Dharamdās a line of forty-two generations (vaṃś) of ācāryas; Scholarly account: the line's texts and seats are dated by scholars to the 18th–19th c.; (confidence low)
**Status:** living

The largest branch of the Kabīr Panth, centred in Chhattisgarh among many lower-caste and tribal followers, led by a hereditary line of ācāryas from Dharamdās.

## Distinctive positions
- A hereditary line of householder ācāryas beginning with Dharamdās's son Cūṛāmaṇi (Muktāmaṇi) Nām.
- The Anurāg Sāgar's cosmogony of Kāl Nirañjan and the Sat Puruṣ, and the caukā ritual with the pān-parvānā (betel-leaf 'passport') for souls.
- Kabīr as the Sat Puruṣ incarnate.


## The ultimate in this lineage

**Names:** Sat Puruṣ, Kabīr Sāhib
**Descriptions:** Lord of Satlok who sent Kabīr and established Dharamdās's line
**Negations:** not Kāl Nirañjan
**Relation to self:** The soul is freed by the Name received from the vaṃś ācārya.
**Relation to world:** The three worlds are Kāl's.
**Caveat:** As for the Kabīr Panth generally; the hereditary line is itself part of the soteriology.
