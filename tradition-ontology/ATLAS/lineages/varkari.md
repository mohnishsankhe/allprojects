**Dates:** Tradition's account: Viṭṭhala has stood on the brick for twenty-eight yugas since Puṇḍalīka's time; the saints appear as a succession within it; Scholarly account: Paṇḍharpūr cult attested from the 12th–13th c.; the saint-tradition from Jñāneśvar and Nāmdev (late 13th c.); pālkhī processions organised from the late 17th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The Vārkarī tradition of Maharashtra worships Viṭṭhala of Paṇḍharpūr through the name, bhajan, kīrtan and the walking pilgrimage (vārī). It looks to a chain of Marathi saints from Jñāneśvar and Nāmdev (13th–14th c.) through Eknāth (16th c.) to Tukārām (17th c.), of every caste, whose songs and commentaries are its scripture. It joins non-dual knowledge (Jñāneśvar's Nāth-derived teaching) to devotion lived in the household.

## Distinctive positions
- God is worshipped as Viṭṭhala (Viṭhobā, Pāṇḍuraṅga) of Paṇḍharpūr, standing on a brick with hands on hips — a form of Kṛṣṇa, identified also with Viṣṇu and, by some saints, with Śiva.
- The name ('Rāma Kṛṣṇa Hari', 'Viṭṭhala') and congregational singing (bhajan, kīrtan) are the main means; no renunciation is required — the householder's ordinary work becomes worship.
- The twice-yearly vārī — walking pilgrimage to Paṇḍharpūr for the Āṣāḍhī and Kārtikī ekādaśīs, in diṇḍīs following the pālkhīs of Jñāneśvar and Tukārām — is the defining observance; initiation is by wearing the tuḷsī-bead necklace (māḷ).
- Knowledge and devotion are held together: Jñāneśvar's non-dual teaching is crowned by devotion that continues after realization.
- Saints of every caste and gender — tailor, potter, gardener, barber, Mahār, maidservant, courtesan's daughter, Kuṇbī, brahmin woman — are equally revered; the saints' company is itself the tīrtha.
- Scripture is in Marathi; the 'three foundations' (prasthānatrayī) of the Vārkarīs are the Jñāneśvarī, the Eknāthī Bhāgavat and the Tukārām Gāthā.
- The sequence of saints is pictured as one temple: Jñāneśvar the foundation, Nāmdev the walls, Eknāth the pillar, Tukārām the pinnacle (Bahiṇābāī).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Viṭṭhala / Pāṇḍuraṅga / Viṭhāī (mother), Hari, the Self known by itself (svasaṃvedya ātmarūpa)
**Descriptions:** the Primal One set forth by the Vedas, known by itself, whose form is the Self (Jñāneśvarī 1.1); the one reality that appears as Śiva and Śakti and sports as the world (Amṛtānubhava); the beautiful form standing on the brick, hands on hips, mother to the devotee; smaller than an atom and vast as the sky (Tukārām)
**Negations:** not described by sat, cit or ānanda, which only exclude their opposites (Amṛtānubhava 5); not reached by the word, which ends before the self-evident (Amṛtānubhava 6)
**Relation to self:** The Self is the Lord; yet devotion continues after knowledge — God worships God.
**Relation to world:** The world is the play of the one consciousness, not a second thing; seen through the saints it is the Lord's (onion and radish are Viṭṭhala).
**Caveat:** The Vārkarīs hold the personal Viṭṭhala and the non-dual Self together; they would reject a reading that makes the form merely provisional for beginners.
