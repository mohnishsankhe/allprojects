The Mahānubhāva panth, founded in 13th-c. Maharashtra by Cakradhar Svāmī, worships one Supreme (Parameśvara) incarnated as the five Kṛṣṇas and rejects the worship of all other gods, caste and Vedic ritual. Its Marathi prose scriptures — the Līḷācaritra and Sūtrapāṭh — are among the oldest Marathi literature; its monks and nuns live by renunciation, wandering and non-violence.

## Distinctive positions
- Four eternal, distinct realities: souls (jīva), deities (devatā), the world (prapañca) and the one Supreme (Parameśvara).
- Parameśvara alone gives liberation; he has incarnated as the five Kṛṣṇas (pañca-kṛṣṇa): Śrī Kṛṣṇa, Dattātreya, Cāṅgadeva Rāüḷ, Govindaprabhu (Guṇḍam Rāüḷ) and Cakradhar.
- The devatās — the gods of the Vedas and Purāṇas — are powerful beings who can grant only worldly fruit; worshipping them binds, so it is forbidden.
- Renunciation (saṃnyāsa) with wandering, begging and strict non-violence is the ideal, open to women and to all castes; caste distinctions and Vedic ritual are rejected.
- Devotion is through remembrance of the incarnations' līlās and through places and relics they touched (sthāna, sambandha).
- Marathi is the sacred language; the scriptures were long kept secret in special scripts.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Parameśvara, the five Kṛṣṇas
**Descriptions:** the one Supreme, distinct from souls, deities and world, who alone liberates and incarnates as the five Kṛṣṇas
**Negations:** not any of the devatās; not the world
**Relation to self:** Souls are eternally distinct from Parameśvara and are liberated by his grace.
**Relation to world:** The world (prapañca) is an eternal reality of māyā, distinct from him.
**Caveat:** The Mahānubhāvas insist on the distinctness of Parameśvara from the Vedic and Purāṇic gods and from souls; they reject the identification of all gods as forms of one truth.
