# Lokottaravāda

**Family:** ascetic
**Alternate names:** Lokottaravādin, Mahāsāṃghika-Lokottaravāda, Shuo chushi bu 說出世部 (Chinese)
**Parent:** [Mahāsāṃghika](mahasanghika.md)
**Regions:** Middle Country (madhyadeśa, per the Mahāvastu colophon), Bamiyan (Xuanzang; manuscripts)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Branched from the Mahāsāṃghika in the second century after the parinirvāṇa (Vasumitra); Scholarly account: c. 2nd c. BCE – 7th c. CE; (confidence low)
**Status:** extinct

The Lokottaravāda, a branch of the Mahāsāṃghika, is named for its teaching that the Buddhas are 'beyond the world' (lokottara) and only appear to conform to worldly life. Its Sanskrit Vinaya texts survive: the Mahāvastu (a vast biography of the Buddha with jātakas and the ten bodhisattva grounds), the Prātimokṣa, the Bhikṣuṇī-Vinaya and the Abhisamācārikā.

## Distinctive positions
- The Buddha's conduct, roots of merit, walking, standing, sitting and lying, his body, robes and food are all supramundane (Mvu i.167–168).
- The Buddhas conform to the world (lokānuvartanā): they wash, eat, drink, take medicine and show old age though they have no dirt, hunger, thirst, illness or ageing (Mvu i.168–169).
- The bodhisattva career runs through four conducts (caryā) and ten grounds (bhūmi) named durārohā … abhiṣeka (Mvu i.1, i.76).
- The Mahāvastu belongs to the Vinaya Piṭaka (its colophon), framing the Buddha's biography as the introduction to the monastic code.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** the supramundane Buddha, nirvāṇa
**Descriptions:** Everything about the Buddhas is beyond the world — conduct, merit, postures, body, robes, food (Mvu i.167–168).; They conform to the world only for beings' sake (Mvu i.168–169).
**Negations:** Nothing about the perfect Buddhas is like the world (Mvu i.159).
**Relation to self:** The aggregates are not self (Mvu iii.335).
**Relation to world:** The Buddha's worldly life is a display conforming to worldly convention.
**Caveat:** The supramundane is predicated of the Buddhas and their qualities, not of a single cosmic ground; the tradition would not equate it with Brahman or a self.
