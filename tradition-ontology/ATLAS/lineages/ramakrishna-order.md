The monastic order and service mission formed by Ramakrishna's young disciples under Vivekananda after 1886; it teaches Vedānta as understood through Ramakrishna's many-path realization and combines monastic sannyāsa with organized service.

## Distinctive positions
- The Divine Mother (Kālī/Śakti) and the impersonal Brahman are one reality, like fire and its power to burn.
- 'As many faiths, so many paths' (yata mat tata path): every sincere religion leads to the same God.
- Serving living beings as Śiva (śiva-jñāne jīva-sevā) is worship; the Mission joins renunciation to service.
- Vivekananda's four yogas — work, devotion, meditation (rāja) and knowledge — as paths to manifest the divinity already in every soul.
- Motto: ātmano mokṣārthaṃ jagad hitāya ca — for one's own liberation and for the welfare of the world.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** Brahman, the Divine Mother (Kālī, Śakti), Saccidānanda
**Descriptions:** Brahman and Śakti are one, like fire and its power to burn; at rest it is called Brahman, in creating, preserving and destroying it is called Śakti.; God is with form and without form, and beyond both; the vijñānī sees that Brahman has become the universe and its beings.; Vivekananda: each soul is potentially divine.
**Negations:** Brahman cannot be described: the salt doll that went to measure the ocean dissolved in it
**Relation to self:** The Self is Brahman; the individual is Śiva (jīva is Śiva), so service to beings is worship.
**Relation to world:** The world is the Mother's play (līlā); to the jñānī it is māyā, to the vijñānī it is Brahman itself.
**Caveat:** The order would reject a reading that ranks form below formless: Ramakrishna held both true. It welcomes the one-truth reading ('as many faiths, so many paths').
