The Vaiṣṇava Vedānta of Nimbārka: Brahman as Kṛṣṇa with Rādhā, and souls and matter as his naturally different-yet-non-different powers. Its root texts are Nimbārka's brief Brahma Sūtra commentary (Vedāntapārijātasaurabha) and the ten-verse Daśaślokī, expanded by Śrīnivāsa's Vedāntakaustubha and Keśava Kāśmīrin's Kaustubhaprabhā; Śrī Bhaṭṭa and Harivyāsadeva gave it Braj-language devotional poetry. It counts itself one of the four Vaiṣṇava sampradāyas (the Sanakādi/Kumāra line) and lives today with seats at Salemabad and in Braj.

## Distinctive positions
- Natural (svābhāvika) difference-and-non-difference: souls and world are both different and non-different from Brahman by their very nature, like sun and rays or snake and coils (BS 3.2.27).
- Three realities: cit (souls), acit (non-conscious: prākṛta, aprākṛta, kāla) and Īśvara.
- Brahman is Kṛṣṇa, free of all faults and a mass of auspicious qualities, worshipped together with Rādhā on his left (Daśaślokī 4–5): yugala-upāsanā.
- The soul is atomic, a knower, a part (aṃśa) of the Lord and wholly dependent on him; infinite in number.
- Five things to be known (arthapañcaka): the worshipped, the worshipper, the fruit of grace, the rasa of devotion, the obstacles.
- Five means (upāya): karma, vidyā, upāsanā (dhyāna), prapatti (surrender) and gurūpasatti (surrender to the teacher).
- Liberation is attainment of the Lord's nature and service after death, with difference preserved; grace arises for the humble (Daśaślokī 9).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Kṛṣṇa (Hari), Brahman, Puruṣottama, Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa (the divine couple, yugala)
**Descriptions:** By nature free of every fault, the single mass of all auspicious qualities, having the vyūhas as his limbs (Daśaślokī 4).; Worshipped with Rādhā, daughter of Vṛṣabhānu, on his left, served by thousands of sakhīs (Daśaślokī 5).; Both material and efficient cause through his powers; the controller of cit and acit, which are naturally different and non-different from him.
**Negations:** Denies an attributeless (nirviśeṣa) Brahman.; Denies that difference is due to adjuncts.
**Relation to self:** The soul is an atomic knower, part of the Lord, dependent on him, different and non-different by nature; liberated, it attains his nature without losing its distinctness.
**Relation to world:** The world is real, a transformation of the Lord's power; acit is prākṛta, aprākṛta or time.
**Caveat:** The school holds its dual-and-non-dual relation to be natural and eternal; it would reject a reading that collapses it into identity or into sheer difference.
