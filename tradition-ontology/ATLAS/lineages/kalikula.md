**Status:** living

The lineages that worship Kālī (and the goddesses of her 'family') as the supreme power. In Kashmir the Kālīkula scriptures (Jayadrathayāmala) fed the Krama; in Nepal the Uttarāmnāya worships Guhyakālī; in eastern India (Bengal, Assam, Mithilā) a large body of later tantras (Kālī, Toḍala, Nirvāṇa, Mahānirvāṇa, Kālīvilāsa, Niruttara, Kulacūḍāmaṇi, Śaktisaṅgama) and digests (Kṛṣṇānanda's Tantrasāra) teach the worship of Dakṣiṇakālī and the ten Mahāvidyās, by dakṣiṇa and vāma (Kaula) modes, which culminates in the Bengali Śākta devotional poetry of Rāmprasād and Kamalākānta.

## Distinctive positions
- Kālī devours (kalana) time itself: Mahākāla is so called because he devours all beings, and she is the primordial Kālikā because she devours Mahākāla (Mahānirvāṇa 4.31-32).
- Without Śakti Śiva is a corpse (śava): this is why Kālī stands upon Sadāśiva lying as a corpse (Toḍala 1.20-25).
- In herself the Goddess is formless; her terrible cremation-ground form is imagined according to her qualities and acts for the benefit of worshippers (Mahānirvāṇa 5.137-140; 13.5-13).
- The ten Mahāvidyās are her forms, shown by Satī to Śiva (Bṛhaddharma Purāṇa, Mahābhāgavata Purāṇa) and matched with Viṣṇu's ten avatāras (Toḍala 10.9-11).
- Worship is graded by disposition (bhāva): paśu, vīra, divya; the Kālī tantras disagree whether vīra and divya worship is allowed in the Kali age (Kālīvilāsa 4.2-3 against Mahānirvāṇa 5-8).
- In Bengal the fierce Mother is approached with intimate devotion (Śyāmā-saṅgīt): the devotee may plead with, reproach and claim her as her child.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Kālī / Ādyā Kālikā, Mahākālī, Śyāmā, Dakṣiṇakālī, Ādyā Śakti
**Descriptions:** she who devours (kalana) Mahākāla, the devourer of all beings; the beginning of all (Mahānirvāṇa 4.31-32); black because every colour disappears in black as all beings enter Kālī (Mahānirvāṇa 13.5-6); pure consciousness, witness of all, watching Time play (Mahānirvāṇa 13.12); the Śakti without whom Sadāśiva is a corpse (Toḍala 1.25)
**Negations:** formless (arūpā): her form is imagined according to her qualities and acts (Mahānirvāṇa 5.140); the formless meditation on her is beyond speech and mind (Mahānirvāṇa 5.137-138)
**Relation to self:** The worshipper is to see all as Brahman ('sarvaṃ brahmamayam', Mahānirvāṇa 3.120, 8.175); in the devotional Kālī tradition the self is her child, and in the Kaula view the bound jīva is Śiva himself (Kulārṇava 9.42).
**Relation to world:** The worlds arise from her and return into her as colours into black; she creates, sustains and devours, and is the Mother of Time (Mahānirvāṇa 5.140; 13.5-8).
**Caveat:** The Kālī tantras make the Goddess the source of Śiva, Brahmā and Viṣṇu (Nirvāṇa Tantra) and treat Viṣṇu's avatāras as her forms (Toḍala 10); they accept a one-truth reading only with her as that truth. The devotional tradition resists dissolving her motherhood into an impersonal absolute.
