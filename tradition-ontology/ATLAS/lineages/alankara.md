The science of drama and poetry from Bharata's Nāṭyaśāstra to Jagannātha. Its central doctrine is rasa: the relish of an emotion, universalized through the art-work, in the heart of the sensitive spectator (sahṛdaya). The debate on how rasa arises (Lollaṭa, Śaṅkuka, Bhaṭṭa Nāyaka, Abhinavagupta), Ānandavardhana's theory of suggestion, and the recognition of śānta ('peace') as a rasa akin to liberation made poetics a theory of aesthetic experience that later fed devotional theology (bhakti-rasa).

## Distinctive positions
- Drama is a fifth Veda made for all classes, since the Vedas may not be heard by śūdras (NŚ 1.12); it imitates the moods of the three worlds (NŚ 1.107) and gives rest to the afflicted (NŚ 1.114).
- Rasa arises from the union of determinants (vibhāva), consequents (anubhāva) and transient states (vyabhicāribhāva) (the rasa-sūtra, NŚ 6).
- There are eight rasas in drama (NŚ 6.15); many later authorities add śānta as the ninth, with dispassion, calm or knowledge of reality as its stable emotion.
- Rasa is relished (āsvādyate) as food flavoured by many ingredients is tasted by the discerning (NŚ 6.31–33).
- Suggestion (dhvani) is the soul of poetry (Dhvanyāloka 1.1), and rasa is known only by suggestion.
- Aesthetic relish is 'the twin brother of tasting Brahman' (brahmāsvāda-sahodara, SD 3.2), free of contact with other objects — akin to, but not identical with, realization.
- For Abhinavagupta, rasa is the relish of one's own consciousness freed from personal limitation through generalization (sādhāraṇīkaraṇa); it is blocked by seven obstacles (vighna).
- Poetry yields fame, wealth, knowledge of conduct, removal of evil, immediate supreme bliss and counsel like that of a beloved (KP 1.2).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** rasa (relish) — as the 'twin' of tasting Brahman, camatkāra (wonder, the life of rasa), brahmāsvāda-sahodara (twin of the taste of Brahman)
**Descriptions:** arising from the predominance of sattva, undivided, self-luminous, made of bliss and consciousness, untouched by any other object, the twin of tasting Brahman, whose life is supermundane wonder (SD 3.2–3); for Abhinavagupta: the relish of one's own consciousness, universalized and at rest (viśrānti)
**Negations:** not identical with tasting Brahman: it depends on determinants and lasts while the work is enjoyed (implied in 'sahodara')
**Relation to self:** In rasa the spectator relishes his own consciousness freed from personal limitation (Abhinavagupta); in śānta the stable emotion is knowledge of the self (tattvajñāna).
**Relation to world:** Drama imitates the moods of the three worlds (NŚ 1.107); rasa is 'supermundane' (alaukika), neither real nor unreal in the ordinary sense.
**Caveat:** Poetics is not a darśana and does not itself posit an ultimate. It compares aesthetic relish with realization, explicitly as a 'twin', not an identity; each author supplies the ultimate from his own school (Abhinavagupta: Trika Śaivism; Viśvanātha: Vedānta; Rūpa Gosvāmī: Kṛṣṇa). Advaita treats the relish of bliss in samādhi (rasāsvāda) as an obstacle (Māṇḍūkya-kārikā 3.45), a contrast to be kept.
