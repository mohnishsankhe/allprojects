# Nairukta school (the Vedic etymologists)

`lin:nairukta` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** nairuktāḥ, nairukta-samaya
**Parent:** [Vedāṅga (the six limbs of the Veda)](vedanga.md)
**Key teachers:** [Yāska](../teachers/yaska.md), [Śākaṭāyana (the ancient grammarian)](../teachers/sakatayana.md), [Aupamanyava](../teachers/aupamanyava.md)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: flourished before and with Yāska (mid-1st millennium BCE); continued in the Nirukta commentaries; (confidence low)
**Status:** absorbed

The school of Vedic word-explanation (nirvacana) to which Yāska belongs and whose opinions he reports; it explained words from verbal roots and interpreted deities and myths through natural phenomena.

## Distinctive positions
- All nouns are derived from verbs (nāmāny ākhyātajāni) — the doctrine of Śākaṭāyana and 'the convention of the Nairuktas' (Nirukta 1.12).
- There are only three deities — Agni on earth, Vāyu or Indra in the atmosphere, Sūrya in the sky — the many names being their functions (Nirukta 7.5).
- Vedic figures are read as natural powers: Vṛtra is the cloud, against the Aitihāsikas' 'an asura, son of Tvaṣṭṛ' (Nirukta 2.16; cf. 12.1 on the Aśvins).
