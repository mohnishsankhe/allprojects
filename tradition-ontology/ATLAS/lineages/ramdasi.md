The Samartha sampradāya founded by Samartha Rāmdās (1608–1681/82) of Maharashtra: devotion to Rāma and Hanumān, discrimination of the self, disciplined effort and service to society, taught in the Dāsbodh and the Manāce Śloka and organised through maṭhs and Māruti temples.

## Distinctive positions
- Worship of Rāma and of Māruti (Hanumān) as the embodiment of strength and service; the thirteen-syllable mantra 'śrī rām jay rām jay jay rām'.
- Devotion joined to discriminating knowledge of the self (Advaita in the Dāsbodh) and to vigilant, effortful action in the world (prayatna).
- The seeker passes through the conditions of the bound, the seeker, the practitioner and the accomplished.
- The ideal leader (mahant) organises, teaches and protects dharma; physical strength is cultivated.
- The mind is instructed directly (Manāce Śloka).


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence low

**Names:** Rāma, formless Brahman
**Descriptions:** Rāma, reached by the path of devotion; the formless Brahman beyond the elements, known by discrimination
**Negations:** not the elements, not the body
**Relation to self:** The Self beyond body and elements is Brahman; the accomplished abide in it.
**Relation to world:** The world is to be lived in with effort and vigilance while knowing the Self.
**Caveat:** Devotion to Rāma and Advaita discrimination are held together; neither is dropped.
