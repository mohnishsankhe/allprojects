A Marathi–Kannada lineage of householder teachers descending, in the tradition's account, from the nine Nāths; documented from Nimbargi and Bhausaheb Maharaj, through Siddharameshwar to Nisargadatta Maharaj of Mumbai, known worldwide through I Am That.

## Distinctive positions
- Initiation with a name-mantra (nāma) and meditation on it; Bhausaheb Maharaj taught long meditation ('the ant's way').
- Siddharameshwar Maharaj taught the quicker 'bird's way' of understanding (vihaṅgama) alongside meditation.
- Nisargadatta: hold to the bare sense 'I am'; it leads beyond itself to the Absolute that is prior to consciousness.
- Householder teachers: the principal masters were lay people.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** Parabrahman, the Absolute, 'I am' (consciousness)
**Descriptions:** The Absolute is prior to consciousness; the sense 'I am' is the first appearance, the door to it.; Nisargadatta: 'I am' is not the ultimate; the Absolute is beyond being and non-being.
**Relation to self:** What one is, is the Absolute; the 'I am' is an appearance in it, dependent on the body.
**Relation to world:** The world is an appearance in consciousness, which itself appears in the Absolute.
**Caveat:** The lineage keeps devotional practice (bhajans, name-mantra) alongside a teaching that negates even consciousness; the one-truth reading must not drop the claim that the Absolute is beyond consciousness.
