The oldest continuous current of Brahma Sūtra interpretation: Brahman is at once one with and different from souls and world, which are its real transformations. Known for Bhartṛprapañca only through fragments reported by his Advaita critics; its classic statement is Bhāskara's Brahmasūtrabhāṣya; Yādavaprakāśa (Rāmānuja's first teacher) gave a 'natural' version; Vijñānabhikṣu revived the frame in the 16th c. No living order continues the school; its ideas passed into the Vaiṣṇava Vedāntas.

## Distinctive positions
- Brahman is both different and non-different from the individual souls and the world: one as cause, many as effect.
- The world is a real transformation (pariṇāma) of Brahman or of its power (śakti), not an appearance (vivarta).
- Bhāskara: the difference of the soul from Brahman is due to real limiting adjuncts (upādhi); non-difference is natural (aupādhika-bhedābheda).
- Yādavaprakāśa: both difference and non-difference are natural (svābhāvika-bhedābheda); Brahman itself becomes Īśvara, souls and matter.
- Liberation requires knowledge combined with lifelong ritual and social duty (jñāna-karma-samuccaya) — Bhartṛprapañca, Bhāskara.
- Bhāskara denies liberation while living (jīvanmukti): release comes when the body, a real adjunct, falls away.
- Bhāskara attacks Śaṅkara's doctrine of māyā as a teaching of the Mahāyāna Buddhists.
- Vijñānabhikṣu (16th c.) restates the position as avibhāga (non-separation) of souls and prakṛti from Brahman, harmonising Vedānta with Sāṃkhya-Yoga.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Brahman, kāraṇa-brahman (Brahman as cause), Īśvara
**Descriptions:** Brahman is one as cause and many as effect; the world and souls are its real transformations (pariṇāma) through its power (śakti).; Bhāskara: difference from the soul is due to real adjuncts (upādhi), non-difference is natural.; Yādavaprakāśa: Brahman is naturally both one and many, transforming into Īśvara, the souls and matter.; Vijñānabhikṣu: souls and prakṛti are non-separate (avibhāga) from Brahman, which is their substrate.
**Negations:** Denies that the world is an illusory appearance (vivarta) of Brahman.; Denies that difference is merely imagined through a positive avidyā.
**Relation to self:** The soul is a part (aṃśa) of Brahman, limited by real adjuncts (Bhāskara) or naturally distinct-yet-one (Yādava); at liberation, after death, it becomes non-different from Brahman.
**Relation to world:** The world is Brahman's real effect and is non-different from it as cause; it is neither false nor wholly other.
**Caveat:** The school would reject a one-truth reading that treats its difference as merely provisional; for Bhāskara the adjuncts that make the difference are real until death.
