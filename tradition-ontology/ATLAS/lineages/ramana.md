The teaching that gathered round Ramana Maharshi at Tiruvannamalai (1896–1950): self-inquiry and surrender, taught in silence and in a few short Tamil and Sanskrit works, without formal initiation or succession.

## Distinctive positions
- Self-inquiry ('Who am I?'): tracing the I-thought to its source in the Heart is the direct path; the ego so sought vanishes and the Self shines as 'I-I'.
- Ramana named no human guru; the tradition holds his realization came without teacher or scripture, and regards Arunachala itself as his guru.
- Silence (mauna) is the highest teaching; he taught by level, holding non-origination (ajāta) as the final truth.
- Surrender to God or guru and self-inquiry reach the same end.
- No formal initiation or successor was appointed.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

**Names:** the Self (ātman), the Heart (hṛdaya), Ulladu (that which is; sat), Arunachala (Śiva)
**Descriptions:** Existence-consciousness-bliss, the one Self that shines as 'I-I' when the ego subsides.; The Heart is the Self, the source from which the I-thought rises.
**Negations:** not the body, the senses, the vital breath, the mind or the ignorance of sleep (Nāṉ Yār)
**Relation to self:** Identical: the Self is what one truly is; the ego is a false knot of consciousness and the body.
**Relation to world:** The world appears when the mind rises and disappears in deep sleep; it has no reality apart from the Self. Taught by level: for beginners creation is admitted; the final truth is non-origination.
**Caveat:** Ramana also spoke of God and guru as the Self and worshipped Arunachala as Śiva; the tradition would reject treating his teaching as mere philosophy apart from silence and grace.
