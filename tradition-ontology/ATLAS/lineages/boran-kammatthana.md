# Borān kammaṭṭhāna (old meditation; the yogāvacara tradition)

`lin:boran-kammatthana` · `skeleton` · confidence low

**Family:** ascetic
**Alternate names:** yogāvacara tradition, old Theravāda meditation, esoteric Theravāda (a scholars' label), kammathan boran
**Parent:** [Theravāda](theravada.md)
**Regions:** Cambodia, Laos, Thailand, Sri Lanka (18th-c. Siyam Nikāya revival)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: presented in its manuals as the Buddha's own method handed down by yogāvacaras; brought to Kandy with the Siamese ordination mission of 1753 (the tradition's account); Scholarly account: attested in manuscripts from at least the 17th–18th c.; probably older; marginalized by the 19th–20th c. reforms; (confidence low)
**Status:** revived

The pre-reform meditation system of mainland Southeast Asian and Sri Lankan Theravāda, known from manuals such as the Yogāvacara's Manual, which uses bodily placement, syllables and ritual; largely displaced by the 19th–20th-century reforms and now studied and partly revived.

## Distinctive positions
- Meditation proceeds by installing meditative qualities (the five kinds of rapture, the tranquillities, etc.) at specific points of the body in a fixed order, using Pali syllables and recitations.
- Symbolic correspondences between the stages of meditation, embryology, the letters of the Pali alphabet and the Abhidhamma lists.
- Teaching was given by a teacher to a qualified pupil after preliminary rites (offerings, requesting the meditation subject).

**Transmissions given:**
