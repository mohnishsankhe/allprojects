The lay insight lineage of Saya Thetgyi, Sayagyi U Ba Khin (1899–1971) and S. N. Goenka (1924–2013), whose standardized ten-day courses of mindfulness of respiration and body-scanning insight spread from Burma and India worldwide.

## Distinctive positions
- A ten-day residential course: five precepts (sīla), mindfulness of respiration at the nostrils (samādhi) for the first days, then insight by systematic observation of bodily sensations with equanimity (paññā), ending with loving-kindness (mettā).
- Reacting to sensations with craving or aversion multiplies saṅkhāras; equanimous observation of sensations as impermanent eradicates them.
- The teaching is presented as non-sectarian and universal; courses are given free, supported by donations of past students.
- U Ba Khin: 'the clock of vipassanā has struck' — the revival of the practice 2,500 years after the Buddha (the tradition's account).

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** nibbāna
**Descriptions:** the ultimate truth beyond mind and matter, experienced when the arising and passing of sensations is fully penetrated and all saṅkhāras are eradicated; described in courses as the stage beyond the field of mind and matter, where nothing arises
**Relation to self:** the 'I' is a mass of vibrations arising and passing; no permanent self
**Relation to world:** the world of mind and matter (nāma-rūpa) is experienced as constantly arising and passing; nibbāna is beyond it
**Caveat:** Presented as a universal law of nature (dhamma) rather than as a theology; the tradition would reject speculation about the ultimate in place of practice.
