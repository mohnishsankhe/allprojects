The Dharmaguptaka, a Sthavira-derived school of the north-west, is known above all for its Four-Part Vinaya (Sifen lü), translated into Chinese in 410–412, which became the monastic code of East Asian Buddhism and carries the only continuous bhikṣuṇī ordination lineage. The Chinese Dīrghāgama and the earliest Gāndhārī manuscripts (British Library) are associated with it.

## Distinctive positions
- Though the Buddha is included in the Saṅgha, a gift to the Buddha yields greater fruit than a gift to the Saṅgha; offerings to stūpas bring great fruit (Vasumitra).
- The liberation of the Buddha and of the disciples is one, though their paths differ (Vasumitra).
- Outsiders (non-Buddhists) cannot obtain the five superknowledges (Vasumitra; low confidence).
- The Four-Part Vinaya: 250 rules for bhikṣus and 348 for bhikṣuṇīs.
- A canon in which the Dīrghāgama (T1) is affiliated with this school; a Bodhisattva Piṭaka and a Dhāraṇī Piṭaka are reported in its canon (low confidence).
