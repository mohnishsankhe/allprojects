Cārvāka or Lokāyata is the Indian school of materialism. Its root text, the Bṛhaspati Sūtra, and all its commentaries are lost; its positions are known from quotations and summaries by opponents — Buddhist (Śāntarakṣita, Kamalaśīla, Candrakīrti), Jain (Haribhadra, Guṇaratna), Nyāya (Jayanta), Vedānta (Śaṅkara, Sadānanda, the Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha) — and from one surviving sceptical work, Jayarāśi's Tattvopaplavasiṃha. Jayanta distinguishes 'rogue' (dhūrta) from 'well-educated' (suśikṣita) Cārvākas.

## Distinctive positions
- (As reported) The principles (tattva) are the four elements — earth, water, fire, air; their aggregate is called body, senses and objects (Bṛhaspati Sūtra fragments).
- (As reported) Consciousness arises from the elements when they are transformed into a body, like the intoxicating power from fermenting ingredients (madaśaktivat); the person is the body qualified by consciousness.
- (As reported) Because there is no one who goes to another world, there is no other world (paralokino 'bhāvāt paralokābhāvaḥ).
- (As reported) Perception is the only means of knowledge; inference is not valid because invariable concomitance cannot be established — or, according to Purandara, inference is valid only within worldly experience.
- (As reported) Pleasure is the aim of life; heaven is pleasure, hell is pain, the only lord is the king known to the world, liberation is the death of the body.
- (As reported) The Vedic sacrifices and the funeral offerings are a livelihood devised by those lacking intelligence and manliness.
- Jayarāśi's Tattvopaplavasiṃha, the only surviving work, argues that no means of knowledge can be established at all, so that even the four elements 'do not stand when examined'.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** the four elements (bhūta-catuṣṭaya) as the only principles, own-nature (svabhāva), the world as far as the senses reach (yāvān indriyagocaraḥ)
**Descriptions:** (As reported) 'Earth, water, fire, air: these are the principles' (Bṛhaspati Sūtra fragment).; (As reported) The variety of the world — fire hot, water cold, wind temperate — is fixed by svabhāva, not by an unseen cause (Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha 1).
**Negations:** No other world, no self that goes to it (paralokino 'bhāvāt paralokābhāvaḥ).; No heaven or liberation other than pleasure and the death of the body; no lord but the king.; (Jayarāśi) Not even the elements 'stand when examined' — no principle is established.
**Relation to self:** (As reported) The self is the living body qualified by consciousness; it ends with the body.
**Relation to world:** (As reported) The world is the perceptible aggregate of four elements.
**Caveat:** The Cārvāka denies any ultimate beyond what perception shows and explicitly denies another world, an unseen cause and liberation; Jayarāśi denies that any principle can be established. Any one-truth reading runs against these denials; the school would call it the priests' 'livelihood'.
