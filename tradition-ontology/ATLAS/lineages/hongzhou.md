The school of Mazu Daoyi (709–788) in Jiangxi, which taught that the ordinary functioning mind is buddha and developed encounter dialogue; its heirs Baizhang, Huangbo, Linji, Guishan and Zhaozhou shaped classical Chan.

## Distinctive positions
- 'This very mind is buddha' and 'neither mind nor buddha' (Mazu).
- 'The ordinary mind is the Way'; all activity is the function of buddha-nature.
- Encounter dialogue: shouts, blows and everyday gestures as direct pointing.


## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** this very mind (jixin 即心), ordinary mind (pingchang xin 平常心)
**Descriptions:** 'The Way needs no cultivation — only do not defile it… the ordinary mind is the Way' (Mazu's sermon, Jingde chuandeng lu juan 28).; 'This very mind is buddha' (Mazu to Damei).
**Negations:** 'Neither mind nor buddha' (Mazu).
**Relation to self:** The very one walking, standing, sitting and lying.
**Relation to world:** 'All dharmas are mind-dharmas; all names are mind-names' (Mazu).
**Caveat:** Zongmi criticised Hongzhou for equating all activity with buddha-nature; Hongzhou would reject his charge (see dsp:hongzhou-all-activity-buddha-nature).
