**Status:** living

The Rāmānandī sampradāya, the largest Vaiṣṇava ascetic order of north India, looks to Rāmānanda of Vārāṇasī and worships Rāma with Sītā through the Rāma mantra and name. Its hagiography (Nābhādās's Bhaktamāl) assigns Rāmānanda disciples of every caste, including Kabīr and Raidās; its greatest poet, Tulsīdās, made the Awadhi Rāmcaritmānas the devotional scripture of the Hindi-speaking world. It comprises ascetic (tyāgī) akhāṛās, householders, and a rasik branch.

## Distinctive positions
- Rāma with Sītā is the supreme Brahman; Hanumān is the model devotee-servant.
- The six-syllable Rāma mantra (rāṃ rāmāya namaḥ) and the name 'Rāma' — the tāraka ('ferrying') mantra Śiva whispers to the dying at Kāśī — are the heart of practice.
- Refuge in Rāma is open to all castes and to women; the tradition remembers Rāmānanda as initiating a weaver, a cobbler, a barber, a Jāṭ farmer, a Rajput king and women; the saying 'jāti pāṃti pūchai nahiṃ koī, hari ko bhajai so hari ko hoī' is ascribed to him in oral tradition.
- Teaching in the vernacular: Tulsīdās's Awadhi Rāmcaritmānas became the scripture of north Indian Rāma-bhakti.
- Rāma is both formless and with form; the name is greater than either (Tulsī).
- Two styles of devotion: the ascetic (tyāgī, tapasvī) and the rasik, who contemplates the eternal love-play of Sītā and Rāma as a companion (sakhī).
- Whether the order descends from Rāmānuja or is independent is debated within the tradition.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** Rāma (with Sītā), Brahman, with and without qualities
**Descriptions:** the supreme Brahman who, formless, becomes embodied through the devotee's love (Mānas 1.116); the master in the form of all moving and unmoving beings (Mānas 4.3); whose name is greater than both his forms (Mānas 1.18–28)
**Negations:** without qualities, without form, unseen, unborn (Mānas 1.116)
**Relation to self:** The soul is Rāma's servant; the exclusive devotee holds 'I am the servant' unwaveringly.
**Relation to world:** The world is full of Sītā and Rāma and to be bowed to; seen through māyā ('I and mine') it binds, and is like a dream beside worship of Hari.
**Caveat:** The tradition would reject any reading that makes the embodied Rāma lower than the formless; the name is held greater than both.
