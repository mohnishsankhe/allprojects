The Śākta tantric culture of Kāmarūpa (Assam) and Bengal: the Kālikā Purāṇa on Kāmākhyā, the pīṭhas and the offerings; the Yoginī and Kāmākhyā Tantras; the Satī–Mahāvidyā myth of the Bṛhaddharma and Mahābhāgavata Purāṇas; the great digests of the 16th–19th centuries (Kṛṣṇānanda's Tantrasāra, Brahmānanda's Śāktānandataraṅgiṇī and Tārārahasya, Pūrṇānanda's Śrītattvacintāmaṇi and Śyāmārahasya, Sarvānanda's Sarvollāsa, the Prāṇatoṣiṇī); the Tārā cult of Tārāpīṭh; and the Śākta devotional songs of Rāmprasād Sen and Kamalākānta.

## Distinctive positions
- Kāmākhyā in Kāmarūpa is the seat where Satī's yoni fell; the Goddess there is the great vidyā in the form of the yoni and is all four aims of life (Kālikā Purāṇa 18.42; Kāmākhyā Tantra 1.4, 1.9).
- The pīṭhas are the places where the parts of Satī's body fell and Śiva stays as a liṅga beside each (Kālikā Purāṇa 18.36-50).
- Satī shows Śiva her ten Mahāvidyā forms, stationed in the ten directions, before going to Dakṣa's sacrifice (Bṛhaddharma Purāṇa madhya 6.125-134).
- Blood offering is taught (Kālikā Purāṇa ch. 67, 'rudhirādhyāya', summary only) together with substitutes (pumpkin, sugarcane, liquor as 'equal to' a victim) and prohibitions for brāhmaṇas.
- The Dakṣiṇakālī form (four-armed, garland of heads, standing on Śiva) is set out in Kṛṣṇānanda's Tantrasāra, quoting the Kālī Tantra.
- The Mahāvidyā mantras are 'siddha' vidyās needing no preliminary checks — which Kṛṣṇānanda reads as praise, not a rule (Tantrasāra, siddhādiśodhana).
- Devotional Śāktism: the poet-saints sing to Kālī/Śyāmā as Mother, and to Umā in the homecoming (āgamanī) and farewell (vijayā) songs.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Mahāmāyā, Kāmākhyā, Ādyā Śakti, Śyāmā / Kālī, Mā (Mother)
**Descriptions:** Kāmākhyā is dharma, artha, kāma and liberation; nothing in the three worlds exists without her (Kāmākhyā Tantra 1.9-13); the subtle root-nature (mūla-prakṛti) who divided herself into māyā and vidyā to bring about creation (Bṛhaddharma Purāṇa madhya 6.98-124); the Mother (Mā) of the devotional songs, both terrible and tender
**Negations:** herself without the three guṇas, she shines through the one who has them (Bṛhaddharma madhya 6.120)
**Relation to self:** The devotee is her child; the digests adopt the Kaula and Vedāntic teaching that the self is one with the Goddess.
**Relation to world:** The earth bears her body in the pīṭhas, where the parts of Satī fell (Kālikā Purāṇa 18.40-50).
**Caveat:** Devotional Bengal Śāktism keeps the relation of child and Mother and resists merging it into an impersonal absolute; ritual texts of the same region keep sharp distinctions of eligibility (caste, bhāva) that a one-truth reading should not erase.
