Korean Chan: introduced from Tang China from the 9th century as the Nine Mountain schools, unified in doctrine and practice by Jinul (1158–1210) around sudden awakening/gradual cultivation and hwadu practice, and carried on by Taego, Hyujeong and the modern Jogye Order.

## Distinctive positions
- Sudden awakening followed by gradual cultivation (dono jeomsu 頓悟漸修) as the normative path (Jinul).
- The mind as 'empty and calm, numinous awareness' (gongjeok yeongji 空寂靈知) to be recognised by tracing back the radiance (Jinul, after Zongmi).
- Harmony of Seon and the doctrinal teachings (seon-gyo ilchi), yet the hwadu 'shortcut' is the highest gate (Jinul, Hyujeong).
- Ganhwa Seon (observing the critical phrase) as the mainstream practice of the Jogye order.
- In the 20th c. Seongcheol argued for sudden awakening/sudden cultivation (dono donsu) against Jinul (see dsp:seon-sudden-cultivation-debate).

**Transmissions received:**

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence high

**Names:** true mind (jinsim 真心), empty and calm, numinous awareness (gongjeok yeongji 空寂靈知), self-nature (jaseong 自性)
**Descriptions:** The mind that is empty and calm yet numinously aware and never obscured is one's original face and the Dharma-seal transmitted by buddhas and patriarchs (Jinul, Susim kyol).; Samādhi is its substance and prajñā its function; calm and always knowing, knowing and always calm (Susim kyol).
**Negations:** It has no form or characteristic, no size, no boundary, no inside or outside, no birth and death (Susim kyol).
**Relation to self:** It is the very one who asks the question — the awareness that sees, hears and acts (Susim kyol).
**Relation to world:** It is not the physical body; the body is 'provisional, arising and ceasing', while 'one thing is ever numinous, covering heaven and earth' (Susim kyol).
**Caveat:** Jinul's language of numinous awareness follows Zongmi and the Heze line; Korean critics (Seongcheol) held that this 'understanding-awakening' is not final realization. The tradition would reject reading the true mind as an entity separate from phenomena.
