# Yoginī Kaula (Yoginīkaula) of Matsyendra

**Family:** vedic
**Alternate names:** Yoginīkaula, Mahāyoginīkaula, Siddha Kaula (as a stage in the Kaulajñānanirṇaya's account), Matsyodara (the Kali-age form of the Kaula)
**Parent:** [Kaula (the Kula tradition)](kaula.md)
**Founders:** [Matsyendranātha](../teachers/matsyendranatha.md)
**Key teachers:** [Gorakṣanātha](../teachers/goraksanatha.md)
**Regions:** Kāmarūpa (Assam), Candradvīpa (traditional place of descent), Nepal (manuscripts)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: the Kaula descended in each age: as Jñānanirṇiti, Mahat, Siddhāmṛta and, in the Kali age, as Matsyodara 'from the fish's belly' (KJN 16.47–48); Scholarly account: the oldest Kaulajñānanirṇaya manuscript (Nepal) was assigned by its editor to c. the 11th c.; the teaching is usually placed c. 9th–11th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** absorbed

The Yoginīkaula is the Kaula current that the Kaulajñānanirṇaya says Macchaghnapāda (Matsyendra) brought down from Candradvīpa and that was 'in every house of the yoginīs' in Kāmarūpa (KJN 22.10). Its texts, edited by P. C. Bagchi, teach the body-liṅga, the centres and nectar of the body, the conquest of death, the Akula beyond all distinctions and the secrecy of the Kaula; the Nāth tradition places Matsyendra at its head.

## Distinctive positions
- The true liṅga is the body-liṅga, worshipped with eight inner flowers (non-harming, sense-restraint, compassion, feeling, patience, conquest of anger, meditation, knowledge) (KJN 3.24–29).
- One whose tendency (vāsanā) rests in the body belongs to the Kula; one whose tendency is outward is a bound 'animal' (paśu) (KJN 3.31).
- Śiva is not without Śakti nor Śakti without Śiva (KJN 17.8); the self is guru, siddha, disciple and Śiva (KJN 11.26).
- Nectar-meditations conquer death and old age; eleven centres of the body grant powers (KJN 5).
- The teaching is given only to a tested, devoted disciple, ear to ear (KJN 5.35; 6.9; 17.10–11).
- The Kaula path is of two kinds, made (kṛtaka) and innate (sahaja); the aroused kuṇḍalī is 'made', the innate abides in samarasa (Akulavīra B.43).
- Kaula rites with meat, wine, fish, sexual union and bodily substances are taught (summary only; restricted) (KJN 8; 11; 18).
