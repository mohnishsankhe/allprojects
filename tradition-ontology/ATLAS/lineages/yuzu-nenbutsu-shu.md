# Yūzū Nenbutsu-shū (the interfusing nenbutsu of Ryōnin)

**Dates:** Tradition's account: 1117, when Amida revealed the interfusing nenbutsu to Ryōnin in samādhi; Scholarly account: early 12th c.; revived by Hōmyō in the 14th c. and recognised as a school in the Edo period; (confidence moderate)
**Status:** living

The school of 'interfusing nenbutsu' traced to the Tendai chant master Ryōnin (1073–1132), to whom Amida is said to have revealed that one person's nenbutsu and all others' pervade one another; Ryōnin enrolled people of every class in a nenbutsu register. Revived in the 14th c. by Hōmyō; head temple Dainenbutsu-ji in Osaka.

## Distinctive positions
- One person is all people, all people are one person; one practice is all practices, all practices are one practice: each person's nenbutsu interfuses with everyone's, so the merit of all is shared (the revelation verse, as transmitted).
- Members inscribe their names in a register (myōchō) and pledge a daily number of recitations.
