**Status:** absorbed

A Śaiva reading of the Brahma Sūtras by Śrīkaṇṭha Śivācārya, structurally close to Rāmānuja's Viśiṣṭādvaita but with Śiva and his Śakti in place of Nārāyaṇa and Śrī. It survived chiefly through Appayya Dīkṣita's commentary Śivārkamaṇidīpikā and through Vīraśaiva authors who adopted it; Śrīpati's Śakti-viśiṣṭādvaita (lin:virasaiva) is its Vīraśaiva counterpart.

## Distinctive positions
- Brahman is Śiva (Parameśvara), inseparably endowed with his power (cit-śakti, identified with Umā); souls (paśu) and the world are his body and qualification.
- Śiva is both material cause (through his śakti, in which the world lies subtly) and efficient cause of the world, which is a real transformation of that power.
- The supreme ether (paramākāśa, dahara) of the Upaniṣads is Śiva's cit-śakti; meditation on the small space in the heart (dahara-vidyā) is central.
- The Veda and the Śaiva Āgamas are not different in authority; both come from Śiva.
- Liberation is freedom from bonds (pāśa) and equality with Śiva (śiva-sāmya) in bliss, without the power of cosmic governance (BS 4.4.17).
- Appayya Dīkṣita reads Śrīkaṇṭha's qualified non-dualism as a stage pointing to Advaita (Śivādvaitanirṇaya) — a reading contested by Vīraśaiva authors.

## The ultimate in this lineage
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Names:** Śiva (Parameśvara, Paraśiva), Brahman, Paramākāśa / dahara (the supreme ether in the heart), Umā-sahāya (accompanied by Umā)
**Descriptions:** Śiva endowed with cit-śakti, free from all defects, the material and efficient cause of the world.; The supreme ether of the Upaniṣads is his śakti; he is to be meditated on in the small space of the heart.
**Negations:** Denies an attributeless Brahman without śakti.; Denies that Śiva is only the efficient cause.
**Relation to self:** Souls (paśu) are his body and attribute, distinct yet inseparable; liberated, they become equal to Śiva in bliss (śiva-sāmya) but not identical.
**Relation to world:** The world is real, a manifestation of the subtle world held in Śiva's śakti.
**Caveat:** Appayya Dīkṣita read the school as a step towards Advaita; the school's own text and its Vīraśaiva heirs hold the qualified non-dualism as final.
