# Lakulīśa

**Alternate names:** Lakulin, Lakulī, Nakulīśa, Nakulīśvara, Lakuṭapāṇi ('club in hand'), Lākulīśa
**Lineages:** [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md), [Atimārga](../lineages/atimarga.md), [Lākula](../lineages/lakula.md), [Kālāmukha](../lineages/kalamukha.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: the 28th and last of Śiva's yoga-teacher incarnations of the Vaivasvata manvantara, in the age when Kṛṣṇa Vāsudeva and Vyāsa Dvaipāyana are born (Liṅga P. 1.24.125-131; Kūrma P. 1.51.9-10); Scholarly account: c. 2nd c. CE (reckoned back ten teacher-generations from the Mathurā pillar inscription of 380 CE); some scholars say 1st–2nd c.; (confidence moderate)
**Places:** Kāyāvarohaṇa / Kāyāvataraṇa / Kāyārohaṇa (Karvan, near Vadodara, Gujarat), Ujjayinī, Ulkāgrāma (birthplace per the Kāravaṇa-māhātmya), Bhṛgukṣetra (Broach region)
**Historicity:** semi-legendary
**Students:** [Kuśika](kusika.md), [Garga (Pāśupata)](garga-pasupata.md), [Mitra (Pāśupata)](mitra-pasupata.md), [Kauruṣya](kaurusya.md)
**Works:** 
  - [Pāśupata Sūtra](../texts/pasupata-sutra.md) — attribution: traditional

Lakulīśa, 'Lord with the club', is regarded as the incarnation of Śiva who founded the Pāśupata observance and taught the Pāśupata Sūtra, and the first of the Pāśupata tīrthakaras. His images show him seated, holding a club and a citron, often ithyphallic; the Liṅga Purāṇa (1.76.38-39) prescribes his image surrounded by disciples, hand raised in exposition. Kauṇḍinya does not use the name Lakulīśa; he speaks of 'the Lord' who descended at Kāyāvataraṇa.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Not a human who became realized but the Lord himself: seeing an abandoned corpse in a cremation ground, Śiva entered it by yogic power and became the brahmacārin Lakulī at the siddha-field called Kāyāvatāra (Liṅga P. 1.24.127-130); Kauṇḍinya says the Lord, in human form, took a brahmin's body, descended at Kāyāvataraṇa and walked to Ujjayinī, where he taught Kuśika; the Kāravaṇa-māhātmya says Śiva was born to Viśvarūpa and Sudarśanā, and after the infant died Śiva entered the body and later merged into the Brahmeśvara liṅga at Kāyāvarohaṇa.
