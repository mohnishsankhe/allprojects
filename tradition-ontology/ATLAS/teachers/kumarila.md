Founder of the Bhāṭṭa school: author of the Ślokavārttika, Tantravārttika and Ṭupṭīkā on Śabara, and a lost Bṛhaṭṭīkā. He defended the authorless Veda and intrinsic validity against Buddhists, rejected omniscience in matters of dharma, a creator and a total dissolution, and set out a path to release through avoidance of desire-prompted and prohibited acts.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Hagiographies of Śaṅkara (e.g. the Śaṅkaradigvijaya ascribed to Mādhava-Vidyāraṇya) present Kumārila as an incarnation of Skanda sent to restore the Vedic path; they tell that he studied among Buddhists, survived a fall from a height by the truth of the Veda, and later entered a husk-fire at Prayāga as expiation, where Śaṅkara met him and was sent on to Maṇḍana Miśra.

_Notes: Hagiographic details are from memory and are not in Kumārila's works (low confidence). The teacher–pupil link with Prabhākara is a Bhāṭṭa legend; its historicity is doubted._
