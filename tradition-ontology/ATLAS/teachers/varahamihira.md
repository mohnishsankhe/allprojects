The great compiler of all three branches of jyotiṣa: Pañcasiddhāntikā (siddhānta), Bṛhajjātaka and Laghujātaka (horā), Bṛhat Saṃhitā (saṃhitā), and works on marriage and royal expeditions (Yogayātrā). Son and pupil of Ādityadāsa, of Avanti, he received a boon from the Sun at Kāpitthaka and composed his horā after studying the views of the seers (BJ 28.9); he bows to the former promulgators (BJ 28.10) and honours the Yavanas (BS 2.14). Tradition counts him among the 'nine jewels' of Vikramāditya's court (scholarly account: a later legend).
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Received a boon of grace from the Sun (savitṛ-labdha-vara-prasāda) at Kāpitthaka.
