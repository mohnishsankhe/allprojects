Founder of the Ekaśaraṇa Dharma (tradition 1449–1568), of the Śiromaṇi Bhuyan family of Bardowā; orphaned and raised by his grandmother, he studied under Mahendra Kandali, made two long pilgrimages, preached exclusive devotion to Kṛṣṇa through the name, converted Mādhavadeva, and — persecuted in the Ahom kingdom — continued under the Koch kings Naranārāyaṇa and Cilarāi. Poet of the Kīrtana-ghoṣā, bargīts and aṅkīyā nāṭs; called the Mahāpuruṣa.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Recognised as the Mahāpuruṣa; Assamese hagiographies tell of a meeting with Caitanya at Purī (not found in Bengali sources).
