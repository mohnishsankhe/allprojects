The name under which the five 'Dharmas of Maitreya' (Tibetan list) and, in Chinese tradition, the Yogācārabhūmi are transmitted; for the tradition, the future buddha Maitreya teaching Asaṅga.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** The tradition holds that the author is the bodhisattva Maitreya (tch:maitreya-bodhisattva), who appeared to Asaṅga after twelve years of fruitless meditation — first as a wounded dog whose maggots Asaṅga, moved by compassion, tried to remove with his tongue — and took him to Tuṣita, where he taught him the five treatises.

_Notes: Whether a historical human teacher named Maitreya(nātha) existed is a scholarly question (Ui Hakuju argued yes; others treat the name as a pious attribution). The registry id is used for the author; the bodhisattva is tch:maitreya-bodhisattva (U39)._
