# Tiruñāṉacampantar

`tch:campantar` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Campantar, Ñāṉacampantar, Āḷuṭaiya Piḷḷaiyār, Jñānasambandha
**Lineages:** [The Nāyaṉmārs and the Tirumuṟai (Tamil Śaiva bhakti)](../lineages/nayanmar.md), [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: lived sixteen years; Scholarly account: 7th c.; contemporary of Appar, the Pāṇṭiya Neṭumāṟaṉ and Ciṟuttoṇṭar (Vātāpi, 642); (confidence moderate)
**Places:** Cīkāḻi (birth), Madurai, Nallūr Perumaṇam (union with Śiva at his wedding)
**Historicity:** historical
**Works:** 
  - [Tēvāram](../texts/tevaram.md) — attribution: accepted

Child-saint and the most prolific Tēvāram poet (Tirumuṟai 1–3); exemplar of the son's path (satputra-mārga, kriyā); champion of Śaivism against the Jains at Madurai.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** At three, left on the tank's edge at Cīkāḻi, he cried and Umā fed him the milk of divine knowledge in a golden cup; asked who had fed him, he sang his first hymn pointing to Śiva and Umā. He went on pilgrimage with the bard Tirunīlakaṇṭa Yāḻppāṇar, was invited to Madurai by the queen Maṅkaiyarkkaraciyār and the minister Kulacciṟaiyār, cured the king's fever with sacred ash, and won the fire and water ordeals against the Jains. At sixteen, at his wedding at Nallūr Perumaṇam, he entered a divine light with his bride and all the guests.
