The 'Lotus-born', the second Buddha of the Nyingma. Tradition: born from a lotus on Lake Dhanakośa in Oḍḍiyāna, adopted by King Indrabhūti, a renunciant and charnel-ground yogin who received teachings from many masters, attained immortality with Mandāravā in the Māratika cave, was invited to Tibet by Trisong Detsen on Śāntarakṣita's advice, subdued the spirits obstructing Samye, gave the empowerments of the Eight Herukas to his disciples, concealed treasures for future times with Yeshe Tsogyal, and departed for the land of the rākṣasas, where he dwells on the Copper-Coloured Mountain. Scholarly: an 8th-c. Indian tantric master whose brief stay in Tibet is recorded in early sources; his hagiography grows from the 12th c. treasures.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Attained the vidyādhara level of immortality and the rainbow body of great transference; appears in eight manifestations.
