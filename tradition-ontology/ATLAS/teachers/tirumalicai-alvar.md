# Tirumaḻicai Āḻvār

Author of the Nāṉmukaṉ Tiruvantāti and the Tiruccanta Viruttam; the Āḻvār who, in the tradition's account, first studied the Buddhist, Jain and Śaiva doctrines and then found refuge in Nārāyaṇa; his verses insist that Nārāyaṇa created Brahmā and Brahmā Śiva.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Born to the sage Bhārgava as a limbless form, abandoned, quickened by the Lord and raised by a woodcutter's family; he passed through the Śākya, Jain and Śaiva teachings before Pēyāḻvār turned him to Viṣṇu. When the Pallava king banished his disciple Kaṇikaṇṇaṉ from Kāñcī, the Āḻvār told the Lord of Tiruveḵkā to roll up his serpent-bed and come too; the Lord did, and returned when they returned — hence 'the Lord who did as he was told' (coṉṉa vaṇṇam ceyta perumāḷ). He cast his writings into the Kāvēri and only two floated back against the current. At Kuṭantai the reclining Lord began to rise when the Āḻvār asked him to speak.
