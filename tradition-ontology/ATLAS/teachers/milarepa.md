The great yogin-poet of Tibet: after using sorcery to avenge his family he turned to the Dharma, endured Marpa's ordeals, practised for years in mountain caves wearing only cotton cloth (repa) and sustained by inner heat, and attained buddhahood in one life by the tradition's account; teacher of Gampopa and Rechungpa; his songs are the Hundred Thousand Songs.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Having killed thirty-five people through sorcery and destroyed crops with hail, he felt remorse, sought Dharma, and was made by Marpa to build and tear down towers until his evil karma was purified; after empowerment he meditated alone for years, living on nettles until his body turned green; he attained realization through inner heat and Mahāmudrā, displayed miraculous powers (flight; the contest with Naro Bönchung at Kailash), and at death, poisoned by a jealous scholar, showed signs of buddhahood.

_Notes: Details of the life are from Tsangnyön Heruka's 1488 version; earlier lives differ in details._
