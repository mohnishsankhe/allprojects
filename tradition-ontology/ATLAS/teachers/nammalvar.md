Chief of the Āḻvārs and 'first head of the clan' (kulapati) of the Śrīvaiṣṇavas: author of the Tiruviruttam, Tiruvāciriyam, Periya Tiruvantāti and Tiruvāymoḻi, held to be the four Vedas in Tamil. His hymns teach the Lord's supremacy and accessibility, knowledge-and-love as the Lord's gift, surrender at Tiruvēṅkaṭam with Śrī as mediator, service to the Lord and his devotees, and love-longing in the voice of Parāṅkuśa-nāyakī; the last decads describe the ascent on the path of light and final union.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Born to Kāri and Uṭaiya Naṅkai of a Vellāḷa family at Kurukūr, the child did not cry or feed and repelled the 'śaṭha' wind that makes newborns forget (hence Śaṭhakopa); his parents laid him in a hollow of the tamarind tree at the temple, where he sat silent in absorption for sixteen years. Madhurakavi, following a light from the north, found him and asked: 'If the small is born in the body of the dead, what will it eat and where will it lie?' — the child answered: 'That it will eat, and there it will lie.' He then sang his four works, which Madhurakavi wrote down. Centuries later he appeared to Nāthamuni in vision and gave him the whole Prabandham.

_Notes: Registry id. U14 references him as the head of the Śrīvaiṣṇava lineage. The 'small/dead' riddle is commonly read as the soul (small) born in matter (dead): it lives on matter's experiences until it knows the Lord. Aṃśa of Viṣvaksena in the tradition's list (moderate). His image-crown Śaṭhāri (caṭāri) bears the Lord's feet (prc:satari)._
