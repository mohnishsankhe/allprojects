Called simply 'the Ācārya' by later Naiyāyikas; the great defender of Īśvara (Nyāyakusumāñjali) and of the self against the Buddhists (Ātmatattvaviveka), commentator on Vācaspati (Pariśuddhi) and on Praśastapāda (Kiraṇāvalī), author of the Lakṣaṇāvalī. He joined Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika and is regarded as the bridge between old and new Nyāya.

_Notes: Hagiographic tradition (low confidence): denied entry to the Jagannātha temple at Puri, he is said to have addressed the Lord: 'Drunk with lordship you disregard me; when the Buddhists come, your existence depends on me.'_
