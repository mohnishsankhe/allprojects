U44 contribution (the siddha of the eighty-four): No. 3 of the eighty-four siddhas (Tōh 2292 order). A monk-scholar (at Nālandā or Somapura) who recited the Vajravārāhī (or Cakrasaṃvara) mantra for twelve years without a sign and threw his rosary into the latrine; that night Nairātmyā appeared and gave him consecration; he ate pigeons and drank wine and was expelled, walked across the Ganges, stopped the sun to go on drinking without paying, and subdued non-Buddhist shrines; his Hevajra teaching is the source of the Sakya Path and Result.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** A monk-scholar (at Nālandā or Somapura) who recited the Vajravārāhī (or Cakrasaṃvara) mantra for twelve years without a sign and threw his rosary into the latrine; that night Nairātmyā appeared and gave him consecration; he ate pigeons and drank wine and was expelled, walked across the Ganges, stopped the sun to go on drinking without paying, and subdued non-Buddhist shrines; his Hevajra teaching is the source of the Sakya Path and Result. Realization song in Tōh 2292 (no. 3): Within mahāmudrā, rest that innate, beyond thought and reasoning, in its own selfless state; it does not fall into annihilation, being self-known in experience; it does not fall into permanence, being without clinging: it is union.
