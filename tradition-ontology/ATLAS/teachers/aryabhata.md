Author of the Āryabhaṭīya, written when he was 23 in the year 3600 of the Kali age (499 CE) and honoured at Kusumapura. He taught the equal quarters of the yuga, the rotation of the earth (the boat simile), eclipses by the earth's shadow and the moon, and an alphabetic numeral notation; he opens by bowing to Ka, the supreme Brahman, and says his knowledge came by Brahmā's grace. His school (āryapakṣa) dominated South India and Kerala; Brahmagupta criticized him for departing from the smṛti.
