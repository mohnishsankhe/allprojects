Renouncer-poet: a rich merchant of Kāvirippūmpaṭṭiṉam who renounced after his adopted son left him an eyeless needle; his songs on impermanence, the body, women and renunciation are among the most loved in Tamil. Guru of Pattirakiriyār; his samādhi is at Tiruvoṟṟiyūr (Chennai). The tradition treats him as one person with Paṭṭiṉattup Piḷḷaiyār of the eleventh Tirumuṟai; scholars usually distinguish two or more poets.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Childless, Tiruveṇkāṭar received a boy (Śiva's gift, Marutavāṇaṉ) who went trading and returned with cakes of dung; the angry father broke them and found gems. The boy vanished, leaving a box with an eyeless needle and a palm-leaf: 'even an eyeless needle will not come with you on the final road'. He renounced at once; survived his sister's poisoned cake ('one's own deed burns oneself; the baked cake burns the house'); returned to cremate his mother, singing ten songs; at Ujjain was condemned to the stake, which burst into flame, and the king Bhadragiri became his disciple; at Tiruvoṟṟiyūr, playing with boys who covered him with a basket, he vanished and a liṅga was found in his place.

_Notes: The story is recalled at moderate confidence from the popular hagiography; details vary between tellings._
