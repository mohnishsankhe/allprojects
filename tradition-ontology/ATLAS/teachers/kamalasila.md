Śāntarakṣita's disciple, author of the Tattvasaṅgrahapañjikā, the Madhyamakāloka and the three Bhāvanākramas, who defended the gradual path at the Samye debate against the Chan master Moheyan.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Tibetan histories (the sBa bzhed and later) tell that he was invited on Śāntarakṣita's instruction, debated Moheyan before the king at Samye, was declared victor so that the gradual path of the six perfections became the norm in Tibet, wrote the Bhāvanākramas at the king's request, and was later murdered.

_Notes: Samye debate date c. 792–794 in scholarly reconstructions; the Chinese Dunhuang sources give Moheyan's side as victorious._
