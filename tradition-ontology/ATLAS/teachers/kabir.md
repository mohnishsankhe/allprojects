Weaver (julāhā) poet-saint of Banaras, raised in a Muslim weaver household, the most influential of the Sants: he taught love of the formless Rām through the Name and the true guru, mocked the formalism of paṇḍit and mullā, yogī and ascetic, rejected caste, idols and pilgrimage, and spoke of the natural state (sahaj) and of dying while alive. His words survive in three recensions (the eastern Bījak, the Rajasthani Granthāvalī/Pañcvāṇī, the Punjabi Ādi Granth) and in a vast oral corpus; the Kabīr Panth reveres him as the incarnate True Being.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Initiated by Rāmānanda, who stumbled on him lying on the steps of the Pañcagaṅgā ghāṭ before dawn and uttered 'Rām' (Rāmānandī and popular account); in the Kabīr Panth he needs no guru, being the Sat Puruṣ himself, and accepts Rāmānanda only to model discipleship. At his death at Maghar his Hindu and Muslim followers disputed his body; under the shroud they found only flowers, which they divided for a samādhi and a tomb.

_Notes: Relation to Rāmānanda is disputed (dsp:kabir-guru-ramananda). Encounters with Sikandar Lodī and with Gorakhnāth are legendary._
