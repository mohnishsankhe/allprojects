Founder figure of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism: Navadvīpa scholar who after initiation at Gayā led nocturnal congregational chanting, took renunciation, travelled to south India and Vṛndāvana, and lived his last eighteen years at Purī absorbed in Rādhā's love for Kṛṣṇa. His only writing is the Śikṣāṣṭaka; his teaching was given to Rūpa, Sanātana, Rāmānanda and others and recorded by his biographers.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Kṛṣṇa himself, appearing with the mood and golden complexion of Rādhā to taste her love (CC 1.1.5–6); the hidden (channa) avatāra and yuga-avatāra of Kali who inaugurated the chanting of the name; in his last years he passed through states of divine madness (divyonmāda); he disappeared at Purī in 1533 (accounts differ on how).
