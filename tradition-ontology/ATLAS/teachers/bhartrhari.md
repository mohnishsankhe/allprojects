The grammarian-philosopher, author of the Vākyapadīya, its Vṛtti (traditionally) and the Mahābhāṣyadīpikā. He taught that Brahman is the Word-principle appearing as the world, that all cognition is permeated by the word, that meaning is borne by the indivisible sphoṭa and grasped as pratibhā, and that grammar is a door to liberation. The later tradition often identifies him with the poet of the Śatakatraya; the Chinese pilgrim Yijing reports a Bhartṛhari who went back and forth between monastic and household life.
