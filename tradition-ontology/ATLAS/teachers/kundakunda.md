The most revered Digambara ācārya, author of the Samayasāra, Pravacanasāra, Niyamasāra, Pañcāstikāyasāra, the Aṣṭapāhuḍa and the Bārasa Aṇuvekkhā (tradition credits him with eighty-four pāhuḍas). He taught the pure self from the standpoint of ultimate reality (niścaya) and the conventional standpoint (vyavahāra) as its teaching device, and upheld monastic nudity against clothed renunciation. The Mūla Saṅgha calls itself his lineage (Kundakundānvaya); Tamil Jains identify him with Elācārya, to whom they ascribe the Tirukkuṟaḷ.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** He travelled by magical power to the living Tīrthaṅkara Sīmandhara in Videha and heard the teaching directly (Devasena's Darśanasāra; later Digambara legend).
