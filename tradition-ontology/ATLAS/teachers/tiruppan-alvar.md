# Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār

**Alternate names:** Yogivāha, Munivāhana, Pāṇperumāḷ, Thiruppan Alvar
**Lineages:** [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: early Kali age (hagiographies); Scholarly account: c. 9th c. CE; (confidence low)
**Places:** Uṟaiyūr, Śrīraṅgam
**Historicity:** semi-legendary
**Works:** 
  - [Amalaṉātipirāṉ](../texts/amalanatipiran.md) — attribution: accepted

Bard of the pāṇar community, held untouchable, who sang to Raṅganātha from across the Kāvēri until the priest Lokasāraṅga was commanded to carry him on his shoulders into the sanctum; there he sang the ten verses of Amalaṉātipirāṉ, describing the Lord from feet to face, and entered the Lord.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Raised in a family of pāṇar bards at Uṟaiyūr, he would not set foot on the holy island and sang from the far bank. When Lokasāraṅga Muni, going for water for the Lord, found him in his way and struck him, the Lord bled and commanded the priest to carry the Āḻvār in on his shoulders. Seeing the Lord from feet to face, he sang that his eyes would see nothing else, and merged into the image.
