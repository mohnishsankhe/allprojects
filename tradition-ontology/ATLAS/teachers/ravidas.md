# Ravidās (Raidās)

**Alternate names:** Raidās, Rohidās, Rūidās, Guru Ravidās, Satguru Ravidās Mahārāj
**Lineages:** [Ravidāsī (Ravidassia) tradition](../lineages/ravidasi.md), [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md), [Dādū Panth](../lineages/dadu-panth.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: born 1377 (Ravidāsī reckoning; birth celebrated on Māgh Pūrṇimā) and very long-lived; Scholarly account: 15th–early 16th c.; (confidence low)
**Places:** Banaras (Sīr Govardhanpur), Chittor (tradition)
**Historicity:** historical

**Students:** [Jhālī Rānī of Chittor](jhali-rani.md), [Mīrābāī](mirabai.md)

  - [Bāṇī of Ravidās (Raidās kī Bāṇī)](../texts/ravidas-bani.md) — attribution: traditional

Leatherworker (Camār) saint of Banaras, venerated as Guru Ravidās: his songs speak of the city without sorrow (Begampura), of God who raises the lowly, of his own despised caste without shame, and of the unity of self and God. Forty of his hymns are in the Ādi Granth; the Ravidāsī community reveres him as its guru.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Rāmānanda's disciple in the Rāmānandī account; the brahmins who challenged his right to worship were defeated by signs (the image came to his lap; Gaṅgā appeared in his leather-soaking bowl); the queen Jhālī of Chittor and Mīrābāī became his disciples.

_Notes: Mīrābāī's discipleship rests on her songs naming Raidās as guru (attribution debated)._
