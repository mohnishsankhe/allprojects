# Poykai Āḻvār

`tch:poykai-alvar` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Saroyogin, Kāsāra-yogin, Poygai Alvar
**Lineages:** [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: born at the close of the Dvāpara age (hagiographies); Scholarly account: c. 6th–7th c. CE; (confidence low)
**Places:** Tiruveḵkā (Kāñcīpuram), Tirukkōvalūr
**Historicity:** semi-legendary
**Works:** 
  - [Mutal Tiruvantāti](../texts/mutal-tiruvantati.md) — attribution: accepted

First of the three 'first Āḻvārs' (Mutal Āḻvārkaḷ); author of the Mutal Tiruvantāti, which lights a lamp of the whole world for the discus-bearer and teaches that the Lord takes whatever form and name his devotees love.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Born, not of a womb, in a lotus in the temple tank (poykai) at Tiruveḵkā in Kāñcī. On a night of rain he sheltered in the narrow vestibule of a hermitage at Tirukkōvalūr — room for one to lie; Pūtattāḻvār came and they sat; Pēyāḻvār came and they stood; then a fourth pressed in among them unseen. Poykai lit a lamp with the earth as its bowl and the sea as its ghee and sang the first antāti; in the light the Lord was seen.

_Notes: Counted by tradition as a descent (aṃśa) of the Lord's conch Pāñcajanya (low confidence; lists vary)._
