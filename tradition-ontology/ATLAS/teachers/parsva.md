# Pārśva (Pārśvanātha)

`tch:parsva` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Pārśvanātha, Pāsa (Prakrit), Pārasnāth
**Lineages:** [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md), [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md), [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: nirvāṇa 250 years before Mahāvīra's (traditionally 777 BCE); Scholarly account: probably historical; c. 9th–7th c. BCE (estimates vary); (confidence low)
**Places:** Vārāṇasī, Sammeda Śikhara (Pārasnāth)
**Historicity:** historical
**Students:** [Keśin (Keśikumāra Śramaṇa)](kesi-kumara.md)

The twenty-third Tīrthaṅkara, emblem the serpent, son of King Aśvasena and Queen Vāmā of Vārāṇasī. As a prince he rescued a pair of serpents burning in the fire of the ascetic Kamaṭha; reborn as Dharaṇendra and Padmāvatī, they later shielded him with their hoods when Kamaṭha, reborn as the god Meghamālin, assailed his meditation with storm and flood. He taught the fourfold restraint (cāujjāma: non-violence, truth, not stealing, non-possession) and his followers wore garments; he lived a hundred years and attained nirvāṇa on Sammeda Śikhara (Pārasnāth hill). Mahāvīra's parents were followers of his teaching, and his followers (such as Keśin) joined Mahāvīra's community.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** He attained omniscience after 84 days of ascetic practice, following Meghamālin's attack from which Dharaṇendra shielded him.

_Notes: Historicity 'historical' reflects the common scholarly view that Pārśva was a real teacher; the dates are uncertain._
