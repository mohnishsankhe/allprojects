Tibetan layman, farmer and translator of Lhodrak who travelled to India (three times by the usual account) and studied with Nāropa, Maitrīpa, Kukkuripa and others; he brought the Hevajra, Cakrasaṃvara, Guhyasamāja and Mahāmāyā tantras, the six dharmas and Mahāmudrā to Tibet and is the Tibetan founder of the Kagyu.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** After years of study and gold-gathering journeys, Marpa received the complete transmissions from Nāropa; when Nāropa manifested Hevajra's maṇḍala Marpa prostrated to the deity rather than to his guru, and Nāropa foretold that his family line would scatter while his Dharma lineage would flourish through his disciples, like a river. He tested and trained Milarepa with harsh ordeals before giving him the instructions.

_Notes: His translations appear with Nāropa as co-translator in the colophons of Tōh 2304, 2330, 2337, 2338 (read locally). Drogmi's role as his first teacher is from the Life (moderate)._
