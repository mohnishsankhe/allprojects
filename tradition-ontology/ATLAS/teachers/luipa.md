No. 1 of the eighty-four siddhas (Tōh 2292 order). A prince (of Oḍḍiyāna in some accounts) who left his kingdom, and on a ḍākinī's rebuke for his remaining pride lived twelve years on the Ganges bank in Bengal eating the fish entrails thrown away by fishermen; attained mahāmudrā; converted King Dārika and his minister Ḍeṅgi; root guru of the Cakrasaṃvara lineage that bears his name and poet of the first Caryāgīti song.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** A prince (of Oḍḍiyāna in some accounts) who left his kingdom, and on a ḍākinī's rebuke for his remaining pride lived twelve years on the Ganges bank in Bengal eating the fish entrails thrown away by fishermen; attained mahāmudrā; converted King Dārika and his minister Ḍeṅgi; root guru of the Cakrasaṃvara lineage that bears his name and poet of the first Caryāgīti song. Realization song in Tōh 2292 (no. 1): As honey smeared on the nose of an untamed wild dog makes it bite in all directions, the guru's instruction given to the dull and unfit burns their own mind-stream; the fortunate who realize the unborn destroy concepts at the mere meeting with appearances, as a sword tied to the trunk of a steady, maddened elephant routs an army.
