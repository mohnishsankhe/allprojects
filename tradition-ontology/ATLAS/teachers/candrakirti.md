Author of the Prasannapadā and the Madhyamakāvatāra, who defended Buddhapālita's method against Bhāviveka, rejected autonomous inference and own-nature at every level, and became for Tibetan traditions the definitive interpreter of Nāgārjuna (the Prāsaṅgika).
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Tibetan histories tell that he studied under Kamalabuddhi, a disciple of Buddhapālita, became abbot of Nālandā and debated the lay master Candragomin there for years; to show that things lack own-nature he milked a painted cow and distributed the milk to the monks.

_Notes: Scholarly account: 7th c.; the tantric commentator of the same name (Pradīpoddyotana) is regarded by scholars as a later author._
