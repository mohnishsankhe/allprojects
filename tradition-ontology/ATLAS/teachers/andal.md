The woman among the Āḻvārs: author of the Tiruppāvai, the thirty verses of the Mārkaḻi vow sung across South India, and the Nācciyār Tirumoḻi of bridal longing for Kṛṣṇa. She wore the Lord's garland before offering it and vowed to marry only the Lord; the tradition says she was wedded to Raṅganātha at Śrīraṅgam.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Found as an infant under a tulasī plant in Periyāḻvār's garden; as a girl she wore the garlands meant for the Lord and looked at herself in a well to see if she was a fit bride. Her father, discovering this, did not offer that garland; the Lord told him in a dream that he wanted only garlands she had worn. Refusing any mortal bridegroom, she was taken in bridal procession to Śrīraṅgam at the Lord's command and entered the image of Raṅganātha.

_Notes: Held to be Bhūdevī (the Earth) descended; hence some lists omit her from the Āḻvārs as not a human soul. Some modern scholars have suggested Periyāḻvār composed in her name; the tradition rejects this._
