Akka Mahādēvi (Mahādēviyakka) of Uḍutaḍi, the woman saint who took Śiva as Cennamallikārjuna for her only husband, left a royal household, wandered clad only in her hair, was examined by Allama at Kalyāṇa and ended at Kadaḷi on Śrīśaila; her vacanas of bridal devotion and fearless renunciation are the tradition's great witness to women's spiritual equality.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Devoted from childhood to Cennamallikārjuna, she refused (or left) the king Kauśika, renounced even clothing, was tested and acknowledged by Allama and the śaraṇas at Kalyāṇa, and attained union in the Kadaḷi forest of Śrīśaila.

_Notes: Whether she was married to Kauśika differs between sources; recorded as the sources' variance._
