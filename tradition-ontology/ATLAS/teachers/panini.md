# Pāṇini

**Alternate names:** Dākṣīputra, Śālāturīya, Pāṇini Dākṣeya
**Lineages:** [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md), [Vedāṅga (the six limbs of the Veda)](../lineages/vedanga.md)

Author of the Aṣṭādhyāyī, the grammar that fixed classical Sanskrit and described the Vedic language; Patañjali calls him 'Dākṣīputra', son of Dākṣī. He names ten predecessors (Āpiśali, Kāśyapa, Gārgya, Gālava, Cākravarmaṇa, Bhāradvāja, Śākaṭāyana, Śākalya, Senaka, Sphoṭāyana).
**Realization — the tradition's account:** After austerities he received the fourteen phoneme-sūtras from Maheśvara (Śiva), who sounded them on his drum; from them he proclaimed the whole of grammar (Pāṇinīya Śikṣā 56–57; Nandikeśvara-kāśikā). A later story (Kathāsaritsāgara) makes him a dull pupil of Varṣa who won Śiva's grace by tapas.
