# Cuntaramūrtti (Cuntarar)

`tch:cuntarar` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Cuntarar, Nampi Ārūrar, Vaṉṟoṇṭar (the obstinate servant), Tampirāṉ Tōḻar (the Lord's friend), Sundaramūrti
**Lineages:** [The Nāyaṉmārs and the Tirumuṟai (Tamil Śaiva bhakti)](../lineages/nayanmar.md), [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: 8th–early 9th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Places:** Tirunāvalūr (birth), Tiruveṇṇeynallūr, Tiruvārūr, Tiruvoṟṟiyūr, Koṭuṅkōḷūr, Kailāsa
**Historicity:** historical
**Works:** 
  - [Tēvāram](../texts/tevaram.md) — attribution: accepted

Third Tēvāram poet (Tirumuṟai 7); exemplar of the friend's path (sakhā-mārga, yoga); author of the Tiruttoṇṭattokai, the list of devotees on which the cult of the sixty-three rests.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** An Ādiśaiva boy of Tirunāvalūr adopted by the chieftain Naracinka Muṉaiyaraiyar; on his wedding day an old brahmin produced a deed showing Cuntarar's grandfather had pledged his line as the brahmin's slaves; followed to Tiruveṇṇeynallūr, the old man vanished into the temple — Śiva himself — and Cuntarar sang 'Pittā' ('Madman!'). Śiva later acted as his messenger to his wife Paravai. With Cēramāṉ Perumāḷ he rode to Kailāsa on a white elephant sent by Śiva.
