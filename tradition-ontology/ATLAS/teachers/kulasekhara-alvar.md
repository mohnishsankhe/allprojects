# Kulaśekhara Āḻvār

`tch:kulasekhara-alvar` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Kulacēkarāḻvār, Kulacēkara Perumāḷ, Kulashekhara Alvar
**Lineages:** [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: early Kali age (hagiographies); Scholarly account: c. 9th c. CE (identification with the Cēra Kulaśekhara of Mahodayapuram is debated); (confidence low)
**Places:** Tiruvañcikkaḷam / Koḍuṅgallūr (Kerala), Śrīraṅgam, Tiruvēṅkaṭam
**Historicity:** historical
**Works:** 
  - [Perumāḷ Tirumoḻi](../texts/perumal-tirumoli.md) — attribution: accepted
  - [Mukundamālā](../texts/mukundamala.md) — attribution: traditional

Cēra king of Kerala who renounced his throne for the company of devotees at Śrīraṅgam; author of the Tamil Perumāḷ Tirumoḻi and (by tradition) the Sanskrit Mukundamālā. His hymns ask to be born even as a fish or a step at Tiruvēṅkaṭam and cling to the Lord 'as a child pushed away by its mother still cries for her'.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** So absorbed was the king in hearing the Rāmāyaṇa that when the reciter told of Rāma fighting Khara's fourteen thousand demons alone, he ordered his army to march to Rāma's aid. His ministers, resenting the devotees who thronged his court, accused them of stealing a jewel; he put his hand into a pot containing a cobra, declaring that the devotees could not steal, and was unharmed. He gave up his kingdom to live among devotees at Śrīraṅgam.

_Notes: Aṃśa of the Kaustubha gem in the tradition's list (moderate). The threshold of the Tirupati sanctum is called 'Kulaśekharaṉ paṭi' after Perumāḷ Tirumoḻi 4.9._
