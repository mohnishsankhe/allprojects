# Padampa Sangye (pha dam pa sangs rgyas)

`tch:padampa-sangye` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Alternate names:** Dampa Sangye, Pha Dampa ('Father Dampa'), Dampa Gyagar ('the Indian holy one')
**Lineages:** [Shije (zhi byed), 'Pacification [of suffering]'](../lineages/shije.md), [Chöd (gcod), 'Cutting'](../lineages/chod.md), [The Mahāsiddhas (the eighty-four great adepts)](../lineages/mahasiddha.md)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: d. c. 1105 or 1117 (birth unknown); (confidence low)
**Places:** South India, Nepal, Dingri Langkhor (Tsang), China (tradition's account)
**Historicity:** historical
**Students:** [Machig Labdrön (ma gcig lab sgron)](machig-labdron.md), [Dampa Kunga (dam pa kun dga')](dampa-kunga.md)
**Works:** 
  - [The Hundred Verses of Dingri: Padampa Sangye's parting testament (pha dam pa sangs rgyas kyi 'da' ka zhal chems ding ri brgya rtsa ma)](../texts/dingri-gyatsa.md) — attribution: traditional
  - [The Collected Teachings of the Early, Middle and Later Pacification (zhi byed snga phyi bar gsum gyi skor)](../texts/zhije-collection.md) — attribution: traditional

South Indian siddha who made several visits to Tibet (five or seven in different accounts), founded the Pacification (zhi byed) tradition, gave instructions to Machig Labdrön that became the 'father' strand of Chöd, and spent his last years at Dingri, where he gave his Hundred Verses to the people of Dingri.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** A siddha who studied with a great number of Indian masters (fifty-four in one account); some Tibetan accounts identify him with earlier Indian figures (low confidence; not recorded here).
