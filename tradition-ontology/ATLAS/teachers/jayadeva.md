# Jayadeva

`tch:jayadeva` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Jayadeva Gosvāmī, Kavirāja Jayadeva
**Lineages:** `lin:jagannatha`
**Dates:** Scholarly account: 12th c. CE; (confidence high)
**Places:** Kenduli / Kindubilva (claimed by Bengal and by Odisha as Kenduli Sasan), Purī, the court of Lakṣmaṇasena (Bengal account)
**Historicity:** historical
**Works:** 
  - [Gīta Govinda](../texts/gita-govinda.md) — attribution: accepted

Sanskrit poet of the Gīta Govinda, the song-poem of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa's love in separation and union, sung daily before Jagannātha at Purī and revered by the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas and in the temple music of Odisha, Bengal, Kerala and the south.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Son of Bhojadeva and Rāmādevī (the poem's closing verse). A wandering ascetic, he was given Padmāvatī in marriage by her father at Jagannātha's command. When he hesitated to write that Kṛṣṇa asked to place Rādhā's foot on his head, he went to bathe; Kṛṣṇa, in Jayadeva's form, came and wrote the line 'dehi pada-pallavam udāram' himself.

_Notes: Birthplace disputed between Bengal (Kenduli, Birbhum), Odisha (Kenduli Sasan near Purī) and Mithilā. Two hymns attributed to him are in the Sikh Ādi Granth (context only). Distinct from the later Jayadeva of the Candrāloka._
