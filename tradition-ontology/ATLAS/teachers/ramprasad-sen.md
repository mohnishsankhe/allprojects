Bengali Śākta poet-saint of Kumārhaṭṭa (Halisahar), whose songs to Kālī and Umā (the 'Rāmprasādī' songs) created Bengal's devotional Śākta tradition: the devotee as the Mother's child who claims, reproaches and surrenders to her, and who treats Kālī's name as the refuge at death. Patronised by Rājā Kṛṣṇacandra of Nadia, who gave him the title Kavirañjana; author of a Vidyāsundara and a Kālīkīrtana.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Working as a clerk in Calcutta he filled his account book with songs to the Mother; his employer, moved, sent him home with a stipend. The Mother came in the form of his little daughter to help him mend a fence; he died while immersing Kālī's image in the Ganges, singing.
