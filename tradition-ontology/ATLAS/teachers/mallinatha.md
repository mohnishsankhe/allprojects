# Mallinātha (Mallī)

**Alternate names:** Mallī (Śvetāmbara), Malli
**Lineages:** [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md), [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md), [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md), [Yāpanīya](../lineages/yapaniya.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: the nineteenth Jina of this era; (confidence low)
**Places:** Mithilā, Sammeda Śikhara
**Historicity:** legendary

The nineteenth Tīrthaṅkara, emblem the water-jar (kalaśa), born at Mithilā to King Kumbha and Queen Prabhāvatī, attained nirvāṇa at Sammeda Śikhara. In the Śvetāmbara account (Jñātādharmakathā 1.8) Mallī was a princess who, courted by six kings (her companions in a former life), showed them a hollow golden image of herself filled daily with food that rotted within, to teach the impurity of the body, and then renounced with them; a woman Tīrthaṅkara is counted among the ten 'wonders' (accheras) of this era. In the Digambara account Mallinātha was a man.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Śvetāmbara: Mallī attained omniscience on the very day of her renunciation; her female birth resulted from a slight deceit in her past life as King Mahābala, who secretly undertook more fasts than his companions.
