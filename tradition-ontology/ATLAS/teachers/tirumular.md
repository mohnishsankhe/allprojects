# Tirumūlar

`tch:tirumular` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Tirumūla Nāyaṉār, Cuntaraṉātar (his name before entering Mūlaṉ's body, per the Periya Purāṇam)
**Lineages:** [The Nāyaṉmārs and the Tirumuṟai (Tamil Śaiva bhakti)](../lineages/nayanmar.md), [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md), [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: lived 3,000 years in yoga; Scholarly account: disputed, c. 5th–10th c. (often 7th–8th); (confidence low)
**Places:** Kailāsa, Cāttaṉūr, Tiruvāvaṭuturai, Chidambaram — samādhi in the popular list
**Historicity:** semi-legendary
**Teachers:** [Nandi (Nantīcar)](nandi.md)
**Students:** [Kālāṅki nātar](kalangi-nathar.md), [Kañcamalaiyaṉ](kanjamalaiyar.md)
**Works:** 
  - [Tirumantiram](../texts/tirumantiram.md) — attribution: accepted
  - [Tirumūla nāyaṉār ñāṉam (the wisdom of Tirumūlar)](../texts/tirumular-nanam.md) — attribution: traditional

Author of the Tirumantiram, one of the sixty-three Nāyaṉmārs and counted among the eighteen Tamil Siddhars.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** A yogī-disciple of Nandi from Kailāsa, travelling south to meet Agastya, found the herdsman Mūlaṉ dead at Cāttaṉūr with his cows grieving; he entered Mūlaṉ's body by yogic power to console them, later found his own body gone by Śiva's will, and sat in yoga under a pipal tree at Tiruvāvaṭuturai, singing one verse a year for three thousand years.

_Notes: U22 contribution; U18 owns the full entry. TM 69 names his seven (or eight) disciples, among them Kālāṅki and Kañcamalaiyaṉ. Counted in the popular list of the eighteen Siddhars (see cpt:eighteen-siddhars); the samādhi place given follows that list and is recalled at low confidence._
