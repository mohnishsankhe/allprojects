Digambara logician who gave Jain epistemology its classical form (the conventionally direct and truly direct perception; memory, recognition, reasoning, inference and scripture as indirect knowledge) and wrote the Tattvārtharājavārttika. The tradition tells that he studied in a Buddhist monastery in disguise with his brother Niṣkalaṅka, who was killed, and later defeated the Buddhists in debate at the court of Himaśītala at Kāñcī, where the goddess Tārā spoke behind a curtain.
