Hōnen's disciple who, laicised and exiled to Echigo in 1207, called himself 'Gutoku' (foolish bald-head), neither monk nor layman, married Eshinni and taught in the Kantō for some twenty years before returning to Kyoto to write. His Kyōgyōshinshō presents teaching, practice, shinjin and realization as Amida's gift; shinjin is the true cause; the evil person is the true object of the vow. Founder (in the tradition's reckoning) of Jōdo Shinshū; he himself said he had not a single disciple.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** After twenty years as a hall monk on Mt Hiei he secluded himself in the Rokkakudō for a hundred days; on the ninety-fifth dawn Prince Shōtoku (Kannon) appeared in a dream, and he went to Hōnen (Eshinni's letters; Godenshō). He 'abandoned the miscellaneous practices and took refuge in the primal vow' in 1201 (Kyōgyōshinshō postscript).
