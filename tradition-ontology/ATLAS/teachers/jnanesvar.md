Marathi saint-philosopher (Jñānadev, Jñānobā), second of the four siblings, disciple of his brother Nivṛttināth in the Nāth line. At about fifteen he spoke the Jñāneśvarī at Nevāse (1290), then the Amṛtānubhava, the Cāṅgadeva Pāsaṣṭī, the Haripāṭh and abhaṅgas to Viṭṭhala; he founded the Vārkarī saint-tradition with Nāmdev, travelled with him, and at twenty-one entered living samādhi (sañjīvana samādhi) at Āḷandī.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Realized from boyhood by his guru's grace; made a buffalo recite the Veda at Paiṭhaṇ, rode a moving wall to meet Cāṅgadev, and entered living samādhi at Āḷandī where he is believed still present.
