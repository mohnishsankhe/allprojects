Fourth of the five Sakya founders, the most learned Tibetan of his age in the five sciences; trained under Drakpa Gyaltsen and the Kashmiri paṇḍita Śākyaśrībhadra; author of the Treasury of Valid Reasoning, the Discrimination of the Three Vows, Clarifying the Sage's Intent, the Gateway to Learning and the Treasury of Good Sayings; debated and defeated the Hindu scholar Harinanda (tradition's account); went to the Mongol prince Köden in 1244–47 and died at Liangzhou.
