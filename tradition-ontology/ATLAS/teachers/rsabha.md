# Ṛṣabha (Ṛṣabhanātha, Ādinātha)

`tch:rsabha` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Ṛṣabhadeva, Ādinātha ('first lord'), Usaha (Prakrit), Vṛṣabha
**Lineages:** [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md), [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md), [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md), [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: lived in the third age (suṣamā-duḥṣamā) of this descending half-cycle; his lifespan is given as 8,400,000 pūrvas; (confidence low)
**Places:** Ayodhyā (Vinītā), Aṣṭāpada (Kailāsa), Purimatāla (omniscience, Śvetāmbara account)
**Historicity:** legendary
**Students:** [Bharata (the first world-emperor)](bharata-cakravartin.md), [Bāhubali (Gommaṭeśvara)](bahubali.md), [Brāhmī](brahmi-jain.md), [Sundarī](sundari-jain.md), [Puṇḍarīka](pundarika-ganadhara.md)

The first Tīrthaṅkara of this era, emblem the bull, son of the last patriarch (kulakara) Nābhi and Marudevī, born at Ayodhyā (Vinītā). As first king he taught humanity the arts of livelihood — arms, writing, agriculture, learning, trade and crafts — writing (the Brāhmī script) to his daughter Brāhmī and arithmetic to Sundarī, then renounced, fasted for about a year until Prince Śreyāṃsa offered him sugarcane juice (remembered on Akṣaya Tṛtīyā), attained omniscience and taught the dharma; he reached nirvāṇa on Mount Aṣṭāpada. His sons were the world-emperor Bharata and Bāhubali. The Bhāgavata Purāṇa also knows him as a renouncer-avatāra.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** After renouncing kingship he undertook a long fast; he attained omniscience (kevala-jñāna) under a banyan tree and preached in the first samavasaraṇa of this era; his mother Marudevī, seeing his glory, became the first soul of this era to reach liberation (Śvetāmbara account).

_Notes: The Śvetāmbara account makes Marudevī the first liberated soul of this era; Digambara accounts differ on her._
