Logician-poet revered by both sects: author of the Prakrit Sanmatitarka on the standpoints and of hymns (dvātriṃśikās); the Nyāyāvatāra and Kalyāṇamandira are ascribed to him (disputed). He held that in the omniscient knowledge and perception are one. The tradition tells that he proposed rendering the Prakrit scriptures into Sanskrit, was sent into twelve years' exile for it, and during it made Pārśva's image appear at the Mahākāla temple of Ujjain, converting king Vikramāditya.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Pārśvanātha's image emerged from the liṅga at the Mahākāla temple when he recited his hymn (legend).
