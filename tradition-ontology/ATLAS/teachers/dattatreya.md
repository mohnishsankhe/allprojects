Son of Atri and Anasūyā, counted the sixth avatāra in the Bhāgavata (1.3.11: he taught ānvīkṣikī to Alarka, Prahlāda and others); Yadu and the Haihayas gained yogic powers from him (2.7.4). The tradition identifies him with the unnamed avadhūta who teaches King Yadu his twenty-four teachers (Bhāgavata 11.7-9), and ascribes to him the Avadhūta Gītā and the Jīvanmukta Gītā. Revered as the ādiguru of the Datta tradition and in the Nāth lineages.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Son of Atri and Anasūyā (a portion of Viṣṇu; in some accounts of the Trimūrti), the sixth avatāra who 'taught ānvīkṣikī to Alarka, Prahlāda and others' (BhP 1.3.11); an avadhūta who hid his yoga under outward impurity (MkP 16-19); in the Bhāgavata he tells of his twenty-four teachers (BhP 11.7-9, U06).
