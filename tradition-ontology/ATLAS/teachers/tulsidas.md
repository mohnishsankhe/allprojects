Poet-saint of Rāma (c. 1532–1623; tradition 1497–1623), a brahmin abandoned at birth and raised by sādhus, disciple of Narharidās. Turned to Rāma, by tradition, by his wife Ratnāvalī's rebuke, he lived at Ayodhyā, Citrakūṭ and Vārāṇasī (Assi ghāṭ), where Hanumān led him to the vision of Rāma. He wrote the Rāmcaritmānas (from 1574), the Vinaya Patrikā, Kavitāvalī, Gītāvalī, Dohāvalī and other works; the Hanumān Cālīsā is traditionally his. The Bhaktamāl calls him Vālmīki reborn for the Kali age.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Hanumān, met through a ghost's guidance, showed him Rāma at Citrakūṭ; Śiva approved the Mānas at Vārāṇasī.
