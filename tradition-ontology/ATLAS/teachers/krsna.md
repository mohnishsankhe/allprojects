# Kṛṣṇa (Vāsudeva)

`tch:krsna` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Vāsudeva, Keśava, Govinda, Mādhava, Janārdana, Hṛṣīkeśa, Madhusūdana, Acyuta, Yogeśvara, Pārthasārathi, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, Bhagavān, Bhagavān (speaker label 'śrī bhagavān uvāca'), Vārṣṇeya, Arisūdana, Śrī Bhagavān, Puruṣottama, Śrī Bhagavān (the Blessed Lord), Hari, Yādava, śrī bhagavān (the Blessed Lord), Prabhu
**Lineages:** [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md), [Early Bhāgavata devotion (the Nārāyaṇīya / Sātvata–Ekāntika dharma)](../lineages/bhagavata-early.md), [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md)

Son of Vasudeva and Devakī, prince of the Vṛṣṇis, Arjuna's charioteer and teacher of the Bhagavad Gītā and the Anugītā. In the Gītā he speaks as the Lord who descends age after age (4.6–8), is the source of all (10.8) and shows Arjuna his universal form (ch. 11); the Nārāyaṇīya names his birth at Mathurā among Nārāyaṇa's manifestations (12.326.82). In the Anuśāsana he learns the thousand names of Śiva from Upamanyu (13.14–17).
**Realization — the tradition's account:** The tradition regards him as the Lord (Bhagavān) himself born as Vasudeva's son; in the Gītā he speaks as unborn and imperishable, coming into being by his own māyā (4.6), and the Nārāyaṇīya presents him as a manifestation of Nārāyaṇa.

_Notes: Historicity recorded as 'legendary' in the scholarly sense only; the tradition holds him to be the Lord incarnate._
