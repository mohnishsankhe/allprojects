Master of Mathurā, fourth in the Sarvāstivāda succession after Mahākāśyapa, Ānanda and Śāṇakavāsin, and a hero of the Aśokāvadāna.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** He subdued Māra and made him display the Buddha's form; he taught Aśoka; a cave was filled with the tally-sticks of those he led to arhatship.
