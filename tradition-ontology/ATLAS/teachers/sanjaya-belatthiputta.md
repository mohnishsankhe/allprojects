# Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta

`tch:sanjaya-belatthiputta` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Alternate names:** Sañcaya Belaṭṭhaputta (Mahāsaṅgīti spelling), Sañjayin Vairaṭṭīputra (Skt.), Sañjaya the wanderer (Vinaya)
**Lineages:** [Ajñāna (the sceptics)](../lineages/ajnana.md), [The śramaṇa movement](../lineages/sramana.md)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 5th c. BCE (contemporary of the Buddha and Mahāvīra); (confidence low)
**Places:** Rājagaha
**Historicity:** historical
**Students:** [Sāriputta](sariputta.md), [Mahāmoggallāna](moggallana.md)

One of the six famous teachers, the type of the 'eel-wriggler'. DN 2 reports that asked whether there is another world, spontaneously born beings, fruit of deeds or a Tathāgata after death, he refused every alternative ('I do not say it is so, nor thus, nor otherwise, nor not so, nor not not so'); King Ajātasattu calls him the most foolish of the six. The Vinaya tells of the wanderer Sañjaya of Rājagaha, teacher of Sāriputta and Moggallāna, who left him with 250 wanderers for the Buddha, whereupon hot blood gushed from Sañjaya's mouth.

_Notes: The Vinaya (Mv 1.23) says only 'Sañjaya the wanderer'; the identification with Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta of DN 2 is the commentaries' and is questioned by some scholars. Do not confuse with tch:sanjaya (the Mahābhārata's narrator)._
