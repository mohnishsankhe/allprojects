# Patañjali

**Alternate names:** Śeṣa / Ananta (as whose incarnation he is regarded), Phaṇīndra, Phaṇipati (lord of serpents)
**Lineages:** [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md), [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: not dated by the tradition (incarnation of Śeṣa); identified with the grammarian; Scholarly account: c. 325–425 CE if author of the Pātañjala Yogaśāstra (Maas); the grammarian Patañjali is dated c. 2nd c. BCE; (confidence moderate)

**Teachers:** [Hiraṇyagarbha](hiranyagarbha.md), [Nandi (Nantīcar)](nandi.md)

**Works:** 
  - [Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali](../texts/yoga-sutra.md) — attribution: traditional
  - [Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra)](../texts/yoga-bhasya.md) — attribution: disputed

Author (re-teacher) of the Yoga Sūtra. The tradition regards him as Śeṣa incarnate and as the same Patañjali who wrote the grammatical Mahābhāṣya; modern scholarship mostly distinguishes the two and debates whether he also wrote the bhāṣya (the 'Pātañjala Yogaśāstra' hypothesis).
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Regarded as an incarnation of Śeṣa (Ananta), the serpent of Viṣṇu, who took a human form out of compassion to teach; the bhāṣya's maṅgala verse praises the 'lord of serpents, giver of yoga'. Later tradition credits him with removing the impurities of speech (grammar), mind (yoga) and body (medicine) — Bhoja's introductory verses praise Bhoja for doing the same 'like the lord of serpents'.
