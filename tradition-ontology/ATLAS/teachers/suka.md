Vyāsa's son, the paramahaṃsa narrator of the Bhāgavata to King Parīkṣit on the bank of the Gaṅgā; one of the twelve 'great ones' (mahājana) who know the dharma of devotion (BhP 6.3.20, 'vaiyāsaki').
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Born liberated and absorbed in Brahman, he was drawn by the qualities of Hari to learn the Bhāgavata from his father (BhP 1.7.9-11); an avadhūta wandering naked whom women did not notice as a man (BhP 1.4.5); he recited the Bhāgavata to Parīkṣit in seven days and ended with the non-dual instruction 'I am Brahman, the supreme abode' (BhP 12.5.11).
