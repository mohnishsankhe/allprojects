# Makkhali Gosāla

`tch:makkhali-gosala` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Alternate names:** Maṅkhaliputta Gosāla (Prakrit), Maskarin Gośālīputra (Skt.), Maṟkali (Tamil), 末伽梨瞿舍利 (Chinese, Dīrgha-āgama), the bamboo-staffed ascetic (maskarin)
**Lineages:** [Ājīvika](../lineages/ajivika.md), [The śramaṇa movement](../lineages/sramana.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: (Jain) died at Sāvatthī sixteen years before Mahāvīra; Scholarly account: c. 5th c. BCE; (confidence low)
**Places:** Saravaṇa (birth, Jain account), Sāvatthī (Hālāhalā's pottery; death), Paṇiyabhūmi
**Historicity:** historical
**Teachers:** [Nanda Vaccha](nanda-vaccha.md), [Kisa Saṅkicca](kisa-sankicca.md)
**Students:** [Upaka the Ājīvaka](upaka-ajivaka.md), [Hālāhalā](halahala.md), [Saddālaputta](saddalaputta.md), [Ayampula](ayampula.md)

Leader of the Ājīvikas. DN 2 reports his doctrine of purification through transmigration: there is no cause for the defilement or purification of beings, no human effort; all beings are shaped by destiny, circumstance and nature (niyati-saṅgati-bhāva) and, fool and wise alike, make an end of suffering after 8,400,000 great aeons, like a ball of string unwinding. AN 3.137 calls his doctrine the worst of all. The Jain Bhagavatī Sūtra (15) tells his birth in a cowshed, six years as Mahāvīra's companion, his claim to be a Jina, the confrontation at Sāvatthī, and his death in delirium followed by repentance; the Jain Isibhāsiyāiṃ (11) nevertheless records a saying of 'the arhat seer Maṅkhaliputta'.
**Realization — the tradition's account (reported by an opponent):** (Ājīvika claim, as reported by the Jains) Gosāla declared himself a Jina and the last tīrthaṅkara of this age; (Jain account) he confessed on his deathbed that he was Mahāvīra's disciple and not a Jina, and will attain liberation only after many births.

_Notes: The name: Jain sources derive 'Gosāla' from his birth in a cowshed (gośālā) and 'Maṅkhaliputta' from his father Maṅkhali, a maṅkha (picture-showman); Buddhaghosa derives 'Makkhali' from 'don't stumble' (mā khali) — both are the traditions' etymologies._
