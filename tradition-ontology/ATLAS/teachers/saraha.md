No. 6 of the eighty-four siddhas (Tōh 2292 order). A brahmin of eastern India who kept Buddhist practice secretly and drank liquor; expelled from caste, he took as his consort a low-caste arrowsmith woman who taught him through her work; he sang dohās to the king, the queen and the people who came to condemn him, and they became his disciples; in another story he meditated twelve years and then asked for the radish curry his consort had made before he entered samādhi.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** A brahmin of eastern India who kept Buddhist practice secretly and drank liquor; expelled from caste, he took as his consort a low-caste arrowsmith woman who taught him through her work; he sang dohās to the king, the queen and the people who came to condemn him, and they became his disciples; in another story he meditated twelve years and then asked for the radish curry his consort had made before he entered samādhi. Realization song in Tōh 2292 (no. 6): Kye ho, friends! That innate is not found elsewhere; it is found from the mouth of the true guru. If the essence of the ultimate is realized, the mind knows no death and the body no destruction.
