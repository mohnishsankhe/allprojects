# Pēyāḻvār

**Alternate names:** Pēy, Mahadyogin, Bhrāntayogin, Peyalvar
**Lineages:** [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: born at the close of the Dvāpara age (hagiographies); Scholarly account: c. 6th–7th c. CE; (confidence low)
**Places:** Mylapore (Tirumayilai), Tirukkōvalūr
**Historicity:** semi-legendary
**Students:** [Tirumaḻicai Āḻvār](tirumalicai-alvar.md)
**Works:** 
  - [Mūṉṟām Tiruvantāti](../texts/munram-tiruvantati.md) — attribution: accepted

Third of the 'first Āḻvārs'; author of the Mūṉṟām Tiruvantāti, which opens with the vision seen in the other two's lamps: 'I have seen Śrī, I have seen the golden form ...'. Called 'the mad one' for his ecstatic devotion; by tradition he turned Tirumaḻicai to Viṣṇu.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Born in a red lily in a well at Mylapore; at Tirukkōvalūr, by the light the others had kindled, he saw the Lord with Śrī, the golden form, the discus and the conch, and sang the third antāti.
