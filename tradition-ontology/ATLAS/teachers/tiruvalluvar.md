# Tiruvaḷḷuvar

**Alternate names:** Vaḷḷuvar, Poyyāmoḻip Pulavar, Nāyaṉār, Teyvap Pulavar, Mutaṟpāvalar
**Lineages:** [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md), [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md), [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md), [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md), [Siddha medicine (cittā maruttuvam)](../lineages/siddha-medicine.md)

**Places:** Mayilāppūr (tradition), Madurai (the academy, tradition)
**Historicity:** semi-legendary
**Works:** 
  - [Tirukkuṟaḷ](../texts/tirukkural.md) — attribution: accepted
  - [Tiruvaḷḷuvar ñāṉam (the wisdom of Tiruvaḷḷuvar)](../texts/tiruvalluvar-nanam.md) — attribution: doubtful

Author of the Tirukkuṟaḷ. His affiliation is contested (Jain, Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava, Buddhist, non-sectarian); the Jain tradition identifies him with Elācārya/Kundakunda; Tamil Śaivas honour him as Tiruvaḷḷuva Nāyaṉār.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Later legends make him a weaver of Mayilāppūr with a devoted wife Vācuki, and a son of the brahmin Pakavaṉ and the Pulaiya woman Āti (from the late Kapilar Akaval — low confidence); his Kuṟaḷ was the only work the academy's plank would bear.
