Founder of Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda). Born at Pājaka near Uḍupi to Madhyageha Bhaṭṭa, he took renunciation as a boy from Acyutaprekṣa, receiving the name Pūrṇaprajña (Pūrṇabodha), and was installed as master of Vedānta with the name Ānandatīrtha; he signs his works 'Madhva'. He toured India debating, twice went to Badarī, installed the image of Kṛṣṇa at Uḍupi and founded the eight Uḍupi maṭhas, and wrote thirty-seven works (the Sarvamūla) on the Brahmasūtras, the Gītā, ten Upaniṣads, the Ṛgveda, the epics and Purāṇas, ten polemical and doctrinal treatises, hymns and ritual manuals. He taught that Viṣṇu alone is independent, that the five differences are real and eternal, that souls are graded by nature, and that liberation comes by Viṣṇu's grace through knowledge and devotion.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Madhva is the third avatāra of Vāyu (Mukhyaprāṇa), after Hanumān and Bhīma — he says so himself at the end of the Mahābhārata-tātparya-nirṇaya (32.162, 32.166) and of the Anuvyākhyāna (4.4.15), reading the Balitthā hymn (Ṛgveda 1.141) as naming Vāyu's three forms; Bhīma was reborn as a brāhmaṇa when 4,300 years of Kali had passed to proclaim the truth about Hari hidden by the daityas (MBTN 32.120). The Sumadhvavijaya tells of boyhood feats, renunciation and naming (4.33, 5.1–2), journeys to Badarī where he met Vyāsa and received his teaching, and closes with the gods hailing him as Hanumān, Bhīma and Madhva and showering him with flowers (16.57–58). The tradition holds that he passed from human sight at Uḍupi while expounding the Aitareya Upaniṣad and dwells with Vyāsa at Badarī; the day is kept as Madhva-navamī.
