# Appar (Tirunāvukkaracar)

`tch:appar` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Tirunāvukkaracu ('King of Speech'), Marunīkkiyār (birth name), Tarumacēṉar (Jain name), Vākīśa
**Lineages:** [The Nāyaṉmārs and the Tirumuṟai (Tamil Śaiva bhakti)](../lineages/nayanmar.md), [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md)
**Dates:** Tradition's account: lived to 81 (tradition); Scholarly account: 7th c.; contemporary of the Pallava Mahendravarman I (r. c. 600–630); (confidence moderate)
**Places:** Tiruvāmūr (birth), Pāṭaliputtiram (Jain monastery), Tiruvatikai, Tiruppukalūr (death)
**Historicity:** historical
**Works:** 
  - [Tēvāram](../texts/tevaram.md) — attribution: accepted

One of the three Tēvāram poets (Tirumuṟai 4–6), a former Jain monk (Tarumacēṉar) who returned to Śaivism; exemplar of the servant's path (dāsa-mārga, caryā); famed for temple service (clearing the temple grounds) and hymns of defiance and surrender.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Raised a Śaiva and cared for by his sister Tilakavati, he became a Jain monk; struck by an incurable colic he returned to Tilakavati at Tiruvatikai, received the sacred ash, and sang his first hymn, and the pain left him. The Jain-influenced Pallava king had him shut in a lime-kiln, fed poison, trampled by an elephant and thrown into the sea tied to a stone; he survived each by singing Śiva's names, floated ashore, and the king turned Śaiva. He died at Tiruppukalūr merging with Śiva.
