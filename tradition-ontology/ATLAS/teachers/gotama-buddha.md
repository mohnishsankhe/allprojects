Siddhattha Gotama of the Sakyan clan, the Buddha of this age: after going forth and six years of striving he awakened under the Bodhi tree at Uruvelā, taught the middle way and the four noble truths at Isipatana, founded the Saṅgha of monks and nuns, taught for forty-five years in the middle Ganges region and attained final nibbāna at Kusinārā at eighty. He calls himself the Tathāgata; the texts present him as the rediscoverer of an ancient path (SN 12.65) and the seventh Buddha of ninety-one aeons (DN 14).
**Realization — the tradition's account:** After six years of striving and the rejection of self-mortification, he recalled the first jhāna, took food, entered the four jhānas and in the three watches of the night attained recollection of past lives, the divine eye and the destruction of the effluents with knowledge of the four truths; 'unshakeable is my liberation, this is my last birth' (MN 26, MN 36, MN 4; SN 56.11; Vin Mv 1.1).
