# Āryadeva

`tch:aryadeva` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate names:** Deva, Kāṇadeva ('one-eyed Deva'), 'phags pa lha (Tibetan), Tipo 提婆 (Chinese), Kāṇadeva / Āryadeva (Jiana Tipo 迦那提婆)
**Lineages:** [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md), [Chan (Chinese Meditation school)](../lineages/chan.md)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 3rd c. CE; (confidence moderate)

**Works:**

Nāgārjuna's principal disciple, author of the Four Hundred Verses (Catuḥśataka), which joins the bodhisattva's practice to the refutation of permanent entities, the self and extreme views.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** The Chinese Life tells that he offered his own eye to a great statue of Maheśvara, defeated the teachers of other schools in debate, and was killed by a disciple of a defeated opponent, whom he forgave while dying, teaching that there is no one who kills and no one who is killed. Tibetan accounts make him the adopted son of a Sinhalese king, born from a lotus, who defeated the Śaiva debater Mātṛceṭa at Nālandā and converted him.

_Notes: The tantric works under this name (e.g. Caryāmelāpakapradīpa) are by a later Āryadeva (scholarly account)._
