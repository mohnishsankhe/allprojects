# Bogar (Pōkar)

`tch:bogar` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Alternate names:** Pōkar, Bhoganāthar, Pōka muṉi
**Lineages:** [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md), [Siddha medicine (cittā maruttuvam)](../lineages/siddha-medicine.md)
**Dates:** Scholarly account: undetermined; Siddhar works are usually placed after c. 1400; (confidence low)
**Places:** Palani — samādhi within the hill temple (tradition), China (Cīṉam), in his own account
**Historicity:** legendary
**Teachers:** [Kālāṅki nātar](kalangi-nathar.md)
**Students:** [Pulippāṇi](pulippani.md), [Koṅkaṇar](konganar.md), [Karuvūrār](karuvurar.md)
**Works:** 
  - [Pōkar ēḻāyiram (Bogar's Seven Thousand; Captakāṇṭam)](../texts/bogar-7000.md) — attribution: traditional

Siddhar of Palani, alchemist and physician: the tradition says he made the image of Murukaṉ as Daṇḍāyudhapāṇi at Palani from nine poisonous minerals (navapāṣāṇam), travelled to China (Cīṉam) and flew through the air, and recorded medicine, alchemy and kāya kaṟpam in the 'Seven Thousand' (Pōkar ēḻāyiram). Disciple of Kālāṅki Nātar; teacher of Pulippāṇi, Koṅkaṇar and Karuvūrār.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Bogar is said to have consecrated the navapāṣāṇa image at Palani and to have entered samādhi beneath the shrine, where he abides; his own verses describe journeys to China and flights by the power of kaḷikai pills (recorded as his claim).

_Notes: The Chinese connection is the text's and tradition's claim; modern theories identifying him with a Chinese Taoist are outside the tradition and not recorded. Counted in the popular list of the eighteen Siddhars (see cpt:eighteen-siddhars); the samādhi place given follows that list and is recalled at low confidence._
