# Mīnapa

**Alternate names:** Mīnanātha, Vajrapāda, mi na pa (Tibetan)

No. 8 of the eighty-four siddhas (Tōh 2292 order). A fisherman swallowed by a great fish; in its belly on the ocean floor he overheard the god Śiva (Maheśvara) teaching Umā the secret yoga and practised it for twelve years before being freed; his feet sank into rock when he danced. Widely identified with Matsyendranātha of the Nāth tradition (identification disputed).
**Realization — the tradition's account:** A fisherman swallowed by a great fish; in its belly on the ocean floor he overheard the god Śiva (Maheśvara) teaching Umā the secret yoga and practised it for twelve years before being freed; his feet sank into rock when he danced. Widely identified with Matsyendranātha of the Nāth tradition (identification disputed). Realization song in Tōh 2292 (no. 8): The fisherman holding his hook was carried by karma into the ocean; relying on the body of the great fish, he practised the great god's instruction, and his qualities became famed as more than earth and stone could bear.

_Notes: Life from Abhayadatta's Caturaśītisiddhapravṛtti recalled from memory (not read locally); the song is read locally in Tōh 2292. The last line ('ma theg') is read as the weight of his realization sinking into rock (cf. the name Vajrapāda); low certainty on this reading._
