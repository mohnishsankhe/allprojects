Chieftain of Tiruvāli-Tirunakari in the Cōḻa country, from the Kaḷḷar community, who turned from robbery to become the most prolific Āḻvār: author of the Periya Tirumoḻi, Tirukkuṟuntāṇṭakam, Tiruneṭuntāṇṭakam, Tiruveḻukkūṟṟirukkai and the two Maṭals (held to be the six aṅgas of the Tamil Veda). He sang some eighty sacred sites, the saving name Nārāyaṇa, and love-longing as Parakāla-nāyakī.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** A commander under the Cōḻa, he loved Kumudavalli, who would marry him only if he took the Vaiṣṇava marks and fed a thousand and eight devotees daily. To keep the vow he turned to robbing travellers, and waylaid a bridal party that was the Lord and Lakṣmī in disguise. Unable to pull the ring from the bridegroom's toe, he demanded the spell that held it; the Lord whispered into his ear the eight-syllable mantra, and he burst into the hymn of the name. He spent his wealth on the walls of Śrīraṅgam — by one story melting down a golden image taken from a Buddhist shrine at Nāgapaṭṭinam — and is credited with beginning the festival of reciting the Tiruvāymoḻi at Śrīraṅgam.
