Bengali prince-turned-monk, scholar of Vikramaśīla, who received bodhicitta and mind-training above all from Serlingpa (Dharmakīrti of Suvarṇadvīpa, with whom the tradition says he spent twelve years) and also from Dharmarakṣita and Maitrīyogin; invited to Tibet by the Ngari kings Yeshe Ö and Jangchub Ö, he arrived in 1042, wrote the Lamp for the Path at Tholing, met Dromtönpa and died at Nyethang in 1054; founder of the Kadam.
**Realization — the tradition's account:** Tārā foretold that going to Tibet would shorten his life but greatly benefit beings; he went nonetheless. The Kadam regard him as holding the complete lineages of the profound view and the vast conduct.
