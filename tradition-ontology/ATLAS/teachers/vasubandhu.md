Author of the Abhidharmakośa and, after his conversion by Asaṅga, of the Twenty and Thirty Verses, the Three Natures and commentaries on the Maitreya texts; honoured in East Asia as the second patriarch of Yogācāra and in Tibet as one of the 'six ornaments'.
**Realization — the tradition's account (Paramārtha's Life of Vasubandhu, T2049; Tibetan histories):** A master of Sarvāstivāda and Sautrāntika who wrote the Abhidharmakośa and scorned the Mahāyāna, he was brought to it when Asaṅga, feigning illness, had him hear the Akṣayamatinirdeśa and Daśabhūmika; repenting, he wished to cut out his tongue, but Asaṅga told him to use that tongue to teach the Mahāyāna instead.
