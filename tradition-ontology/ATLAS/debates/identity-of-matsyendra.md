# Who is Matsyendranātha — a Śaiva siddha, a form of the bodhisattva Karuṇāmaya, or the Buddhist siddha Lūipa?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md)
Matsyendra is the disciple of Ādinātha and guru of Gorakṣa, bringer of the Yoginīkaula; Bhairava himself as fisherman (Macchaghna) recovered the Kaula scripture.
- KJN colophons and ch. 16.
- HYP 1.4–5; Jñāneśvarī 18.
**Texts:** 
  - [Kaulajñānanirṇaya](../texts/kaulajnananirnaya.md) — ref: 16.27-36
  - [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) — ref: 1.4-9
### `lin:newar-vajrayana`
In the Kathmandu valley the deity brought from Kāmarūpa to end Gorakṣa's drought is Karuṇāmaya (Avalokiteśvara), called Buṅga-dyaḥ and Macchindranāth.
- Newar chronicles and the Rāto Macchindranāth festival (not extracted).
### [The Mahāsiddhas (the eighty-four great adepts)](../lineages/mahasiddha.md)
Tibetan tradition relates Matsyendra to the siddha Lūipa ('fish-gut eater') or Mīnapa among the eighty-four.
- Caturaśītisiddhapravṛtti (Abhayadatta).
**Texts:** 
  - [Caturaśītisiddhapravṛtti (the Lives of the Eighty-four Siddhas)](../texts/caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti.md) — ref: Lūipa; Mīnapa (refs not recalled)

**Explanation:** One revered figure is described from the Śaiva, Newar Buddhist and Tibetan standpoints, each within its own lineage; the traditions agree on the fish-legend and the siddha's power.
The shared elements (fish, Kāmarūpa, siddha of the Kali age) are recorded in each tradition's own terms; the claim that it is one historical person is not made here.
**The traditions' own objections:** Each tradition claims him as its own and would reject being read as a form of the other's deity or saint.
