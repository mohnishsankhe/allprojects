# Should consecrated images of the Jinas be worshipped?

`dsp:image-worship-and-inner-worship` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Śvetāmbara Mūrtipūjaka](../lineages/murtipujaka.md)
Yes: the image is the Jina 'placed' (sthāpanā) and a support for recalling his qualities; material worship by householders leads to inner worship, and the good of the act outweighs the slight harm.
- The canon shows gods (Sūryābha) and Draupadī worshipping Jina images
- The Anuyogadvāra recognizes representation (sthāpanā) as a way the Jina is present to the worshipper
- Material worship is a householder's means; mendicants worship inwardly
**Texts:** 
  - [Rājapraśnīya](../texts/rajaprasniya.md) — ref: suryabha
  - [Jñātādharmakathā](../texts/jnatadharmakatha.md) — ref: 1.16
  - [Anuyogadvāra Sūtra](../texts/anuyogadvara-sutra.md) — ref: niksepa
  - [Lalitavistarā of Haribhadra](../texts/lalitavistara-haribhadra.md) — 
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
Yes (Bīsapantha and Terāpantha), though the Terāpantha offers only non-living substances and rejects attendant deities.
- The Digambara temple tradition and the pañcakalyāṇaka consecrations
**Texts:** 
  - [Mokṣamārga Prakāśaka](../texts/moksamarga-prakasaka.md) — ref: 7
### [Sthānakavāsī](../lineages/sthanakavasi.md)
No: the thirty-two Āgamas contain no injunction to worship images; worship with water, flowers and lamps harms one-sensed beings; the canon's 'caitya' does not mean image-shrine in the passages cited; devotion is to the Jina's qualities and to living mendicants.
- Non-harm to the six classes of beings (Ācārāṅga 1.1)
- Absence of any command to worship images in the accepted canon
- The Sūryābha and Draupadī passages describe gods' or pre-Jain conduct, not an injunction
**Texts:** 
  - [Ācārāṅga Sūtra](../texts/acaranga-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.2-7
  - [Daśavaikālika Sūtra](../texts/dasavaikalika-sutra.md) — ref: 4
### [Śvetāmbara Terāpanth](../lineages/terapanthi.md)
No, with the Sthānakavāsīs.
- As above; Bhikṣu's order is aniconic
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhikṣu Granth Ratnākar (collected works of Ācārya Bhikṣu)](../texts/bhiksu-granth-ratnakar.md) — 
### [Tāraṇapantha (Samaiyā)](../lineages/taranapantha.md)
No: the shrines hold scriptures, not images.
- Tāraṇa Svāmī's teaching on inner worship
**Texts:** 
  - [The fourteen works of Tāraṇa Svāmī](../texts/taranasvami-granthas.md) — 
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Yogins see Śiva in the Self, not in images; images are imagined for the ignorant; for the renouncer image-worship leads to rebirth.
- Darśana 4.59
- Maitreya 2.26
**Texts:** 
  - [Darśana Upaniṣad](../texts/darsana-upanisad.md) — ref: 4.48-59
  - [Maitreya Upaniṣad](../texts/maitreya-upanisad.md) — ref: 2.26
### [Rāmānandī sampradāya](../lineages/ramanandi.md)
Forms are given to the formless for the worshippers' sake; the deity is worshipped in yantra and mantra, and without the yantra is not pleased.
- Rāmapūrvatāpanī 1.7; 4–5
**Texts:** 
  - [Rāmatāpanī Upaniṣad](../texts/ramatapani-upanisad.md) — ref: pūrva 1.7

**Historical debate — Loṅkā Śāh's critique** (15th c. (c. 1451–1476)): Sthānakavāsī accounts: in copying the Āgamas he found no warrant for image worship; Mūrtipūjaka accounts present him as an opponent of the canon's commentaries. Origin of the aniconic Śvetāmbara orders.

**Historical debate — Vijayānandasūri's return to image worship** (1870s): A Sthānakavāsī monk who, studying the canon and commentaries, became a Mūrtipūjaka (recent). Revival of Mūrtipūjaka mendicancy in Punjab.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path, P4-stage
**Explanation:** All sides agree that liberation comes from the inner state (bhāva) and that the Jina grants nothing; they differ over whether material worship, which involves some harm, is a legitimate means for householders.
Mūrtipūjaka teachers themselves confine material worship to laypeople (P4) and treat it as a devotional-ritual path (P3) leading to inner worship, which the aniconic orders also practise.
**The traditions' own objections:** Sthānakavāsīs and Terāpanthīs deny that any worship involving harm can be dharma and deny its canonical basis; Mūrtipūjakas regard aniconism as a rejection of canonical practice.
