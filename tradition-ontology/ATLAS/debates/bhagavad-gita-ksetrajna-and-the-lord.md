# When the Lord says 'know me also as the knower of the field (kṣetrajña) in all fields' (BhG 13.3 in this edition; 13.2 in the 700-verse numbering) and names the highest puruṣa in the body the great lord and the supreme self (13.23; 13.22 in the 700-verse numbering), is the knower of the field in each body the Lord himself, or is the Lord the self of the knowers, distinct from them?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The knower of the field in all bodies is the Lord himself, free of all adjuncts; its appearance as a transmigrating agent and experiencer belongs to the field and is due to ignorance (avidyā); knowing this identity is the knowledge the verse speaks of.
- 'know me also (api)': the Lord is identified with the kṣetrajña named in 13.2 (recalled)
- 13.23 names the highest puruṣa in this body the supreme self (paramātman) (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Śaṅkara](../texts/gita-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: on 13.2 (700-verse numbering)
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
The Lord is the self (ātman) of all the knowers of the field, who with their bodies are his body; 'kṣetrajña' in 'know me also as the kṣetrajña' denotes the Lord as having them for his body, so they are one with him as body with self while remaining distinct individual selves.
- co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya) of words denoting the body with the self that has it (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Rāmānuja](../texts/gita-bhasya-ramanuja.md) — ref: on 13.2 (700-verse numbering)
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
The Lord is the independent knower in all fields, eternally distinct from the dependent individual knowers whom he controls.
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya (Madhva)](../texts/gita-bhasya-madhva.md) — ref: on 13.2 (700-verse numbering; to verify)
