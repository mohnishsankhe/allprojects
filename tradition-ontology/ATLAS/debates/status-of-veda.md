# What is the status of the Veda: authorless and eternal (apauruṣeya), breathed out or composed by the omniscient Lord, or without authority?

`dsp:status-of-veda` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G11

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
The Veda is authorless (apauruṣeya) and eternal: the relation of word and meaning is original, not made by any person; names in the Veda are of expounders, not authors, or mere similarity of sound; since faults in testimony arise from dependence on a speaker, a speakerless Veda is free of them and its cognitions are never overturned; every study of the Veda is preceded by the teacher's study; no omniscient author can be established.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mīmāṃsā Sūtra](../texts/mimamsa-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.5; 1.1.27-31
  - [Śābarabhāṣya](../texts/sabara-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.2; 1.1.5
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: codanā; sambandhākṣepaparihāra; vākya
  - [Prakaraṇapañcikā](../texts/prakaranapancika.md) — ref: ch. 7
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The Veda is eternal — no independent author of it is remembered, and the world with its fixed forms arises from the Vedic word; yet Brahman is its source, from which it is breathed out effortlessly, and it is the only means of knowing Brahman. For the knower, in the highest state, 'the Vedas are no Vedas'; the Veda is the lower knowledge, the knowledge of the imperishable the higher.
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 1.1.3; 1.3.28-29
  - [Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad](../texts/brhadaranyaka-upanisad.md) — ref: 4.3.22
  - [Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad](../texts/mundaka-upanisad.md) — ref: 1.1.4-5
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
The Veda is authorless; no author of it is known, and to assume one would be needless; the Ṛg and other Vedas, the Bhārata and the Pañcarātra are the scriptures that teach Viṣṇu.
**Texts:** 
  - [Viṣṇutattvavinirṇaya](../texts/visnutattvavinirnaya.md) — ref: ch. 1
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
The Veda is valid because it is the word of a reliable, omniscient speaker (the Lord) — like mantras and Āyurveda, whose validity is seen; the charges of falsehood, self-inconsistency and repetition fail. Nyāya is foremost among sciences because it protects the Veda's authority.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 2.1.57-69
  - [Tattvacintāmaṇi](../texts/tattvacintamani.md) — ref: śabda-khaṇḍa
  - [Nyāyamañjarī](../texts/nyayamanjari.md) — ref: āhnika 1
### [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md)
The authority of the sacred tradition follows from its being the statement of 'that' (the Lord / the seer); the composition of sentences in the Veda is preceded by understanding.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vaiśeṣika Sūtra](../texts/vaisesika-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.3; 6.1.1-2
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
The Vedas are not eternal, since scripture speaks of their being produced; nor are they of personal authorship, since there is no author; their validity is intrinsic.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Sūtra](../texts/samkhya-sutra.md) — ref: 5.45-51
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
The Gītā: the Vedas have the three guṇas as their domain — be free of the three; for the knowing brāhmaṇa there is as much use in all the Vedas as in a well when there is flood all around; yet the one who casts aside scripture's injunctions attains neither perfection nor happiness; 'I alone am to be known by all the Vedas'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) — ref: 2.42-46; 15.15; 16.23-24
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
An authorless Veda cannot be shown true in meaning: truth of speech depends on the qualities of a speaker, and without a speaker there is no guarantee; the Mīmāṃsā denial of any being who sees supersensible things is refuted; the Veda's claims about the supersensible have no means of knowledge behind them.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: 3.225-227 (numbering as recorded)
  - [Tattvasaṅgraha of Śāntarakṣita](../texts/tattvasangraha.md) — ref: Atīndriyārthadarśiparīkṣā
  - [Madhyamakahṛdayakārikā](../texts/madhyamakahrdaya.md) — ref: ch. 9
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
The brāhmaṇas versed in the three Vedas teach a path to union with Brahmā though neither they nor their teachers to the seventh generation have seen Brahmā — like a line of blind men.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tevijja Sutta](../texts/tevijja-sutta.md) — ref: DN 13 §15
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The Vedas, the epics and other non-Jain scriptures are 'false scripture' (mithyāśruta) for one of wrong view, though they can serve right knowledge for one of right view; the Jain twelve-limbed canon 'never was not, never is not, never will not be'. Not by the syllable Oṃ is one a brāhmaṇa, but by conduct.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nandī Sūtra](../texts/nandi-sutra.md) — ref: on mithyāśruta; dvādaśāṅga
  - [Uttarādhyayana Sūtra](../texts/uttaradhyayana-sutra.md) — ref: ch. 25
### [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md)
The Veda and the Āgama are both true, both the Lord's scripture — one general, the other special (Tirumantiram); Śrīkaṇṭha sees no difference between the Veda and the Śivāgama; the Kaula texts make the Vedic auxiliaries the limbs of the Kula (see dsp:agama-veda-authority).
**Texts:** 
  - [Tirumantiram](../texts/tirumantiram.md) — ref: 2397
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śrīkaṇṭha (Śrīkaṇṭhabhāṣya)](../texts/srikantha-bhasya.md) — ref: 2.2.38
  - [Kulārṇava Tantra](../texts/kularnava-tantra.md) — ref: 2.85
### [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md)
Do not say the Vedas and the Books are false; false is the one who does not reflect (Kabīr); I speak of what I have seen, you of what is written on paper; the Bāuls follow the present (bartamān), not the inferred; Basava will put the Veda to the touchstone; Tukārām: we alone know the Veda's meaning, others carry its burden.
**Texts:** 
  - [The oral and popular corpus of Kabīr (sākhīs, dohās, padas and bhajans)](../texts/kabir-oral-corpus.md) — ref: as recorded
  - [Bāul gān (the corpus of Bāul songs of other masters)](../texts/baul-gan.md) — ref: as recorded
  - [Vacanas of Basavaṇṇa (Basavaṇṇanavara vacanagaḷu)](../texts/basavanna-vacanas.md) — ref: as recorded
  - [Tukārām Gāthā](../texts/tukaram-gatha.md) — ref: as recorded
### [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](../lineages/carvaka.md)
(As reported) Rejected: the Veda is tainted by falsehood, inconsistency and repetition; its makers were buffoons, knaves and demons; its rites are a livelihood of priests; the Bārhaspatyas call it a mere cover.
- the slain beast and the sacrificer's father
- oil on a quenched lamp
**Texts:** 
  - [Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha](../texts/sarvadarsanasangraha.md) — ref: ch. 1
  - [Kauṭilīya Arthaśāstra](../texts/arthasastra.md) — ref: 1.2.4-5
### [The śramaṇa movement](../lineages/sramana.md)
(As reported) Ajita: there is no gift, no sacrifice, no offering.

**Texts:**

### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
Sacrifice of animals and claims of birth-purity are refuted; the priests are cheats (Bhūridatta).
- if the slain go to heaven, let brāhmaṇas slay brāhmaṇas
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhūridatta Jātaka](../texts/bhuridatta-jataka.md) — ref: 161-173
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
The charge of falsehood, inconsistency (vyāghāta) and repetition fails: a failed rite is due to defects in the act, agent or means.
- NS 2.1.58–59
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 2.1.58-59

**Historical debate — The Mīmāṃsā–Buddhist exchange on scripture and omniscience (textual)** (c. 6th–8th c. CE (scholarly, approximate)): Ślokavārttika (codanā, sambandhākṣepaparihāra); Pramāṇavārttika; Tattvasaṅgraha (Śrutiparīkṣā, Atīndriyārthadarśiparīkṣā) Each holds its own case established.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Vedic schools agree the Veda is valid but disagree whether it has an author (Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta vs Nyāya), and the śramaṇa, Cārvāka and some bhakti and Sant voices deny or relativize its authority.
Within the Vedic schools Vedānta takes a middle course — breathed out by Brahman yet not freely composed, re-manifest in each cycle with the same names and forms (BSBh 1.1.3; 1.3.28-30) — which accommodates the Nyāya concern (a source in the omniscient) and the Mīmāṃsā concern (no human author) but is accepted by neither. The Upaniṣads and the Gītā themselves relativize the Veda for the knower (MuU 1.1.4-5; BĀU 4.3.22; BhG 2.46).
**The traditions' own objections:** Mīmāṃsā rejects any author, the Lord included (ŚV sambandhākṣepaparihāra 66-68); Nyāya holds that validity requires a reliable speaker (NS 2.1.69); the Buddhists and Jains deny the Veda's claim over the supersensible; the Cārvākas deny it outright.
