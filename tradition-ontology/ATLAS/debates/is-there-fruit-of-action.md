# Do good and bad deeds bear fruit (kiriyavāda) or not (akiriyavāda)?

`dsp:is-there-fruit-of-action` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [The śramaṇa movement](../lineages/sramana.md)
(As reported) Pūraṇa: the doer of any violence does no evil, the giver gains no merit; Pakudha: the seven bodies are unmade and unaffected, so no one kills; Ajita: there is no fruit of good and bad deeds.
- a heap of flesh made of all beings brings no evil (DN 2)
- the sword passes between the seven bodies (DN 2)
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāmaññaphala Sutta](../texts/samannaphala-sutta.md) — ref: DN 2 §17, 23, 26
  - [Sandaka Sutta](../texts/sandaka-sutta.md) — ref: MN 76 §7–19
### [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](../lineages/carvaka.md)
(As reported) There is no unseen potency of deeds; things differ by own-nature; merit and demerit are not; the rites bear no fruit.
- fire is hot and water cold by svabhāva
- no one returns from the other world
**Texts:** 
  - [Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha](../texts/sarvadarsanasangraha.md) — ref: ch. 1
  - [Ṣaḍdarśanasamuccaya (Haribhadra)](../texts/saddarsanasamuccaya-haribhadra.md) — ref: 80
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
Deeds bear fruit; the doctrine of action is the safe course; Gosāla's 'no deed' is the worst doctrine.
- the safe wager (MN 60)
- the six classes are by conduct (AN 6.57)
**Texts:** 
  - [Apaṇṇaka Sutta](../texts/apannaka-sutta.md) — ref: MN 60 §13–20
  - [Chaḷabhijāti Sutta](../texts/chalabhijati-sutta.md) — ref: AN 6.57
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The true kiriyavāda knows influx, bondage and the wearing away of karma; the akiriyavādins, who call the world barren and fixed, are like a blind man with a lamp.
- Sūtr 1.12.4–8, 1.12.11–22
**Texts:** 
  - [Sūtrakṛtāṅga](../texts/sutrakrtanga.md) — ref: 1.12

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the deniers explicitly reject any fruit of deeds; the affirmers make it the ground of the holy life.

**The traditions' own objections:** Buddhist and Jain reporters call the akiriya teachings a negation of the holy life (MN 76); the reported Pūraṇa would reject any reconciliation that restores merit and demerit.
**Candidate readings:** P1-level: 'no doer, no killer' (Pakudha) might be read as a statement about unchanging substance (ultimate) against the conventional reality of harm — the Buddhist and Jain reporters reject this as licence for killing.; P5-neyartha: none — no source treats the akiriya teaching as provisional.
