# How many members does a demonstration (inference for others) need?

`dsp:members-of-inference` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Five: proposition, reason, example, application, conclusion; together they embody all four means of knowledge. A ten-member view (adding desire to know, doubt etc.) is rejected; stating fewer is the point of defeat 'saying too little'.
- The members correspond to testimony, inference, perception and comparison, their convergence shown in the conclusion (NBh 1.1.1).
- NS 5.2.12 (DCS) counts omission of a member as a point of defeat.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.32; 5.2.1
  - [Nyāyabhāṣya](../texts/nyaya-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1; 1.1.32
### `lin:pramana-buddhist`
Fewer members suffice: Dignāga states thesis, reason and example; Dharmakīrti holds that for the competent the reason with its pervasion and presence in the subject is enough, and stating superfluous members is itself a fault in debate.
- The logical force lies in the triple character of the reason, not in the number of statements.
**Texts:** 
  - `src:pramanasamuccaya` — ref: ch. 4 (from memory)
  - `src:nyayabindu` — ref: ch. 3 (from memory)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Three members suffice — either the first three or the last three (Vedāntaparibhāṣā); Mīmāṃsakas also hold three.
- Recalled from the Vedāntaparibhāṣā's inference chapter (low confidence).
**Texts:** 
  - [Vedāntaparibhāṣā](../texts/vedantaparibhasa.md) — ref: anumāna chapter
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The number depends on the listener: two members for the sharp, more (up to ten) for others (Jain logicians, as usually reported).
- Recalled with low confidence.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Principles:** P4-stage, P6-upaya
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: each side treats the other's practice as a fault in debate.
**The traditions' own objections:** Nyāya holds five members necessary for a complete demonstration to others; Dharmakīrti holds the extra members superfluous. Neither concedes the other's count.
**Candidate readings:** P4-stage / P6-upaya: the number of stated members depends on the hearer's capacity — a principle the Jain side states explicitly and Nyāya approaches by distinguishing inference for oneself (no verbal members) from inference for others.; The shared logical core (pervasion plus the reason's presence in the subject) is common to all sides.
