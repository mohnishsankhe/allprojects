# Should Yudhiṣṭhira renounce the kingdom for the forest, or rule? (MBh 12.7–36)

### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Rule: his brothers, Draupadī, Vyāsa and Kṛṣṇa argue that the householder's and king's duty, protecting the people with the rod, is his dharma; Sahadeva teaches that 'mine' is death and 'not mine' the eternal Brahman — renunciation is inner.
- Nakula: the householder's stage is the best; Arjuna: wealth sustains dharma; Sahadeva: 12.13.4; Janaka's queen rebukes her husband's renunciation (12.18).

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** In the narrative Yudhiṣṭhira is persuaded: renunciation proper belongs to its stage of life, and inner non-possession ('na mama') is possible while ruling; the Mokṣadharma then teaches liberation to the ruling king.
The same tension recurs in Sulabhā's test of Janaka (dsp:sulabha-janaka) and in the Gītā's saṃnyāsa–karma question (dsp:renunciation-or-action-gita). Janaka tells Śuka that the āśramas are passed in order but one already free may renounce at once (12.312–313).
**The traditions' own objections:** Renunciant traditions hold formal renunciation to be required for the highest liberation and read the persuasion as addressed to one not yet ready.
