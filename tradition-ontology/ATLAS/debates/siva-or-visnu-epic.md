# Who is supreme in the epic — Nārāyaṇa/Viṣṇu or Śiva?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Early Bhāgavata devotion (the Nārāyaṇīya / Sātvata–Ekāntika dharma)](../lineages/bhagavata-early.md)
Nārāyaṇa-Vāsudeva is supreme: the culmination seen in all five systems, including Pāśupata (12.337.63–67); the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma names him the one refuge (13.135); in the Gītā Śaṃkara is one of Kṛṣṇa's manifestations (10.23).

### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Śiva is supreme: Kṛṣṇa himself learns Śiva's thousand names from Upamanyu and obtains his wishes by devotion to him (13.14–17); Śiva proclaims the Pāśupata knowledge (12.337.62), grants Arjuna the Pāśupata weapon (3.38–41) and teaches Umā dharma (13.126–134).

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The epic states that Rudra and Nārāyaṇa are one being made twofold, manifest in all actions (12.328.24), so the praises of each are read as the one being praised under two forms.
**The traditions' own objections:** Madhva's Mahābhāratatātparyanirṇaya holds Viṣṇu supreme and Śiva his devotee; Śaiva readers hold Śiva supreme; both reject mere identity as the epic's purport.
