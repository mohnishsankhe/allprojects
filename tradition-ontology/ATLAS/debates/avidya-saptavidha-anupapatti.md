# Is Advaita's avidyā — beginningless, indescribable, located in and concealing Brahman — tenable?

`dsp:avidya-saptavidha-anupapatti` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A6; G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Yes: avidyā is beginningless, neither real nor unreal (anirvacanīya), located in Brahman (Vivaraṇa) or the individual (Bhāmatī); it conceals and projects; it is known by experience ('I am ignorant') and by postulation, and is removed by the knowledge arising from the great sayings.
- the experience 'I do not know'
- the need to explain the appearance of plurality
- texts such as 'veiled by the unreal'
**Texts:** 
  - [Pañcapādikāvivaraṇa](../texts/pancapadika-vivarana.md) — ref: passim
  - [Bhāmatī](../texts/bhamati.md) — ref: passim
  - [Advaitasiddhi](../texts/advaitasiddhi.md) — ref: passim
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
No: it is untenable in seven ways — locus, concealment, nature, indescribability, proof, remover and removal; bondage is due to karma, which scripture calls avidyā.
- no locus: not Brahman (pure knowledge), not the jīva (itself a product of avidyā)
- self-luminous Brahman cannot be concealed
- what is neither real nor unreal is not known
- a knowledge itself unreal cannot remove it
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1
  - [Śatadūṣaṇī](../texts/satadusani.md) — ref: passim
  - [Vedārthasaṅgraha](../texts/vedarthasangraha.md) — ref: critique of the three schools
