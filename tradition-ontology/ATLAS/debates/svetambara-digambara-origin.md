# How did the Śvetāmbara–Digambara division arise?

`dsp:svetambara-digambara-origin` · `skeleton` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
During a twelve-year famine foreseen by Bhadrabāhu, the saṅgha went south with him and Candragupta; those who stayed in the north under Sthūlabhadra (or Rāmilla and Sthūlācārya) took up a half-cloth to beg in hard times and later a full white garment, becoming the Śvetāmbaras (at Valabhī, per Devasena, 136 years after Vikrama).
- Hariṣeṇa's Bṛhatkathākośa (931)
- Devasena's Darśanasāra (933)
- Ratnanandi's Bhadrabāhucarita (15th c.)
- The Śravaṇabeḷagoḷa inscriptions of the migration

  - [Inscriptions of Śravaṇabeḷagoḷa](../texts/sravanabelagola-inscriptions.md) — ref: 1
### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
The Digambaras (Boṭikas) arose from Śivabhūti, who at Rathavīrapura, 609 years after the nirvāṇa, angered over a blanket, declared the naked Jinakalpa the only true renunciation; because his sister could not go naked, his followers denied liberation to women.
- Jinabhadra's Viśeṣāvaśyaka Bhāṣya and the Āvaśyaka commentaries
- The Pāṭaliputra council after the famine was held by the continuing community
**Texts:** 
  - [Viśeṣāvaśyaka Bhāṣya](../texts/visesavasyaka-bhasya.md) — ref: botika
