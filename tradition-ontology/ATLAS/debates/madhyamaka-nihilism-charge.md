# Is the Madhyamaka a nihilism — does 'everything is empty' destroy karma, the path and the four truths?

`dsp:madhyamaka-nihilism-charge` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md)
If all is empty there is no arising or ceasing, so the four truths, their knowledge and cultivation, the fruits, the Saṅgha, the Dharma and the Buddha, dharma and adharma and all worldly conventions are destroyed.
- Emptiness denies arising and ceasing.
- Without arising there is no suffering, cause, cessation or path.
- Without the path there are no fruits, noble persons or Three Jewels.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mūlamadhyamakakārikā](../texts/mulamadhyamakakarika.md) — ref: 24.1-6 (the objector's words, reported by Nāgārjuna)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The view of the śūnyavādin is contradicted by all the means of knowledge and deserves no detailed refutation; a world experienced by every pramāṇa cannot be denied.
- Perception and the other means of knowledge establish the world.
- A doctrine that nothing exists cannot even be stated consistently.
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 2.2.31
### [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md)
Kumārila examines and rejects the doctrine of the void (śūnyavāda) together with the doctrine that cognitions have no object: without real objects and cognitions there is no basis for ritual, merit or knowledge.
- Cognitions require objects.
- Denial of the objects of cognition undermines all action.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: Śūnyavāda and Nirālambanavāda sections
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
Some who misunderstand the Perfection of Wisdom deny even the inexpressible reality that is the basis of designation; this 'wrongly grasped emptiness' is worse than a view of self. Something must exist for anything to be empty of something.
- Emptiness is of the imagined in the dependent; the dependent and the perfected exist.
- Total denial destroys the basis of practice.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yogācārabhūmi](../texts/yogacarabhumi.md) — ref: Bodhisattvabhūmi, Tattvārtha chapter
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
The objector does not understand the purpose, nature and meaning of emptiness. Emptiness is dependent origination; for whom emptiness is possible everything is possible, while if things had own-nature there could be no arising, suffering, path or awakening. The Mādhyamika is not a nihilist: he does not deny action, agent and fruit but their own-nature.
- Two truths: the conventional is not denied (24.8–10).
- Own-nature would make the world static and the path impossible (24.16–38).
- Emptiness wrongly grasped harms; rightly grasped it is the middle way (24.11, 24.18).
**Texts:** 
  - [Mūlamadhyamakakārikā](../texts/mulamadhyamakakarika.md) — ref: 24.7-40
  - [Vigrahavyāvartanī](../texts/vigrahavyavartani.md) — ref: 70
  - [Prasannapadā](../texts/prasannapada.md) — ref: on 17.30 and 18.7

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level
**Explanation:** Under P1-level the Madhyamaka affirms karma, path and the truths as conventional truth while denying their own-nature ultimately; the charge fits a nihilist reading that the Madhyamaka itself rejects (MMK 24.11).
The reconciliation removes the charge only for those who accept that conventional truth needs no ultimately real basis.
**The traditions' own objections:** Advaita, Mīmāṃsā and Nyāya hold that the empirical world must rest on something real (for Advaita, Brahman; for the realists, the world itself); Yogācāra holds that the dependent nature must be real. None accepts that an entirely empty conventional truth avoids nihilism.
