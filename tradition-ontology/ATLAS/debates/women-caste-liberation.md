# Can women, and people of every birth (varṇa, jāti), attain liberation — and are they eligible for the scriptures, initiation and renunciation that lead to it?

**Coverage:** G12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
The orthodox order: four classes with distinct duties; the śūdra is not twice-born and may not hear, study or perform the Veda — severe penalties are laid down for one who listens to it; a woman is guarded by father, husband and sons and is not independent; eligibility for the Vedic rite belongs to the twice-born, husband and wife performing jointly. The Veda is not within hearing of women and śūdras — hence the Bhārata was composed for them.

### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Śūdras are not eligible for the knowledge that comes through Vedic study (BS 1.3.34-38); but Śaṅkara grants that those like Vidura and Dharmavyādha attained knowledge through the impressions of former lives, that the fruit of knowledge once attained cannot be withheld, and that all four classes may hear the Itihāsa and Purāṇa. In the Manīṣāpañcaka, whoever has realized the one consciousness is the teacher, be he caṇḍāla or brāhmaṇa.
**Texts:**

### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Maitreyī was a discusser of Brahman; Satyakāma, who did not know his father, was accepted as a brāhmaṇa for his truthfulness; Jānaśruti, addressed as 'śūdra', was taught by Raikva.
**Texts:**

### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
Women cannot attain liberation in a female body: they cannot give up clothing and so cannot be without possessions; they lack the strongest bodily constitution needed for the highest pure meditation; as they cannot fall to the lowest hell they cannot rise to the highest state; the scripture's 'female siddhas' means psychological, not physical, sex (Prabhācandra); subtle living beings arise in their bodies (Aṣṭapāhuḍa). The monk's sign is 'like the newborn' — naked.
**Texts:**

### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
Women can attain liberation: the nineteenth Tīrthaṅkara, Mallī, was a woman; the liberated include those liberated in a female form (Prajñāpanā's fifteen kinds); nuns keep their robes by monastic law.

### [Yāpanīya](../lineages/yapaniya.md)
Śākaṭāyana: there is liberation for women as for men, since its cause — the three jewels — is present in them; a woman is not a non-soul, not incapable of liberation, not opposed to right faith, not non-human.

### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
Women who go forth are capable of the fruits up to arahantship (the Buddha to Ānanda), though it is impossible that a woman be a fully awakened Buddha; Mahāpajāpatī's ordination came with eight principles of respect. Somā: what does womanhood matter when the mind is concentrated? Class is not by birth: among humans there is no difference of species; one is a brāhmaṇa by deeds.

### [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md)
The nāga king's eight-year-old daughter attains buddhahood (the Lotus Sūtra, in which she turns into a man); the goddess in Vimalakīrti's house shows Śāriputra that 'woman' has no fixed mark; Amitābha's thirty-fifth vow; in Zen Dōgen urges bowing to anyone who has attained the Way, man or woman.

### [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md)

### [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md)

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the orthodox Vedic order restricts eligibility for Vedic study and rite by birth and sex, the Digambaras deny liberation in a female body, while the Śvetāmbaras and Yāpanīyas, the Buddhists, the bhakti, Vīraśaiva, Sant and Siddha voices and the Kaula tantras open liberation (and in varying degrees initiation) to all.
A distinction used within the orthodox tradition itself narrows the dispute: eligibility (adhikāra) for Vedic study is one thing, liberation another — Śaṅkara (BSBh 1.3.38) grants liberation through smṛti, Purāṇa and former-life impressions, and the Manīṣāpañcaka makes realization, not birth, the mark of the teacher. The critics reject birth-based adhikāra itself. Early Buddhism affirms women's arahantship but denies a female sammāsambuddha.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Digambaras insist on nudity as a condition of liberation and deny it to women (Prameyakamalamārtaṇḍa); the Śvetāmbaras and Yāpanīyas reject that argument (Strīnirvāṇaprakaraṇa); Dharmaśāstra and Mīmāṃsā hold the varṇa restrictions to be Vedic; the Sants, Vīraśaivas and Siddhas deny that birth confers any spiritual rank.
