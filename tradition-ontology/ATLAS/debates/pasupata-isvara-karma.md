# Does the Lord act independently of the karma of souls?

`dsp:pasupata-isvara-karma` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Coverage:** G10

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md)
The Lord depends neither on karma nor on the soul; he produces the effect at will, all at once or in sequence.
- Those who desire the fruits of action depend on Maheśvara, not he on them (PABh on PS 2.6).
- Karma bears fruit when favoured by the Lord's will and can be fruitless without it, as Yayāti's was; beings act because they depend on his will (SDS ch. 6).
- The Lord, whose desires are fulfilled, needs nothing karma produces; effects do not arise at once because his power of action follows his will (SDS ch. 6).
**Texts:** 
  - [Pañcārthabhāṣya](../texts/pancarthabhasya.md) — ref: on PS 2.5-6
  - [Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha](../texts/sarvadarsanasangraha.md) — ref: ch. 6
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The Lord's dispensation depends on beings' karma; otherwise he would be partial and cruel; and a Lord who is merely the efficient cause cannot escape either partiality or circular dependence on karma.
- Partiality and cruelty do not belong to the Lord because he depends [on karma] (BS 2.1.34).
- A Lord who makes beings low, middle and high would have passion and aversion; if he depends on karma, Lord and karma depend on each other, and beginninglessness does not remove the fault (Śaṅkara on BS 2.2.37).
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahma Sūtra](../texts/brahma-sutra.md) — ref: 2.1.34
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 2.2.37
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
The Lord is the cause who makes human effort fruitful; results do not arise without human action (Nyāya Sūtra 4.1.19-21 as read by its commentators).
- Human action is sometimes seen to be fruitless, so the Lord is a cause; yet without human action there is no fruit; the objection fails because the result is brought about by him.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 4.1.19-21

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Pāśupatas deny that the Lord depends on karma; Vedānta holds that denying dependence makes him partial.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Pāśupata tradition explicitly denies that the Lord is karma-dependent (PABh 2.6); Śaṅkara explicitly argues that a karma-independent Lord would be partial.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the Pāśupata speaks from the divine standpoint (the Lord's freedom), Vedānta and Nyāya from the causal standpoint (karma's regularity); the SDS reply itself gives karma a fruit-bearing role under the Lord's will.; P1-level: at the conventional level karma governs results; the Lord's freedom concerns the level of the ultimate cause.; No reconciliation: the traditions disagree about whether the Lord could act otherwise than karma requires.
