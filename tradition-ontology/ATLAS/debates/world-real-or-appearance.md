# Is the world real, or an appearance (mithyā) that is neither real nor unreal, superimposed on the one reality — and is māyā an indeterminable ignorance or the Lord's real power?

`dsp:world-real-or-appearance` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G3

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The world is mithyā: not real, since it is sublated by the knowledge of Brahman; not unreal, since it appears; indeterminable as either — like the silver seen in shell. Duality is mere māyā; non-duality is the ultimate truth. Three orders are distinguished: the ultimate (Brahman), the empirical (the world, real for all practical purposes — Śaṅkara defends external objects against the Buddhist idealist), and the illusory (dream, rope-snake). Falsity is defended under five definitions against the Dvaita critique.
**Texts:** 
  - [Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (Gauḍapāda Kārikā, Āgamaśāstra)](../texts/mandukya-karika.md) — ref: 1.17; 2.6; 2.31
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 2.1.14; 2.2.28
  - [Advaitasiddhi](../texts/advaitasiddhi.md) — ref: prathama pariccheda (mithyātva)
  - [Tattvapradīpikā (Citsukhī)](../texts/tattvapradipika-citsukha.md) — ref: mithyātva
  - [Dṛg-Dṛśya Viveka (Vākyasudhā)](../texts/drg-drsya-viveka.md) — ref: 32-37
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
The world of souls and matter is real, the body of Brahman. The Advaitin's avidyā is untenable in seven ways — no locus, no power to conceal self-luminous Brahman, no coherent nature, no sense to 'indescribable', no means of knowledge, no remover, no removal; no means of knowledge establishes an undifferentiated reality; consciousness is always someone's and of something.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1 (mahāpūrvapakṣa and mahāsiddhānta); 2.1.15
  - [Siddhitraya](../texts/siddhitraya.md) — ref: Saṃvitsiddhi
  - [Vedārthasaṅgraha](../texts/vedarthasangraha.md) — ref: critique of three schools
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
The world is real: the witness (sākṣin) establishes it as unsublated; what is sublated produces no effect; even dream-objects are real products of impressions. None of the five Advaita definitions of falsity is coherent, and the inferences from 'perceivable', 'insentient' and 'limited' fail. Reality is twofold: the independent (Viṣṇu) and the dependent (souls and matter) — dependent, but real.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāyāmṛta](../texts/nyayamrta.md) — ref: prathama pariccheda
  - [Anuvyākhyāna](../texts/anuvyakhyana.md) — ref: 1.4.90-103
  - [Prapañcamithyātvānumānakhaṇḍana](../texts/prapancamithyatvanumanakhandana.md) — ref: whole
  - [Tattvasaṅkhyāna](../texts/tattvasankhyana.md) — ref: 1
  - [Vādāvalī](../texts/vadavali.md) — ref: 1
### [Bhedābheda Vedānta (Bhāskara's school and the early difference-and-non-difference Vedāntins)](../lineages/bhedabheda.md)
The effect is real and both different and non-different from its cause; those who expound māyā lead people astray (Bhāskara).
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Bhāskara (Bhāskarabhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-bhaskara.md) — ref: 1.4.25; 2.1.14
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
The world is a real transformation of the Lord's external power; the souls are his marginal power, distinct yet dependent. To call the world false and the Lord's lordship imagined is the māyāvāda that the Gītā (16.8) describes and the Padma Purāṇa calls 'Buddhism in disguise'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Paramātmasandarbha](../texts/paramatma-sandarbha.md) — ref: jīva and world sections; anuccheda 71
  - [Caitanya Caritāmṛta](../texts/caitanya-caritamrta.md) — ref: 1.7
### [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
The universe is the real self-manifestation of free consciousness on its own screen; māyā is the Lord's power of self-concealment (Māyā-śakti), his supreme freedom — not an indeterminable ignorance. Those who say 'in the beginning this was being' stand at the level of the Īśvara-tattva, and some knowers of the Upaniṣads take prāṇa as the self: partial roles of the one consciousness. (The Vijñāna Bhairava nevertheless teaches contemplating the universe as a magic show as one means to peace.)
**Texts:** 
  - [Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya](../texts/pratyabhijnahrdayam.md) — ref: sūtras 1-2, 8 with commentary
  - [Paramārthasāra](../texts/paramarthasara.md) — ref: 15
  - [Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā](../texts/isvarapratyabhijna-karika.md) — ref: 1.5.1
  - [Vijñānabhairava](../texts/vijnana-bhairava-tantra.md) — ref: 102; 133
### [Śākta traditions](../lineages/sakta.md)
Māyā is the Goddess's own power; she is the manifest and the unmanifest; her reality is beyond both duality and non-duality (see dsp:maya-sakti-or-avidya).
**Texts:** 
  - [Lalitā Sahasranāma](../texts/lalita-sahasranama.md) — ref: 86-87
  - [Saundaryalaharī](../texts/saundarya-lahari.md) — ref: 35
  - [Kulārṇava Tantra](../texts/kularnava-tantra.md) — ref: 1.110
### [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md)
The world is real, produced from real māyā by Śiva as efficient cause; 'advaita' in the Śivajñānabodha means non-separateness, not numerical identity; the Advaita and other views are set out and refuted.
**Texts:** 
  - [Civañāṉa Pōtam](../texts/sivananabodham.md) — ref: 1
  - [Civañāṉa Māpāṭiyam](../texts/civanana-mapatiyam.md) — ref: sūtra 2
  - [Civañāṉa Cittiyār](../texts/sivananasiddhiyar.md) — ref: parapakkam
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
The world is real, being produced by faultless causes and never sublated; bondage cannot be due to an unreal avidyā — and a real avidyā would abandon non-dualism.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Sūtra](../texts/samkhya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.20-26; 6.52
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
Absolute non-duality is refuted: if all were one there would be no distinction of act and fruit, merit and demerit, this world and the next, knowledge and ignorance, bondage and release.
**Texts:** 
  - [Āptamīmāṃsā](../texts/aptamimamsa.md) — ref: 24-27
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
The idealist and illusionist thesis lacks a reason: the notion of dream-objects depends on prior waking experience, as memory does; false cognition presupposes a real object mistaken.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 4.2.31-37
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
All dharmas, the fruits of the path and buddhahood itself are like an illusion, like a dream — appearing conventionally, empty of own-nature; form is emptiness and emptiness form (the Prajñāpāramitā background shared by the Mahāyāna schools).
**Texts:** 
  - [Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā](../texts/astasahasrika-prajnaparamita.md) — ref: ch. 2
  - [Prajñāpāramitāhṛdaya](../texts/prajnaparamita-hrdaya.md) — ref: §4

**Historical debate — The Nyāyāmṛta–Advaitasiddhi exchange** (early 16th – 17th c. CE (scholarly, approximate)): Vyāsatīrtha's Nyāyāmṛta attacks the Advaita definitions of falsity and the inferences for it; Madhusūdana Sarasvatī's Advaitasiddhi answers it section by section; Vyāsa Rāmācārya's Nyāyāmṛta-taraṅgiṇī defends the Nyāyāmṛta; Brahmānanda Sarasvatī's Laghucandrikā (Gauḍabrahmānandī) defends the Advaitasiddhi; later rejoinders continued (e.g. the Nyāyāmṛta-kaṇṭakoddhāra and the Nyāyāmṛta-āmoda in the Dvaita line). Each tradition holds that its own side prevailed; neither presents the exchange as closed.

**Historical debate — Rāmānuja and Yajñamūrti at Śrīraṅgam** (tradition: Rāmānuja's lifetime (11th–12th c. CE)): Guruparamparāprabhāvam (Ārāyirappaṭi) — the tradition's account Śrīvaiṣṇava: after eighteen days the Advaita renouncer Yajñamūrti yielded and became Rāmānuja's disciple.

**Historical debate — Śaṅkara's debates in the digvijaya narratives** (tradition: Śaṅkara's lifetime): Śaṅkaradigvijaya (the tradition's account) Advaita: Śaṅkara defeated the dualist and ritualist schools of his time.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: Advaita's own levels of reality make the world real empirically and false ultimately, but Viśiṣṭādvaita, Dvaita, Śaiva Siddhānta and Kashmir Śaivism reject the very distinction as applied to the world, each for its own reasons.
Advaita offers P1 in its own terms (the world is vyāvahārika-sat; BSBh 2.2.28 upholds external objects against the Buddhist idealist). The theistic Vedāntas deny that anything can be empirically real and ultimately false (Śrībhāṣya 1.1.1; Nyāyāmṛta 1). Kashmir Śaivism agrees that the world depends on one consciousness but calls it that consciousness's real self-display and māyā its power, not avidyā — a difference both sides keep.
**The traditions' own objections:** Rāmānuja's seven untenabilities of avidyā (Śrībhāṣya 1.1.1); Vyāsatīrtha's refutation of the five definitions of falsity (Nyāyāmṛta); Madhva's rejection of vivarta (Anuvyākhyāna 1.4.95-98); the Kashmir Śaiva view of māyā as Śakti (Paramārthasāra 15); Advaita in turn holds that any real second to Brahman conflicts with the non-dual texts (MK 1.17; Advaitasiddhi).
