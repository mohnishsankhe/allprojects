# Was there being (sat) or non-being (asat) in the beginning?

`dsp:sat-or-asat-in-the-beginning` · `sourced` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Being was born from non-being (RV 10.72.2–3), a view the Taittirīya Upaniṣad repeats ('in the beginning this was non-being; from it being was born', TU 2.7).
- The Ṛgveda states it twice in 10.72.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 10.72.2–3
  - [Taittirīya Upaniṣad](../texts/taittiriya-upanisad.md) — ref: 2.7
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Then there was neither non-being nor being (RV 10.129.1); the seers found the bond of being in non-being (10.129.4).
- The Nāsadīya denies both at the start and leaves the origin open.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 10.129.1, 4
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
In the beginning this was being alone, one only; how could being come from non-being? (Uddālaka in the Chāndogya, rejecting those who say asat was first).
- Being cannot arise from nothing.
**Texts:** 
  - [Chāndogya Upaniṣad](../texts/chandogya-upanisad.md) — ref: 6.2.1–2

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Śaṅkara reads the 'non-being' of the creation texts as the unmanifest (name and form not yet distinguished), not absolute nothing, so 'asat' and 'sat' describe the same origin from the standpoints of the manifest and the unmanifest.
The Nāsadīya's 'neither being nor non-being' is read by Vedāntins as the state before name and form; the Ṛgveda itself does not give this gloss.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Chāndogya explicitly rejects the asat view as stated; the reconciliation is Advaita's reading and not the hymns' own; other schools (e.g. Nyāya on new effects) would frame the question differently.
