# May Buddhists (monastics) eat meat?

`dsp:is-meat-eating-permitted` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md)
No: the bodhisattva, seeing all beings as his only child and as former relatives, must not eat meat; the earlier permission of 'three-fold pure' meat was given step by step and is now withdrawn.
- Laṅkāvatāra ch. 8.
- Mahāparinirvāṇa juan 4: 'eating meat cuts off the seed of great compassion'.
- Brahmajāla minor precept on meat.
**Texts:** 
  - [Laṅkāvatāra-sūtra](../texts/lankavatara-sutra.md) — ref: ch. 8
  - [Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra](../texts/mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana.md) — ref: T374 juan 4, 386a
  - [Fanwang jing (Brahmā's Net Sūtra, Mahāyāna)](../texts/fanwang-jing.md) — ref: minor precepts
### [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
Meat that is pure in three respects (not seen, heard or suspected to have been killed for the monk) may be accepted; the Buddha refused Devadatta's demand to make abstention from meat compulsory.
- Jīvaka Sutta (MN 55).
- Devadatta's five points (Vinaya, Cullavagga 7).
**Texts:** 
  - [Majjhima Nikāya](../texts/majjhima-nikaya.md) — ref: MN 55
  - [Vinaya Piṭaka](../texts/vinaya-pitaka.md) — ref: Cullavagga 7

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P5-neyartha
**Explanation:** The Mahāparinirvāṇa itself presents the three-fold-pure rule as a provisional, stage-by-stage permission later withdrawn for bodhisattvas.
**The traditions' own objections:** Theravāda holds the three-fold-pure rule to be the Buddha's final rule and the Laṅkāvatāra's chapter (absent from its earliest Chinese version) not the Buddha's word; Tibetan traditions have varied in practice.
