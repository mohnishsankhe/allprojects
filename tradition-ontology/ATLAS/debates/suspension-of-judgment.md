# Should one refuse to affirm or deny anything about what lies beyond experience (the other world, fruit of deeds, the perfected one after death)?

`dsp:suspension-of-judgment` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Ajñāna (the sceptics)](../lineages/ajnana.md)
(As reported) Yes: to every such question, in each of its four forms, 'I do not say it is so, nor thus, nor otherwise, nor not so, nor not not so'; knowing is useless and ignorance better.
- avoids false speech, grasping, and refutation (DN 1)
- who knows whether the soul exists, and what use is knowing it? (Śīlāṅka)
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāmaññaphala Sutta](../texts/samannaphala-sutta.md) — ref: DN 2 §32
  - [Brahmajāla Sutta](../texts/brahmajala-sutta.md) — ref: DN 1 §2.23-2.29
  - [Sūtrakṛtāṅga-vṛtti of Śīlāṅka](../texts/sutrakrtanga-vrtti-silanka.md) — ref: on 1.12
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
It is eel-wriggling born of fear or dullness; yet the Buddha himself sets some questions aside as not leading to the goal and declares what he knows beyond all views.
- DN 1 §3.29–3.31
- MN 76: the dull teacher is an unreliable holy life
- DN 16: the Buddha sets aside Subhadda's question about the six
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmajāla Sutta](../texts/brahmajala-sutta.md) — ref: DN 1
  - [Sandaka Sutta](../texts/sandaka-sutta.md) — ref: MN 76

### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The ignorant cannot settle their inquiry in ignorance or teach others; they are blind leading the blind.
- Sūtr 1.1.2.17; 1.12.2
**Texts:** 
  - [Sūtrakṛtāṅga](../texts/sutrakrtanga.md) — ref: 1.1.2.14-23; 1.12.2

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Ajñānika refuses to assert; Buddhist and Jain both assert, and both insist their own reticence (the undeclared points; the 'inexpressible' of the sevenfold predication) differs in kind from his.

**The traditions' own objections:** Jains: syādvāda asserts under a standpoint, ajñāna asserts nothing. Buddhists: the Buddha knows and declares what is beyond views; the eel-wriggler knows nothing.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: Sañjaya's refusal of four alternatives resembles the Jain 'avaktavya' and the later catuṣkoṭi — a resemblance noted by scholars (metadata only), rejected by both traditions.; P4-stage: the Buddha's setting-aside is tied to what benefits the hearer — not a general refusal.
