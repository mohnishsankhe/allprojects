# Can the Veda be a means of knowledge about an already-existing thing (siddha), or only about what is to be done (sādhya / kārya)?

`dsp:siddha-or-sadhya` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/prabhakara-mimamsa.md)
Vedic sentences are authoritative about what is to be done; words mean in connection with the kārya, and scripture does not make known accomplished things for their own sake — so, e.g., a blissful liberation is not established by Upaniṣadic statements.
- Words are learned from elders' usage in action-guiding sentences (anvitābhidhāna).
- The Veda's function is to prompt; statements of fact have a purpose only through injunctions.
**Texts:** 
  - [Prakaraṇapañcikā](../texts/prakaranapancika.md) — ref: ch. 7, ch. 8
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
Passages that do not enjoin (arthavāda) are meaningful by forming one sentence with injunctions; the Brahma Sūtra reports Jaimini as holding that the self taught in the Upaniṣads is subsidiary to action and fruit-statements for knowledge are praise.
- Āmnāya is for action (MS 1.2.1); arthavādas praise injunctions (MS 1.2.7).
- The self-knowledge taught serves as the agent-knowledge rites require.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mīmāṃsā Sūtra](../texts/mimamsa-sutra.md) — ref: 1.2.1, 1.2.7
  - [Brahma Sūtra](../texts/brahma-sutra.md) — ref: 3.4.2
### [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md)
Later Bhāṭṭas grant that Upaniṣads teaching the agent-self serve ritual injunctions, while those teaching the non-transmigrating self have their own purport as required by injunctions of meditation; self-knowledge serves both rite and person.
- The eternal self must be known for rites with other-worldly fruit (Śāstradīpikā).
- Meditation injunctions need the Upaniṣadic descriptions (Prabhāvalī).
**Texts:** 
  - [Śāstradīpikā](../texts/sastradipika.md) — ref: 1.1.5
  - [Prabhāvalī](../texts/prabhavali.md) — ref: 1.2.1
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The Upaniṣads are authoritative about Brahman, an existent reality, because their purport rests there; knowledge of Brahman is not enjoined and its fruit, liberation, is not produced by action.
- The Upaniṣads' purport is shown by their opening, close, repetition and the rest (the marks of purport).
- A sentence can convey an existent thing, as in ordinary speech.
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 1.1.4
  - [Brahma Sūtra](../texts/brahma-sutra.md) — ref: 3.4.8

**The traditions' own objections:** Prābhākaras deny that any Vedic sentence independently conveys an accomplished thing; Advaitins deny that knowledge of Brahman is enjoined.
**Candidate readings:** P4-stage: the ritual sections address the one eligible for action, the Upaniṣads the one eligible for knowledge (Śaṅkara's adhikāra-bheda) — accepted by Advaita, rejected by the Prābhākaras, for whom the issue is how sentences mean, not who hears them.; P2-standpoint: the later Bhāṭṭa view (Upaniṣads have their own purport as required by meditation) narrows the gap to the question whether meditation or knowledge is enjoined.; P8-six-marks: both sides use the same interpretive criteria but apply them to different passages; a text-by-text comparison may settle particular cases.
