# Is there a sphoṭa — a unitary word-entity distinct from the letters — that conveys meaning?

`dsp:sphota` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md)
Yes. The letters, perishing one after another, can convey meaning neither singly nor together; the word is a partless, sequence-less sphoṭa manifested by the sounds, grasped when the last sound, helped by the impressions of the earlier ones, makes it clear; finally only the sentence-sphoṭa is real.
- the letters never co-exist
- a single letter would convey the whole meaning if it sufficed
- we cognize 'one word', not a series
- sound is a quality (dhvani) of the word, as the drum-beat's extent varies while the word stays the same

  - [Vākyapadīya](../texts/vakyapadiya.md) — ref: 1.44; 1.73; kāṇḍa 1 sphoṭa verses
  - [Sphoṭasiddhi of Maṇḍana Miśra](../texts/sphotasiddhi.md) — ref: whole

  - [Sphoṭavāda of Nāgeśa](../texts/sphotavada-nagesa.md) — ref: whole
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
No. 'The letters alone are the word' (Upavarṣa): the last letter, together with the impressions left by the earlier ones, conveys the meaning; a sphoṭa is never perceived and is a needless assumption.
- the word is what is grasped by the ear, i.e. the letters
- the impressions of earlier letters suffice
- the sphoṭa is imperceptible
**Texts:** 
  - [Śābarabhāṣya](../texts/sabara-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.5
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: sphoṭavāda
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Śaṅkara follows Upavarṣa against the sphoṭa, while accepting that the world is preceded by the Vedic word (words as eternal universals).
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 1.3.28
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
No separate sphoṭa: the letters are apprehended in sequence and their meaning is understood from the final letter with the recollection of the earlier ones; sound is a non-eternal quality of ether.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāyamañjarī](../texts/nyayamanjari.md) — ref: (section on sphoṭa)
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
No: the word 'gauḥ' is the letters g, au, ḥ (Upavarṣa); meaning arises from the letters (through the impression of the earlier ones), as fire is known from smoke; a sphoṭa is never perceived and is superfluous.
- Śabara on MS 1.1.5; ŚV sphoṭavāda 134–137.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śābarabhāṣya](../texts/sabara-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.5
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: sphoṭavāda
### [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md)
Yes: the letters, perishing in sequence, cannot jointly convey meaning; the indivisible sphoṭa, manifested by the sounds, conveys it (Bhartṛhari); Maṇḍana Miśra's Sphoṭasiddhi defends this against Kumārila.
**Texts:**

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: whether a unitary meaning-bearer exists over and above the letters.
**The traditions' own objections:** Kumārila and Śaṅkara reject any separate entity; the grammarians reject a merely serial conveyance by letters.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the grammarians describe the word as understood (one), the Mīmāṃsakas the word as heard (letters); both agree that meaning is grasped only at the end of the utterance with the help of impressions.; P1-level: Bhartṛhari's sphoṭa belongs to the level of the undivided Word-principle, the letters to the level of articulated speech (vaikharī).; P2-standpoint: the letter-sequence (as heard) and the unitary word (as understood) may be two standpoints on one process — the Mīmāṃsakas reject a separate entity, the grammarians a merely serial conveyance.
