# Is the mind (cognition) self-luminous, or is it seen by a distinct, unchanging seer?

`dsp:is-mind-self-luminous` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
The mind is not self-illuminating because it is seen; the unchanging puruṣa always knows its activities.
- Like the senses and their objects, the mind is seen, so not self-luminous; fire is no counter-example (YBh 4.19).
- Subject and object cannot both be ascertained in one moment (4.20).
- If one cognition were known by another, infinite regress and confusion of memories would follow; the destructionists who deny the puruṣa throw everything into confusion (4.21, YBh 4.21).
**Texts:** 
  - [Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali](../texts/yoga-sutra.md) — ref: 4.18-22
  - [Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra)](../texts/yoga-bhasya.md) — ref: 4.19-21
### `lin:pramana-buddhist`
Cognition is self-aware (svasaṃvedana): every cognition is aware of itself in being aware of its object; no separate permanent seer is needed.
- Memory of a cognition presupposes its self-awareness (Dignāga).
- Momentary cognitions suffice; a permanent self is not established.
**Texts:** 
  - `src:pramanasamuccaya` — ref: 1.9-12
  - `src:pramanavarttika` — ref: pratyakṣa chapter
