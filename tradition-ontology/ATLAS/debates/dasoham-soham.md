# Should the devotee say 'I am He' (so'ham) or 'I am your servant' (dāsō'ham)?

`dsp:dasoham-soham` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Śaraṇa–vacana tradition](../lineages/sarana-vacana.md)
Not 'I am He' but 'I am your servant': the devotee's stance is humble service to Śiva and his devotees.
- Basava's vacana.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vacanas of Basavaṇṇa (Basavaṇṇanavara vacanagaḷu)](../texts/basavanna-vacanas.md) — ref: 'sōhaṃ endenisade dāsōhaṃ endenisayya'
### [Pañcācārya (Pañcapīṭha) tradition](../lineages/pancacarya.md)
At the māheśvara stage the worshipper rejects total non-duality because Lord and servant must differ (10.37–43), but in śivayoga-samādhi the supreme self is contemplated as 'I am He' (12.21–24), at aikya the feeling 'I am Śiva' establishes oneness (14.5–7), and one who no longer thinks 'I am servant, Śiva master' stands in non-duality (14.37).
- SSM 10.37–43; 12.21–24; 14.5–9; 14.37.
**Texts:** 
  - [Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi](../texts/siddhantasikhamani.md) — ref: 10.37–43; 12.21–24; 14.5–9; 14.37

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The SSM itself assigns servanthood to the stages of practice and 'I am He' to śivayoga and union (P4); the vacana speaks from the devotional standpoint, the SSM's aikya verses from the absolute (P2).
**The traditions' own objections:** Basava-centred readers hold dāsōhaṃ to be a permanent ethic of humility and service, not a stage to be outgrown, and hear 'sōhaṃ' in the vacana as the voice of ego.
