# How does the unchanging puruṣa 'know' the mind's activities — by a single reflection of consciousness in the buddhi, or by mutual reflection?

`dsp:reflection-single-or-mutual` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Vācaspati Miśra: the buddhi, transformed into the object's form, receives a reflection (chāyā) of consciousness; the resulting 'cognition' is a property of the buddhi alone; the puruṣa is not reflected into.
- Consciousness does not pass over into objects (YS 4.22); only the buddhi changes.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvavaiśāradī](../texts/tattvavaisaradi.md) — ref: 1.4; 1.7; 2.20 (position as reported by Vijñānabhikṣu)
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Vijñānabhikṣu: reflection is mutual — consciousness is reflected in the buddhi and the buddhi's activity is reflected back in consciousness; the fruit of cognition is consciousness qualified by that reflection.
- Taking the fruit to be a property of the buddhi abandons the plain sense of the bhāṣya's word 'pauruṣeya'.
- A mere reflection cannot itself be cognition or do the work of illuminating.
- Mutual reflection is established by śruti and smṛti.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yogavārttika](../texts/yogavarttika.md) — ref: 1.7; 1.4

**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: both commentators interpret YS 4.22 and YBh 1.7 and each rejects the other's model.
