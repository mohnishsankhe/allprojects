# Is birth settled only at death with the Buddha's welcome, or already in this life?

`dsp:deathbed-welcome-or-settled-in-life` · `skeleton` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Jōdo-shū (the Pure Land school of Hōnen)](../lineages/jodo-shu.md)
Right mindfulness at death and Amida's welcoming descent are sought; the nenbutsu is continued so that the practitioner is welcomed.
**Texts:** 
  - [Passages on the Selection of the Nenbutsu in the Original Vow (Senchakushū)](../texts/senchakushu.md) — ref: ch. 7
  - [Essentials of Birth (Ōjōyōshū)](../texts/ojoyoshu.md) — ref: ch. 6
### [Pure Land (Jingtu / Jōdo): birth in Amitābha's land](../lineages/pure-land.md)
Deathbed rites and assisted recitation secure right mindfulness; Daochuo's wax seal: the moment life ends is the moment of birth.
**Texts:** 
  - [The Dharma Gate of the Merits of the Samādhi of Contemplating the Ocean-like Marks of Amitābha](../texts/guannian-famen.md) — ref: 24b
  - [Collection on the Land of Peace and Bliss (Anle ji)](../texts/anle-ji.md) — ref: 11b
### [Jōdo Shinshū (the True Pure Land school of Shinran)](../lineages/jodo-shinshu.md)
The welcome belongs to those of various practices; the person of shinjin is settled at the moment of shinjin and need not wait for the moment of death.
**Texts:** 
  - [Lamp for the Latter Ages (Mattōshō)](../texts/mattosho.md) — ref: letter 1

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** Shinran himself assigns the welcome at death to practitioners of the nineteenth vow and settlement in life to those of the eighteenth — a distinction by the practitioner's stage.
**The traditions' own objections:** Jōdo-shū does not accept that its practitioners are of the nineteenth vow.
