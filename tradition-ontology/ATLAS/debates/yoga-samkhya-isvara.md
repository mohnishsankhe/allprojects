# Is Īśvara to be admitted, and is devotion to him a means to liberation? (Yoga against nirīśvara Sāṃkhya)

`dsp:yoga-samkhya-isvara` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Īśvara is a special puruṣa, ever free, unsurpassed in knowledge, the teacher of the ancients, designated by Oṃ; devotion to him brings samādhi near.
- The seed of omniscience admits degrees, so it has a limit where it is unsurpassed (YBh 1.25).
- Two equal lords could not both have their contrary wishes fulfilled, so lordship without equal exists (YBh 1.24).
- Won over by devotion, Īśvara favours the yogin by mere intention (YBh 1.23).
- Vijñānabhikṣu: Sāṃkhya teaches liberation from knowledge of the self alone; Yoga shows knowledge of the Supreme Lord as the swift cause of liberation (YV 1.51), and Sāṃkhya is contained in Yoga as rivers in the ocean (YV intro.).

  - [Yogavārttika](../texts/yogavarttika.md) — ref: 1.51; intro. v.4
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Liberation comes from discriminating knowledge of puruṣa and prakṛti; Īśvara is not established by any means of knowledge and is not needed to explain the working of prakṛti.
- Prakṛti acts for the puruṣa's purpose unconsciously, as milk flows for the calf (Sāṃkhya Kārikā 57).
- 'Because Īśvara is not established' (Sāṃkhya Sūtra 1.92).
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — ref: 57
  - [Sāṃkhya Sūtra](../texts/samkhya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.92; 5.2-12

**Historical debate — The Sāṃkhya–Yoga contest reported by Bhīṣma** (epic (Mahābhārata, Śāntiparvan)): Mahābhārata 12 (Mokṣadharma; c. ch. 289 CE): the Yogins argue 'how can one without a Lord be liberated?', the Sāṃkhyas praise knowledge; both are declared valid. ## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path, P5-neyartha
**Explanation:** The Gītā (5.4–5) and the Mokṣadharma treat Sāṃkhya and Yoga as two approaches to one goal (P3), and Vijñānabhikṣu reads the Sāṃkhya denial of Īśvara as a provisional, concessive argument (P5).
What is reconciled is the soteriological convergence (both end in discriminative knowledge and kaivalya); the ontological question whether a special puruṣa exists is not settled by P3.
**The traditions' own objections:** Nirīśvara Sāṃkhya authors argue on their own terms that Īśvara is unproven, not merely provisionally set aside; Vijñānabhikṣu's reading is a Yoga-Vedānta commentator's view.
