# Is the mind one abiding thing with many objects, or only momentary cognitions each fixed to one object? (YBh 1.32)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Vyāsa: the mind is one, has many objects and abides; otherwise every mind would be one-pointed, no mind distracted; memory, the enjoyer of the karma-store, and the single notion 'I' would be unaccountable.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra)](../texts/yoga-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.32
### None
The unnamed opponent in the bhāṣya: the mind is only a cognition fixed to each object (pratyartha-niyata) and momentary; the one-pointedness of a stream of like cognitions belongs to the stream or to its parts. Vyāsa does not name the opponent.
