# To which tradition does the Tirukkuṟaḷ belong — Jain, Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava, Buddhist, or none?

`dsp:affiliation-of-tirukkural` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** I2

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The Kuṟaḷ is a Jain work, its author identified by Jain tradition with Elācārya (Kundakunda).
- The ethics of non-killing and abstention from meat (chapters 26, 33) are central, above sacrifice (259).
- 'He who walked upon the flower' (3) is the Jina, who walks on golden lotuses; 'the one of eight qualities' (9) is the liberated Siddha with eight qualities; 'the ocean/wheel of virtue' (8) is the Jina of the dharma-wheel.
- No Vedic deity or ritual is endorsed; the Jain commentator of the Nīlakēci cites the Kuṟaḷ as 'our scripture' (moderate confidence).
**Texts:** 
  - [Tirukkuṟaḷ](../texts/tirukkural.md) — ref: 3; 8; 9; 259
  - [Maṇakkuṭavar's commentary on the Tirukkuṟaḷ](../texts/manakkutavar-urai.md) — ref: passim
### [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md)
The Kuṟaḷ is Śaiva: Tiruvaḷḷuvar is honoured as a Nāyaṉār; its God of eight qualities is Śiva with the eight attributes of the Āgamas; its 'two deeds' and 'feet of the Lord' are Śaiva teaching; Śaiva authors (Umāpati's Tiruvaruṭpayaṉ) follow its form.
- Parimēlaḻakar glosses eṇkuṇattāṉ with Śiva's eight Āgamic qualities.
- Kuṟaḷ 5 speaks of the two deeds (iruviṉai) as Tamil Siddhānta does.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tirukkuṟaḷ](../texts/tirukkural.md) — ref: 5; 9; 10
  - [Tiruvaruṭpayaṉ](../texts/tiruvarutpayan.md) — ref: 1.1
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
The Kuṟaḷ belongs to the Vedic-Vaiṣṇava world: it alludes to the one who measured the worlds with his feet (610) and the world of the lotus-eyed one (1103); its three books follow dharma, artha and kāma, as the Vaiṣṇava commentator Parimēlaḻakar reads it.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tirukkuṟaḷ](../texts/tirukkural.md) — ref: 610; 1103
  - [Parimēlaḻakar's commentary on the Tirukkuṟaḷ](../texts/parimelalakar-urai.md) — ref: passim
### [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md)
A Buddhist reading (notably by the Tamil Buddhist revivalist Iyothee Thass, recent) takes the Lord of the first chapter as the Buddha, walker on lotuses and turner of the dharma-wheel, and the ethics as Buddhist.
- The lineage id is approximate: the claim is modern and not tied to a specific Buddhist school.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tirukkuṟaḷ](../texts/tirukkural.md) — ref: 3; 8
### [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md)
Non-sectarian reading (much modern Tamil scholarship): the Kuṟaḷ names no sectarian god and is 'the common scripture' (potumaṟai) for all; each tradition reads its own deity into epithets the author chose to leave open.
- The first chapter's epithets are generic; no sect's rites are prescribed.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tirukkuṟaḷ](../texts/tirukkural.md) — ref: 1-10

**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: authorship is a historical question no principle settles; the ethical teachings are shared by all claimants.
Under P2 each tradition reads the unnamed Lord of chapter 1 from its own standpoint, and all accept the ethics; this does not decide the author's affiliation.
**The traditions' own objections:** Jain and Śaiva claimants each hold that the epithets have one correct referent; the non-sectarian side itself rejects any single referent.
