# Should the Goddess be worshipped in the right-hand (dakṣiṇa) mode, or is the left-hand/Kaula (vāma) mode with the five tattvas higher?

`dsp:daksina-vs-vama` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A8

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Kaula (the Kula tradition)](../lineages/kaula.md)
The seven modes rise from Veda to Kaula; Vāma is higher than Dakṣiṇa and Kaula highest of all (Kulārṇava 2.7-8); in the Kula dharma enjoyment itself becomes yoga (2.23-24); worship of the primordial Śakti without the five tattvas is fruitless 'like seed on rock' (Mahānirvāṇa 5.22-24).
- the bliss of Brahman latent in the body is made manifest in the Kula rite (Kulārṇava 5.79-80)
- the Kula scripture is Vedic in essence (Kulārṇava 2.85)
**Texts:** 
  - [Kulārṇava Tantra](../texts/kularnava-tantra.md) — ref: 2.7-8; 2.23-24; 5.79-80
  - [Mahānirvāṇa Tantra](../texts/mahanirvana-tantra.md) — ref: 5.22-24
### [Kālīkula (the Kālī lineages)](../lineages/kalikula.md)
(Kālīvilāsa) In the Kali age all classes must worship in the paśu disposition, bathing thrice daily and eating no meat; the vīra and divya dispositions do not exist in this age (Kālīvilāsa 4.2-3).
- each disposition belongs to its age (6.10-16)
- the great exemplars (Rāma, the Sanakas) were perfected in the paśu disposition
**Texts:** 
  - [Kālīvilāsa Tantra](../texts/kalivilasa-tantra.md) — ref: 4.2-6; 6.10-16
### [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md)
(Samaya Śrīvidyā, Lakṣmīdhara) The Kaula and 'mixed' modes are to be abandoned: the sixty-four tantras aim at worldly powers, deceive even their knowers and are far from the Veda; only the inner Samaya worship is Vedic.
**Texts:** 
  - [Lakṣmīdharā (commentary on the Saundaryalaharī)](../texts/laksmidhara.md) — ref: 31
  - [Saundaryalaharī](../texts/saundarya-lahari.md) — ref: 31

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The texts themselves tie the modes to the practitioner's disposition and eligibility (bhāva, adhikāra) and provide substitutes, so right- and left-hand worship are prescribed for different practitioners.
The Mahānirvāṇa itself restricts the tattvas and substitutes the sweet triad for householders in the Kali age (6.14; 8.170-171); the Kulārṇava restricts Kula knowledge to the tested and eligible (2.30-36; 14.9-14) and condemns misuse (5.96-105). This covers the Kaula–Dakṣiṇa difference as one of stage; it does not cover the Kālīvilāsa's denial that the vīra disposition exists at all in this age, nor the Samaya rejection of Kaula as non-Vedic.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Kaula texts hold Kaula to be the highest for all who are eligible, not merely an option; the Kālīvilāsa denies the vīra and divya dispositions in the Kali age; Lakṣmīdhara's Samaya school rejects the Kaula way outright.
