# Do the Vedic mantras have meaning, or are they effective sound only?

`dsp:are-mantras-meaningful` · `sourced` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vedāṅga (the six limbs of the Veda)](../lineages/vedanga.md)
Kautsa: the mantras are meaningless — their wording and order are fixed, their ritual use is fixed by the Brāhmaṇas, they state impossible or mutually inconsistent things, address insentient objects and contain unintelligible words; so the study of their meaning is useless.
- fixed wording and order
- application prescribed by the Brāhmaṇa
- inconsistencies and impossibilities
- addresses to insentient things
- obscure words
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 1.15
### [Nairukta school (the Vedic etymologists)](../lineages/nairukta.md)
Yāska: the mantras are meaningful, because their words are the same as in ordinary speech; a rite is perfect when the recited verse fits the act; fixed order is found in ordinary speech too; if the blind do not see the post, the fault is theirs; one who recites without understanding is a load-bearing post.
- shared vocabulary with ordinary speech
- the Brāhmaṇa's own principle of fit between verse and act
- the fault lies in the unlearned
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 1.16
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 1.18
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Kautsa: 'the mantras are meaningless' — their words and order are fixed, their form prescribed by the Brāhmaṇa, their sense sometimes impossible or mutually opposed (vipratiṣiddha), and they address those who already know.
- Fixed wording; ritual prescription; impossible and mutually opposed statements ('O axe, do not hurt him'; one Rudra vs countless Rudras).
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 1.15
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Yāska: the mantras are meaningful, for their words are the words of ordinary speech; fixed order and apparent opposition occur in ordinary speech too; obscurity is not the post's fault if the blind man cannot see it; one who recites without understanding is a post bearing a load.
- Identity with ordinary language; the Brāhmaṇa's own requirement that the verse describe the act.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 1.16; 1.18

**Historical debate — Kautsa's objection and Yāska's reply** (late Vedic (Nirukta)): Nirukta 1.15–16; the Mīmāṃsā Sūtra takes up similar objections (1.2.31ff., reference from memory) Yāska's view prevails in the tradition

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Kautsa speaks from the standpoint of ritual application (fixed by the Brāhmaṇas), Yāska from that of meaning; both grant the fixed ritual use.
**The traditions' own objections:** Yāska does not accept Kautsa's view as a standpoint but refutes it; the tradition (and later Mīmāṃsā) adopts the meaningfulness of mantras.

_Notes: Kautsa is known only as reported by Yāska (reported_by_opponent on tea:nirukta:1.15)._
