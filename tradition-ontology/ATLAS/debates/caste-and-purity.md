# Is purity and worth determined by birth into a class (varṇa), or by conduct?

`dsp:caste-and-purity` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
The brahmin is the highest class, born from the mouth of the cosmic Person / of Brahmā, Brahmā's heir; the classes are ordained by birth, and purity belongs to the brahmin class.
- The Puruṣasūkta derives the brāhmaṇa from the Person's mouth (RV 10.90.12).
- The Dharmaśāstra assigns duties by birth (Manusmṛti 1.31, 1.87–91).
- As stated by brahmin interlocutors in the suttas: 'only brahmins are pure, only brahmins are Brahmā's sons' (DN 27; MN 93).
**Texts:** 
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 10.90.12
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: 1.31; 1.87–91
  - [Aggañña Sutta](../texts/agganna-sutta.md) — ref: DN 27 (3)
  - [Assalāyana Sutta](../texts/assalayana-sutta.md) — ref: MN 93
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
One is a brahmin or an outcaste not by birth but by deeds; all four classes can do good and evil and all can be purified; the classes arose from occupations; the Dhamma, not birth, is best among people.
- Brahmin women are seen to menstruate and give birth (DN 27; MN 93).
- Plants and animals differ by species, humans do not; names follow occupations (MN 98).
- Fire from any wood burns alike; all can develop love and wash off dirt (MN 93).
- The true brahmin is the one who has nothing and clings to nothing (Dhp 396; Snp 1.7).
**Texts:** 
  - [Aggañña Sutta](../texts/agganna-sutta.md) — ref: DN 27
  - [Assalāyana Sutta](../texts/assalayana-sutta.md) — ref: MN 93

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Buddhist texts deny that birth confers purity, which the Brahmanical sources affirm.
**The traditions' own objections:** The suttas reject birth-status even at the conventional level (DN 27, MN 93); orthodox Dharmaśāstra holds varṇa by birth as dharma. Neither accepts a reconciliation that makes the other's claim merely a standpoint.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the Brahmanical varṇa as a ritual and social order (ethical-social standpoint) versus spiritual worth by conduct (seeker standpoint) — the Brahmanical tradition itself sometimes defines the true brahmin by conduct (e.g. the Yakṣapraśna, Vajrasūcī Upaniṣad); P1-level: birth-based duties as conventional, liberation as open to all — but the suttas also reject birth-based rank conventionally
