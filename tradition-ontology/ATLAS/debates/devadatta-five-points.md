# Should forest dwelling, alms-food, rag robes, tree-root dwelling and abstention from fish and meat be compulsory for life?

`dsp:devadatta-five-points` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
(Devadatta) These five practices conduce to few wishes, contentment, effacement and energy; they should be binding on all monks for life.
- Cv 7.3.14.
**Texts:** 
  - [Cullavagga](../texts/cullavagga.md) — ref: Cv 7.3.14
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
(The Buddha) Whoever wishes may dwell in the forest, live on alms, wear rag robes; the root of a tree is allowed for eight months; fish and meat are allowed when not seen, heard or suspected to have been killed for the monk.
- Cv 7.3.15.
**Texts:** 
  - [Cullavagga](../texts/cullavagga.md) — ref: Cv 7.3.15

**Historical debate — Devadatta's schism** (late in the Buddha's life (tradition)): Cv 7 Sāriputta and Moggallāna bring the seceding monks back.

**Status:** partially-reconciled
