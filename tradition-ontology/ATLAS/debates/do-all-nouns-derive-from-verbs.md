# Are all nouns derived from verbal roots?

`dsp:do-all-nouns-derive-from-verbs` · `sourced` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nairukta school (the Vedic etymologists)](../lineages/nairukta.md)
Yes (Śākaṭāyana and the convention of the etymologists): every noun is derived from a verb; derivation should always be attempted, by meaning if not by form.
- the principle of the Nairuktas
- never fail to derive (Nirukta 2.1)
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 1.12
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 2.1
### [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md)
Not all (Gārgya and some grammarians): only where accent and formation fit a root and the derivative is regular; words like 'cow', 'horse', 'man' are conventional, else anyone who moved would be called 'horse'.
- over-application: every doer of the act would bear the name
- irregular forms
- names precede the actions they would derive from
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 1.12

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The etymologist's derivation by meaning and the grammarian's derivation by form answer different questions; Yāska ranks formal derivation first and semantic derivation second (2.1).
**The traditions' own objections:** Gārgya's side denies that semantic derivations of conventional words are valid at all.
