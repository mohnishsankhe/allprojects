# Why did Viṣṇu appear as the Buddha (and how are the Buddhist and Jain teachings to be regarded)?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md)
To delude the enemies of the gods and lead them away from the Veda (BhP 1.3.24; VP 3.17-18 Māyāmoha; Agni 16).
- BhP 1.3.24
- VP 3.17-18
- AgP 16.1-4
**Texts:** 
  - [Viṣṇu Purāṇa](../texts/visnu-purana.md) — ref: 3.17-18
  - [Agni Purāṇa](../texts/agni-purana.md) — ref: 16.1-4
### [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md)
Later Vaiṣṇava poetry (Jayadeva's daśāvatāra hymn) praises the Buddha avatāra as compassion condemning animal sacrifice.
- Gīta Govinda 1 (U25 owns)
**Texts:** 
  - [Gīta Govinda](../texts/gita-govinda.md) — ref: 1 (daśāvatāra hymn)
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
The Buddha is the fully awakened teacher of the true dharma, not an avatāra of Viṣṇu.
- Buddhist self-understanding (U36)

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Purāṇic account is an opponent's report; the Buddhist tradition rejects the avatāra framing altogether.
**The traditions' own objections:** Buddhist and Jain traditions reject both the avatāra identification and the charge of delusion.
**Candidate readings:** P6-upāya: the Purāṇic 'delusion' as a skilful means for beings of a certain temperament (a Purāṇic reading, rejected by Buddhists).; Two Vaiṣṇava readings (deluding vs compassionate) recorded side by side.
