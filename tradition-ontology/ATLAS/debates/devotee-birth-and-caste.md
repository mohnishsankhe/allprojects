**Historical debate — Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār carried into Śrīraṅgam** (the tradition's account (c. 9th c. on the scholarly dating of the Āḻvār)): The priest who struck the pāṇar bard was commanded by the Lord to carry him into the sanctum on his shoulders (Guruparamparāprabhāvam). The Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition takes it as the Lord's own judgement against birth-pride.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level
**Explanation:** The bhakti texts themselves pair equality among devotees (the soul's belonging to the Lord) with continued conventional duty (NBS 12–14 with 72–73; Deśika), so the two claims can be read at the ultimate and conventional levels.
What is equal is the devotee's worth and eligibility for devotion and liberation; what is retained in some voices is the conventional order of rites and Vedic study. The reading does not make the Brahma Sūtra's exclusion and the Āḻvārs' 'even caṇḍālas are our masters' say the same thing.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Teṅkalai teachers hold that judging devotees by birth is an offence, not a matter of convention; the Brahma Sūtra and Dharmaśāstra tradition holds the exclusion from Vedic study to be real and binding; Vīraśaiva, Sant and Siddha critics reject caste even at the conventional level (see dsp:virasaiva-caste-and-pollution).
