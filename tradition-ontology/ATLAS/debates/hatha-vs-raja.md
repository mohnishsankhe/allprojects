# Is haṭha a necessary means to rājayoga, and has it any value apart from it?

`dsp:hatha-vs-raja` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
Haṭha and rājayoga need each other: haṭha is taught solely for rājayoga, both are practised to completion, and mere haṭha without knowing rājayoga is fruitless labour (the Haṭhapradīpikā and Śiva Saṃhitā).
- HYP 1.2, 2.76, 4.79, 4.103, 4.104
- SS 5.181
**Texts:** 
  - [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) — ref: 2.76; 4.79; 4.103
  - [Śiva Saṃhitā](../texts/siva-samhita.md) — ref: 5.181
### [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
Rājayoga needs no haṭha: the mindless state arises effortlessly by the guru's grace; breath-control, mudrās and similar methods are useless or harmful (the Amanaska, and in part the Yogatārāvalī).
- Amanaska 2 (critique of other methods; effortless amanaska)
**Texts:** 
  - [Amanaska (Amanaskayoga)](../texts/amanaska.md) — ref: ch. 2
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Rājayoga is Patañjali's yoga of cessation (asamprajñāta); haṭha, laya and mantra are auxiliary means subsumed under the eight limbs (the Jyotsnā's gloss; Nārāyaṇatīrtha).
- Jyotsnā on HYP 1.1
- Yogasiddhāntacandrikā (per U10)
**Texts:** 
  - [Jyotsnā (commentary on the Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika-jyotsna.md) — ref: 1.1

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P3-path
**Explanation:** The texts themselves assign methods by the student's fitness (SS 5.10–14) and say eight-limbed yoga and haṭha differ in practice, not fruit (DYŚ 119–120).
Under P4 haṭha is the means for the 'strong' aspirant and nāda/laya the easy means for the less able (HYP 4.65, 4.80), while the state itself (sahaja, amanaska) arises of itself once śakti is awakened (HYP 4.11) — the point the Amanaska makes. Under P3 the haṭha and eight-limbed paths share one fruit (DYŚ 119–120).
**The traditions' own objections:** The Amanaska denies that laborious methods lead to the mindless state at all; the Haṭhapradīpikā denies that haṭha is fruitful without rājayoga. The reconciliation claims only that both sides agree on the goal (the mindless state) and differ on means for different students; it does not claim the Amanaska accepts haṭha.
