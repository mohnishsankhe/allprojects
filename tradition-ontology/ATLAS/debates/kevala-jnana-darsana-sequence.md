# In the omniscient, do knowledge (jñāna) and perception (darśana) occur in sequence, simultaneously, or are they one?

`dsp:kevala-jnana-darsana-sequence` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B2 / G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
In sequence (kramavāda): following the canon, the omniscient knows in one moment and perceives in the next, since two cognitive activities cannot occur at once.
- Two upayogas cannot occur in the same instant (Jinabhadra).
**Texts:** 
  - [Viśeṣāvaśyaka Bhāṣya](../texts/visesavasyaka-bhasya.md) — 
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
Simultaneously (yugapadvāda): since the obscurations of both are destroyed together, the omniscient knows and perceives at once, as the sun gives light and heat at once.
- Kundakunda's simile of the sun's light and heat (Niyamasāra, recalled).
**Texts:** 
  - [Niyamasāra](../texts/niyamasara.md) — 
### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
They are identical (abhedavāda): in the omniscient the distinction of general grasping (perception) and particular grasping (knowledge) falls away; there is one cognition (Siddhasena Divākara).
- Distinct cognitions presuppose distinct obscurations, which no longer exist.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sanmatitarka (Sammaisutta)](../texts/sanmati-tarka.md) — ref: kāṇḍa 2

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Yaśovijaya's Jñānabindu relates the sequential, simultaneous and identity views to different standpoints (recalled; to be checked).
The later Śvetāmbara synthesis treats each view as true from a standpoint; the reconciliation is the tradition's own (Yaśovijaya), not imposed.
**The traditions' own objections:** Jinabhadra's followers held the sequential view as the literal teaching of scripture; the Digambaras hold simultaneity; neither accepts the others as equal.
