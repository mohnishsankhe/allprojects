# Is the outcome of life decided by fate (daiva) or by human effort (pauruṣa)?

`dsp:daiva-or-paurusa` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Effort is everything; fate is nothing but one's own former effort and is overcome by present effort guided by scripture and the good; reliance on fate is delusion.
- fate, unlike effort, is never perceived
- 'fate' is a name given to the fruit of one's own deeds
- yesterday's misdeed is corrected by today's good conduct
- beings became Indra, Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva by effort
**Texts:** 
  - [Mokṣopāya](../texts/moksopaya.md) — ref: 2.4-2.9
  - [Yoga Vāsiṣṭha](../texts/yoga-vasistha.md) — ref: 2.4-9
### [Ājīvika](../lineages/ajivika.md)
Everything is fixed by destiny (niyati); human effort has no power (as reported by Buddhist and Jain opponents).
- beings are purified or defiled without cause or effort, ripened by destiny (reported)
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāmaññaphala Sutta](../texts/samannaphala-sutta.md) — ref: DN 2 (Makkhali Gosāla's view)
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Both fate and effort are needed, as seed and field; effort is the field without which fate bears no fruit (the common epic and smṛti position).
- the analogy of seed and field (recalled from the Mahābhārata's discussions of daiva and puruṣakāra)
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahābhārata](../texts/mahabharata.md) — ref: Anuśāsanaparvan 13.6 (low confidence)

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The Yoga Vāsiṣṭha itself redescribes fate as past effort, so 'fate' and 'effort' name one causal process seen from past and present standpoints.
Under P2 (causal standpoint) the epic 'both' position and the Yoga Vāsiṣṭha's 'effort only' position differ in wording more than in substance; the Ājīvika determinism, known only from opponents' reports, is not reconciled.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Ājīvika view as reported denies any efficacy to effort; the Yoga Vāsiṣṭha rejects the independent existence of fate.
