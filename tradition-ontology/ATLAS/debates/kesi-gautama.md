### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
Gautama's answer (for Mahāvīra's line): the law is stated according to the disciples of each era — simple and slow, simple and wise, crooked and slow; the outward sign is for recognition in the world; the true means are knowledge, faith and conduct.
- Disciples' dispositions differ by era
- Outward marks are conventional
**Texts:** 
  - [Uttarādhyayana Sūtra](../texts/uttaradhyayana-sutra.md) — ref: 23

**Historical debate — Meeting of Keśin and Gautama at Śrāvastī** (Mahāvīra's time (tradition)): Uttarādhyayana 23. Keśin and his followers accepted the fivefold law.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The text itself reconciles the two rules by the capacity of the disciples of each era (adhikāra): same goal, rules adapted to the hearers.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Digambaras do not accept the Uttarādhyayana and hold that the monks of every Tīrthaṅkara went naked; the reconciliation is the Śvetāmbara canon's own.
