# What does a word mean: a real universal, or the exclusion of others (apoha)?

`dsp:apoha` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
Mature form (Śāntarakṣita, Ratnakīrti): the word-meaning is neither bare negation nor a real universal but a positive (conceptual) object qualified by the exclusion of others; exclusion is grounded in the particulars' capacity for similar effects.
- Apohasiddhi; Tattvasaṃgraha, Śabdārthaparīkṣā
**Texts:** 
  - [Apohasiddhi (Ratnakīrti)](../texts/apohasiddhi.md) — 
  - [Tattvasaṅgraha of Śāntarakṣita](../texts/tattvasangraha.md) — ref: Śabdārthaparīkṣā
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Exclusion cannot be the meaning: to exclude 'non-cow' one must already know 'cow'; the word 'cow' makes one understand a positive cow, not a negation (Uddyotakara, as recalled).
- Nyāyavārttika (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāyavārttika](../texts/nyaya-varttika.md) — ref: 2.2 (recalled)
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
A real universal (ākṛti) — MS 1.3.33; Kumārila's apohavāda rejects exclusion: an exclusion presupposes a positive grasp of what is excluded.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mīmāṃsā Sūtra](../texts/mimamsa-sutra.md) — ref: 1.3.33
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: apohavāda
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
Words signify by excluding what is other (anyāpoha); there are no real universals (Dignāga, Dharmakīrti).
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇasamuccaya](../texts/pramanasamuccaya.md) — ref: ch. 5
