# Is this world a 'frame of illusion' to be seen through, or a 'mansion of mirth' to be enjoyed in the Lord?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Bengali and Assamese Śākta tantra](../lineages/bengal-assam-sakta.md)
(Rāmprasād) 'This world is a frame of illusion' — the mind should not be deceived by it but turn to the Mother.
**Texts:** 
  - [Rāmprasādī songs (Rāmprasād's padāvalī)](../texts/ramprasadi-songs.md) — ref: Ei saṃsār dhokār ṭāṭi
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
(Āju Gosāi, a Vaiṣṇava contemporary, in the tradition's account) 'This world is a mansion of mirth' (majār kuṭi), where one eats, drinks and makes merry with the Lord.

**Historical debate — Rāmprasād and Āju Gosāi's song-exchange** (18th c.): Bengali oral tradition; later cited by Ramakrishna (U52). Remembered as a friendly contest of songs.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The same world is a trap to the one still bound and a mansion of mirth to the one who has realised the Lord (Mother) in it — the reading given in the Ramakrishna tradition.
Recorded as the later tradition's reconciliation (U52 to anchor in the Kathāmṛta); both songs are quoted there.
**The traditions' own objections:** None recorded; the exchange is remembered as good-humoured.
