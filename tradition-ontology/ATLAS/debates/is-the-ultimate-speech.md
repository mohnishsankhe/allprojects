# Is the ultimate of the nature of speech (śabdādvaita), and is all cognition word-permeated?

`dsp:is-the-ultimate-speech` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md)
Yes: Brahman is the Word-principle appearing as the world; no cognition is free of the word; speech is the consciousness of beings.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vākyapadīya](../texts/vakyapadiya.md) — ref: 1.1; 1.123-124
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
No: an eternal single Word-brahman cannot produce successive, various things; if the world is its transformation it is non-eternal, if its appearance it is unreal; perception is free of words and conceptual construction.
- the non-conceptual character of perception
- the momentariness of all that is produced
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvasaṅgraha of Śāntarakṣita](../texts/tattvasangraha.md) — ref: Śabdabrahma-parīkṣā
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
No: Prabhācandra refutes Word-monism among the doctrines opposed to the Jain view of many real substances and conceptual and non-conceptual cognition.
**Texts:** 
  - [Prameyakamalamārtaṇḍa](../texts/prameyakamalamartanda.md) — ref: (śabdādvaita section; not fixed)
### [Mantraśāstra (the science of mantras: the smārta-tantric mantra manuals)](../lineages/mantrasastra.md)
Śabda-brahman is not the word nor the meaning, both insentient, but the consciousness of all beings — speech is consciousness, not consciousness speech.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śāradātilaka](../texts/saradatilaka.md) — ref: 1.12-13
### [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md)
Supreme speech is the reflexive awareness of consciousness, not the grammarians' paśyantī.
**Texts:** 
  - [Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā](../texts/isvarapratyabhijna-karika.md) — ref: 1.5.13

**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: whether reality is Word, and whether cognition is word-permeated.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Buddhists deny any permanent, single ultimate; the Jains deny monism; the Śaivas deny that paśyantī is final.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the grammarians, the mantra manuals and the Pratyabhijñā may all describe the reflexive, 'speaking' side of consciousness, differing on whether it is the whole of the ultimate.; P1-level: word-permeation may hold of conceptual (vyāvahārika) cognition, which the Buddhists also treat as word-bound, while the Buddhists deny it of perception.
