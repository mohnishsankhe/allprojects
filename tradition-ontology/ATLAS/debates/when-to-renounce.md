# May one renounce directly, at any stage of life, or only after passing through the householder's duties?

`dsp:when-to-renounce` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Saṃnyāsa — the renunciate ideal of the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads](../lineages/sannyasa.md)
Renounce on the very day dispassion arises, from studentship, household or forest; true renunciation is inner union of jīva and Self.
- Jābāla 4: 'yad ahar eva virajet tad ahar eva pravrajet'
- Nāradaparivrājaka 3: renunciation without dispassion leads to a fall; the self-controlled may renounce unmarried
- Maitreya 2.17: renunciation is not abandoning rites but union with the Self
**Texts:** 
  - [Jābāla Upaniṣad](../texts/jabala-upanisad.md) — ref: 4
  - [Nāradaparivrājaka Upaniṣad](../texts/naradaparivrajaka-upanisad.md) — ref: 3.12-15
  - [Maitreya Upaniṣad](../texts/maitreya-upanisad.md) — ref: 2.17
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Renunciation comes in due order, after the three debts (study, offspring, sacrifice) are paid; seeking liberation before paying them leads downward.
- Manusmṛti 6.35–37 on the three debts
- the Kuṇḍikā and Kaṭharudra themselves describe renunciation after studentship, marriage and sacrifices
**Texts:** 
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: 6.35-37
  - [Kuṇḍikā Upaniṣad](../texts/kundika-upanisad.md) — ref: 1-8
  - [Kaṭharudra Upaniṣad](../texts/katharudra-upanisad.md) — ref: 1

**Historical debate — Brahmasūtra 3.4.18–20: are the other stages of life enjoined?** (): The sūtras report Jaimini's view that scripture only mentions the other stages without enjoining them and Bādarāyaṇa's that they are to be practised; Śaṅkara, commenting, cites the Jābāla's rule on renouncing directly. Advaita: renunciation is enjoined and may be taken directly.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P1-level
**Explanation:** The texts tie the timing to the renouncer's qualification: in due order for most, at once for one of intense dispassion; and the Maitreya distinguishes outer ritual renunciation from inner renunciation.
The Nāradaparivrājaka itself says that renouncing without dispassion causes a fall, which grounds the adhikāra reading (P4); the Maitreya's definition places true renunciation on the level of knowledge (P1).
**The traditions' own objections:** Ritualists (Mīmāṃsā) hold that lifelong Vedic duties cannot be set aside; the Jābāla's partisans hold that dispassion alone decides, whatever one's stage.
