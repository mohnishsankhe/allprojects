# Is there another world, spontaneously born beings, and fruit of good and bad deeds?

`dsp:is-there-another-world` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](../lineages/carvaka.md)
(Pāyāsi, and Ajita Kesakambalī as reported) There is no other world, no spontaneously born beings, no fruit of deeds; experiments with dying criminals show nothing leaving the body.
- As reported in DN 23 and DN 2.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pāyāsi Sutta](../texts/payasi-sutta.md) — ref: DN 23 (2–29)
  - [Sāmaññaphala Sutta](../texts/samannaphala-sutta.md) — ref: DN 2 (22–24)
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
(Kumārakassapa) The other world cannot be seen with the fleshly eye; the dead cannot return to tell; similes of the dream, the blind man, the conch-blower show the flaws in the 'experiments'.
- DN 23 similes.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pāyāsi Sutta](../texts/payasi-sutta.md) — ref: DN 23

**Historical debate — Kumārakassapa and Pāyāsi at Setavyā** (after the parinibbāna (DN 23)): DN 23 (Buddhist account) Pāyāsi goes for refuge; after death he is reborn as a minor god.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: materialism and the doctrine of rebirth are opposed.
**The traditions' own objections:** Each side denies the other's premises.
