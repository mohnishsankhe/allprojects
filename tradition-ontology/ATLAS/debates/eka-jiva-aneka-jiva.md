# Is there one jīva or are there many?

`dsp:eka-jiva-aneka-jiva` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A6

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Eka-jīva-vāda (Prakāśānanda; surveyed by Madhusūdana): there is one jīva; other beings are its dream-like projections; with its knowledge all is liberated.
- Ignorance is one; plurality is an appearance within it, like dream figures.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vedāntasiddhāntamuktāvalī](../texts/vedanta-siddhanta-muktavali.md) — ref: 1
  - [Siddhāntabindu](../texts/siddhantabindu.md) — ref: 1
### [Bhāmatī school (Bhāmatī-prasthāna)](../lineages/bhamati.md)
Aneka-jīva-vāda: jīvas are many, each with its own ignorance or inner organ, so one is liberated while others remain bound.
- Scripture and experience show some liberated (Vāmadeva) while others are bound.
- Pot-spaces are not all soiled when one is (GK 3.5).
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhāmatī](../texts/bhamati.md) — ref: locus
  - [Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (Gauḍapāda Kārikā, Āgamaśāstra)](../texts/mandukya-karika.md) — ref: 3.5

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level, P6-upaya
**Explanation:** Both are empirical-level accounts; at the ultimate level neither one nor many jīvas exist (GK 3.48); the tradition lists both among the methods.
Grounded in Sureśvara's principle that any method leading to knowledge of the inner self is valid (BĀU-Vārttika 1.4.402) and Appayya's statement that the ancients, intent on the oneness of the self, were indifferent to how the empirical is explained (SLS opening).
**The traditions' own objections:** Proponents of many jīvas regard the one-jīva view as unable to explain scripture's accounts of others' liberation; the one-jīva side regards plurality as unexplained without it.
