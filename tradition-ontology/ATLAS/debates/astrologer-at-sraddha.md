# Is the astrologer to be honoured or excluded at a śrāddha?

`dsp:astrologer-at-sraddha` · `skeleton` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Manu lists 'one who lives by the asterisms' among those to be kept away from a śrāddha.
- living by the stars is a trade unfit for a brahmin invited to feed the ancestors
**Texts:** 
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: 3.162
### [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md)
Varāhamihira: the twice-born who knows this whole science in text and meaning should eat first at a śrāddha, honoured as a purifier of the row; the reader of it does not go to hell.
- knowledge of the whole science purifies
**Texts:** 
  - [Bṛhat Saṃhitā](../texts/brhat-samhita.md) — ref: 2.11-13

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P7-arthavada, P4-stage
**Explanation:** Manu excludes one who makes a living by the stars; Varāhamihira praises the brahmin learned in the whole science — his praise reads as arthavāda encouraging study, and the two refer to different persons (tradesman vs learned brahmin).
The wording supports the distinction: Manu's 'nakṣatrair yaś ca jīvati' (lives by) against BS 'granthataś cārthataś ca … kṛtsnaṃ jānāti' (knows wholly).
**The traditions' own objections:** The jyotiṣa authors would not accept that their statements are mere praise; strict smṛti readers may exclude any professional astrologer.
