# Should the liberated renouncer act for the world's good — teach, take disciples, follow scriptural conduct?

`dsp:lokasangraha-for-the-renouncer` · `sourced` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Saṃnyāsa — the renunciate ideal of the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads](../lineages/sannyasa.md)
No: he undertakes nothing for the world's welfare, takes no disciples, keeps no books, gives no lectures; gathering disciples binds.
- Nāradaparivrājaka 5.19–22
- Sannyāsa 2.79–85
**Texts:** 
  - [Nāradaparivrājaka Upaniṣad](../texts/naradaparivrajaka-upanisad.md) — ref: 5.19-22
  - [Sannyāsa Upaniṣad](../texts/sannyasa-upanisad.md) — ref: 2.79-85
### [Saṃnyāsa — the renunciate ideal of the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads](../lineages/sannyasa.md)
Yes, freely: though his work is done, he may keep to the scriptural path out of a wish to help the world — it does him no harm.
- Avadhūta 23
**Texts:** 
  - [Avadhūta Upaniṣad](../texts/avadhuta-upanisad.md) — ref: 23

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The prohibitions bind the renouncer on the way; the Avadhūta speaks of the one whose work is done, and the Sannyāsa's rule itself exempts disciples taken out of compassion.
Sannyāsa 2.79–85 names gathering disciples for service, gain, worship or fame — not out of compassion — as binding.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Nāradaparivrājaka states the prohibition without such an exception.

_Notes: Compare the Bhagavad Gītā's teaching on acting for the world's welfare (src:bhagavad-gita 3.20–25)._
