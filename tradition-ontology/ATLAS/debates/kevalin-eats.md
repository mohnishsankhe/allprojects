# Does the omniscient (kevalin) take food?

`dsp:kevalin-eats` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Coverage:** B2 / G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
Yes: while his gross (audārika) body lasts, the omniscient takes food and bears hunger, thirst and the other hardships that arise from feeling-producing karma.
- Tattvārthasūtra 9.11 says eleven hardships occur in the Jina; they are the ones due to feeling-karma (9.16), which persists until the last stages
- The canon narrates Mahāvīra's illness and his recovery on food fetched from Revatī (Bhagavatī 15)
- Eating without desire does not diminish omniscience, as walking and preaching do not
- The Śvetāmbara list of eighteen faults absent in the Jina does not include hunger and thirst
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvārthādhigamabhāṣya](../texts/tattvartha-bhasya.md) — ref: 9.11
  - [Bhagavatī Sūtra (Vyākhyāprajñapti)](../texts/bhagavati-sutra.md) — ref: 15
  - [Samavāyāṅga Sūtra](../texts/samavayanga.md) — ref: 34
### [Yāpanīya](../lineages/yapaniya.md)
Yes, with systematic arguments (Śākaṭāyana's Kevalibhuktiprakaraṇa).
- The body requires nourishment as long as the life-span and feeling karmas operate
- Eating is not a sign of desire in one without delusion
- Scripture speaks of the Jina eating
**Texts:** 
  - [Kevalibhuktiprakaraṇa of Śākaṭāyana](../texts/kevalibhukti-prakarana.md) — ref: whole
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
No: the omniscient is free of hunger and thirst, has no bodily pleasure or pain, and his body is sustained without morsel-eating.
- Hunger and thirst are among the eighteen faults absent in the reliable teacher (Niyamasāra 6)
- Without deluding karma, feeling-karma is powerless to produce hunger; 'eleven in the Jina' is figurative (Sarvārthasiddhi 9.11)
- Hunger is a form of suffering incompatible with infinite bliss
- The omniscient has passed beyond the senses (Pravacanasāra 1.20)
- His body is maintained by subtle matter (nokarma), not by morsels (Prabhācandra)
**Texts:** 
  - [Niyamasāra](../texts/niyamasara.md) — ref: 6
  - [Sarvārthasiddhi](../texts/sarvarthasiddhi.md) — ref: 9.11
  - [Pravacanasāra](../texts/pravacanasara.md) — ref: 1.20
  - [Prameyakamalamārtaṇḍa](../texts/prameyakamalamartanda.md) — ref: kevalibhukti
### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
Yes: the embodied omniscient keeps the feeling (vedanīya) karma and a gross body that is sustained by food; scripture narrates Mahāvīra's eating and illness; the Tattvārthasūtra says eleven hardships occur in the Jina.
- TS 9.11 read literally: hunger, thirst and the other feeling-caused hardships occur in the Jina.
- Feeling karma, once bound, must ripen; the omniscient's gross body needs nourishment like any body.
- Yaśovijaya defends this against the Digambara-leaning Adhyātmīs.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvārthasūtra](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md) — ref: 9.11
  - [Adhyātmamataparīkṣā](../texts/adhyatmamatapariksa.md) — 
### [Yāpanīya](../lineages/yapaniya.md)
Yes: the Yāpanīya grammarian Śākaṭāyana's Kevalibhuktiprakaraṇa argues that eating does not contradict omniscience.
- Eating arises from feeling karma, not from delusion, so it is compatible with the passionless state (as usually reported).
**Texts:** 
  - [Kevalibhuktiprakaraṇa of Śākaṭāyana](../texts/kevalibhukti-prakarana.md) — 
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
No: eating presupposes desire and the working of delusion, which the omniscient has destroyed; feeling karma without delusion is powerless, like a seed without water; the omniscient's body is sustained without eating; TS 9.11 is figurative.
- Sarvārthasiddhi on 9.11: the eleven hardships are spoken of only nominally in the Jina.
- Prabhācandra's Prameyakamalamārtaṇḍa and Nyāyakumudacandra refute the Śvetāmbara-Yāpanīya arguments at length.
