# After understanding the mahāvākya, is repeated meditative contemplation (prasaṅkhyāna) required for liberating knowledge?

`dsp:prasankhyana` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A6

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Maṇḍana Miśra: yes — verbal knowledge is indirect and does not remove deep impressions; repeated contemplation makes it direct.
- Even after hearing, one still experiences difference.
- Scripture enjoins contemplation (nididhyāsitavyaḥ).
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasiddhi](../texts/brahmasiddhi.md) — ref: 4
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Śaṅkara (US 18) and Sureśvara: no — for the qualified hearer the understood sentence itself gives direct knowledge; contemplation serves only to remove obstacles where understanding is incomplete.
- The self is always immediate; knowledge of it cannot be made 'more direct' by an act.
- Repetition is for one who has not understood (BSBh 4.1.2).
**Texts:** 
  - [Upadeśasāhasrī](../texts/upadesa-sahasri.md) — ref: padya.18
  - [Naiṣkarmyasiddhi](../texts/naiskarmyasiddhi.md) — ref: 3
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 4.1.2

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** Śaṅkara's own gloss (BSBh 4.1.2) makes repetition needed for those who do not understand at once and useless for those who do — a stage-relative reading.
Maṇḍana makes contemplation necessary for all; Śaṅkara only for the less-prepared. The reconciliation covers practice, not Maṇḍana's claim that verbal knowledge is intrinsically indirect.
**The traditions' own objections:** Maṇḍana (and the Bhāmatī) hold that verbal knowledge is by nature mediate for everyone; Śaṅkara's school holds that it is by nature immediate when the object is the self.
