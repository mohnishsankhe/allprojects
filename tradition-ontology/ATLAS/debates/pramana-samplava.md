# Can one object be known by more than one means of knowledge?

`dsp:pramana-samplava` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Yes (saṃplava): fire is known from testimony, inferred from smoke and perceived when near; some objects are restricted (heaven to scripture), and knowledge culminates in perception.
- NBh 1.1.3.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāyabhāṣya](../texts/nyaya-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.3
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
No (vyavasthā): perception grasps only unique particulars and inference only universals; the two means have distinct objects.
- Dignāga (as usually reported).
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇasamuccaya](../texts/pramanasamuccaya.md) — ref: ch. 1 (from memory)
