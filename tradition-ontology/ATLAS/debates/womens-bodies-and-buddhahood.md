# Can a woman, in a woman's body, attain buddhahood?

`dsp:womens-bodies-and-buddhahood` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md)
Womanhood is empty like all dharmas: dharmas are neither male nor female (the goddess in Vimalakīrti); Queen Śrīmālā teaches the one vehicle and is predicted to buddhahood; the goddess of the Ganges is predicted; the nāga princess attains buddhahood in an instant.
- Vimalakīrti 6.14-16.
- Śrīmālā ch. 1.
- Aṣṭasāhasrikā ch. 19.
- Lotus ch. 12.

### [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md)
A woman's body cannot be a buddha: Śāriputra objects so in the Lotus, and the nāga princess becomes male before appearing as a buddha; Amitābha's and Bhaiṣajyaguru's vows promise women release from the woman's state; the Pali Bahudhātuka Sutta (MN 115) says a woman cannot be a fully awakened buddha.
- Lotus 12 (Śāriputra's objection; the transformation).
- Bhaiṣajyaguru's vows.
- MN 115 (U36).

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level
**Explanation:** The Vimalakīrti itself places the question on two levels: ultimately dharmas are neither male nor female; conventionally bodies and their karmic conditions are spoken of.
Under P1 the goddess's teaching (ultimate) and the sex-change narratives and vows (conventional) do not exclude each other; whether the conventional condition binds final buddhahood is not settled by P1.
**The traditions' own objections:** Traditions that hold the thirty-two marks (including the male mark) to be required of a buddha keep the conventional restriction as binding.
