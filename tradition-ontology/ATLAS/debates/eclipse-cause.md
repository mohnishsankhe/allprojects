# Is an eclipse caused by Rāhu or by shadow?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
The asura's immortal head, cut off by Viṣṇu's discus at the churning, swallows the Sun and Moon in lasting enmity; the Ṛgveda tells of Svarbhānu piercing the sun with darkness.
- the account of the churning of the ocean
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahābhārata](../texts/mahabharata.md) — ref: 1.17
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 5.40.5-9
### [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md)
The siddhāntas: the moon covers the sun and the earth's shadow covers the moon; Varāhamihira argues against every form of the Rāhu account (head, serpent, darkness, two Rāhus) and declares 'Rāhu is not the cause here — this is the truth of the śāstra'.
- eclipses occur only half a circle apart and are computable
- the shadow lies opposite the sun
- a solar eclipse differs by place
**Texts:** 
  - [Āryabhaṭīya](../texts/aryabhatiya.md) — ref: 4.37
  - [Bṛhat Saṃhitā](../texts/brhat-samhita.md) — ref: 5.1-13

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The tradition reconciles it itself: the shadow is the cause (cosmic standpoint), while Rāhu, by Brahmā's boon, is present at the eclipse and receives its offerings, so the rites stand and his name is used figuratively (ritual standpoint).
BS 5.14–15: Brahmā granted the asura Rāhu that he would be nourished by the gifts and oblations offered at eclipses; hence he is 'spoken of' as the eclipser, and the node in computation bears his name.
**The traditions' own objections:** A literal Purāṇic reading would keep Rāhu as the physical eclipser; later authors (Brahmagupta is reported to have defended the smṛti view on social grounds) — report unverified here.
