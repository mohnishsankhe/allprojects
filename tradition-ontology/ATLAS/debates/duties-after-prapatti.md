# Must the surrendered keep the scriptural duties, and are deliberate offences after surrender to be expiated?

`dsp:duties-after-prapatti` · `skeleton` · confidence low

**Coverage:** A6; G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vaṭakalai (northern school of Śrīvaiṣṇavism)](../lineages/vadakalai.md)
Yes: duties are the Lord's command (ājñā); neglecting them offends him; deliberate offences require expiation or repeated surrender as expiation.
- śruti–smṛti are the Lord's commands
**Texts:** 
  - [Rahasyatrayasāra](../texts/rahasyatrayasara.md) — ref: Uttarakṛtya; Aparādhaparihāra
### [Teṅkalai (southern school of Śrīvaiṣṇavism)](../lineages/tenkalai.md)
Duties are kept as service (kainkarya) and for the example of the world, not as means nor under threat; the Lord's grace covers offences, though offences against devotees are gravest.
- pāratantrya
- doṣa-bhogya
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrīvacanabhūṣaṇam](../texts/srivacana-bhusanam.md) — ref: 3

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Both schools require the surrendered to keep scriptural conduct; they differ on its motive and on expiation — the ethical-social practice converges.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Vaṭakalai hold the 'command' character essential; the Teṅkalai hold expiation superfluous for one whose means is the Lord.
