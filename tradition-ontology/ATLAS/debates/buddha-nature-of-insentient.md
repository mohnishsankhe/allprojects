# Do insentient things (walls, tiles, grasses) have buddha-nature or preach the Dharma?

`dsp:buddha-nature-of-insentient` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Chan (Chinese Meditation school)](../lineages/chan.md)
Nanyang Huizhong: the buddha-mind is walls, tiles and pebbles; the insentient preach the Dharma, heard when free of the feelings of consciousness.
**Texts:** 
  - [Record of the Transmission of the Lamp compiled in the Jingde era (Jingde chuandeng lu)](../texts/jingde-chuandeng-lu.md) — ref: juan 28; juan 5
### [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md)
(Objection voiced by a monk, citing the Nirvāṇa-sūtra) Buddha-nature is what is apart from walls and insentient things; buddha-nature is permanent, mind impermanent.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra](../texts/mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana.md) — ref: as cited in Jingde chuandeng lu juan 28
### [Sōtō Zen](../lineages/soto.md)
Dōgen: whole being is buddha-nature; impermanence is buddha-nature.
**Texts:** 
  - [Busshō (Buddha-Nature)](../texts/bussho.md) — 

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level, P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Huizhong's own answer: 'deluded, they differ; awakened, they do not' — like ice and water — distinguishes the standpoint of delusion from that of awakening.
**The traditions' own objections:** Scholastic readers of the Nirvāṇa-sūtra keep the distinction between sentient buddha-nature and insentient things; Tiantai (Zhanran) argued the insentient have buddha-nature on other grounds (U54).
