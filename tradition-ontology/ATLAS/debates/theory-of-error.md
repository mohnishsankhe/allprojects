# What happens in erroneous cognition (khyāti)?

`dsp:theory-of-error` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Misplaced apprehension (anyathākhyāti): a real property located elsewhere is apprehended as qualifying the thing before one (silver in mother-of-pearl).
- Tarkasaṅgraha's definition of invalid experience; Gaṅgeśa.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tarkasaṅgraha](../texts/tarkasangraha.md) — ref: buddhi
  - [Tattvacintāmaṇi](../texts/tattvacintamani.md) — ref: Pratyakṣa-khaṇḍa
### [Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/prabhakara-mimamsa.md)
Non-apprehension (akhyāti): two cognitions — a perception and a memory — are not distinguished; no cognition is strictly erroneous.
- As usually reported.
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Apprehension of the indeterminable (anirvacanīyakhyāti): the silver is neither real nor unreal but an appearance produced by ignorance.
- As usually reported.
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
Self-apprehension (ātmakhyāti): cognition's own form is taken as external.
- As usually reported.

**The traditions' own objections:** Each school's theory of error encodes its ontology; Nyāya rejects the indeterminable and non-apprehension accounts explicitly.
**Candidate readings:** P1-level: Advaita's account presupposes levels of reality that Nyāya does not recognize; within the empirical level the accounts overlap in treating error as a presentation of what is not there.
