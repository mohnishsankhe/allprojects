# Are the rules of ritual purity and conventional conduct binding on the knower?

`dsp:purity-impurity-kaula` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Purity and impurity, and the rules of varṇa and āśrama, are part of dharma and binding.
**Texts:** 
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: ch. 5 (purity)
### [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md)
The purity prescribed by those of little knowledge is impurity in Śiva's view; nothing is pure or impure to the thought-free (VBT 123); rules based on difference are not a direct means to Śiva, though the samaya is to be kept by those who need it (TĀ 4.258-270); the knower eats whatever comes and is untouched by merit and sin (Paramārthasāra 69-71).
**Texts:** 
  - [Vijñānabhairava](../texts/vijnana-bhairava-tantra.md) — ref: 123
  - [Tantrāloka](../texts/tantraloka.md) — ref: 4
  - [Paramārthasāra](../texts/paramarthasara.md) — ref: 69-71

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P1-level
**Explanation:** The Trika itself keeps rules as valid for those in the state of difference and declares them non-binding only for the knower.
Under P4 the orthodox rules address the bound seeker; under P1 the Trika's denial of purity speaks from the ultimate level. This reading follows TĀ 4.261 (the samaya is to be preserved) and VBT 123.
**The traditions' own objections:** Dharmaśāstra does not accept exemption on the basis of Kaula realization; the Trika holds that the half-knowing's purity is itself impurity - the reconciliation does not erase this judgement.
