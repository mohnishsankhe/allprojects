# Is the world false (mithyā), and can the Advaita definitions of falsity withstand Vyāsatīrtha's critique? (The Nyāyāmṛta–Advaitasiddhi controversy)

`dsp:nyayamrta-advaitasiddhi` · `skeleton` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
The world is real. None of the five Advaita definitions of falsity is coherent; the inferences from 'perceivable', 'insentient' and 'limited' fail; perception, scripture and above all the witness (sākṣin) establish the world's reality, and what the witness establishes is never sublated. The Upaniṣads do not teach an impartite meaning; the flawless Brahman is absolutely different from the flawed souls; liberation is not mere cessation of ignorance, and the liberated are graded.
- the subject 'the world in dispute' and the qualifier 'indeterminable' are unestablished
- 'perceivable' would make the self false too, or prove what both accept
- the witness perceives time and the unsublatedness of what it grasps
- the scriptures teach difference ('atat tvam asi'); their great purport is Viṣṇu's supremacy
- meditation-born realization pleases the Lord; grace liberates

### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The world is mithyā: not ultimately real, since it is sublated by the knowledge of Brahman, yet not unreal like a hare's horn, since it appears and functions; Madhusūdana defends each of the five definitions of falsity point by point. Perception shows only empirical existence and does not contradict the Upaniṣadic teaching of non-duality, whose purport is the one Brahman; difference belongs to the empirical order; liberation is the cessation of ignorance by knowledge of identity.
- falsity as 'not the locus of either being or non-being', as 'the counterpositive of a negation in all three times in its own substrate', and the other definitions are defended against Vyāsatīrtha
- the empirical reality perception grants is compatible with ultimate sublation
- the Upaniṣads' purport, known by the six marks, is non-duality
- the witness is pure consciousness and does not certify the ultimate reality of objects

**Historical debate — The Nyāyāmṛta–Advaitasiddhi exchange** (16th–18th c. CE (Nyāyāmṛta early 16th c.; Advaitasiddhi 16th c.; Taraṅgiṇī and Laghucandrikā later)): A written controversy: Vyāsatīrtha's Nyāyāmṛta; Madhusūdana Sarasvatī's point-by-point reply, the Advaitasiddhi; Vyāsa Rāmācārya's Nyāyāmṛta-taraṅgiṇī defending Vyāsatīrtha; Brahmānanda Sarasvatī's Laghucandrikā defending Madhusūdana; further replies on both sides (not all identified in this pass). Each tradition regards its own side as having prevailed; the Mādhva tradition reveres the Nyāyāmṛta and its commentaries, the Advaita tradition the Advaitasiddhi and Laghucandrikā.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: both sides reject the level, standpoint and stage readings.

**The traditions' own objections:** Dvaita rejects all three: difference is 'real in every state, including liberation' (MBTN 1.68–71), the witness shows the world to be unsublated at all times (Anuvyākhyāna 1.4.99–103), and Advaita's two levels of reality are themselves what the Nyāyāmṛta refutes; to call difference 'empirical' is to deny it. Advaita rejects P2 and P4 as reconciliations too: non-duality is not one standpoint among others but the sole reality, and Dvaita's teachings of eternal difference are, for it, errors to be sublated.
