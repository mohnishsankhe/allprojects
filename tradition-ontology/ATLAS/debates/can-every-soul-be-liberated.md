# Can every soul eventually be liberated, or are some souls by nature never to be liberated?

`dsp:can-every-soul-be-liberated` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
Souls are of three innate classes: those fit for liberation, those who transmigrate forever, and those fit for eternal blinding darkness; this gradation is beginningless and unalterable, and liberation and darkness are both final.
- the classification is stated in doctrinal treatises (Tattvasaṅkhyāna, Tattvaviveka) and in MBTN 1
- fitness is intrinsic; no means can alter it (MBTN 1.95)
- scripture speaks of eternal darkness for the enemies of Hari
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvasaṅkhyāna](../texts/tattvasankhyana.md) — ref: 3-7
  - [Tattvaviveka](../texts/tattvaviveka.md) — ref: 4-7
  - [Mahābhārata-tātparya-nirṇaya](../texts/mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya.md) — ref: 1.86-95
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Every soul is in truth Brahman; bondage is ignorance, and any soul that gains the knowledge of identity is liberated; no self is by nature excluded from that knowledge.
- the self of all is one Brahman
- liberation is the removal of ignorance, open to all who are qualified
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: passim
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
All bound souls are capable of liberation through the Lord's grace, won by devotion or surrender; the tradition knows no class of souls eternally excluded from it.
- the Lord's grace is universal in scope
- surrender (prapatti) is open to all
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: passim
  - [Rahasyatrayasāra](../texts/rahasyatrayasara.md) — ref: passim

**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled.

**The traditions' own objections:** Dvaita rejects both readings: fitness is 'beginningless and endless, and no one can alter it' (MBTN 1.95), and the classification appears in treatises of classification, not in praise or blame. The other schools reject Dvaita's premise of intrinsically unliberable souls.

_Notes: An analogous Jain doctrine (souls 'incapable of liberation', abhavya) is noted as a term equivalence, not as a side. The Advaita and Viśiṣṭādvaita positions are summarized from general knowledge._
