# Is the Rām of the Sants the avatāra, Rāma son of Daśaratha?

`dsp:kabir-ram-and-the-avatara` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md)
No: 'the son of Daśaratha is praised in the three worlds, but the secret of Rām's name is other'; Rām is the unborn Lord in every body.
- Rām pervades every body (musk in the deer)
- what is born and dies is not the Lord
**Texts:** 
  - [The oral and popular corpus of Kabīr (sākhīs, dohās, padas and bhajans)](../texts/kabir-oral-corpus.md) — ref: 'dasrath sut tihuṃ lok bakhānā'
### [Sant Mat (Radhasoami and its successor lines)](../lineages/sant-mat.md)
The avatāras come from the regions of Kāl to keep order; the Saints come from the highest region; the Saints are above the avatāras.
- the avatāras do not take souls beyond the three worlds
**Texts:** 
  - [The Path of the Masters](../texts/path-of-the-masters.md) — 
### [Kabīr Panth](../lineages/kabir-panth.md)
Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheś are Kāl's sons; Kabīr, not the avatāras, frees souls.
**Texts:** 
  - [Anurāg Sāgar](../texts/anurag-sagar.md) — 
### [Rāmānandī sampradāya](../lineages/ramanandi.md)
Yes: the one whom the Vedas sing and sages meditate on is the son of Daśaratha, lord of Kosala, for the sake of his devotees; there is no difference between saguṇa and nirguṇa.
- the formless takes form out of love for devotees
**Texts:** 
  - [Rāmcaritmānas](../texts/ramcaritmanas.md) — ref: Bālakāṇḍa 116–118 (from memory)

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path
**Explanation:** Under P3-path the same Rām is approached through the Name within (Sants) and through the avatāra's form (Rāmānandīs); Tulsīdās's own 'no difference between saguṇa and nirguṇa' supports this from the saguṇa side.
The reconciliation claims only that both paths address one Lord; it does not claim the Sants accept the avatāra as supreme.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Kabīr Panth and Sant Mat reject equality: for them the avatāras belong to Kāl's domain. The Rāmānandīs reject any view that makes Rāma less than the supreme Brahman.
