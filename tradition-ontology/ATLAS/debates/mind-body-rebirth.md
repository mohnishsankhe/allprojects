# Does consciousness arise from the body alone, so that death is the end?

`dsp:mind-body-rebirth` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](../lineages/carvaka.md)
Yes: consciousness arises from the four elements when combined as a body, like the intoxicating power from fermenting ingredients; there is no other world.
- As reported by Buddhist and other opponents (e.g. in the Pramāṇavārttika and Tattvasaṃgraha)
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: 1.36ff (pūrvapakṣa)
  - [Tattvasaṅgraha of Śāntarakṣita](../texts/tattvasangraha.md) — ref: Lokāyataparīkṣā
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
No: breath, senses and cognition do not arise from the body alone without a prior moment of their own kind; the first cognition of a life comes from a previous cognition; hence rebirth, and the cultivation of compassion over many lives, are possible.
- PV Pramāṇasiddhi; Dharmottara's Paralokasiddhi
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: 1.36-53
  - [Paralokasiddhi (Dharmottara)](../texts/paralokasiddhi-dharmottara.md) — 

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Lokāyata and the Buddhists disagree about whether mind depends on the body alone.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Lokāyata rejects inference as a means of knowing the unseen; the Buddhists reject the materialist account.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the Lokāyata speaks only of what perception establishes; Dharmakīrti argues from the causal requirement of a homogeneous prior cause — the disagreement concerns the admissibility of inference.
