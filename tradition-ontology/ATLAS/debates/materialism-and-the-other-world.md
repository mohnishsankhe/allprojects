# Does anything survive the body's death — is there another world and rebirth, or is consciousness a product of the body that ends with it?

`dsp:materialism-and-the-other-world` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](../lineages/carvaka.md)
(As reported) Consciousness arises from the four elements formed into a body, like intoxicating power, and perishes with them; there is no one who goes to another world, so there is none; the burnt body does not return; no one comes back to tell.
- madaśakti simile
- no memory of past births (suśikṣita)
- the experiments on criminals (Pāyāsi, Paesi)
- 'I am fat' shows the body is the self

### [The śramaṇa movement](../lineages/sramana.md)
(As reported) Ajita: the person of four elements is annihilated at death; there is no other world.
- the elements return to their masses (DN 2)
**Texts:**

### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
There is another world; the denier loses the wager either way; Kumāra Kassapa answers each of Pāyāsi's arguments with a simile; Śāntarakṣita argues that a first cognition presupposes a prior one, so mind is a beginningless continuum.
- the safe wager (MN 60)
- dream, hot iron ball, conch, fire-sticks (DN 23)
- cognition from cognition (TS 1865–1964)

### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The soul is other than the body: Kesi answers Paesi; the Sūtrakṛtāṅga answers the five-element and soul-is-body teachers.
- the fire-sticks simile
- they go from darkness to darkness
**Texts:** 
  - [Rājapraśnīya](../texts/rajaprasniya.md) — ref: Paesi
  - [Sūtrakṛtāṅga](../texts/sutrakrtanga.md) — ref: 1.1.1
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The self is distinct from the body: consciousness is absent in the corpse, and what experiences the elements cannot be one of them.
- BS 3.3.54
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 3.3.53-54
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Having granted a knower that lasts through the body, the suśikṣita Cārvāka cannot show a cause of its destruction; so it is eternal and passes to other bodies.
- no cause of destruction of a partless self
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāyamañjarī](../texts/nyayamanjari.md) — ref: āhnika 7

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Lokāyata explicitly denies another world and a surviving self; every other party affirms them.

**The traditions' own objections:** The Cārvāka (as reported) calls talk of the other world a priests' livelihood; all opponents call the Cārvāka view the root of wrong conduct.
**Candidate readings:** P1-level: the Lokāyata speaks only of what perception establishes (the vyāvahārika world) — but it denies, not merely brackets, the other world.; P2-standpoint: the suśikṣita Cārvāka's 'a knower lasting as long as the body' concedes part of the opponents' case (Jayanta) — a partial convergence on the living knower only.; P6-upaya: the tradition's account that Bṛhaspati taught the doctrine to delude the asuras (Maitrī Up. 7.9) makes it a deliberate device — a reading the Cārvākas as reported would reject.
