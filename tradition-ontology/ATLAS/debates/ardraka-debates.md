# Ārdraka's debates with rival ascetics (Sūtrakṛtāṅga 2.6)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
Ārdraka: true non-harm requires knowledge and restraint in every act; intention alone does not excuse killing; feeding brāhmaṇas or killing one elephant a year does not free one from harm; the Jina's teaching of many to crowds is without attachment.
- The standard of non-harm to all beings
- Knowledge must guide action
**Texts:** 
  - [Sūtrakṛtāṅga](../texts/sutrakrtanga.md) — ref: 2.6
### [Ājīvika](../lineages/ajivika.md)
Gośāla (as reported by the Jain text): Mahāvīra changed from solitary ascetic to teacher of crowds, which is inconsistent.
- Reported by the Sūtrakṛtāṅga
**Texts:** 
  - [Sūtrakṛtāṅga](../texts/sutrakrtanga.md) — ref: 2.6
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
Buddhist monks (as reported by the Jain text): the moral quality of an act lies in intention; an unintended killing is not sinful.
- Reported by the Sūtrakṛtāṅga
**Texts:** 
  - [Sūtrakṛtāṅga](../texts/sutrakrtanga.md) — ref: 2.6
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Brāhmaṇas (as reported by the Jain text): feeding two thousand snātakas brings heaven.
- Reported by the Sūtrakṛtāṅga
**Texts:** 
  - [Sūtrakṛtāṅga](../texts/sutrakrtanga.md) — ref: 2.6

- Reported by the Sūtrakṛtāṅga
