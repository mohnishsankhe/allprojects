# In Prāsaṅgika Madhyamaka, is the ultimate only a non-affirming negation of true existence, or the union beyond all four extremes in which nothing is apprehended?

`dsp:mipham-gelug-madhyamaka` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nyingma (the Ancient / Old Translation school)](../lineages/nyingma.md)
Mipham: the enumerated ultimate is the negation of true existence, but the actual (non-enumerated) ultimate is beyond all four extremes, and in equipoise nothing — not even emptiness — is grasped; Madhyamaka has no thesis from the ultimate standpoint.
- The distinction of enumerated and non-enumerated ultimate.
- Nāgārjuna's refusal of all four extremes.
**Texts:** 
  - [Norbu Ketaka (nor bu ke ta ka), Mipham's commentary on Bodhicaryāvatāra ch. 9](../texts/ketaka-mipham.md) — 
  - [Ngeshe Drönme (nges shes rin po che'i sgron me, the Beacon of Certainty)](../texts/ngeshe-dronme.md) — ref: q1, q7
### [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
Gelug scholars (e.g. Pari Rabsel, Drakar Lobzang Palden) defended Tsongkhapa: the object of negation is true existence specifically; emptiness is a non-affirming negation and is apprehended in equipoise; to negate without qualification risks nihilism.
- Unqualified negation would undermine conventional existence and karma.
- Tsongkhapa's identification of the object of negation.
**Texts:** 
  - [Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to Enlightenment (lam rim chen mo)](../texts/lamrim-chenmo.md) — ref: insight section

**Historical debate — Mipham's exchanges with Gelug critics** (late 19th - early 20th c.): Written replies and counter-replies (rtsod lan). Each side regards its view as upheld.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level, P4-stage
**Explanation:** Mipham himself reconciles by levels: the Gelug non-affirming negation is valid as the enumerated ultimate for analysis; the union beyond extremes is the non-enumerated ultimate of equipoise.
**The traditions' own objections:** Gelug scholars reject the claim that their view is only the 'enumerated' ultimate.
