# Are there objects external to cognition?

`dsp:external-objects` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
No (as the pūrvapakṣa is stated): the notion of means and objects of knowledge is like the notion of objects in a dream, or like magic, a gandharva city or a mirage.
- Dream argument (NS 4.2.31–32 as pūrvapakṣa); Vasubandhu's Viṃśatikā.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 4.2.31–32 (pūrvapakṣa)
  - [Viṃśatikā (Viṃśatikāvijñaptimātratāsiddhi)](../texts/vimsatika.md) — ref: vv. 1–2 (from memory)
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Yes: the idealist thesis lacks a reason; dream-cognition depends on prior experience like memory; false cognition is removed by true knowledge as dream-notions are on waking, which presupposes real objects.
- NS 4.2.33–37; Udayana's refutation of the denial of external objects.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 4.2.26–37
  - [Ātmatattvaviveka](../texts/atmatattvaviveka.md) — ref: bāhyārthabhaṅga section
### [Sautrāntika](../lineages/sautrantika.md)
External objects exist but are not directly perceived; they are inferred from the images they cause in cognition (the 'Sautrāntika' position in later doxography).
- Held by the opponents of the Viṃśatikā and by Śubhagupta's Bāhyārthasiddhikārikā (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Bāhyārthasiddhikārikā](../texts/bahyarthasiddhikarika.md) — 
  - [Viṃśatikā (Viṃśatikāvijñaptimātratāsiddhi)](../texts/vimsatika.md) — ref: 2 (objection)
### [Vaibhāṣika](../lineages/vaibhasika.md)
External objects are directly perceived: sense-cognition grasps the aggregate of atoms.
- The opponent of Viṃśatikā 11–14 and Ālambanaparīkṣā 1–5
**Texts:** 
  - [Viṃśatikā (Viṃśatikāvijñaptimātratāsiddhi)](../texts/vimsatika.md) — ref: 11-14
  - [Ālambanaparīkṣā](../texts/alambanapariksa.md) — ref: 1-5
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
The object-condition cannot be external atoms (Dignāga); blue and its cognition are necessarily apprehended together and so are not different (Dharmakīrti); but Dharmakīrti also argues at the level where external objects are assumed.
- Ālambanaparīkṣā 1–2, 6; Pramāṇaviniścaya 1.54; PV 3.354 (conventional)
**Texts:** 
  - [Ālambanaparīkṣā](../texts/alambanapariksa.md) — ref: 1-8
  - [Pramāṇaviniścaya](../texts/pramanaviniscaya.md) — ref: 1.54
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: 2.354 (e-text)
### [Yogācāra-Madhyamaka](../lineages/yogacara-madhyamaka.md)
Conventionally there are no external objects (mind-only); ultimately mind too is neither one nor many.
- Śāntarakṣita's Madhyamakālaṃkāra (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyamakālaṃkāra](../texts/madhyamakalamkara.md) — ref: 91-93 (recalled)
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
Yes: waking cognitions are not overturned as dream cognitions are; cognition is formless while the object has form; a cloth-cognition regularly arises only from threads (the Vṛttikāra in Śabara; ŚV nirālambanavāda); in dreams too the objects are real ones of other times and places.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śābarabhāṣya](../texts/sabara-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.5
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: nirālambanavāda
  - [Śāstradīpikā](../texts/sastradipika.md) — ref: 1.1.5
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
No independent external object is established: cognition and its object are always apprehended together (sahopalambha-niyama) and cognitions arise as in dreams from latent impressions.
**Texts:** 
  - [Viṃśatikā (Viṃśatikāvijñaptimātratāsiddhi)](../texts/vimsatika.md) — ref: vv. 1–2
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: ch. 3
