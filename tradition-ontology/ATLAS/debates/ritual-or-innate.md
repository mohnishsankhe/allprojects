# Are ritual, consecration and contrived meditation needed, or is the innate realized directly?

`dsp:ritual-or-innate` · `skeleton` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [The Mahāsiddhas (the eighty-four great adepts)](../lineages/mahasiddha.md)
The innate is not reached by maṇḍala, fire offering, mantra, austerity or contrived meditation; only the guru's instruction and resting in the natural state.
- Saraha's critique of all observances (People Dohā v2–15, v24).
- Queen Dohā v32; Advayavajra's verse 'no mantra recitation … that is the mantra recitation'.
- Tilopa's Dohākoṣa: do not worship gods.

### [Vajrayāna (Mantranaya) in India](../lineages/vajrayana.md)
Consecration, pledges, the two accumulations and sādhana are indispensable; without them practice is theft and leads to hell.
- Queen Dohā v15–17 (without consecration, the vajra hell).
- Dārikapa: without the two accumulations and a guru, no great bliss.
- Advayavajra: the beginner's practice continues even in the mad observance.
- The ritual manuals (Vajrāvalī, Kriyāsaṃgraha).
**Texts:**

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level, P4-stage
**Explanation:** Ultimately (P1) no ritual touches the innate; conventionally, for one in the causal state (P4), all the beginner's practices, consecration and accumulations are required — as Advayavajra states.
The same authors hold both sides (Saraha's Queen Dohā requires consecration and pledges; Advayavajra both writes 'no mantra, no homa' and insists on ādikarma), which the tradition itself explains by the states of cause, fruit and activity.
**The traditions' own objections:** Some songs reject contrived meditation even as a stage (People Dohā v25: 'do not spoil the pure mind with meditations').
