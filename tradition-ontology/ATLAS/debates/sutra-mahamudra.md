# Can Mahāmudrā be introduced and realized apart from tantric empowerment — Gampopa's 'sūtra Mahāmudrā', the 'white self-sufficient remedy' — or is Mahāmudrā only the wisdom arising from empowerment and the two stages?

`dsp:sutra-mahamudra` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B6; G6 (related)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Kagyu (the Kagyu transmissions: Marpa / Dakpo Kagyu, with the Shangpa grouped by name)](../lineages/kagyu.md)
Mahāmudrā is the nature of one's own mind; a realized guru can point it out through blessing to a ripe disciple, also on the basis of the sūtra teaching of buddha-nature, and recognizing it suffices ('the white self-sufficient remedy'); the tantric path of method is a second, complementary path, not the only door.
- Indian precedent: Maitrīpa's non-mentation cycle and Sahajavajra's Mahāmudrā joined with the pāramitā path; Saraha's dohās (Gö Lotsāwa).
- Tilopa: Mahāmudrā is not seen through the tenets of mantra, pāramitā or vinaya but by not engaging the mind (Gaṅgā Mahāmudrā).
- Persons differ: the simultaneous way is medicine for those with prior training, poison for beginners, and vice versa (Ājñāsamyakpramāṇa) — the sudden recognition is not for all.
- The Ratnagotravibhāga is the treatise of Mahāmudrā (tradition's report of Gampopa).

### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
Mahāmudrā, in the tantras, is the wisdom born from the empowerments and the completion stage; a 'Mahāmudrā' taught without them is not the Buddha's Mahāmudrā; the 'white self-sufficient remedy' is the teaching of the Chinese Hashang, refuted at Samye; the path needs the gradual accumulation of merit and wisdom.
- The term mahāmudrā belongs to the mantra vehicle; outside it, 'Mahāmudrā' is a misnomer.
- Present-day Mahāmudrā is in essence the Chinese Great Perfection.
- A single remedy that dispenses with method contradicts the Mahāyāna union of method and wisdom (Sakya Paṇḍita's reasoning as reported).
**Texts:** 
  - [Discrimination of the Three Vows (sdom pa gsum gyi rab tu dbye ba)](../texts/domsum-rabye.md) — ref: ch.3
### [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
There is both a sūtra Mahāmudrā — the emptiness of mind realized by a mind that has established emptiness by reasoning — and a tantric Mahāmudrā — the clear light of great bliss; the Ganden (Gelug) Mahāmudrā tradition teaches both (First Panchen Lama).
- recorded from memory; U47 owns the Gelug sources
### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
Sakya Paṇḍita: the Mahāmudrā taught by the Buddha is only the wisdom born of empowerment and the two stages; the present-day 'white self-sufficient remedy' taught without empowerment is the Chinese (Hwashang) Great Perfection under another name, and a fool's meditation on it leads to animal or formless rebirth or the śrāvaka cessation.
- No such Mahāmudrā is found in the tantras.
- It repeats the simultaneist teaching rejected at Samye.
**Texts:** 
  - [Discrimination of the Three Vows (sdom pa gsum gyi rab tu dbye ba)](../texts/domsum-rabye.md) — ref: ch. 3
### [Kagyu (the Kagyu transmissions: Marpa / Dakpo Kagyu, with the Shangpa grouped by name)](../lineages/kagyu.md)
The Kagyu teach a Mahāmudrā of pointing out the nature of mind that can be introduced on the basis of sūtra (the Ratnagotravibhāga and Maitrīpa's teaching of non-attention), as Gampopa did; later Kagyu scholars answered Sakya Paṇḍita that his target was not their teaching.
- Maitrīpa's amanasikāra teaching is Indian and not Chinese.
- The Ratnagotravibhāga's buddha-nature supports a sūtra approach.
**Texts:** 
  - [The Jewel Ornament of Liberation](../texts/jewel-ornament-of-liberation.md) — 
### [Ganden Oral Lineage of Mahāmudrā (Ganden Kagyu / Ensa Hearing Lineage)](../lineages/ganden-oral-lineage.md)
The First Panchen Lama: Mahāmudrā has a sūtra approach (emptiness of mind as Nāgārjuna taught) and a mantra approach (clear light of great bliss); the various Mahāmudrā instructions converge in one intent.
- The sūtra approach is meditation on emptiness taught by Nāgārjuna.
**Texts:** 
  - [Highway of the Conquerors: root text of the Gelug/Kagyu Mahāmudrā (dge ldan bka' brgyud rin po che'i phyag chen rtsa ba rgyal ba'i gzhung lam)](../texts/gyalwai-shunglam.md) — 

**Historical debate — Sakya Paṇḍita's critique of Kagyu Mahāmudrā and the Kagyu replies** (13th c. critique; replies 15th–16th c.): Sakya Paṇḍita's Discrimination of the Three Vows (and Clarifying the Sage's Intent) attack the 'white self-sufficient remedy' and Mahāmudrā without empowerment; Kagyu authors (Gö Lotsāwa, Pema Karpo, Dakpo Tashi Namgyal) answer by Indian precedent and by distinguishing approaches. Kagyu: the critique misreads Gampopa's intent and ignores the Indian sūtra-Mahāmudrā precedent; Sakya: the criticism stands; Gelug: accepts a sūtra-level Mahāmudrā within its own Madhyamaka.

**The traditions' own objections:** Sakya Paṇḍita rejects any Mahāmudrā outside the mantra vehicle; the Kagyu reject the identification of Gampopa's teaching with the Hashang's.
**Candidate readings:** P4-stage: 'sūtra Mahāmudrā' is for persons of the simultaneous type with prior training (Ājñāsamyakpramāṇa 271a.3); Sakya objection — the Hashang made the same claim.; P1-level / terminological: the word names two things — the emptiness-luminosity of mind recognized through blessing, and the tantric wisdom of the fourth empowerment; if so the dispute is partly about the name; Sakya objection — the name is proper only to the tantric referent; Kagyu objection — the referent is one.; P6-upaya: blessing functions as an empowerment for the ripe disciple; Sakya objection — formal empowerment is required.
