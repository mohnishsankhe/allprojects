# Are the puruṣas many, or is there one Puruṣa? (MBh 12.294–296, 12.338)

**Coverage:** G4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
The Sāṃkhya-Yoga thinkers hold that there are many puruṣas and do not accept a single one (as reported in the Mokṣadharma).
- 12.338.2.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mokṣadharma (Mokṣadharmaparvan)](../texts/moksadharma.md) — ref: 12.338.2
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
The many puruṣas have one source, the universal Puruṣa of the Puruṣa-sūkta (12.338.3–24); the twenty-fifth, awakening, attains the state of the twenty-sixth (12.296.7–12), and Vasiṣṭha contrasts the knowledge of plurality with that of unity.
- 12.338.3: 'as the many have one source'; Brahmā to Rudra 12.338.22–24.
- 12.296.7–12.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mokṣadharma (Mokṣadharmaparvan)](../texts/moksadharma.md) — ref: 12.294–296; 12.338

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The Mokṣadharma itself frames the answer as 'many as individuals, one as their source' (12.338.3), a difference of standpoint (the individuals vs their ground).
**The traditions' own objections:** Classical Sāṃkhya insists on the real plurality of puruṣas; Advaita reads the passages as identity; Dvaita as eternal dependence without identity; none accepts the others' reading as the epic's meaning.
