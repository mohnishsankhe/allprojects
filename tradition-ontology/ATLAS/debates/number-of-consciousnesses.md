# How many kinds of consciousness are there — six, eight or nine?

`dsp:number-of-consciousnesses` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md)
Six: the five sense-consciousnesses and mental consciousness.
- Abhidharmakośa (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhidharmakośa (Abhidharmakośakārikā)](../texts/abhidharmakosa.md) — ref: ch. 1–2
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
Eight: the six, the afflicted mind and the store-consciousness.
- Triṃśikā; Laṅkāvatāra; Cheng weishi lun
**Texts:** 
  - [Triṃśikā (Triṃśikāvijñaptikārikā)](../texts/trimsika.md) — ref: 1-16
  - [Cheng weishi lun (*Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi)](../texts/cheng-weishi-lun.md) — 
### [Shelun school (Chinese 'Mahāyānasaṃgraha' school)](../lineages/shelun.md)
Nine: beyond the store-consciousness there is a stainless consciousness (amalavijñāna) identical with suchness.
- Paramārtha's Jueding zang lun and Zhuanshi lun
**Texts:** 
  - [Jueding zang lun](../texts/jueding-zang-lun.md) — 
  - [Zhuanshi lun (Treatise on the Evolution of Consciousness)](../texts/zhuanshi-lun.md) — 
### `lin:faxiang`
Eight only: 'amala' is a name of the purified eighth consciousness, not a ninth (Kuiji).
- Cheng weishi lun shuji (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Cheng weishi lun shuji](../texts/cheng-weishi-lun-shuji.md) — 

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Principles:** P2-standpoint, P4-stage
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the count of consciousnesses differs among schools.
**The traditions' own objections:** Faxiang rejects a separate ninth; Shelun insists on it; Sarvāstivāda rejects the seventh and eighth.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the ninth consciousness may be the eighth considered in its purified aspect (as Faxiang says), so that the difference is one of standpoint.; P4-stage: six are taught to disciples, eight to bodhisattvas (the Saṃdhinirmocana's 'not taught to the childish').

_Notes: The Pramāṇa school's six-consciousness usage is a Tibetan doxographic observation, recorded under cpt:eight-consciousnesses._
