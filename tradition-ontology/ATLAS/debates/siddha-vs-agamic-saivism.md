# Are temple, image, Āgamic ritual and initiation the way to Śiva, or are they to be abandoned for the Lord within?

`dsp:siddha-vs-agamic-saivism` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md)
The Lord is within the body; the planted stone does not speak; temple and tank are in the mind; karma does not go by Kāśī or the Gaṅgā; chiselled stone and moulded image are not the Real; the realized need no hymns, mantras or seals.
- The Lord is within; the pot does not know the taste of the curry (Sivavākkiyar).
- Pilgrimage and bathing do not remove karma (Kaṭuveḷi 15).
- Realization came without ablutions or flowers (Aḻukaṇṇi 37).
**Texts:** 
  - [Civavākkiyam (the songs of Sivavākkiyar)](../texts/sivavakkiyam.md) — ref: naṭṭa kallai; kōyilāvatētaṭā
  - [Kaṭuveḷic cittar: Āṉantak kaḷippu (the Joy-song)](../texts/kaduveli-ananda-kalippu.md) — ref: 15
  - [Pattirakiriyār: Meyññāṉap pulampal (lament for true knowledge)](../texts/bhadragiriyar-meynana-pulampal.md) — ref: 16
  - [Kutampaic cittar pāṭalkaḷ (the songs of Kuṭampai)](../texts/kudambai-padalgal.md) — ref: 7, 30
### [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md)
Śiva is worshipped in the temple image and in his devotees; the Āgamas are his word; initiation, temple service and ritual (caryā and kriyā) are the first two of the four paths, and even the liberated worships the devotees and the temple form as Śiva himself (Civañāṉa Pōtam 12).
- The Veda and the Āgama are both the Lord's true scripture (TM 2397).
- The four pādas lead through ritual to knowledge.
- The released soul honours the devotees' form and the temple image as Hara.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tirumantiram](../texts/tirumantiram.md) — ref: 2397; 63
  - [Civañāṉa Pōtam](../texts/sivananabodham.md) — ref: sūtra 12

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** P4-stage: outer worship is for the lower rungs (caryā, kriyā), the Siddhars speak from the stage of the realized for whom such means are done with.
The Tirumantiram — root text of both sides — teaches both the four pādas and the body as temple; Kuṭampai's refrain ('for those who have X, why Y?') is explicitly addressed to the realized. The Siddhar polemic also targets ritual without inner knowledge.
**The traditions' own objections:** Śaiva Siddhānta holds that temple worship continues after release and that initiation is necessary; Sivavākkiyar denies that the stone is god at all, not merely that it is for beginners. The reconciliation does not claim either side accepts it.
