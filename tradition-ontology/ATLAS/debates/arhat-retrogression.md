# Can an arhat fall back from arhatship?

`dsp:arhat-retrogression` · `skeleton` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md)
Yes: of six kinds of arhat, five ('liberated by occasion') can fall back from the fruit; the unshakeable cannot; stream-enterers cannot fall back; one fallen does not die before regaining it.
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhidharmakośa (Abhidharmakośakārikā)](../texts/abhidharmakosa.md) — ref: 6.56–60
  - [Samayabhedoparacanacakra](../texts/samayabhedoparacanacakra.md) — ref: Sarvāstivāda tenets
### [Saṃmitīya](../lineages/sammitiya.md)
Yes (Kathāvatthu commentary on Kv 1.2 names Sammitiyas, Vajjiputtakas, Sabbatthivādins and some Mahāsaṅghikas).
**Texts:** 
  - [Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā](../texts/kathavatthu-atthakatha.md) — ref: on Kv 1.2
### [Sautrāntika](../lineages/sautrantika.md)
No: defilements abandoned by seeing do not return, and there is no falling back from arhatship either; what is lost is only the enjoyment of attainments.
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhidharmakośabhāṣya](../texts/abhidharmakosabhasya.md) — ref: 6.58, Pradhan p. 375
### [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
No: the arahant does not fall away (Kv 1.2).
**Texts:** 
  - [Kathāvatthu](../texts/kathavatthu.md) — ref: 1.2
### [Mahāsāṃghika](../lineages/mahasanghika.md)
Arhats cannot fall back; stream-enterers can (Vasumitra).
**Texts:** 
  - [Samayabhedoparacanacakra](../texts/samayabhedoparacanacakra.md) — ref: Mahāsāṃghika tenets

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** All sides agree that the unshakeable (akopya) arhat and the 'falling back from enjoyment' of attainments are not in question; the remaining disagreement is whether the fruit itself can be lost.
The Kośa's threefold falling back (from what is obtained, not obtained, enjoyment) lets the Sautrāntika accept loss of enjoyment while denying loss of the fruit.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Sarvāstivāda insists five kinds of arhat lose the fruit itself (6.58 'phalād dhāniḥ'); the Theravāda denies it; neither accepts that the difference is merely one of standpoint.
