# Is the ultimate empty of its own nature like all phenomena (rang stong, self-emptiness), or empty only of what is other than it — the adventitious stains — while not empty of its own nature and qualities (gzhan stong, other-emptiness)?

`dsp:rangtong-shentong` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G7

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Jonang (jo nang pa)](../lineages/jonang.md)
Dolpopa: buddha-nature — the dharmakāya, the dharmadhātu — is the ultimate truth; it is empty of all conventional phenomena, which are adventitious and other than it, but not empty of its own nature (other-emptiness). Conventional phenomena are empty of their own nature, like a rabbit's horns. An emptiness that is nothing whatsoever is annihilatory emptiness and not the ultimate. The final-wheel sūtras, the Maitreya treatises and the Kālacakra teach the definitive meaning; the dharmakāya is the supreme self.

  - [Mountain Doctrine: Ocean of Definitive Meaning (ri chos nges don rgya mtsho)](../texts/mountain-doctrine.md) — ref: whole
  - [The Great Calculation of the Doctrine which has the Significance of a Fourth Council (bka' bsdu bzhi pa'i don bstan rtsis chen po)](../texts/fourth-council-dolpopa.md) — ref: whole
### [Jonang (jo nang pa)](../lineages/jonang.md)
Tāranātha: the thoroughly established nature is self-aware, self-illuminating primordial wisdom free of subject and object, unchanging, realized in meditative equipoise and not empty of itself; self- and other-emptiness agree that all conventional phenomena are empty of own-nature and differ over the ultimate — with twenty-one further differences following.
**Texts:** 
  - [The Essence of Other-Emptiness (gzhan stong snying po)](../texts/zhentong-nyingpo-taranatha.md) — ref: whole
  - [Twenty-one Differences regarding the Profound Meaning (zab don khyad par nyer gcig pa)](../texts/twenty-one-differences-taranatha.md) — ref: whole
### [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
Tsongkhapa and Khedrup Je: nothing whatsoever, the ultimate included, is established by its own nature; the ultimate truth is the mere absence of inherent existence, a non-affirming negation, and emptiness itself is empty. An ultimate that is truly established, permanent and empty only of other would be an object of grasping at true existence — the extreme of permanence, like the self of the non-Buddhists; the Saṃdhinirmocana's three natures do not teach such an ultimate.
**Texts:** 
  - [Essence of Eloquence (drang nges legs bshad snying po)](../texts/legs-bshad-snying-po.md) — ref: Madhyamaka and Cittamātra sections
  - [Great Digest on Emptiness (stong thun chen mo)](../texts/tongthun-chenmo.md) — ref: whole
### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
Gorampa: three views are held in Tibet to be Madhyamaka — the eternalist extreme (Dolpopa: the ultimate empty of other but not of itself), the nihilist extreme (Tsongkhapa: the ultimate as mere negation of true existence) — and the Madhyamaka free from all extremes, which is Nāgārjuna's; clinging to emptiness as mere negation is itself an extreme.
**Texts:** 
  - [Distinguishing the Views (lta ba'i shan 'byed theg mchog gnad kyi zla zer)](../texts/taway-shenje.md) — ref: whole
### [Karma Kagyu (Kamtsang Kagyu)](../lineages/karma-kagyu.md)
Rangjung Dorje: the beginningless element, basis of all phenomena and of nirvāṇa, is mind's nature, luminous and empty, obscured by adventitious stains.
**Texts:** 
  - [Treatise on Buddha-Nature (the 3rd Karmapa)](../texts/treatise-on-buddha-nature-rangjung-dorje.md) — ref: opening
### [Nyingma (the Ancient / Old Translation school)](../lineages/nyingma.md)
Mipham: buddha-nature is the union of luminosity and emptiness — its emptiness keeps it from being an existent self, its luminosity means the qualities are primordially present; 'other-emptiness' as the Nyingma accept it — the ultimate wisdom empty of adventitious conditioned phenomena — does not conflict with self-emptiness, since that ultimate too is not established by its own nature.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bde gshegs snying po'i stong thun chen mo seng ge'i nga ro (Mipham)](../texts/sugatagarbha-lions-roar-mipham.md) — ref: whole
  - [The Lion's Roar Affirming Other-Emptiness (gzhan stong khas len seng ge'i nga ro)](../texts/zhentong-khelen-sengei-ngaro.md) — ref: whole
### [Rimé (ris med), the non-sectarian movement](../lineages/rime.md)
Jamgön Kongtrul: the self-emptiness Madhyamaka of Nāgārjuna's reasonings and the other-emptiness Great Madhyamaka of the Maitreya treatises are each valid in their own sphere — the self-empty approach cuts through elaboration in study and reflection, the other-empty view suits meditative experience; the element is empty of separable stains and not empty of inseparable qualities.
**Texts:** 
  - [The Treasury of Knowledge (shes bya kun khyab mdzod)](../texts/sheja-kunkhyab.md) — ref: tenets section
  - [Rgyud bla ma'i 'grel pa phyir mi ldog pa seng ge'i nga ro (Jamgön Kongtrul)](../texts/uttaratantra-commentary-kongtrul.md) — ref: view
### [Tathāgatagarbha (Buddha-nature) tradition](../lineages/tathagatagarbha.md)
The Indian scriptural ground cited by both sides: the tathāgatagarbha is empty of the separable defilements and not empty of the inseparable buddha-qualities (Śrīmālādevī); nothing is to be removed from it and nothing added (Ratnagotravibhāga 1.154-155); yet the Laṅkāvatāra says the tathāgatagarbha is taught meaning emptiness, not the tīrthika self.

  - [Ratnagotravibhāga (Mahāyānottaratantraśāstra)](../texts/ratnagotravibhaga.md) — ref: 1.154-155

### [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
Only 'self-emptiness' is correct: every phenomenon, the ultimate included, is empty of its own inherent existence; an ultimate empty only of other (adventitious) things but not of itself would be truly existent and a form of eternalism.
- Emptiness is itself empty.
- A truly existent ultimate cannot withstand analysis.
**Texts:** 
  - [Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to Enlightenment (lam rim chen mo)](../texts/lamrim-chenmo.md) — ref: insight
### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
Gorampa classes Dolpopa's other-emptiness as 'Madhyamaka of the eternalist extreme'; Shākya Chokden, in contrast, held that self-emptiness is the view for cutting conceptual elaboration and other-emptiness the view for meditative experience, both valid.
- (Gorampa) the ultimate is free of all extremes, including permanence.
- (Shākya Chokden) the Maitreya texts' other-emptiness describes the luminous wisdom experienced in meditation.
**Texts:** 
  - [Distinguishing the Views (lta ba'i shan 'byed theg mchog gnad kyi zla zer)](../texts/taway-shenje.md) — 

**Historical debate — Dolpopa's other-emptiness and its Tibetan reception** (14th–17th c. CE): Mountain Doctrine and Fourth Council (Dolpopa); Legs bshad snying po and the Tong thun chen mo (Gelug critiques); lta ba'i shan 'byed (Gorampa); Tāranātha's twenty-one differences. In the 17th century Jonang monasteries in central Tibet were converted to the Gelug under the Ganden Phodrang government and Jonang works restricted (historical and tradition accounts; details moderate confidence); other-emptiness was later re-transmitted through the Rimé movement. Jonang: other-emptiness is the definitive Great Madhyamaka; Gelug: other-emptiness is not Madhyamaka; Sakya (Gorampa): both Dolpopa and Tsongkhapa fall to extremes; Rimé: each valid in its sphere.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P3-path
**Explanation:** Partly reconciled in the Rimé and Nyingma traditions' own terms — self-emptiness for cutting elaboration in study and reflection, other-emptiness for meditative experience (Kongtrul), and other-emptiness understood as not self-established compatible with self-emptiness (Mipham); the Jonang–Gelug disagreement over whether the ultimate is truly established remains not yet reconciled.
P4-stage: Kongtrul assigns the two views to the phases of study/reflection and meditation. P3-path: the analytic temperament and the contemplative (practice-lineage) temperament. Tāranātha himself states the common ground (all conventional phenomena self-empty; elaboration ceases in equipoise) before the differences.
**The traditions' own objections:** Gelug (Tsongkhapa; Khedrup Je): a truly established ultimate is eternalism like the tīrthika self — no reconciliation. Jonang (Dolpopa): self-emptiness applied to the ultimate is annihilatory emptiness. Sakya (Gorampa): both are extremes. Tāranātha lists twenty-one differences, so the Jonang do not accept that the two views differ only in phase of practice.
