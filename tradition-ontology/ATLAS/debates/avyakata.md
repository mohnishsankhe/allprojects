# Should the questions about the eternity and extent of the world, soul and body, and the Tathāgata after death be answered?

`dsp:avyakata` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [The śramaṇa movement](../lineages/sramana.md)
The wanderers of other sects each hold one answer as 'this alone is true, all else is false' (the world is eternal; the soul is the body; the Tathāgata exists after death …), and quarrel over it.
- As reported in MN 63, MN 72, SN 44, Ud 6.4, DN 9.
**Texts:** 
  - [Udāna](../texts/udana.md) — ref: Ud 6.4
  - [Aggivacchagotta Sutta](../texts/aggivacchagotta-sutta.md) — ref: MN 72
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
The Buddha sets the ten questions aside: they are not beneficial and do not lead to nibbāna; the Tathāgata has abandoned speculative views; being liberated from reckoning by the aggregates, 'reborn' and 'not reborn' do not apply; answering Vacchagotta 'there is a self' or 'there is no self' would side with eternalism or annihilationism.
- The poisoned arrow (MN 63).
- The extinguished fire (MN 72).
- Silence before Vacchagotta (SN 44.10).
- Not apprehended in truth even here (SN 22.86).
**Texts:** 
  - [Cūḷamāluṅkya Sutta](../texts/culamalunkya-sutta.md) — ref: MN 63
  - [Aggivacchagotta Sutta](../texts/aggivacchagotta-sutta.md) — ref: MN 72
  - [Abyākata Saṃyutta](../texts/abyakata-samyutta.md) — ref: SN 44.10
  - [Anurādha Sutta](../texts/anuradha-sutta.md) — ref: SN 22.86

**Historical debate — Vacchagotta's questions** (the Buddha's lifetime (tradition)): MN 72; SN 44.7–11 Vacchagotta goes for refuge (MN 72) and later becomes an arahant (MN 73).

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P6-upaya, P1-level
**Explanation:** Reconciled under P6 (the Buddha's silence fitted to the questioner, SN 44.10) and P1 (the questions presuppose a self or Tathāgata that is not found in truth, MN 72, SN 22.86); the rival sectarian positions themselves are not reconciled.
The texts give both reasons themselves: the questions are unbeneficial, and they are framed in terms the Tathāgata's liberation does not fit.
**The traditions' own objections:** Theravāda rejects reading the silence as implying an inexpressible self (a reading later associated with the Pudgalavāda, U38); the wanderers' side would reject the claim that their questions are ill-framed.
