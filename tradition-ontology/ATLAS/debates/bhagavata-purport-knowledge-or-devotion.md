# Is the Bhāgavata's final purport non-dual knowledge or devotion?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Non-dual knowledge: the Bhāgavata's mark is the oneness of Brahman and self, its sole aim kaivalya (12.13.12); forms are superimposed (1.3.30-33); Śuka's last word is 'I am Brahman' (12.5.11); devotion purifies for knowledge.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: each school reads the other's key verses as subordinate; P1 (level) and P4 (stage) are candidate principles but both traditions reject them.
**The traditions' own objections:** Gauḍīya, Mādhva and Vallabha authors deny that devotion is a means to be transcended; Advaitins deny that personal distinction survives liberation.
**Candidate readings:** P1-level: the devotional passages speak at the conventional level, the non-dual ones at the ultimate (the Advaita reading; rejected by Vaiṣṇavas).; P4-stage: knowledge is a stage within devotion, or devotion within knowledge (each school's own subordination).; The text's own synthesis: BhP 1.2.11 names one non-dual reality as Brahman, Paramātman and Bhagavān, and 11.2.42 has devotion, experience and detachment arise together.
