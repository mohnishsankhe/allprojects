# Is the ultimate truth an object of knowledge, and is emptiness realised by valid cognition (perception and inference)?

`dsp:is-the-ultimate-knowable` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
The ultimate truth (emptiness) is an object of knowledge: it is found by a reasoning consciousness analysing the final mode of being, first realised by inference and then directly by yogic perception, in which it appears without dualistic appearance.
- If emptiness could not be known, the path to liberation could not be practised.
- Inference ascertains the absence of inherent existence as a non-affirming negation.
**Texts:** 
  - [Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to Enlightenment (lam rim chen mo)](../texts/lamrim-chenmo.md) — ref: insight
### [Kadam](../lineages/kadam.md)
Emptiness is not realised by perception and inference as Buddhist logicians define them — to say so is the talk of the near-sighted; otherwise all tenet-holders measuring by valid cognition would agree. It is 'seen' by not seeing, non-conceptually; the valid cognitions were set up to refute opponents.
- Disagreement among all logicians shows valid cognition does not settle dharmatā.
- Scripture says it is not known by conceptual or non-conceptual knowledge (citing Bhavya).
**Texts:** 
  - [Entry into the Two Truths (Satyadvayāvatāra; bden pa gnyis la 'jug pa)](../texts/satyadvayavatara.md) — ref: v10–14
### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
The ultimate is beyond the scope of mind and words; it is not an object of knowledge in the ordinary sense, and is 'known' only as the non-dual state of meditative equipoise (Gorampa).
- An object requires a subject; in the ultimate subject and object are not divided.
**Texts:** 
  - [Distinguishing the Views (lta ba'i shan 'byed theg mchog gnad kyi zla zer)](../texts/taway-shenje.md) — 

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint, P1-level
**Explanation:** All three agree the direct realisation is non-dual and non-conceptual; they differ on whether that realisation may be called 'knowing an object' and on the role of inference beforehand (standpoint of conventional description vs. of the equipoise itself).
Atiśa's denial targets emptiness as the object of the logicians' two pramāṇas taken dualistically; the Gelug accepts that in direct realisation there is no dualistic appearance ('like water poured into water'), while still classifying emptiness as an object of knowledge conventionally.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Gelug insists that denying emptiness is an object of knowledge makes the path unknowable; Gorampa insists that calling it an object reifies it. The reconciliation claims only a shared account of the non-dual realisation, not agreement on the classification.
