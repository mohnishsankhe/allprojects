# Are the Āgamas and tantras authoritative — equal to, above, or outside the Veda?

`dsp:agama-veda-authority` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G (status of the Veda); A4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
Only the authorless Veda is an independent authority; the dharma-teachings accepted by the Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Pāñcarātra, Pāśupata, Buddhists and Jains are not grounded in the Veda and are set aside.
- smṛti conflicting with śruti is not authoritative
- such systems have other motives and no Vedic root
**Texts:** 
  - [Tantravārttika](../texts/tantravarttika.md) — ref: 1.3.4
### [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md)
Śiva's scripture is the higher knowledge revealing the Lord; the Veda is the lower knowledge revealing the bound soul and its bonds; they differ by the eligibility of their hearers; Vedic religion is a lower stream than the Atimārga and Mantramārga.
- knowledge is twofold by adhikāra (Kāmika)
- fivefold ascending hierarchy of streams
**Texts:** 
  - [Kāmikāgama](../texts/kamika-agama.md) — ref: purva.1.14-19
  - [Niśvāsamukha](../texts/nisvasamukha.md) — ref: 1-4
### [Pāñcarātra](../lineages/pancaratra.md)
The Pāñcarātra is valid because the omniscient Lord spoke it; it is Vedic in root (the Ekāyana branch, per Vedānta Deśika); condemnations of 'tantras' concern the Śaiva and other schools, not it.
- authorship by the omniscient Lord guarantees validity
- its followers are brahmins
- Bādarāyaṇa, a Bhāgavata, did not refute it
**Texts:** 
  - [Āgamaprāmāṇya](../texts/agamapramanya.md) — ref: siddhanta
  - [Pāñcarātrarakṣā](../texts/pancaratraraksa.md) — 
### [Vaikhānasa](../lineages/vaikhanasa.md)
Vaikhānasa temple worship is itself Vedic: every rite follows the śruti and is performed with Vedic mantras by brahmins of the first of the sūtras.
- ten reasons for the Vaikhānasa Sūtra's pre-eminence
**Texts:** 
  - [Daśavidhahetunirūpaṇa](../texts/dasavidhahetunirupana.md) — ref: p2.1-12
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Where an Āgamic system contradicts scripture (e.g. the origination of the soul in the Pāñcarātra, the Lord as mere efficient cause in the Pāśupata/Māheśvara systems) it is rejected; its devotional worship may be accepted where it does not contradict.
- BS 2.2.35–45 as read by Śaṅkara
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 2.2.42-45

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the sides disagree on what makes a scripture authoritative.
**The traditions' own objections:** Mīmāṃsakas deny authority to any personally authored scripture; Vaikhānasas reject being grouped with tantric Āgamas; Śaiva texts do not concede the Veda's superiority.
**Candidate readings:** P4-stage: the Kāmika itself grades Veda and Āgama as lower and higher knowledge for different adhikārins, which could frame the relation as one of eligibility rather than of truth.; P2-standpoint: Veda as general and Āgama as special teaching (a formula of Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta, to be sourced by U18).; Partial acceptance: Mīmāṃsā and Advaita accept Āgamic teaching only where it does not conflict with the Veda.
