# Is the root impurity (āṇava mala) a real substance removable only by ritual initiation, or is it ignorance - a contraction of consciousness - removed by knowledge?

`dsp:mala-substance-or-ignorance` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md)
Āṇava mala is a real, beginningless substance (dravya) that covers the soul's powers of knowing and acting; as a substance it cannot be destroyed by knowledge but only by an act - initiation (dīkṣā) empowered by Śiva - after its ripening; the liberated soul becomes equal to Śiva but remains distinct.
- knowledge cannot remove a substance, as knowing a disease does not cure it
- scripture prescribes dīkṣā as the cause of liberation
**Texts:** 
  - [Mṛgendratantra](../texts/mrgendra-tantra.md) — ref: vidyāpāda
  - [Nareśvaraparīkṣā](../texts/naresvarapariksa.md) — ref: work
### [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md)
Impurity is ignorance, the cause of the sprouting of saṃsāra (MVT 1.23); ignorance is not the absence of knowledge but its incomplete manifestation (TĀ 1.25-26); knowledge is the sole cause of liberation (TĀ 1.22); the āṇava impurity is consciousness losing its freedom (ĪPK 3.2.4); grace does not depend on the ripening of impurity or on a balance of karma but on the Lord's freedom (TĀ 13); initiation works because it is itself a form of knowledge.
- if mala were a substance, the one consciousness would have a second reality beside it
- the same mala is removed in the highly graced by knowledge alone (anupāya)
**Texts:** 
  - [Mālinīvijayottaratantra](../texts/malinivijayottara-tantra.md) — ref: 1.23
  - [Tantrāloka](../texts/tantraloka.md) — ref: 1.22-26; 13
  - [Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā](../texts/isvarapratyabhijna-karika.md) — ref: 3.2.4-5
### [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md)
Mala is a substance (dravya); knowledge cannot remove it any more than knowing a cataract cures it; Śiva removes it through the initiation rite.
**Texts:** 
  - [Kiraṇavṛtti](../texts/kiranavrtti.md) — ref: 1-6
  - [Mṛgendravṛtti](../texts/mrgendravrtti.md) — ref: vidyāpāda
### [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md)
Āṇava is eternal and is never destroyed; grace makes its power ineffective, like a snake's poison stilled by a mantra; knowledge given by grace (not the soul's own effort) accompanies this.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tiruvaruṭpayaṉ](../texts/tiruvarutpayan.md) — ref: ch.3
  - [Civañāṉa Cittiyār](../texts/sivananasiddhiyar.md) — ref: cupakkam.11

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Principles:** P1-level, P4-stage
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the two schools disagree on what mala is.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Siddhānta rejects any reading that makes mala unreal or knowledge sufficient; the Trika rejects mala as a substance.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: 'substance' and 'ignorance' describe the same bondage from an ontological (ritual) and an epistemic (gnostic) standpoint.; P4-stage: Abhinavagupta himself keeps initiation for those whose grace is not intense enough for knowledge alone, so the Siddhānta account could be read as addressed to that stage.; Not reconcilable while one side holds souls eternally distinct and the other holds them identical with Śiva.
