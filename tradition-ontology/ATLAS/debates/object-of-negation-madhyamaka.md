# What does Madhyamaka reasoning negate: inherent existence only, or all four extremes including existence itself?

`dsp:object-of-negation-madhyamaka` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Coverage:** G8 (related)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
The object of negation is inherent existence (existence from the object's own side, not posited by conceptual designation). Things are not negated conventionally; emptiness is a non-affirming negation of inherent existence, and conventional existence, cause and effect and valid cognition remain. To negate existence as such is to negate too much and fall into nihilism.
- If the negandum is not identified one cannot ascertain its absence (the thief and the house).
- Not being found by ultimate analysis is not the same as being refuted by it.
- Nāgārjuna's and Candrakīrti's negations of arising etc. carry an implicit qualifier 'ultimately / inherently' made explicit by the Prāsaṅgika commentators.

### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
The final view is freedom from elaborations: at the level of ultimate analysis all four extremes — existence, non-existence, both, neither — are negated without qualification, and clinging to emptiness as a mere negation is itself an extreme; Tsongkhapa's system, by adding the qualifier and holding the ultimate to be a non-affirming negation, is 'Madhyamaka of the nihilist extreme' and also leaves a subtle grasping.
- Nāgārjuna and Candrakīrti negate arising without qualification.
- Emptiness as an object of a conceptual negation is still an elaboration.
- Parting from the Four Attachments: 'if grasping arises, it is not the view'.
