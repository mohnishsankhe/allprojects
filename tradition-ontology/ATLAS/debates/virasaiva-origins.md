# Did the Vīraśaiva/Liṅgāyata tradition begin with Basava and the śaraṇas of twelfth-century Kalyāṇa, or is it a beginningless Śaiva dharma renewed in every age by the five ācāryas?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pañcācārya (Pañcapīṭha) tradition](../lineages/pancacarya.md)
Vīraśaiva dharma is anādi: Śiva taught it in the later portion of the Āgamas and to the Goddess and Nandin; Reṇuka emerged from the Someśvara liṅga at Kollipāki and taught Agastya; the five ācāryas appear from liṅgas in every yuga and founded the five pīṭhas. Basava is a great devotee and reviver within this stream.
- The SSM's own frame of transmission (1.28–31; 3.83–87; 4.1).
- The Āgamas 'from Kāmika to Vātula' teach Vīraśaiva doctrine in their uttara-bhāga (5.14).
- The Tattvapradīpikā already names the Pañcācārya lineage and links Siddharāma to Revaṇasiddha.
**Texts:** 
  - [Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi](../texts/siddhantasikhamani.md) — ref: 1.28–31; 3.83–87; 4.1; 5.14
  - [Tattvapradīpikā (Maritoṇṭadārya's commentary on the Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi)](../texts/tattvapradipika-maritontadarya.md) — ref: introduction; ch. 6 commentary
### [Śaraṇa–vacana tradition](../lineages/sarana-vacana.md)
The tradition was inaugurated by Basava with Allama Prabhu, Cennabasava, Akka Mahādēvi and the śaraṇas in Kalyāṇa; the vacanas are its founding scripture; the Pañcācārya narratives and the Sanskrit Āgamic framing come later.
- The vacanas and the earliest hagiographies (Harihara's ragaḷes) centre on Basava and the śaraṇas.
- The Śūnyasampādane presents Allama and Basava's Anubhava Maṇṭapa as the tradition's origin-scene.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** not yet reconciled: the two accounts differ on a historical question (who began the tradition and when) to which the eight principles do not apply as a hermeneutic
**The traditions' own objections:** Each strand holds its account as historical fact, not as a standpoint.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the Pañcācārya account speaks from the standpoint of the dharma's eternal transmission, the śaraṇa account from that of its historical renewal — each side rejects this as diminishing its account.; Two streams — a Śaiva ācārya/maṭha stream and the Kalyāṇa devotional movement — later merged (a scholarly hypothesis, labeled; it also invokes a Kālāmukha background, see brw:kalamukha-to-virasaiva).; Basava as reviver of an older dharma (the Pañcācārya reading) — rejected by the śaraṇa strand.

_Notes: Scholarly account (labeled): the śaraṇa movement is datable to the 12th c.; the Sanskrit Pañcācārya literature is attested later and its dating is debated; some scholars also point to pre-Basava Kannada Śaiva devotees (Dēvara Dāsimayya, Ēkānta Rāmayya) and to Kālāmukha antecedents._
