# Is perception only non-conceptual, or can a perception be determinate (conceptual)?

`dsp:is-perception-non-conceptual` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
Perception is free of conceptual construction; any cognition joined with names or genera is conceptual and not perception; that perception is non-conceptual is itself established by perception.
- PS 1.3; NB 1.4–6; PV 3.123–124 (conventional numbering)
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇasamuccaya](../texts/pramanasamuccaya.md) — ref: 1.3
  - [Nyāyabindu](../texts/nyayabindu.md) — ref: 1.4-6
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: 2.123-124 (e-text)
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Perception arises from contact of sense and object and is 'determinate' (vyavasāyātmaka); later Nyāya admits both an indeterminate and a determinate stage, both perceptual.
- Nyāya Sūtra 1.1.4; Vācaspati's commentaries (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.4
### [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md)
There is first a bare, non-conceptual 'seeing' (ālocana) and then a conceptual cognition of the same object; both are perception.
- Kumārila, Ślokavārttika, pratyakṣa chapter (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: pratyakṣa-pariccheda
### [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md)
There is no cognition in the world that is not accompanied by word; all knowledge appears as if interwoven with language (Bhartṛhari).
- Vākyapadīya, Brahmakāṇḍa (verse number differs by edition; recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Vākyapadīya](../texts/vakyapadiya.md) — ref: 1.123 / 1.131

**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the schools define perception differently.
**The traditions' own objections:** Dignāga's definition was framed against the Nyāya definition; Bhartṛhari's thesis denies any language-free cognition; each tradition regards its definition as the correct one.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the Buddhists call 'perception' only the first, non-conceptual moment; Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā extend the word to the conceptual judgment that follows — partly a difference of definition.; P1-level: the Buddhist particular is the ultimately real; the schools that accept real universals describe the conventional object.
