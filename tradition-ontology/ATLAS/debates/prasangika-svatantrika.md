# Should a Mādhyamika establish emptiness by autonomous inferences (svatantra-anumāna) or only by consequences (prasaṅga) that draw out untenable consequences of the opponent's own thesis — and does this difference of method reflect a difference of view?

`dsp:prasangika-svatantrika` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G8

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
Buddhapālita: things do not arise from themselves, because their arising would be pointless (what already exists need not arise again) and would never end — the argument proceeds by drawing out consequences (prasaṅga) of the opponent's thesis.
**Texts:** 
  - [Buddhapālita-Mūlamadhyamakavṛtti](../texts/buddhapalita-vrtti.md) — ref: 1.1
  - [Mūlamadhyamakakārikā](../texts/mulamadhyamakakarika.md) — ref: 1.1
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
Bhāviveka: Buddhapālita's argument is inadequate — it states no reason and example, does not answer the faults the opponent would raise, and being a consequence it implies by reversal that things arise from another, against the Mādhyamika's own tenet. The Mādhyamika should state an independent inference: 'ultimately the inner sense-bases do not arise from themselves, because they exist, like consciousness'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Prajñāpradīpa](../texts/prajnapradipa.md) — ref: 1.1
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
Candrakīrti: for one who is a Mādhyamika it is not right to state an autonomous inference, since he accepts no other position; as Āryadeva said, one who has no position — existent, non-existent or both — cannot be refuted; Nāgārjuna: 'if I had a thesis the fault would be mine; I have none'. Consequences suffice to show the opponent the untenable consequences of his own thesis; Buddhapālita was right.
**Texts:** 
  - [Prasannapadā](../texts/prasannapada.md) — ref: ch. 1
  - [Catuḥśataka](../texts/catuhsataka.md) — ref: 16.25
  - [Vigrahavyāvartanī](../texts/vigrahavyavartani.md) — ref: 29
### [Yogācāra-Madhyamaka](../lineages/yogacara-madhyamaka.md)
Śāntarakṣita: entities lack own-nature because they are neither one nor many, like a reflection; the conventional is what is agreeable only when unexamined; relying on mind-only one sees there are no external things, relying on the Middle Way that even mind lacks self — riding both vehicles one reaches the Mahāyāna.
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyamakālaṃkāra](../texts/madhyamakalamkara.md) — ref: 1; 64; 92-93
### [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
Tsongkhapa: the difference is one of view, not only of method — the Svātantrikas accept that things have their own character conventionally and therefore use inferences whose subject appears alike to both parties; the Prāsaṅgikas accept no inherent nature even conventionally, so no such common subject exists and they lead the opponent by consequences and inferences 'renowned to the other'; Prāsaṅgika is the higher view, and the object of negation must be identified exactly.
**Texts:** 
  - [Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to Enlightenment (lam rim chen mo)](../texts/lamrim-chenmo.md) — ref: insight section
  - [Essence of Eloquence (drang nges legs bshad snying po)](../texts/legs-bshad-snying-po.md) — ref: Madhyamaka section
### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
Gorampa: Tsongkhapa's adding of the qualifier 'inherently/truly' to what is negated is not in Nāgārjuna and Candrakīrti, who negate arising and existence without qualification; the Madhyamaka ultimate is freedom from all four extremes, and the negation of true existence alone is only a step.
**Texts:** 
  - [Distinguishing the Views (lta ba'i shan 'byed theg mchog gnad kyi zla zer)](../texts/taway-shenje.md) — ref: whole
### [Nyingma (the Ancient / Old Translation school)](../lineages/nyingma.md)
Mipham: for the provisional, enumerated ultimate, emptiness as the negation of true existence is taught (the Svātantrika emphasis); the actual, non-enumerated ultimate is freedom from all elaboration (the Prāsaṅgika emphasis); from the standpoint of that ultimate there is no thesis to hold, while conventionally the Mādhyamika asserts; the two are to be approached in sequence.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ngeshe Drönme (nges shes rin po che'i sgron me, the Beacon of Certainty)](../texts/ngeshe-dronme.md) — ref: questions 1, 7
  - [Norbu Ketaka (nor bu ke ta ka), Mipham's commentary on Bodhicaryāvatāra ch. 9](../texts/ketaka-mipham.md) — ref: whole
### [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
The difference is in the view, not only in method: Svātantrikas accept that phenomena exist by their own character conventionally and therefore use autonomous inferences; Prāsaṅgikas deny this even conventionally and so use consequences and 'other-acknowledged' inferences. Hence Prāsaṅgika's object of negation is subtler.
- The commonly appearing subject required for autonomous inference implies inherent existence.
**Texts:** 
  - [Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to Enlightenment (lam rim chen mo)](../texts/lamrim-chenmo.md) — ref: insight
  - [Essence of Eloquence (drang nges legs bshad snying po)](../texts/legs-bshad-snying-po.md) — ref: Madhyamaka
### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
The two differ only in how the view is generated in another's mind — by autonomous inference or by consequence — not in the view realised, which is freedom from elaboration for both (Gorampa).
- The ultimate realised by both is the same freedom from extremes.
**Texts:** 
  - [Distinguishing the Views (lta ba'i shan 'byed theg mchog gnad kyi zla zer)](../texts/taway-shenje.md) — 

**Historical debate — The Buddhapālita–Bhāviveka–Candrakīrti exchange on MMK 1.1** (c. 5th–7th c. CE (scholarly, approximate)): Buddhapālita-vṛtti 1.1; Bhāviveka's Prajñāpradīpa 1.1 criticises it; Candrakīrti's Prasannapadā 1.1 defends Buddhapālita and criticises Bhāviveka's use of autonomous inference. Indian: Candrakīrti had few Indian followers until Jayānanda and others (scholarly account, labelled); in Tibet his defence became the defining text of Prāsaṅgika.

CE onward): The labels thal 'gyur ba (Prāsaṅgika) and rang rgyud pa (Svātantrika) are Tibetan doxographic categories, commonly associated with the translation of Candrakīrti's works in the 11th–12th c. (Pa tshab Nyi ma grags); the Indian authors did not use them as names of schools. Recorded here as metadata only: this entry does not treat the categories as Indian self-designations. Gelug: Prāsaṅgika the highest view and different in view; Sakya (Gorampa) and Nyingma (Mipham): the difference concerns method and the approach to the ultimate, stated in varying ways.

**Historical debate — Gelug monastic debate on the Madhyamaka (tradition's account)** (from the 15th c. CE): In the Gelug monastic colleges the Madhyamaka is studied through Candrakīrti's Madhyamakāvatāra with Tsongkhapa's commentaries and the colleges' own textbooks, and the Prāsaṅgika–Svātantrika distinction is argued in formal courtyard debate (tradition's account; details moderate confidence). Gelug: the curriculum upholds Tsongkhapa's account.

**Historical debate — Mipham's exchanges with Gelug scholars** (late 19th c. ## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P6-upaya
**Explanation:** Partly reconciled in the Nyingma and Sakya traditions' own terms: the Svātantrika approach addresses the enumerated ultimate as a step and the Prāsaṅgika the non-enumerated freedom from extremes (P4-stage), and whether one argues by autonomous inference or by consequence is a matter of how the opponent is led (P6-upaya); the Gelug position that the two differ in view — acceptance of own-character conventionally — is not reconciled.
All the Indian parties agree that nothing arises from self, other, both or neither (MMK 1.1); the Indian exchange concerns method. The Tibetan schools disagree whether the method difference entails a difference in view. The categories themselves are Tibetan (recorded as metadata above).
**The traditions' own objections:** Candrakīrti: a Mādhyamika should not use autonomous inference at all (Prasannapadā 1.1); Bhāviveka: consequences alone are faulty (Prajñāpradīpa 1.1); Tsongkhapa: Svātantrikas leave a subtle object of negation unrefuted — a difference of view that a stage or skilful-means reading erases; Gorampa rejects Tsongkhapa's qualifier.
