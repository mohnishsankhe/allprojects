# Is brahman the persons in the sun, moon and other phenomena, or the self into which the sleeper withdraws?

`dsp:gargya-ajatasatru-debate` · `sourced` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Gārgya Bālāki: brahman is the person in the sun, the moon, lightning, space, wind, fire, water, a mirror, the sound following a walker, the quarters, the shadow, the person in the self (body).
- each is offered as 'brahman' to be venerated
**Texts:** 
  - [Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad](../texts/brhadaranyaka-upanisad.md) — ref: 2.1.1-15
  - [Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad](../texts/kausitaki-upanisad.md) — ref: 4.1-18
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Ajātaśatru: each of these is venerated only as a limited form with a limited fruit; brahman is the self into which the person made of understanding withdraws in sleep and from which all breaths, worlds and gods come forth like sparks — the real behind the real.
- the sleeping man does not answer to names but wakes when rubbed (BAU 2.1.15-17)
- sparks from fire (2.1.20)
**Texts:** 
  - [Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad](../texts/brhadaranyaka-upanisad.md) — ref: 2.1.16-20
  - [Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad](../texts/kausitaki-upanisad.md) — ref: 4.19-20

**Historical debate — Gārgya Bālāki and Ajātaśatru at Kāśī** (undated (Upaniṣadic narrative)): BAU 2.1; KauU 4 Gārgya falls silent and becomes the king's pupil; the king remarks that a brahmin being taught by a kṣatriya reverses the natural order

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level
**Explanation:** In the text itself Ajātaśatru accepts each of Gārgya's persons as an object of limited veneration and places brahman, the self of the sleeper, above them.
**The traditions' own objections:** The text treats Gārgya's claim that these are brahman as mistaken, not as a partial truth to be kept; the reconciliation preserves the text's ranking.
