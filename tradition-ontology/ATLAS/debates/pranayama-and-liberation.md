# Does breath-control (prāṇāyāma) serve meditation and liberation?

`dsp:pranayama-and-liberation` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B2 / G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Yes, as a preparatory limb: after posture, regulating the breath thins the covering of the light and makes the mind fit for concentration.
- YS 2.49–53.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali](../texts/yoga-sutra.md) — ref: 2.49-53
### [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
Yes: when the breath moves the mind moves; when the breath is still the mind is still — so the breath must be controlled.
- Haṭhayogapradīpikā 2.2 (as recalled).
**Texts:** 
  - [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) — ref: 2.2
### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
No, not as a means to liberation: restraining the breath causes pain, pain disturbs the mind, and the laborious filling, holding and expelling distract; stillness comes from indifference, not force (Hemacandra).
- YŚ 6.4–5; YŚ 12 (audāsīnya).
**Texts:** 
  - [Yogaśāstra (Hemacandra)](../texts/yogasastra-hemacandra.md) — ref: 6.4-5
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
Breath-control can steady the body and reveal omens, but pursued for powers it brings distress and is not the cause of liberation (Śubhacandra).
- Jñānārṇava, prāṇāyāma sections (recalled).
**Texts:** 
  - [Jñānārṇava](../texts/jnanarnava.md) — ref: prāṇāyāma sections
