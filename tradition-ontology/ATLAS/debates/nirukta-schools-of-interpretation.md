# How are the Vedic deities and stories to be interpreted — as natural powers, as persons of old narratives, or through the rite?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nairukta school (the Vedic etymologists)](../lineages/nairukta.md)
The etymologists: the figures are natural powers — Vṛtra is the cloud; the Aśvins are heaven and earth, or day and night, or sun and moon; the deities are three by station.

### [Aitihāsikas (the legend-school of Vedic interpretation)](../lineages/aitihasika.md)
The legend-tellers: the figures are persons of old stories — Vṛtra an asura, son of Tvaṣṭṛ; the Aśvins two pious kings; Yama and Yamī twins of Saraṇyū.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Yāska himself admits several planes of meaning — 'the flower and fruit of speech are the sacrifice and the deity, or the deity and the self' (Nirukta 1.20).
Later commentators (Durga, Sāyaṇa) read mantras on the planes of the sacrifice, the deities and the self.
**The traditions' own objections:** Each school regarded its own reading as primary; the reconciliation is Yāska's plural reading, not an assertion that the schools agreed.
