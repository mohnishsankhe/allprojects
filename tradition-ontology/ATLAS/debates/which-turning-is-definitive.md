# Which turning of the dharma-wheel is of definitive meaning (nītārtha): the teaching of emptiness (second) or the 'well-distinguished' teaching of the three natures and of buddha-nature (third)?

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
The sūtras teaching emptiness, the signless, no self and no being are definitive; those teaching self, a being, or a truly existent base are provisional (Akṣayamati); the tathāgatagarbha is emptiness taught with an intention (Laṅkāvatāra).
- The Akṣayamatinirdeśa's criterion of nītārtha.
- The Laṅkāvatāra's own gloss of the tathāgatagarbha as emptiness taught to remove fear of no-self.
- Candrakīrti (Madhyamakāvatāra 6.94-95, U40) reads the Laṅkāvatāra so.
**Texts:** 
  - [Akṣayamatinirdeśa-sūtra](../texts/aksayamatinirdesa.md) — ref: reliances section
  - [Laṅkāvatāra-sūtra](../texts/lankavatara-sutra.md) — ref: Vaidya p. 33
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
The first two turnings are surpassable and provisional; the third, which distinguishes the three natures and three naturelessnesses, is unsurpassed and definitive (Saṃdhinirmocana ch. 7).
- The Saṃdhinirmocana's own statement about the three turnings.
**Texts:** 
  - [Saṃdhinirmocana-sūtra](../texts/samdhinirmocana-sutra.md) — ref: ch. 7
### [Jonang (jo nang pa)](../lineages/jonang.md)
The third turning's tathāgatagarbha sūtras and the Ratnagotravibhāga are definitive: the ultimate (dharmakāya, buddha-nature) is empty of what is other than it — the adventitious stains — and not empty of its own qualities (shentong).
- Śrīmālā ch. 9 and RGV 1.154-155: empty of stains, not empty of qualities.
- Dolpopa's Mountain Doctrine (U48).
