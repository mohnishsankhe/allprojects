# Does killing prescribed in Vedic sacrifice incur demerit?

`dsp:does-sacrificial-killing-incur-demerit` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G (Sāṃkhya–Mīmāṃsā)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Yes, slightly: the Vedic means are impure because of the killing; the general prohibition of harm still applies.
- The Vedic means are bound up with impurity (SK 2), explained as the killing of animals (Gauḍapāda, Vācaspati).
- Pañcaśikha: 'a slight admixture, removable, bearable' — the demerit is small and expiable.
- The prohibition 'do not harm any being' and the injunction to offer the animal have different objects: one makes known harm to the agent, the other service to the sacrifice (Vācaspati).

### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
No: killing enjoined by the Veda for the sacrifice is dharma; the specific injunction prevails over the general prohibition.
- As stated by the opponent in the Tattvakaumudī on SK 2: the general rule 'do not harm' is set aside by the specific injunction to offer the animal to Agni-Soma.
- Mīmāṃsā's own discussion (Śabara on MS 1.1.2, the śyena rite) is recalled here, not checked.
