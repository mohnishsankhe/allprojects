# Is the killing of animals in Vedic sacrifice dharma?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
For animal sacrifice: in the dispute over the injunction 'one should sacrifice with aja', the gods held that aja means a goat (MBh 12.324.3); Syūmaraśmi, from within the cow, defends the Vedas' authority against Kapila (12.260).

### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Against it: the seers held that aja means seed-grain; Vasu fell for siding with the gods (12.324); Kapila, Tulādhāra and Vicakhnu praise non-violent sacrifice and say slaughter was introduced by the greedy (12.253–257, 12.260); the gleaner's merit is destroyed by the thought of killing (12.264); the mongoose ranks the gleaner's gift above the horse sacrifice (14.92–93); the Nārāyaṇīya's worship is without slaughter.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the epic records both the authority of Vedic animal sacrifice and a strong teaching of bloodless sacrifice.
**The traditions' own objections:** Mīmāṃsā holds the Vedic injunctions of animal sacrifice authoritative and the killing in them not sinful; Vicakhnu's claim that slaughter was introduced by the greedy is rejected by that view.
**Candidate readings:** P4-stage: animal sacrifice for householders with desires, non-violent worship for renouncers and ekāntins (Kapila: the āśramas' duties have one aim, 12.260.12); yuga reading: bloodless sacrifice belongs to the Kṛta age (12.336.58; Tulādhāra's 'as the good sacrificed of old'); P7-arthavāda: the praise of non-violent offerings as praise rather than prohibition
