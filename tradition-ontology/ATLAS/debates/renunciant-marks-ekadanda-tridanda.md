# Should the renouncer keep outer marks — triple staff, topknot, sacred thread — or abandon them (one staff, or none)?

`dsp:renunciant-marks-ekadanda-tridanda` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Saṃnyāsa — the renunciate ideal of the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads](../lineages/sannyasa.md)
Advaitin Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads: the paramahaṃsa abandons topknot and thread (knowledge is his topknot and thread); the staff of knowledge makes the true ekadaṇḍin; the highest renouncers bear no marks.
- Paramahaṃsa: the staff of knowledge vs the wooden staff
- Jābāla 6: marks cast into water
- Nāradaparivrājaka 3.77–85: shave with topknot, abandon outer thread
**Texts:**

  - [Nāradaparivrājaka Upaniṣad](../texts/naradaparivrajaka-upanisad.md) — ref: 3.77-85
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
Vaiṣṇava tridaṇḍin: the renouncer keeps triple staff, topknot and thread till death; they are Viṣṇu's marks, and abandoning them is a grave sin.
- Śāṭyāyanīya 6–11: the five articles kept till death; Viṣṇu's twofold mark
- Śāṭyāyanīya 26–29: abandoning the discipline equals killing a brāhmaṇa
**Texts:**

**Historical debate — Medieval disputes between Advaitin ekadaṇḍins and Śrīvaiṣṇava tridaṇḍins over the renouncer's marks** (c. 11th c. onward): Yādavaprakāśa's Yatidharmasamuccaya defends the tridaṇḍa renunciation with scriptural citations. Each order keeps its own marks.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P1-level
**Explanation:** Both sides also read the staff inwardly (three restraints; staff of knowledge), and the Advaitin texts grade marks by kind of renouncer, but the Śāṭyāyanīya binds all kinds to the marks.
Nāradaparivrājaka 5 and the Bhikṣuka let kuṭīcaka and bahūdaka keep marks while the paramahaṃsa drops them (P4); both traditions give the tridaṇḍa an inner meaning (Nāradaparivrājaka 6.5; Sannyāsa 2.97) (P1). The Śāṭyāyanīya's claim that all four kinds must keep the marks is not yet reconciled with the Advaitin paramahaṃsa.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Śāṭyāyanīya calls abandoning the marks a fall; the Paramahaṃsa calls the wooden staff without knowledge the way to hell.
