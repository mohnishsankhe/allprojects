# Is the ultimate personal and with qualities (saguṇa), impersonal and without qualities (nirguṇa), or both?

`dsp:saguna-nirguna` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G13

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Brahman is without attributes (nirguṇa): not even by place (adjunct) can it have a twofold character, for everywhere it is taught as attributeless; it is formless, that being the principal purport; texts about form are not purposeless, like light taking shapes. Brahman is known in two forms — qualified by adjuncts, as the object of worship in the state of ignorance, and free of them, as the object of knowledge; of 'is, shines, is dear, name and form', the first three are Brahman. (Madhusūdana Sarasvatī nonetheless closes: 'I know no reality higher than Kṛṣṇa'.)
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahma Sūtra](../texts/brahma-sutra.md) — ref: 3.2.11-21
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 1.1.12 intro
  - [Dṛg-Dṛśya Viveka (Vākyasudhā)](../texts/drg-drsya-viveka.md) — ref: 20
  - [Gūḍhārthadīpikā](../texts/gudharthadipika.md) — ref: closing verse
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
No means of knowledge establishes an undifferentiated object — even the first, non-conceptual perception grasps a qualified thing; Brahman is ever qualified, free of all defects and full of auspicious qualities (twofold mark, ubhaya-liṅga); 'not so, not so' denies only that Brahman is limited to what was just described. The texts calling Brahman nirguṇa deny bad qualities.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1; 3.2.11-22
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
All Viṣṇu's qualities are real; he is full of infinite qualities and free of defects; even the names of other gods in the Veda are his names; his descents are not births among beings.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahābhārata-tātparya-nirṇaya](../texts/mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya.md) — ref: 1.68-71
  - [Viṣṇutattvavinirṇaya](../texts/visnutattvavinirnaya.md) — ref: ch. 3
  - [Karmanirṇaya](../texts/karmanirnaya.md) — ref: 1
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
The knowers of reality call the one non-dual knowledge by three names — Brahman, Paramātman and Bhagavān: realised without its distinctions and powers it appears as Brahman, the effulgence of Bhagavān's body; Bhagavān, Kṛṣṇa, is its fullest disclosure. Caitanya reads the sūtras and Upaniṣads as speaking directly of the personal Lord.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../texts/bhagavata-purana.md) — ref: 1.2.11
  - [Bhagavatsandarbha](../texts/bhagavat-sandarbha.md) — ref: Brahman section
  - [Brahma Saṃhitā (fifth chapter)](../texts/brahma-samhita.md) — ref: 5.40
  - [Caitanya Caritāmṛta](../texts/caitanya-caritamrta.md) — ref: Madhya 6
### [Puṣṭimārga (Vallabha Sampradāya; Śuddhādvaita Brahmavāda)](../lineages/pustimarga.md)
Brahman is the locus of contradictory attributes (viruddha-dharmāśraya): scripture's statements that it is attributeless and full of qualities, formless and with form, are all true of it.
**Texts:** 
  - [Aṇubhāṣya (Brahmasūtrāṇubhāṣya)](../texts/anubhasya.md) — ref: 3.2
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
The Gītā ch. 12: those who worship the Lord with form, ever yoked with supreme faith, are held the most yoked; those who worship the imperishable, indefinable, unmanifest also reach him, but greater is the difficulty of those set on the unmanifest, for that goal is hard for the embodied. 'I am the foundation of Brahman'; the supreme person is beyond the perishable and the imperishable.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) — ref: 12.1-5; 14.27; 15.16-18
### [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md)
Kabīr: repeat the name of the attributeless (nirguṇa) Rām; fix the mind where there is no form or sign; the son of Daśaratha is praised in the three worlds, but the secret of Rām's name is other; if worshipping a stone brought Hari I would worship a mountain.
**Texts:** 
  - [Kabīr Granthāvalī](../texts/kabir-granthavali.md) — ref: as recorded
  - [The oral and popular corpus of Kabīr (sākhīs, dohās, padas and bhajans)](../texts/kabir-oral-corpus.md) — ref: as recorded
### [Rāmānandī sampradāya](../lineages/ramanandi.md)
Tulsīdās: there is no difference at all between the embodied (saguṇa) and the formless (aguṇa), sing the sages; Bhuśuṇḍi, taught the formless by Lomaśa, kept asking for the Rāma with form and was cursed and then blessed for his devotion.
**Texts:** 
  - [Rāmcaritmānas](../texts/ramcaritmanas.md) — ref: 1.116; 7.110-113
  - [Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa](../texts/adhyatma-ramayana.md) — ref: 1.1
### [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
In truth Bhairava is not the ninefold, not the mass of sounds, not the three-headed god, not made of nāda and bindu — these are told to the unawakened, like a mother's sweets, to make them take up practice; the Tāntrikas hold the self transcends the universe, the Kula schools that it is the universe, the Trika that it is both.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vijñānabhairava](../texts/vijnana-bhairava-tantra.md) — ref: 7-13
  - [Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya](../texts/pratyabhijnahrdayam.md) — ref: 8 with commentary
### [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md)
The Goddess is without support, stainless, eternal, formless, without qualities — and she is the manifest and the unmanifest; meditation on her is of two kinds, with form and formless.
**Texts:** 
  - [Lalitā Sahasranāma](../texts/lalita-sahasranama.md) — ref: 43-45; 86-87
  - [Mahānirvāṇa Tantra](../texts/mahanirvana-tantra.md) — ref: 5.137-140
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
One God hidden in all beings, all-pervading, the inner self of all, overseer of actions, the witness, pure consciousness, alone and without qualities (Śvetāśvatara) — the Upaniṣad names the personal God with the attributeless predicates.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad](../texts/svetasvatara-upanisad.md) — ref: 6.7-11

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path, P1-level, P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Reconciled in part in the traditions' own terms: the Gītā has both the worshipper of the Lord with form and the worshipper of the unmanifest reach him, the latter with greater difficulty (P3-path); Advaita places the qualified Brahman as the object of worship and the unqualified as the object of knowledge (P1-level); the Bhāgavata names one non-dual reality Brahman, Paramātman and Bhagavān by approach, Tulsīdās and the Trika hold both true (P2-standpoint); which is higher remains not yet reconciled.
Each reconciler ranks: Advaita puts nirguṇa above saguṇa (the latter vyāvahārika); the Gauḍīyas put Bhagavān above Brahman; Rāmānuja and Madhva deny an attributeless Brahman altogether; Kabīr and the Sants deny that the avatāra is the true Rām. The disputed point is therefore the ranking and the reality-status of the qualities, not whether both idioms occur in scripture.
**The traditions' own objections:** Rāmānuja: no means of knowledge establishes an undifferentiated reality (Śrībhāṣya 1.1.1); Madhva: all Viṣṇu's qualities are real; Advaita: qualities belong to Brahman only through adjuncts (BS 3.2.11; BSBh 1.1.12); Gauḍīyas: the impersonal Brahman is the effulgence of Bhagavān, a partial realization; Kabīr: the Rām of the Name is not Daśaratha's son.
