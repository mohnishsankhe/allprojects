# Are Pūrva and Uttara Mīmāṃsā one continuous śāstra, so that inquiry into dharma must precede inquiry into Brahman?

`dsp:purva-uttara-mimamsa-unity` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
Yes: the Vṛttikāra cited by Rāmānuja says the inquiry into Brahman follows the knowledge of rites, and that the Śārīraka is joined with Jaimini's work of sixteen chapters to form one śāstra; Vedānta Deśika's Seśvaramīmāṃsā reads the Mīmāṃsā Sūtra accordingly.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1
  - [Seśvaramīmāṃsā](../texts/sesvara-mimamsa.md) — ref: whole
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
No: the 'then' (atha) of BS 1.1.1 follows the fourfold qualification (discrimination, dispassion, the six virtues, longing for release), not the inquiry into dharma; the two inquiries differ in object, fruit and eligible person.
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 1.1.1
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
Mīmāṃsā authors acknowledge Vedānta as a related study: Upavarṣa deferred the proof of the self to the Śārīraka (per Śaṅkara), Kumārila says Vedānta makes self-knowledge firm, and later Bhāṭṭas read the Upaniṣads as serving rites and meditations.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: ātmavāda 148
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 3.3.53

**The traditions' own objections:** Rāmānuja's school holds the unity as textual (one work, twenty chapters); Advaita denies that knowledge of rites is a prerequisite.
**Candidate readings:** P4-stage: ritual inquiry as a preparation for some students, not a necessary precondition for all — Advaita accepts this for purification, Viśiṣṭādvaita insists on the continuity of the śāstra.
