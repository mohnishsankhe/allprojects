# What is the self common to all men (vaiśvānara)?

`dsp:vaisvanara-six-views` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Each of the five householders and Uddālaka venerates one thing as the vaiśvānara self: the sky, the sun, the wind, space, water or the earth.
- each view had its own fruit
**Texts:** 
  - [Chāndogya Upaniṣad](../texts/chandogya-upanisad.md) — ref: 5.12-5.17
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Aśvapati Kaikeya: each is only one part (head, eye, breath, trunk, bladder, feet); the vaiśvānara self is to be venerated as the whole, a span in size and beyond measure.
- the whole eats food in all worlds, beings and selves (5.18.1)
**Texts:** 
  - [Chāndogya Upaniṣad](../texts/chandogya-upanisad.md) — ref: 5.18

**Historical debate — Aśvapati instructs the six brahmins** (undated (Upaniṣadic narrative)): ChU 5.11-18 The brahmins accept the king's teaching of the whole

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The text itself treats each view as a true but partial aspect of one whole.
**The traditions' own objections:** Aśvapati warns that each partial view alone would have brought harm; the parts are not sufficient in isolation.
