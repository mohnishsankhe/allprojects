# How many tastes (rasa) are there?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md)
One (Bhadrakāpya) — not other than water; two (Śākunteya) — eliminating and pacifying; three (Pūrṇākṣa Maudgalya); four (Hiraṇyākṣa) by wholesomeness and sweetness; five (Kumāraśiras) by the elements; six (Vāyorvida) by qualities; seven (Nimi) adding alkali; eight (Baḍiśa) adding the unmanifest; innumerable (Kāṅkāyana) by substrates.
**Texts:** 
  - [Caraka Saṃhitā](../texts/caraka-samhita.md) — ref: Sū 26.8
### [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md)
Ātreya: six — sweet, sour, salt, pungent, bitter, astringent; water is their source; alkali is a substance, the unmanifest a state, and innumerability belongs to the substrates.
**Texts:** 
  - [Caraka Saṃhitā](../texts/caraka-samhita.md) — ref: Sū 26.9

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Ātreya shows that most counts classify tastes by another standpoint (source, action, element, quality, substrate) while rejecting alkali and the unmanifest as tastes.
**The traditions' own objections:** Ātreya explicitly denies that alkali and the unmanifest are tastes and that tastes are innumerable.
