# Are the Old Tantras (rnying rgyud) authentic Indian scripture?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Kadam](../lineages/kadam.md)
Critics of the later diffusion questioned tantras translated in the early period for which no Indian original was known, and condemned practices they held to be perversions of tantra.
- No Sanskrit originals were found in India for many Old Tantras.
- Royal ordinances of the western Tibetan kings (Lha bla ma Yeshe Ö, late 10th c.; Zhiwa Ö, late 11th c.) and a polemic attributed to 'Gö Khukpa Lhetse (Refutation of Perverted Mantras) rejected certain tantras and practices.
- Butön excluded most Old Tantras from his canon for lack of Indian originals.
### [Nyingma (the Ancient / Old Translation school)](../lineages/nyingma.md)
The Old Tantras were translated under imperial sponsorship by Indian masters and Tibetan translators; their Indian lineages are recorded; the Guhyagarbha has Indian commentaries and a Sanskrit manuscript was reported found at Samye; later editors placed an Old Tantra section in the Kangyur.
- Colophons name Indian translators (e.g. Śrī Siṃha and Vairocana for the Kunjed Gyalpo; Dharmabodhi, Dānarakṣita and Che btsan skyes for the Dupa Do).
- The Derge Kangyur contains an Old Tantra section.
- Nyingma apologists (e.g. Sokdokpa Lodrö Gyaltsen, 16th c.) answered the critiques point by point.
