# Are the Vedic rites that hold out heaven, enjoyment and power all there is to seek ('there is nothing else')?

`dsp:bhagavad-gita-vedavada` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### None
There is nothing else (nānyad astīti): the goal is heaven, enjoyment and power, obtained through the many particular rites, with birth as the fruit of action.
- (Known only through the Gītā's report.) They are full of desire and intent on heaven, hold that there is nothing beyond, and their speech, abounding in particular rites aimed at enjoyment and power, offers birth as the fruit of action (2.42–43).

### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Such speech is 'flowery' and proclaimed by the undiscerning; those carried away by it lack resolute understanding established in samādhi; the Vedas have the three guṇas as their domain and one should be free of them; for the knowing brāhmaṇa all the Vedas are of as much use as a well in a flood.
- For those attached to enjoyment and power, whose minds are carried away by that speech, the resolute understanding is not established in samādhi (2.44).
- The Vedas have the three guṇas as their domain; be free of the three guṇas and of the pairs of opposites, free of acquiring and keeping, possessed of the self (2.45).
- For the knowing brāhmaṇa all the Vedas are of as much use as a well amid a flood (2.46) — a simile the commentators read in opposite directions.
- The understanding must become indifferent to what is to be heard and what has been heard, and stand immovable in samādhi though perplexed by what it has heard (2.52–53).
- The same speaker enjoins action for the sake of sacrifice and the keeping of the sacrificial wheel (3.9–16); the criticism of 2.42–44 is aimed at those who are desire-souled, intent on heaven and say 'there is nothing else'.
