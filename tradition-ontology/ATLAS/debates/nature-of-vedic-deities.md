# Are the Vedic gods embodied persons, or are they what the mantras name?

`dsp:nature-of-vedic-deities` · `sourced` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Yāska reports three views: the gods are like persons (they are praised and addressed as sentient beings with limbs and belongings); they are not like persons (what is seen of them is fire, wind, sun, earth); or both.
- Hymns address the gods as persons; their visible forms are natural powers.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 7.6–7
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
For Mīmāṃsā the deity of a rite is what the mantra-word designates as recipient of the offering; embodied gods who eat the offerings and grant rewards are not needed — the rite produces its result (apūrva) itself.
- What the Veda requires for the rite is only the word naming the recipient.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śābarabhāṣya](../texts/sabara-bhasya.md) — ref: (on the devatā sections; locus from memory)
### [Vedānta (Uttara Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/vedanta.md)
The gods are embodied, can take several bodies at once, and are eligible for the knowledge of Brahman (Brahma Sūtra 1.3.26–33, with Śaṅkara).
- Scripture speaks of the gods' forms and of their seeking knowledge (e.g. Indra with Prajāpati).
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahma Sūtra](../texts/brahma-sutra.md) — ref: 1.3.26–33

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Yāska's own third answer — the gods may be 'of both kinds' — treats the personal and the non-personal descriptions as standpoints (as addressed in praise, as seen in nature).
The Mīmāṃsā–Vedānta disagreement remains: it concerns what the Veda requires one to accept, not only how to describe.
**The traditions' own objections:** Mīmāṃsakas reject embodied gods as unnecessary; Vedāntins reject the word-only deity. Neither accepts the other's view as a mere standpoint.
