# Is śānta (peace) a rasa, and what is its stable emotion?

`dsp:santa-rasa` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
Yes: the Nāṭyaśāstra's śānta passage (in some recensions) makes calm its stable emotion and liberation its aim; Ānandavardhana makes it the principal rasa of the Mahābhārata; Abhinavagupta makes knowledge of reality — the self — its stable basis and the ground of all rasas; Mammaṭa (dispassion) and Viśvanātha (calm) count it the ninth.
- the Mahābhārata's purport is dispassion and liberation
- knowledge of reality underlies all emotions as their natural state

  - [Dhvanyāloka of Ānandavardhana](../texts/dhvanyaloka.md) — ref: 3.26 vṛtti; 4.5 vṛtti

  - [Sāhityadarpaṇa of Viśvanātha Kavirāja](../texts/sahityadarpana.md) — ref: 3.245-250
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
No, not in drama: Bharata counts eight rasas; calm, being the cessation of all activity, cannot be developed or shown on stage (Dhanañjaya and Dhanika).
- the Nāṭyaśāstra's own count is eight (NŚ 6.15)
- calm cannot be represented by action
**Texts:** 
  - [Nāṭyaśāstra](../texts/natyasastra.md) — ref: 6.15
  - [Daśarūpaka of Dhanañjaya](../texts/dasarupaka.md) — ref: 4.35 (number uncertain)
  - [Avaloka of Dhanika on the Daśarūpaka](../texts/dasarupavaloka.md) — ref: on 4.35
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
Rūpa Gosvāmī counts śānta as the first and least intimate of the five primary devotional rasas — awareness of the Lord's greatness without personal love.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhaktirasāmṛtasindhu](../texts/bhaktirasamrtasindhu.md) — ref: 3

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Much of the disagreement concerns the medium: the deniers speak of dramatic performance (nāṭya), the affirmers of poetry and of the audience's experience; in poetry both sides grant śānta.
Abhinavagupta rejects the restriction and argues for śānta in drama too; the reconciliation holds only for the poetry/drama distinction.
**The traditions' own objections:** Abhinavagupta insists śānta is a rasa in drama; Dhanika denies calm can be a stable emotion at all.
