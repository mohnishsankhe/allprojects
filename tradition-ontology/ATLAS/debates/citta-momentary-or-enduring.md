# Is the mind a series of momentary cognitions, or one enduring mind with many objects?

`dsp:citta-momentary-or-enduring` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
The mind is one, has many objects and endures; properties inhere in an enduring property-bearer.
- If the mind were momentary and tied to one object per cognition, every mind would be one-pointed and none distracted (YBh 1.32).
- Memory and the enjoyment of karma require one experiencer (YBh 1.32, 3.14).
- The self-experienced 'I who saw now touch' requires one cognizer (YBh 1.32).
**Texts:** 
  - [Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra)](../texts/yoga-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.32; 3.14
### [Sautrāntika](../lineages/sautrantika.md)
All conditioned things, cognition included, are momentary; continuity is that of a series (santāna), not of an enduring substrate.
- Momentariness follows from destruction being uncaused (Buddhist argument).
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhidharmakośa (Abhidharmakośakārikā)](../texts/abhidharmakosa.md) — ref: 4.2-3
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: svārthānumāna chapter
