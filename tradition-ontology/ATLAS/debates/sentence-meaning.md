# How does a sentence convey its meaning: by the connection of word-meanings already expressed (abhihitānvaya) or by words that express meanings already connected (anvitābhidhāna)?

`dsp:sentence-meaning` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md)
Words convey their own meanings and cease; the expressed meanings, through expectancy, compatibility and proximity, yield the sentence-meaning, the word-meanings being an intermediate step like the flame in cooking.
- Śabara on MS 1.1.25; ŚV vākya 342–343; defended by Vācaspati's Tattvabindu.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śābarabhāṣya](../texts/sabara-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.25
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: vākyādhikaraṇa 342–343
  - [Tattvabindu](../texts/tattvabindu.md) — ref: whole
### [Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/prabhakara-mimamsa.md)
Words express their meanings only as connected with one another and with the 'to-be-done', since word-meaning is learned from elders' action-guiding usage; the Bhāṭṭa appeal to secondary signification ('the sentence-meaning is always indicated') is rejected.
- Prakaraṇapañcikā reports and answers the Bhāṭṭa saying 'vākyārtho lakṣyamāṇo hi sarvatraiveti naḥ sthitiḥ'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Prakaraṇapañcikā](../texts/prakaranapancika.md) — ref: ch. 7; the passage citing the Bhāṭṭa saying
### [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md)
The sentence is an indivisible unit (vākya-sphoṭa) whose meaning flashes as an intuition (pratibhā); word-meanings are abstractions.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vākyapadīya](../texts/vakyapadiya.md) — ref: book 2
