# Are the individual souls one with Brahman/God, eternally distinct from him and from one another, or both different and non-different?

`dsp:souls-one-or-distinct` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The individual self is Brahman alone: 'that you are', 'I am Brahman', 'this self is Brahman'. The one Lord is the knower of the field in all fields; the plurality and transmigration of souls belong to adjuncts. Of the three early teachers in the Brahmasūtra, Kāśakṛtsna's view — that the supreme Lord himself abides as the individual self — is the one in accord with scripture.
**Texts:** 
  - [Chāndogya Upaniṣad](../texts/chandogya-upanisad.md) — ref: 6.8.7
  - [Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad](../texts/brhadaranyaka-upanisad.md) — ref: 1.4.10
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 1.4.19-22
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Śaṅkara](../texts/gita-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 13.2
  - [Vedāntasāra](../texts/vedantasara.md) — ref: tat tvam asi
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
Difference is real and eternal — fivefold (pañca-bheda): between the Lord and souls, the Lord and matter, soul and soul, soul and matter, and matter and matter. 'Tat tvam asi' does not teach identity (it is read as 'sa ātmā atat tvam asi', 'you are not that', or of the soul's dependence on and likeness to the Lord); those who, proud with 'I am Brahman', read the Veda as oneness go to darkness. Liberated souls remain distinct and graded.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahābhārata-tātparya-nirṇaya](../texts/mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya.md) — ref: 1.68-71
  - [Chāndogyopaniṣad-bhāṣya (Madhva)](../texts/chandogya-upanisad-bhasya-madhva.md) — ref: 6.8.7
  - [Viṣṇutattvavinirṇaya](../texts/visnutattvavinirnaya.md) — ref: ch. 1
  - [Tattvoddyota](../texts/tattvoddyota.md) — ref: opening
  - [Nyāyāmṛta](../texts/nyayamrta.md) — ref: ch. 2
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
Souls are eternal and many and are modes and body of Brahman, inseparable from him (apṛthak-siddhi). In 'tat tvam asi' both words denote Brahman — 'that' as the cause of the world, 'you' as having the soul as its body; scripture's texts of difference, of non-difference and the mediating texts are all true. The self is a part of Brahman as an attribute is part of what it qualifies; the Gītā's 'never was there a time when I was not, nor you' teaches the eternal plurality of selves.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vedārthasaṅgraha](../texts/vedarthasangraha.md) — ref: on tat tvam asi; ghaṭaka-śruti
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 2.3.43; 4.4
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Rāmānuja](../texts/gita-bhasya-ramanuja.md) — ref: 2.12
### [Bhedābheda Vedānta (Bhāskara's school and the early difference-and-non-difference Vedāntins)](../lineages/bhedabheda.md)
The soul is a part (aṃśa) of Brahman, different from it by its limiting adjuncts and non-different in essence (Bhāskara: difference adventitious).
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Bhāskara (Bhāskarabhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-bhaskara.md) — ref: 2.3.43
### [Dvaitādvaita (Nimbārka Sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaitadvaita.md)
Because scripture declares both, the soul is a part of Brahman both different and non-different by nature (svābhāvika), like the snake and its coils; the released soul abides in non-separation from Brahman while remaining a distinct knower.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vedāntapārijātasaurabha](../texts/vedanta-parijata-saurabha.md) — ref: 2.3.43; 3.2.27; 4.4.4
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
The souls are the Lord's marginal power, eternally distinct from yet dependent on him — inconceivable difference and non-difference (acintya-bhedābheda).
**Texts:** 
  - [Paramātmasandarbha](../texts/paramatma-sandarbha.md) — ref: jīva section
### [Vedānta (Uttara Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/vedanta.md)
The Brahmasūtra itself records three earlier views on the passages that identify the self with Brahman: Āśmarathya (identity as the fulfilment of the promise that by knowing one all is known), Auḍulomi (identity when the soul departs in liberation), Kāśakṛtsna (the supreme self abides as the individual self). The soul is called a part 'because of the declarations of difference and otherwise'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahma Sūtra](../texts/brahma-sutra.md) — ref: 1.4.20-22; 2.3.43
### [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md)
The Lord is one with souls and yet other than them, and with them — like the soul with the body, the vowel with the consonants; non-duality means inseparability, not identity; the liberated soul becomes equal to Śiva but remains a soul.
**Texts:** 
  - [Civañāṉa Pōtam](../texts/sivananabodham.md) — ref: sūtra 2
  - [Civañāṉa Cittiyār](../texts/sivananasiddhiyar.md) — ref: cupakkam 2
### [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
Maheśvara alone is the self of all beings, of the form of the universe, full of the undivided awareness 'I'; the liberated becomes (like) Śiva.
**Texts:** 
  - [Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā](../texts/isvarapratyabhijna-karika.md) — ref: 4.1
  - [Śivasūtra](../texts/siva-sutra.md) — ref: 3.25
### [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md)
Selves are many, not one: though pleasure, pain and cognition arise alike, their distinct states (bondage, release, experience) show plurality.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vaiśeṣika Sūtra](../texts/vaisesika-sutra.md) — ref: 3.2.15-17
  - [Tarkasaṅgraha](../texts/tarkasangraha.md) — ref: ātman
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Though the seen has ceased for the one whose purpose is fulfilled, it has not ceased for others, being common to them: puruṣas are many.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali](../texts/yoga-sutra.md) — ref: 2.22
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Asked whether there are many puruṣas or one, the Mokṣadharma reports that the Sāṃkhya-Yoga thinkers hold many, and teaches that all are grounded in the one Puruṣa.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mokṣadharma (Mokṣadharmaparvan)](../texts/moksadharma.md) — ref: 12.338
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
Souls are infinitely many, each an independent substance; absolute non-duality is refuted.
**Texts:** 
  - [Āptamīmāṃsā](../texts/aptamimamsa.md) — ref: 24-27

**Historical debate — Nyāyāmṛta and Advaitasiddhi on the Upaniṣadic identity sentences** (16th–17th c. CE (scholarly, approximate)): Nyāyāmṛta ch. 2 denies that 'tat tvam asi' and 'satyaṃ jñānam anantam' convey an impartite (akhaṇḍa) meaning; the Advaitasiddhi replies (see also dsp:world-real-or-appearance). Each tradition holds its own reading established.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: identity (Advaita), eternal difference (Dvaita) and qualified or natural difference-in-non-difference (Viśiṣṭādvaita, Bhedābheda, Śaiva Siddhānta) are each defended as the purport of the same texts.
The Brahmasūtra already records the plurality of readings (1.4.20-22). Advaita holds difference to be empirical; the bhedābheda schools hold difference and non-difference both real; Dvaita holds difference real in every state including release.
**The traditions' own objections:** Madhva brands the identity reading an asura view (Chāndogya-bhāṣya 6.8.7) and holds the five differences real (MBhTN 1.68-71); Advaita holds difference due to adjuncts only (BSBh 1.4.22); Rāmānuja rejects both identity of essence and absolute difference (Vedārthasaṃgraha).
