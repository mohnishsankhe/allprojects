# Are the four orders of life a free choice, a fixed sequence, or is only the householder's order valid?

`dsp:asrama-vikalpa-samuccaya-badha` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Coverage:** D (life stages)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Option (vikalpa): after completing studentship one may enter whichever order one wishes; each, lived by its rules, leads to safety.
- VDh 7.3 lets the student who has kept his vow choose any order
- GDh 3.1 reports 'some' who allow a choice
- ĀpDh 2.9.21.2: in any order, lived undistracted, one attains safety
**Texts:**

  - [Gautama Dharmasūtra](../texts/gautama-dharmasutra.md) — ref: 3.1
  - [Āpastamba Dharmasūtra](../texts/apastamba-dharmasutra.md) — ref: 2.9.21.1-2
### [Saṃnyāsa — the renunciate ideal of the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads](../lineages/sannyasa.md)
Option by dispassion: one may renounce from studentship, household or forest — on the very day one becomes dispassionate.
- Jābāla Upaniṣad 4 (as usually cited): 'yad ahar eva virajet tad ahar eva pravrajet'
**Texts:** 
  - [Jābāla Upaniṣad](../texts/jabala-upanisad.md) — ref: 4
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Sequence (samuccaya): student, householder, forest-dweller, renouncer in due order; the debts to seers, gods and ancestors must be paid before the mind is set on liberation.
- MDh 6.33–37: renounce in the fourth quarter of life; seeking liberation before paying the debts one sinks down
- MDh 6.88: followed in order, all four lead to the highest state
**Texts:** 
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: 6.33-37
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: 6.87-90
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Sublation (bādha, aikāśramya): there is only one order, the householder's, because only it is directly enjoined in the Veda and the others do not beget offspring; the division of orders was made by the asura Kapila to rival the gods.
- GDh 3.36: 'the teachers' hold there is one order
- BDh 2.6.11.27–28: the Kapila story; the wise should not heed the division
- ĀpDh 2.9.21.14–16: the claim that one attains safety on awakening is contradicted by the śāstras, for the knower would feel no pain here
- the Vedic debts (TS 6.3.10.5) presuppose the householder
**Texts:**

**Historical debate — Āpastamba on the renouncer's claim** (Dharmasūtra period): ĀpDh 2.9.21–24 quotes the renouncers' view and verses praising the celibate path of the 88,000 seers, then argues that one lives on in one's offspring. Āpastamba favours the householder while listing four orders.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The later synthesis grades the orders by qualification: the sequence for most, early renunciation only for one whose dispassion is complete.
Manu's sequence with debts first and the Jābāla option 'on the day one becomes dispassionate' are read as instructions for different adhikārins; Manu's own 'Veda-renouncer' who stays at home (6.86–96) is a further intermediate form.
**The traditions' own objections:** The aikāśramya teachers (GDh 3.36; BDh 2.6.11.27–28) deny any Vedic basis to the celibate orders and so reject grading them; Manu insists the debts be paid first (6.35–37); Āpastamba denies that knowledge alone brings safety in this life (2.9.21.14–16).
