# Can an arahant fall away from arahantship?

`dsp:kv-arahant-falling-away` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md)
An arahant can fall away from arahantship (held, per the commentary, by Sammitīyas, Vajjiputtakas, Sabbatthivādins and some Mahāsaṅghikas).
**Texts:** 
  - [Kathāvatthu](../texts/kathavatthu.md) — ref: 1.2
### [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
One whose fetters are destroyed by the path cannot fall away; only mundane attainments (jhānas, 'temporary liberation') can be lost.
- The paths cut off the defilements; what is cut off does not return.
- The Puggalapaññatti distinguishes persons liable to fall away (from attainments) from those not liable.
**Texts:** 
  - [Kathāvatthu](../texts/kathavatthu.md) — ref: 1.2

**Historical debate — Third Council at Pāṭaliputta** (3rd c. BCE (tradition: reign of Asoka)): Theravāda chronicles and the Samantapāsādikā: holders of wrong views were expelled and the Kathāvatthu was recited Theravāda: the Vibhajjavāda confirmed as the Buddha's teaching; other schools' accounts of the period differ

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** What can be lost is the pleasant abiding of attainments, not the destruction of the fetters — the two sides may speak of different things.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Theravāda denies any falling from arahantship itself; the reported opponents held that arahantship proper can be lost.
