# Is consciousness reflexively aware of itself (svasaṃvedana)?

`dsp:svasamvedana` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
Every cognition is aware of itself as it is aware of its object; this self-awareness is the result of the means of knowledge, and memory of the experience proves it.
- A cognition that is not itself known could not make its object known.
- Memory of having seen presupposes self-awareness.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇasamuccaya](../texts/pramanasamuccaya.md) — ref: ch.1
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — 
### [Yogācāra-Madhyamaka](../lineages/yogacara-madhyamaka.md)
Accepted conventionally: consciousness arises as the opposite of the inert, and its not being inert is its self-awareness; it is not an act of an agent on an object.
- Self-awareness is the mark of consciousness (MAL 16–17).
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyamakālaṃkāra](../texts/madhyamakalamkara.md) — ref: 16-17
### [Prāsaṅgika-Madhyamaka](../lineages/prasangika.md)
Rejected even conventionally: mind does not see mind, as a sword's edge does not cut itself; memory is explained without self-awareness.
- The Buddha said mind does not see mind (BCA 9.17).
- Memory does not require reflexive awareness (MA 6.73–75).
**Texts:** 
  - [Bodhicaryāvatāra](../texts/bodhicaryavatara.md) — ref: 9.17-25
  - [Madhyamakāvatāra](../texts/madhyamakavatara.md) — ref: 6.72-77

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Prāsaṅgika refuses self-awareness even at the conventional level, so P1-level alone does not bridge the positions.
**Candidate readings:** P1-level: self-awareness as conventional description only, never ultimately (Śāntarakṣita).; P2-standpoint: epistemological (pramāṇa) standpoint vs. soteriological analysis of the self.; P6-upaya: accepted as a device for refuting external objects, then discarded.
