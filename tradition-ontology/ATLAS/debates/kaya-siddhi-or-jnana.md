# Must the body be perfected and made deathless (kāya-citti, kaṟpam) for liberation, or is knowledge alone enough, the body being a pot to be seen through?

`dsp:kaya-siddhi-or-jnana` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md)
The body must be perfected: when it perishes the life perishes and firm true knowledge cannot be reached; nourishing the body nourishes the life (TM 724); kāya-citti removes karma (Tiruvaḷḷuvar ñāṉam 14); vāci and kaṟpam give a lasting body (TM 722, 727).
- Without a lasting body there is no time to reach knowledge (TM 724).
- The Lord has made the body his temple (TM 725).

  - [Tirumantiram](../texts/tirumantiram.md) — ref: 722–727
  - [Tiruvaḷḷuvar ñāṉam (the wisdom of Tiruvaḷḷuvar)](../texts/tiruvalluvar-nanam.md) — ref: 14, 17
  - [Pōkar ēḻāyiram (Bogar's Seven Thousand; Captakāṇṭam)](../texts/bogar-7000.md) — ref: kaṟpam sections
### [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md)
Knowledge and grace alone: for the knower who has seen the true thing, what need of kaṟpam or yoga? (Kuṭampai 2, 22); stilling the mind is higher than every power (Tāyumāṉavar); the only medicine against death is never forgetting the Lord's feet (Paṭṭiṉattār); the body is a leaking pot to be left (Aḻukaṇṇi 8).
- The means are not the goal (Kuṭampai).
- Powers do not still the mind (Tāyumāṉavar).

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** P4-stage: body-perfection is a means for the seeker (TM 724 names it as the condition for reaching knowledge); Kuṭampai speaks to the one who has already reached it.
TM 724 itself frames the body's care instrumentally ('firm true knowledge cannot be reached'); Kuṭampai's couplets are addressed to the realized (meyññāṉi).
**The traditions' own objections:** Siddhars who seek the deathless body as the goal itself (the alchemical stream, Bogar) would not accept that it is only a means; Paṭṭiṉattār's contempt for the body is not merely a higher-stage view.
