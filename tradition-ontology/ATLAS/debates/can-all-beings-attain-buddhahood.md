# Can all beings attain buddhahood, or are there beings (icchantikas, those without lineage) who never will?

`dsp:can-all-beings-attain-buddhahood` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Tathāgatagarbha (Buddha-nature) tradition](../lineages/tathagatagarbha.md)
All sentient beings have buddha-nature, including the icchantika; there is no fixed icchantika; all will attain buddhahood.
- All beings are tathāgatagarbhas because the dharmakāya pervades them, suchness is undivided and the lineage is present (RGV 1.27-28).
- 'All beings have buddha-nature — even the icchantikas; because they will surely attain unsurpassed awakening in the future it is said all have it' (Mahāparinirvāṇa juan 27).
- One who understands the four alternatives should not insist that the icchantika definitely lacks it (juan 36).
- The Lotus: there is only one vehicle and all who hear become buddhas.

### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
Beings have distinct lineages (gotra); some belong to the śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha lineage and some have no lineage (agotra) and do not attain buddhahood; the teaching of universal buddha-nature was given with a hidden intention.
- The Laṅkāvatāra lists five lineages, the fifth being 'no lineage'.
- The icchantika who has abandoned the roots of merit by rejecting the bodhisattva collection does not enter nirvāṇa (Laṅkāvatāra p. 28) — though it adds that the Tathāgata's power may revive his roots.
- The Saṃdhinirmocana (ch. 7) says the śrāvaka of fixed lineage cannot be led to awakening even by all buddhas; the one vehicle was taught with an intention (from memory, chapter-level).
**Texts:** 
  - [Laṅkāvatāra-sūtra](../texts/lankavatara-sutra.md) — ref: ch. 2, Vaidya pp. 27-28
  - [Saṃdhinirmocana-sūtra](../texts/samdhinirmocana-sutra.md) — ref: ch. 7
### [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md)
Faxian's six-fascicle Nirvāṇa Sūtra (418) and its first Chinese readers: icchantikas are excluded from buddha-nature.
- The early part of the Mahāparinirvāṇa says icchantikas, having cut off the roots of good, lack buddha-nature (in the sense of wholesome qualities) (T374 juan 26 retains this).
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra](../texts/mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana.md) — ref: T376 (Faxian); T374 juan 9, 26

**Historical debate — Daosheng on the icchantika** (c. 428-430 CE, Jiankang): Daosheng taught, against the six-fascicle Nirvāṇa Sūtra, that icchantikas too can become buddhas and was expelled from the capital's saṅgha; when Dharmakṣema's 40-fascicle version arrived it contained that teaching. Chinese accounts: Daosheng vindicated.

**Historical debate — The 'three-one' controversy (Saichō and Tokuitsu)** (c. 817-821 CE, Japan): Saichō (Tendai) defended the one vehicle and universal buddha-nature; Tokuitsu (Hossō) defended the three vehicles and the five lineages. Each school claimed victory; Tendai's position became dominant in Japan.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Principles:** P5-neyartha, P6-upaya, P4-stage
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: each side uses the tradition's own P5/P6 hermeneutic to make the other's teaching provisional, so the principle cannot decide between them.
**The traditions' own objections:** Tiantai/Tendai and Huayan reject any being permanently without buddhahood; the Faxiang/Hossō tradition rejects universal attainment as the definitive teaching.
**Candidate readings:** P5 (Yogācāra reading): 'all have buddha-nature' is neyārtha, spoken to encourage beings of indeterminate lineage.; P5/P6 (Tathāgatagarbha and Tiantai reading): the five-lineage teaching is a skillful means; the one vehicle and universal buddha-nature are nītārtha.; P4 (Mahāparinirvāṇa juan 36 and Laṅkāvatāra p. 28 themselves): 'icchantika' names a present condition, not an eternal nature — the Tathāgata's power can revive the roots of good.; P1 (Faxiang's own distinction, reported from memory): buddha-nature as principle (lixing) all beings have; buddha-nature as practice (xingxing) some lack.
