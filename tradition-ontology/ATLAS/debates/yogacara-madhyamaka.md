# Is cognition (the dependent nature) real, or is it empty of own-being like everything else? — Yogācāra and Madhyamaka

`dsp:yogacara-madhyamaka` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
The dependent nature — unreal imagination — exists; only the duality it projects is absent; emptiness is the absence of that duality and itself exists. Taken literally, 'all dharmas lack own-nature' is a provisional teaching (second turning); its intended meaning is the three naturelessnesses. To deny the dependent and the perfected as well is the worst nihilism.
- If nothing existed, there would be neither defilement nor purification, and effort would be fruitless (MAV 1.22).
- Emptiness must be the emptiness of something: of duality in unreal imagination (MAV 1.2, 1.14).
- The Bodhisattvabhūmi (tattvārtha chapter) condemns those who deny the inexpressible reality as nihilists (recalled).
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyāntavibhāga](../texts/madhyantavibhaga.md) — ref: 1.2-3, 1.21-22
  - [Triṃśikā (Triṃśikāvijñaptikārikā)](../texts/trimsika.md) — ref: 20-25
  - [Saṃdhinirmocana-sūtra](../texts/samdhinirmocana-sutra.md) — ref: ch. 7
  - [Bodhisattvabhūmi](../texts/bodhisattvabhumi.md) — ref: tattvārtha-paṭala
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
Cognition is as empty of own-being as its objects; if the dependent existed by own-being it could not originate dependently. The ālaya and cognition-only were taught provisionally for those who fear emptiness; reflexive awareness is incoherent, as a sword cannot cut itself.
- Bhāviveka criticizes Yogācāra in the Madhyamakahṛdaya (ch. 5) and Prajñāpradīpa (recalled).
- Candrakīrti's Madhyamakāvatāra ch. 6 refutes cognition-only, the ālaya as ultimately real and reflexive awareness.
- Śāntideva (Bodhicaryāvatāra ch. 9) refutes reflexive awareness with the lamp and sword examples.
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyamakahṛdayakārikā](../texts/madhyamakahrdaya.md) — ref: ch. 5
  - [Madhyamakāvatāra](../texts/madhyamakavatara.md) — ref: ch. 6
  - [Bodhicaryāvatāra](../texts/bodhicaryavatara.md) — ref: ch. 9
### [Yogācāra-Madhyamaka](../lineages/yogacara-madhyamaka.md)
Mind-only is correct conventionally and is the step by which external objects are negated; ultimately mind too is neither one nor many and so without own-being; the Laṅkāvatāra's verses show the sequence.
- Śāntarakṣita, Madhyamakālaṃkāra (vv. 91–93, recalled): riding the chariot of the two systems.
- Kamalaśīla reads Laṅkāvatāra 10.256–258 as stages of meditation.
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyamakālaṃkāra](../texts/madhyamakalamkara.md) — ref: 91-93 (recalled)
  - [Bhāvanākrama](../texts/bhavanakrama.md) — ref: I
  - [Laṅkāvatāra-sūtra](../texts/lankavatara-sutra.md) — ref: 10.256-258
### [Alīkākāravāda (False-Aspect Yogācāra)](../lineages/alikakaravada.md)
Image-free luminous awareness is the ultimate meaning of both Yogācāra and Madhyamaka; the images alone are false.
- Ratnākaraśānti presents his view as the Madhyamaka of the Prajñāpāramitā (recalled).

**Historical debate — Bhāviveka and Dharmapāla** (6th c. (tradition)): Xuanzang's record: Bhāviveka travelled to debate Dharmapāla, who declined; Bhāviveka then retired to a cave to await Maitreya's coming (recalled). no debate held

**Historical debate — Candrakīrti and Candragomin at Nālandā** (7th c. (tradition)): Tibetan histories: a debate of many years, Candragomin (for Yogācāra) consulting Avalokiteśvara at night. Tibetan accounts praise both; no decisive victory recorded

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P1-level
**Explanation:** P4/P1: the Laṅkāvatāra's own four-stage sequence and Yogācāra-Madhyamaka treat mind-only as a stage and a conventional truth on the way to the imageless; this reconciles the two as successive levels for those who accept that sequence.
The reconciliation is itself a school position (Śāntarakṣita, Kamalaśīla), not a neutral one.
**The traditions' own objections:** Classical Yogācāra (Asaṅga, Vasubandhu, Sthiramati, Dharmapāla) holds the third turning, not the second, to be definitive and would reject being ranked as a lower stage; Prāsaṅgika (Candrakīrti) rejects even the conventional status of cognition-only and reflexive awareness as taught by Yogācāra.
