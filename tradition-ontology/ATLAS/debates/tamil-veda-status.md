# Are the Tamil hymns of the Āḻvārs (above all the Tiruvāymoḻi) Veda, equal in authority to the Sanskrit Upaniṣads?

`dsp:tamil-veda-status` · `skeleton` · confidence low

**Coverage:** A6; G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
Yes: the Tiruvāymoḻi is the Tamil Upaniṣad (Dramiḍopaniṣad), the Veda's essence in Tamil; the teachers comment on both as one Vedānta (ubhaya-vedānta).
- Nāthamuni received it from Nammāḻvār
- Yāmuna's homage to Nammāḻvār
- Deśika's Dramiḍopaniṣat works
**Texts:** 
  - [Ācārya Hṛdayam](../texts/acarya-hrdayam.md) — ref: passim

  - [Stotraratna](../texts/stotraratna.md) — ref: 5
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
As the objection is reported in Śrīvaiṣṇava works: only the authorless Sanskrit Veda is śruti; a composition by a human author, in Tamil, by one not a brahmin, can at most be a devotional smṛti.
- authorlessness (apauruṣeyatva) of the Veda
**Texts:** 
  - [Ācārya Hṛdayam](../texts/acarya-hrdayam.md) — ref: pūrvapakṣa (recalled)
