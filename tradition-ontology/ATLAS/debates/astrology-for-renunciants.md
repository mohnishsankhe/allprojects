# May monks and renunciants practise astrology or live by it?

`dsp:astrology-for-renunciants` · `skeleton` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
No: predicting eclipses, the courses of moon, sun and asterisms, meteors and earthquakes, fixing auspicious days and performing pacification rites are 'low arts' by which some ascetics live wrongly; the Buddha abstains, his follower must not practise astrology, and monks may not learn or teach such arts.
- wrong livelihood (micchā-ājīva) on food given in faith
- 'what can the stars do?'
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmajāla Sutta](../texts/brahmajala-sutta.md) — ref: 1.21-27
  - [Sutta Nipāta](../texts/sutta-nipata.md) — ref: 927
  - [Vinaya Piṭaka](../texts/vinaya-pitaka.md) — ref: Cullavagga 5.33.2
  - [Jātaka](../texts/jataka.md) — ref: 49
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
No: the monk is one who does not live by the sciences of portents, sky-signs, earth-signs, dreams, bodily marks, sounds and the like.
- such sciences are a livelihood incompatible with the mendicant life
**Texts:** 
  - [Uttarādhyayana Sūtra](../texts/uttaradhyayana-sutra.md) — ref: 15.7
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
The Vedic renouncer too must never seek alms by portents, omens, astrology or palmistry.
- the ascetic's alms must not be earned by such arts
**Texts:** 
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: 6.50
### [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md)
Jyotiṣa is the eye of the Veda and needed by all: even forest-dwelling renunciants without possessions consult the knower of the lights; the chart itself shows who will renounce and into which order; one who condemns the science without knowing it goes to hell.
- the science serves dharma and the times of rites
- renunciation itself appears in the chart (BJ 15)
**Texts:** 
  - [Bṛhat Saṃhitā](../texts/brhat-samhita.md) — ref: 2.6-8
  - [Bṛhajjātaka](../texts/brhat-jataka.md) — ref: 15.1
  - [Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra](../texts/brhat-parasara-hora-sastra.md) — ref: 2.11-13

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The Buddhist, Jain and Manu rules forbid the renunciant to live by astrology; the jyotiṣa texts address householders, kings and the rite. The rule is one of adhikāra (the renunciant's stage), not a verdict on the science for all.
All three renunciant codes, Vedic included, share the prohibition, which shows it concerns livelihood and the mendicant's stage. Later Jain monks (Hemaprabha) and Buddhist monks (Anavamadarśī; the Kālacakra tradition) nonetheless wrote jyotiṣa, which the rule as livelihood does not strictly cover.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Nakkhatta Jātaka denies the stars efficacy for anyone, not only for monks; the jyotiṣa side claims the science leads to Brahmaloka (BS 2.12) and blames its critics (BPHS 2.13), which the Buddhist and Jain sides do not accept.
