# What makes one a brāhmaṇa — birth, body, learning, action, or realization of the Self?

`dsp:who-is-a-brahmana` · `sourced` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Not jīva, body, birth, learning, action or dharmic deeds; the brāhmaṇa is one who has realized the non-dual Self and is free of faults; caste belongs to convention.
- Vajrasūci: seers born of deer, jackal, anthill, fisherwoman
- Nirālamba: caste is not of skin, blood, flesh or bone; imagined for convention
**Texts:** 
  - [Vajrasūci Upaniṣad](../texts/vajrasuci-upanisad.md) — ref: prose
  - [Nirālamba Upaniṣad](../texts/niralamba-upanisad.md) — ref: 1
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Varṇa is by birth, with conduct and rites determining standing within it.
- the Vajrasūci's own opening states the received view: 'of the four castes the brāhmaṇa is chief, say the Vedas and smṛtis'
**Texts:** 
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — 

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level
**Explanation:** The Nirālamba itself places caste in convention (vyavahāra) and the true brāhmaṇa at the level of realized truth, so both can hold on different levels.
Read by levels of truth, birth-caste operates in conventional social and ritual life, while the Vajrasūci defines the brāhmaṇa by realization.
**The traditions' own objections:** Dharmaśāstra authors would not accept that varṇa is merely imagined; the Vajrasūci claims its definition is the intent of all scripture, not merely a higher level.
