# Is complete nudity required of the renunciant (and for liberation)?

`dsp:monastic-nudity` · `skeleton` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
Yes: the fully renounced monk is naked like a newborn; a clothed person cannot be liberated even if he were a Tīrthaṅkara; clothes are possessions.
- Mahāvīra went naked
- Any garment, however small, needs washing, mending and guarding and shows attachment
- Nakedness is among the twenty-eight root qualities
- The seventh and higher stages require the giving up of all possessions
**Texts:** 
  - [Aṣṭapāhuḍa](../texts/astapahuda.md) — ref: sutta.23
  - [Aṣṭapāhuḍa](../texts/astapahuda.md) — ref: sutta.18
  - [Mūlācāra](../texts/mulacara.md) — ref: 1.2-3
  - [Pravacanasāra](../texts/pravacanasara.md) — ref: 3
### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
No: possession is attachment; clothes and bowls kept for restraint are not possessions; the canon allows monks three, two, one or no garments; nakedness belonged to the Jinakalpa, which lapsed after Jambū.
- 'Possession is attachment' (Tattvārthasūtra 7.17; Daśavaikālika 6.20–21)
- Ācārāṅga 1.8 describes monks with three, two, one or no robes
- Keśin and Gautama agreed that the outward sign is secondary to knowledge, faith and conduct
- Nuns may not go unclothed (Bṛhatkalpa), yet women are liberated
**Texts:** 
  - [Daśavaikālika Sūtra](../texts/dasavaikalika-sutra.md) — ref: 6.20-21
  - [Ācārāṅga Sūtra](../texts/acaranga-sutra.md) — ref: 1.8.4-7
  - [Uttarādhyayana Sūtra](../texts/uttaradhyayana-sutra.md) — ref: 23
  - [Bṛhatkalpa Sūtra](../texts/brhatkalpa-sutra.md) — ref: 5
### [Yāpanīya](../lineages/yapaniya.md)
Nudity is the normal sign, but a garment may be kept by exception, and clothed persons and women are not excluded from liberation.
- The Bhagavatī Ārādhanā's normal and exceptional signs
- Aparājita defends clothes by exception from the canon

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** Both agree that the naked mode is the highest and the Jinas' own; they differ on whether it is required now of every renunciant — a difference the Ācārāṅga and Uttarādhyayana themselves frame by capacity and era.
The Ācārāṅga's graded allowance (three robes to none) and the Keśin–Gautama explanation by the dispositions of each era's disciples adapt the outward sign to the practitioner's capacity (P4). The Śvetāmbara jinakalpa/sthavirakalpa distinction keeps nudity as the highest mode.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Digambaras reject the Śvetāmbara texts cited and deny that a clothed person can reach the higher stages; the Śvetāmbaras deny that nudity is required. The reconciliation claims only a shared ranking of the naked mode, not agreement on liberation.
