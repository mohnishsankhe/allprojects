# Are māyā and avidyā the same entity?

`dsp:maya-avidya-distinction` · `skeleton` · confidence low

**Coverage:** A6

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Identical (Vivaraṇa usage; Saṃkṣepaśārīraka; Vivekacūḍāmaṇi calls māyā 'beginningless avidyā'): one indescribable power, called māyā in its cosmic aspect and avidyā as individual obscuration.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vivekacūḍāmaṇi](../texts/vivekacudamani.md) — ref: 108
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Distinct (Vidyāraṇya): prakṛti with pure sattva is māyā, adjunct of the omniscient Īśvara who controls it; with impure sattva it is avidyā, adjunct of the jīva who is controlled by it.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pañcadaśī](../texts/pancadasi.md) — ref: 1.15–17

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Vidyāraṇya's own distinction is of one prakṛti under two aspects (pure/impure sattva), so both sides describe the same entity from the Lord's and the jīva's standpoints.
Both sides treat māyā/avidyā as one indescribable principle; the difference is whether the aspects warrant two names.
**The traditions' own objections:** Some Advaitins insist on a real distinction of function (the Lord is not deluded by māyā; the jīva is by avidyā), which the reconciliation preserves.
