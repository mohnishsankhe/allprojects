# Does killing enjoined as part of a Vedic rite incur demerit?

`dsp:sacrificial-killing` · `skeleton` · confidence low

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
Violence is prohibited, and rites whose aim is violence (the Śyena) are harmful; but the Veda states such rites only as means for one who wishes to harm, not as duties. (The Bhāṭṭa position, generally reported, is that killing enjoined as a subsidiary of a rite is not sin, the specific injunction limiting the general prohibition.)
- Śabara on MS 1.1.2.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śābarabhāṣya](../texts/sabara-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.2
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
The Vedic means are impure (aviśuddhi, Sāṃkhya Kārikā 2): the killing that helps the rite still brings demerit to the person, since the general and the specific injunction concern different ends.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — ref: 2
  - [Sāṃkhyatattvakaumudī](../texts/tattvakaumudi.md) — ref: on 2
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
All killing is violence and binds karma, whatever the motive of ritual.
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
Animal sacrifice is not a wholesome act.
