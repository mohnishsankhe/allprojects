# Did Pātañjala Yoga take its shared meditative and ethical vocabulary (the four attitudes, the five faculties, the afflictions, samāpatti, dharmamegha) from Buddhism, or do Yoga, Buddhism and Jainism draw on a common śramaṇa milieu?

**Coverage:** C

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Yoga is an ancient teaching (the tradition names Hiraṇyagarbha as its first teacher and Patañjali as its compiler); the Yoga text marks its difference from the Buddhists, rejecting the view that objects are constructions of a single mind; no dependence on Buddhism is acknowledged.
- one object is shared by many minds and does not depend on a single mind (YS 4.15–16)
- the bhāṣya states and refutes the idealist argument (YBh 4.14)
**Texts:** 
  - [Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali](../texts/yoga-sutra.md) — ref: 4.14-16
  - [Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra)](../texts/yoga-bhasya.md) — ref: 4.14-15
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
The suttas themselves acknowledge that other wanderers taught the abandoning of the hindrances and the four immeasurables, and that the bodhisatta learned the formless attainments from other teachers; the Buddha's distinctiveness lies in knowing where these culminate and in the liberating insight beyond them.
- 'we too teach our disciples thus' (SN 46.54)
- each liberation of mind has a stated culmination only the Tathāgata can explain (SN 46.54)
- the formless attainments learned from Āḷāra and Uddaka were judged insufficient (MN 26)
**Texts:** 
  - [Bojjhaṅga Saṃyutta](../texts/bojjhanga-samyutta.md) — ref: 46.54
  - [Ariyapariyesana Sutta](../texts/ariyapariyesana-sutta.md) — ref: 15-17
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The five great vows and the four reflections belong to the Jina's teaching; Haribhadra treats the yoga taught by others as one path of qualities under different names, correlating his eight views with the limbs beginning with yama.
- the five vows and the name mahāvrata (TS 7.1–2)
- the four reflections toward classes of beings (TS 7.11)
- the eight views correlated with the eight limbs (YDS 16)
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvārthasūtra](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md) — ref: 7.1-2, 7.11
  - [Yogadṛṣṭisamuccaya](../texts/yogadrstisamuccaya.md) — ref: 16
