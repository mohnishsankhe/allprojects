# Who or what is the imperishable person (akṣara, called kūṭastha) of BhG 15.16, set beside the perishable (all beings) and below the highest person, and so what does it mean that the Lord is 'higher even than the imperishable' (15.18)?

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The imperishable is the Lord's power of māyā, the seed from which the perishable arises, in which the impressions of the desires and actions of transmigrating beings rest; the highest person is distinct from both the perishable and this imperishable.
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Śaṅkara](../texts/gita-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: on 15.16-18
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
The perishable are the bound selves joined to non-conscious matter, called 'all beings'; the imperishable is the released self, free from connection with matter and so unchanging (kūṭastha); the Lord is other than both.
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Rāmānuja](../texts/gita-bhasya-ramanuja.md) — ref: on 15.16-18
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
The perishable are all souls, whose bodies perish; the imperishable is Lakṣmī, whose body never perishes; the highest person is Viṣṇu, beyond both.

_Notes: The text's own statement: there are two puruṣas in the world, the perishable (all beings) and the imperishable, called the one standing firm (kūṭastha) (15.16); the highest puruṣa is another, called the supreme self, the imperishable lord who enters and sustains the three worlds (15.17); being beyond the perishable and higher even than the imperishable, the speaker is renowned as puruṣottama (15.18). The imperishable worshipped in 12.3 is also called kūṭastha (see dsp:bhagavad-gita-lord-or-unmanifest); the commentators do not all identify the two.
