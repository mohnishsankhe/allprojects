# Does scripture (śruti) teach the Sāṃkhya's unconscious pradhāna as the cause of the world?

`dsp:is-pradhana-taught-in-sruti` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G (Sāṃkhya–Vedānta)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Yes: pradhāna is established by inference and confirmed by scripture; reliable testimony teaches what inference does not reach.
- Supersensibles are known by inference and, beyond that, by reliable scripture (SK 6).
- Scripture too speaks of the world as pradhāna's product (SS 5.12); prakṛti is the first material cause, others being products per scripture (SS 6.32).
- The sophist who conflicts with scripture does not attain the self (SS 6.34); Kapila recalled the Veda from the previous eon (Vācaspati on SK 5).
- Sāṃkhya readings of the Kaṭha's 'unmanifest' (3.11) and the Śvetāśvatara's 'unborn one' (4.5) as prakṛti are known from the Vedānta opponent's statement of them (BS 1.4.1, 1.4.8).

### [Vedānta (Uttara Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/vedanta.md)
No: the scriptural cause 'saw' (reflected) before creating, so the unconscious pradhāna is not scriptural; the passages cited for Sāṃkhya mean something else, and Kapila's smṛti yields where it conflicts with śruti.
- BS 1.1.5: because of 'seeing', pradhāna is non-scriptural (ChU 6.2.3 'it saw: may I be many').
- BS 1.4.1: the 'unmanifest' of the Kaṭha is taken from the simile of the body (chariot image).
- BS 1.4.11: the 'five fives' (BĀU 4.4.17) do not teach the twenty-five principles.
- BS 2.1.1–3: rejecting Kapila's smṛti where it conflicts is no fault, since the other interpretation would leave other smṛtis without scope; the same refutes Yoga.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: each side holds the other's reading of the same scriptures to be wrong.
**The traditions' own objections:** Śaṅkara treats Sāṃkhya as the chief opponent whose refutation refutes the others (on the last sūtra of BS 1.4) and does not accept it as a partial Vedānta; classical Sāṃkhya rejects a conscious creator (SS 5.2–12).
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: Sāṃkhya enumerates principles analytically to discriminate self from non-self, while the Brahma Sūtra asks what the Upaniṣads teach as the conscious first cause — answers to different questions (rejected by both: Śaṅkara denies pradhāna is scriptural; classical Sāṃkhya denies any conscious director).; P4-stage: Advaita accepts the discrimination of self from the non-self as preparatory while rejecting an independent pradhāna.; Theistic Sāṃkhya synthesis (Vijñānabhikṣu, recalled): prakṛti as material cause under the Lord taught by śruti.
