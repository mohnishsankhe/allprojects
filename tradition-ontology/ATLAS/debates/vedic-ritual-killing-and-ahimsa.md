# Is the killing prescribed in Vedic ritual an injury (hiṃsā) that brings demerit?

`dsp:vedic-ritual-killing-and-ahimsa` · `sourced` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
No: the injury prescribed by the Veda is non-injury, for dharma shone forth from the Veda (MDh 5.44); animals were created for sacrifice.
- the Veda is the sole authority on dharma
**Texts:** 
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: 5.39-44
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Abstention is better: meat cannot be had without injury, killing does not lead to heaven (MDh 5.48); eating meat is natural activity, abstention bears great fruit (MDh 5.56).
- the nature of meat
- the fruit of nivṛtti
**Texts:** 
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: 5.48-56
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
The Vedic means are impure (aviśuddhi) because they involve killing; their fruit is also perishable (Sāṃkhyakārikā 2 as its commentators explain).
- the general prohibition 'do not harm any being' is not cancelled by the special injunction
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — ref: 2
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
Injury to living beings is never dharma, whether in ritual or not; the Śramaṇa traditions reject animal sacrifice.
- non-injury as the highest dharma
**Texts:** 
  - [Ācārāṅga Sūtra](../texts/acaranga-sutra.md) — ref: 1.4
