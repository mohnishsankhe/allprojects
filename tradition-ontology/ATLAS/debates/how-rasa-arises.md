# How does rasa arise from the union of determinants, consequents and transient states, and where is it?

`dsp:how-rasa-arises` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
Lollaṭa: rasa is the stable emotion intensified by the determinants, existing in the character (Rāma) and, by imitation, in the actor.
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhinavabhāratī (Nāṭyavedavivṛti) of Abhinavagupta](../texts/abhinavabharati.md) — ref: on NŚ 6 rasa-sūtra
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
Śaṅkuka: rasa is the stable emotion inferred in the actor, who is taken as Rāma like a painted horse taken as a horse.
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhinavabhāratī (Nāṭyavedavivṛti) of Abhinavagupta](../texts/abhinavabharati.md) — ref: on NŚ 6 rasa-sūtra
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
Bhaṭṭa Nāyaka: rasa is neither perceived, produced nor suggested; by the powers of bhāvakatva and bhojakatva it is enjoyed in a sattva-filled repose akin to tasting Brahman.
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhinavabhāratī (Nāṭyavedavivṛti) of Abhinavagupta](../texts/abhinavabharati.md) — ref: on NŚ 6 rasa-sūtra
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
Abhinavagupta: rasa is manifested (suggested) and relished in the sensitive spectator whose latent stable emotion is awakened by generalized determinants; it consists in the relish itself (Mammaṭa and Viśvanātha follow).
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhinavabhāratī (Nāṭyavedavivṛti) of Abhinavagupta](../texts/abhinavabharati.md) — ref: on NŚ 6

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** Abhinavagupta himself presents the earlier views as rungs of a ladder of discrimination by which the intellect climbs to the truth — a reconciliation by stages of understanding, from the tradition's own mouth.
The reconciliation is Abhinava's; it ranks rather than equates the views. Mahimabhaṭṭa (inference) and Dhanika (purport) remain outside it.
**The traditions' own objections:** Lollaṭa's and Śaṅkuka's own works are lost; later critics (Mahimabhaṭṭa) reject Abhinava's view of suggestion.
