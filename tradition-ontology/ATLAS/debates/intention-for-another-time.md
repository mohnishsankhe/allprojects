# Does the promise of birth by vows or ten recitations bring birth at once, or only at 'another time' (Mahāyānasaṃgraha)?

`dsp:intention-for-another-time` · `skeleton` · confidence high


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
(Mahāyānasaṃgraha teachers, as reported) Sayings that one is born by merely vowing are an 'intention for another time': they create a cause that ripens only in a distant future, as one coin buys a thousand only in time.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahāyānasaṃgraha](../texts/mahayanasamgraha.md) — ref: on the four intentions
### [Pure Land (Jingtu / Jōdo): birth in Amitābha's land](../lineages/pure-land.md)
(Daochuo) A treatise cannot contradict the sūtra; the ten-recitation reciter has hidden past causes and is born at once. (Huaigan) 'By vow alone' means a bare vow without practice; sincere recitation is practice of body, speech and mind, so birth follows at once.
**Texts:** 
  - [Collection on the Land of Peace and Bliss (Anle ji)](../texts/anle-ji.md) — ref: 10a11
  - [Resolving the Many Doubts about the Pure Land (Shi jingtu qunyi lun)](../texts/shi-jingtu-qunyi-lun.md) — ref: 39a

**Historical debate — The bieshiyi controversy** (6th–7th c.): Anle ji; Shi jingtu qunyi lun; Shandao's commentary. Pure Land: refuted.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P5-neyartha
**Explanation:** Huaigan's distinction: the 'another time' statement concerns a bare vow (provisional encouragement), while calling the name with the three acts is practice that brings birth — the two refer to different cases.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Yogācāra side's own later response is not recorded here.
