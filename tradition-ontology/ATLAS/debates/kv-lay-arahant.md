# Can a householder be an arahant?

`dsp:kv-lay-arahant` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Uttarāpathakas ('Northerners', as named in the Kathāvatthu commentary)](../lineages/uttarapathaka.md)
A householder can be an arahant (held, per the commentary, by the Uttarāpathakas).
**Texts:** 
  - [Kathāvatthu](../texts/kathavatthu.md) — ref: 4.1
### [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
An arahant has no householder's fetter; one who attains arahantship does not remain a householder.
- The fetter of the household life is abandoned by the arahant.
**Texts:** 
  - [Kathāvatthu](../texts/kathavatthu.md) — ref: 4.1

**Historical debate — Third Council at Pāṭaliputta** (3rd c. BCE (tradition: reign of Asoka)): Theravāda chronicles and the Samantapāsādikā: holders of wrong views were expelled and the Kathāvatthu was recited Theravāda: the Vibhajjavāda confirmed as the Buddha's teaching; other schools' accounts of the period differ

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** Attainment may occur while in lay dress, but the state is not lived as a householder's life.
The commentarial tradition (Milindapañha, U36) holds that a lay person who attains arahantship must go forth or pass away the same day.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Theravāda still denies a householder-arahant; the reported opponents held that one could remain a householder.
