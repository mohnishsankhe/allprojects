# Is the Lord who has come into manifestation in a human body only a manifest, human being?

`dsp:bhagavad-gita-the-lords-embodiment` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### None
(As the Gītā reports it; no school is named) the unintelligent think of him as unmanifest come into manifestation (7.24); the deluded disregard him as one who has taken on a human body.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) — ref: 7.24; 9.11
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Kṛṣṇa: they do not know his supreme state (paraṃ bhāvam), imperishable and unsurpassed, as the great lord of beings; veiled by his yoga-māyā he is not manifest to all, and the deluded world does not recognise him as unborn and imperishable; he knows all beings past, present and future, but no one knows him.
- his supreme state is imperishable and unsurpassed (7.24)
- he is veiled by yoga-māyā (7.25)
- he is the great lord of beings (9.11)
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) — ref: 7.24–26; 9.11

_Notes: The commentators differ on exactly which view 7.24 rejects (that the Lord, formerly unmanifest, has now become manifest like an ordinary being; or that he is merely a manifest person). Bears on the coverage-map G question whether the ultimate is personal or impersonal; no reconciliation attempted here._
