# How many gods are there — 3,339, thirty-three, three, or one?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
The hymns count thirty-three gods (eleven in heaven, on earth and in the waters) or 3,339.
- Counts given in the liturgy itself.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 1.139.11; 3.9.9
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
The etymologists: only three deities — Agni (earth), Vāyu or Indra (atmosphere), Sūrya (heaven); the other names belong to them by function.
- Classification by region.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 7.5
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
One: the One Being is called by many names (RV 1.164.46); the one Self of the deity is praised in many ways, the other gods are its limbs (Nirukta 7.4).
- Explicit statements in the hymns.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 1.164.46
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 7.4
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Asked by Vidagdha Śākalya, Yājñavalkya reduces the 3,306 gods to thirty-three, six, three, two, one and a half and one — the breath, called brahman: the larger numbers are 'only their powers (mahiman)'.
- The many are greatnesses of fewer gods.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad](../texts/brhadaranyaka-upanisad.md) — ref: 3.9.1–9

**Historical debate — Yājñavalkya and Vidagdha Śākalya at Janaka's court** (Upaniṣadic period (tradition)): Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.9 Śākalya, unable to answer the final question, dies; Yājñavalkya's reduction stands

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The tradition itself reconciles the counts: the many are the 'greatnesses' (mahiman) or 'limbs' of fewer gods and finally of one (BĀU 3.9; Nirukta 7.4) — counts from the standpoints of function and of essence.
**The traditions' own objections:** Mīmāṃsā keeps each deity as the distinct recipient named in its rite; Vaiṣṇava Vedānta (e.g. Madhva) reads the One as Viṣṇu and keeps the gods eternally distinct from him and graded — it accepts 'many names of one' only as all names ultimately denoting Viṣṇu.

_Notes: Whether the Padapāṭha's Śākalya is Vidagdha Śākalya is disputed (see tch:sakalya)._
