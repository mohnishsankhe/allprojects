# Do the paths taught by the brahmins of the three Vedas lead to union with Brahmā?

`dsp:tevijja-union-with-brahma` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G11

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
The various paths taught by the brahmins versed in the three Vedas — Addhariya, Tittiriya, Chandoka, Bavhārija — all lead to union with Brahmā, as many paths lead to one village.
- Reported by the young brahmins Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja in DN 13.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tevijja Sutta](../texts/tevijja-sutta.md) — ref: DN 13 (8–10)
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
None of the brahmins or their teachers has seen Brahmā; their teaching is like a line of blind men; the path to companionship with Brahmā is the four divine abidings of a mind without hatred, like Brahmā's.
- A line of blind men (DN 13.15).
- One cannot call across the river Aciravatī to the far shore to come over (DN 13).
- The brahmavihāras as the path (DN 13.76–81).
**Texts:** 
  - [Tevijja Sutta](../texts/tevijja-sutta.md) — ref: DN 13

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Buddha denies that the three Vedas' paths lead to Brahmā and substitutes the brahmavihāras.
**The traditions' own objections:** For the suttas union with Brahmā is at most a heavenly rebirth, not the goal; the Vedāntic traditions take brahmaloka or Brahman as final.
**Candidate readings:** P3-path: the Upaniṣadic and Buddhist traditions both teach companionship with Brahmā through inner qualities; the dispute concerns which path — but the Buddhist side treats the Brahmā world as within saṃsāra, not as liberation
