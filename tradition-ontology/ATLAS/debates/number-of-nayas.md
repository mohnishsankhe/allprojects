# How many standpoints (nayas) are there?

`dsp:number-of-nayas` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B2 / G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
Seven: naigama, saṃgraha, vyavahāra, ṛjusūtra, śabda, samabhirūḍha, evambhūta.
- TS 1.33 (Digambara).
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvārthasūtra](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md) — ref: 1.33
### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
Five, with the first divided in two and the verbal standpoint in three (Śvetāmbara recension of the sūtra); Siddhasena reduces all to the two root standpoints of substance and mode with their subdivisions.
- Śv TS 1.34–35.
- Sanmati 1.3.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvārthasūtra](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md) — ref: 1.34-35 (Śvetāmbara)
  - [Sanmatitarka (Sammaisutta)](../texts/sanmati-tarka.md) — ref: 1.3

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The enumerations divide the same field of partial views differently; the tradition itself treats the lists as interconvertible.
The Śvetāmbara five-with-subdivisions yields the same seven; all lists reduce to the two root standpoints.
**The traditions' own objections:** Siddhasena's reported refusal to count naigama as a separate standpoint (recalled, low confidence) is a substantive disagreement not erased by this reconciliation.
