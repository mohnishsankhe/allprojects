## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Twenty-five principles (prakṛti, its twenty-three evolutes and puruṣa).
- classical Sāṃkhya enumeration
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — ref: 3
### [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md)
Twenty-four with time as the twenty-fifth (Kapila); and, per the Uddhava Gītā, all the seers' counts (9, 11, 5, 3, 26, 25, 7, 6, 4, 17, 16, 13) are right, since the principles enter into one another and each counts by his intention.
- mutual inclusion of the principles (11.22.7-8)
- the dispute arises from the Lord's powers and ends with calm (11.22.5-6)
**Texts:** 
  - [Uddhava Gītā](../texts/uddhava-gita.md) — ref: 11.22.1-9
  - [Kapila Gītā](../texts/kapila-gita.md) — ref: 3.26.10-15
### [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
Thirty-six principles from Śiva to earth.
- the pure and impure orders of manifestation
**Texts:** 
  - [Tantrāloka](../texts/tantraloka.md) — ref: ch. 9

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The Uddhava Gītā reconciles the counts itself: the principles interpenetrate, so each enumeration is valid from the speaker's intended standpoint.
Applied beyond the Bhāgavata's own list (to Sāṃkhya's 25 and Śaiva 36) this is an interpretive extension by U06.
**The traditions' own objections:** Schools that hold their count to be exhaustive (e.g. the Śaiva claim that Sāṃkhya's 25 stops short of the pure tattvas) would not accept that the counts are merely perspectival.
