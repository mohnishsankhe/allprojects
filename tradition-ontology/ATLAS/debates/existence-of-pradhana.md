# Does a single, unconscious primal matter (pradhāna) exist as the material cause of the manifold world?

`dsp:existence-of-pradhana` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G (Sāṃkhya–Buddhists)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Yes: the unmanifest is inferred from its effects, which pre-exist in it.
- Prakṛti is unperceived through subtlety, not non-existence; it is known from effects (SK 8).
- Because particulars are limited, homogeneous (pleasure, pain, delusion), active through potency, distinguished as cause and effect and merge back, an unmanifest cause exists (SK 14–16).
- The rootless root (SS 1.67); both prakṛti and puruṣa are established by inference from general correlation (SS 1.103). Momentariness, mere consciousness and emptiness are rejected (SS 1.34–47).
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — ref: 8, 14-16
  - [Sāṃkhya Sūtra](../texts/samkhya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.34-47; 1.62-67; 1.103
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
No: a single permanent cause cannot produce successive, momentary effects; the arguments from homogeneity and pre-existence fail.
- Śāntarakṣita's examination of prakṛti (Tattvasaṅgraha, first chapter, with Kamalaśīla's commentary) and Bhāviveka's chapter on Sāṃkhya (Madhyamakahṛdaya 6) argue against pradhāna and satkārya (content recalled; to be extracted from their texts).
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvasaṅgraha of Śāntarakṣita](../texts/tattvasangraha.md) — ref: ch. 1
  - [Madhyamakahṛdayakārikā](../texts/madhyamakahrdaya.md) — ref: ch. 6

**Historical debate — Vindhyavāsa against Buddhamitra** (c. 4th–5th c. CE (per the biography)): Paramārtha's Life of Vasubandhu: the Sāṃkhya master Vindhyavāsa defeated Vasubandhu's teacher Buddhamitra in debate before a king at Ayodhyā and was rewarded; Vasubandhu replied with a (lost) Paramārthasaptati refuting Sāṃkhya. Buddhist account: Buddhamitra lost, Vasubandhu later refuted the Sāṃkhya text; no Sāṃkhya account known.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: permanence and momentariness are both insisted upon.
**The traditions' own objections:** Buddhist pramāṇa authors deny any permanent substance; Sāṃkhya calls the emptiness view 'mere slander by the unawakened' (SS 1.45).
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: substance (dravyārthika) vs mode (paryāyārthika) — enduring prakṛti vs changing modifications; both traditions reject this: Sāṃkhya holds the modifications real transformations of one substance, Buddhists deny any enduring substrate.
