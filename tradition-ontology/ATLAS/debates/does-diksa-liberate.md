# Does initiation (dīkṣā) itself liberate, or is knowledge the direct cause of liberation?

`dsp:does-diksa-liberate` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G (works, knowledge or grace); A7

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md)
Initiation liberates, by Śiva's power working through the ācārya; knowledge alone cannot remove a substance.
**Texts:** 
  - [Kiraṇavṛtti](../texts/kiranavrtti.md) — ref: 1-6
  - [Somaśambhupaddhati](../texts/somasambhupaddhati.md) — ref: dīkṣā
### [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md)
Initiation by the guru (in seven modes) destroys stored karma, and knowledge of Śiva by grace brings union; both are Śiva's grace, and the jñāna-pāda is the culmination of the four.
**Texts:** 
  - [Civañāṉa Cittiyār](../texts/sivananasiddhiyar.md) — ref: cupakkam.8
### [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md)
Liberation comes through the initiation rite, which destroys the bonds by mantra-power; initiation is the cause of the other means, and the incapable are liberated by it with devotion.
- the rite acts on the soul
- bonds paralysed by mantras
- impurity is a substance removed by action (Saiddhāntika exegesis; recorded from memory)
**Texts:** 
  - [Kiraṇatantra](../texts/kirana-tantra.md) — ref: 6.5-16
### [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md)
Knowledge is the direct cause of liberation; initiation liberates by bringing about knowledge (recognition) of one's identity with Śiva.
- Abhinavagupta's Tantrāloka and Tantrasāra
**Texts:** 
  - [Tantrāloka](../texts/tantraloka.md) — 

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled with the Trika position; the Tamil emphasis on jñāna is within the Siddhānta frame.
**The traditions' own objections:** Saiddhāntikas deny that knowledge alone removes impurity; Trika exegetes deny that ritual without knowledge liberates.
**Candidate readings:** P3-path: the Kiraṇa itself says some are fit for ritual, others for knowledge, others for conduct, each liberated accordingly (6.5–8).; P1-level: ritual as the conventional means, knowledge as the ultimate one — a reading the Saiddhāntikas would reject.
