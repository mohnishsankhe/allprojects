# Should blood offerings (bali) be made to the Goddess?

`dsp:blood-sacrifice` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A8

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Bengali and Assamese Śākta tantra](../lineages/bengal-assam-sakta.md)
(Kālikā Purāṇa) The Goddess is pleased by offerings; by them liberation and heaven are won and a king conquers his enemies; mantra-purified blood becomes nectar (67.1-12; 67.19-22). The same text restricts them: brāhmaṇas must not offer wine or certain victims, a human offering needs the king's permission, and pumpkin, sugarcane or spirits are 'equal to' a victim (67.23; 67.46-52; 67.113-117).
**Texts:** 
  - [Kālikā Purāṇa](../texts/kalika-purana.md) — ref: 67
### [Kālīkula (the Kālī lineages)](../lineages/kalikula.md)
(Kālīvilāsa) In the Kali age worship is to be performed in the paśu disposition, with pure food and without meat (nirāmiṣa) (4.3).
**Texts:** 
  - [Kālīvilāsa Tantra](../texts/kalivilasa-tantra.md) — ref: 4.3
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
(Vaiṣṇava) Killing animals is not true worship; the Bhāgavata warns that animals slain in sacrifice await their slayer in the next world (Bhāgavata 4.25.7-8, recalled), and the Goddess is Kṛṣṇa's power acting by his will (Brahmasaṃhitā 5.44); Bengal Vaiṣṇavas reject bali.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhāgavata Purāṇa](../texts/bhagavata-purana.md) — ref: 4.25.7-8
  - [Brahma Saṃhitā (fifth chapter)](../texts/brahma-samhita.md) — ref: 5.44

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Kālikā Purāṇa enjoins what the Vaiṣṇava and paśu-bhāva positions forbid.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Kāmākhyā tradition holds bali to be enjoined by scripture; Vaiṣṇavas (and Jains and Buddhists) reject any reconciliation that allows killing.
**Candidate readings:** P4-stage / adhikāra: the Kālikā Purāṇa itself grades offerings by class and permits substitutes; the substitutes (anukalpa) could be read as the tradition's own path for those who will not kill.; P7-arthavada: the promise of liberation through bali (67.5) as praise of the rite rather than doctrine of liberation.; Not reconcilable while one side holds killing to be an offering and the other a sin (ahiṃsā).
