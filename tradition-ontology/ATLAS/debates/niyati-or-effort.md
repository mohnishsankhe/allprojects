# Is everything fixed by destiny (niyati), or do effort and deeds shape a being's course?

`dsp:niyati-or-effort` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Ājīvika](../lineages/ajivika.md)
(As reported by opponents) Everything is fixed: there is no cause for defilement or purification, no exertion, strength, energy or human effort; beings are transformed by destiny, circumstance and nature and reach the end of suffering after a fixed round that no vow or austerity can shorten.
- the ball of string runs out of itself (DN 2)
- we do not pass beyond destiny, as the ocean does not pass its shore (Ja 545)
- suffering is neither self-made nor other-made but saṅgatiya (Sūtr 1.1.2)

### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
Deeds, action and energy are effective; all Buddhas teach it; there is an element of initiative, and beings are seen to initiate. Destiny, God's creation and no-cause alike end in inaction.
- Makkhali's doctrine is the worst, like a hair blanket (AN 3.137)
- one who comes and goes of himself cannot deny self-doing (AN 6.38)
- the doctrine of cause is the safe wager (MN 60)
- only one Ājīvaka reached heaven in 91 aeons, and he taught deeds (MN 71)
**Texts:**

### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
Effort exists: the Ājīvika potter makes his pots by effort and would punish one who broke them; a god gained his state by exertion. In the developed doctrine niyati is one of five co-causes, wrong only when taken alone.
- the pot argument (Uvāsagadasāo 7)
- Kuṇḍakoliya's question to the god (Uvāsagadasāo 6)
- the five causes together are right view (Sanmati 3.53)
**Texts:**

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Ājīvika position as reported denies effort any role, and the Buddhist position rejects destiny as a cause; no principle joins them without erasing one side.

**The traditions' own objections:** Ājīvikas (as reported): 'there is no exertion'. Buddhists: destiny is one of the tenets that end in inaction (AN 3.61). Jains accept the five-cause synthesis but call one-sided niyati false view.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the Jain synthesis (Sanmati 3.53) treats niyati and effort as co-causes seen from different standpoints — the tradition's own reconciliation, but it rejects exactly the Ājīvika claim that effort is nothing.; P1-level: fixity might describe the whole round seen at once, effort the being's lived course — no text proposes this for the Ājīvikas.; P4-stage: none recorded.
