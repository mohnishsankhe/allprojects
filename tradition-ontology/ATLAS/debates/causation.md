# Does the effect pre-exist in its cause (satkārya), is it newly produced (asatkārya/ārambha), is it an apparent transformation of an unchanging cause (vivarta), a real manifestation of consciousness (ābhāsa), a transformation of God that leaves him unchanged (avikṛta-pariṇāma), or dependently arisen and ultimately unarisen?

`dsp:causation` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G2

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
The effect exists in its material cause before it is produced (satkārya): because what does not exist cannot be produced; because a definite material cause is taken for a definite effect; because not everything arises from everything; because a capable cause produces only what it is capable of; because the effect has the nature of its cause. Production is manifestation.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — ref: 9
  - [Sāṃkhya Sūtra](../texts/samkhya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.114-121
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Past and future exist in their own form, differing in their time-paths; change is transformation of property, time-mark and state in an enduring substrate; the instrumental cause does not set the material causes going but only removes the obstruction, as a farmer breaches a dyke.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali](../texts/yoga-sutra.md) — ref: 3.13; 4.3; 4.12
  - [Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra)](../texts/yoga-bhasya.md) — ref: 4.12
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
The effect is a new thing, non-existent before production (asatkārya, ārambhavāda): before it is produced the fruit is not existent in the Sāṃkhya sense, for existence and non-existence are opposed; it comes about from inherent, non-inherent and instrumental causes and is the counterpositive of its prior absence. Nor does existence arise from non-existence as such (the sprout from the destroyed seed).
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 4.1.14-18; 4.1.48-50
  - [Tarkasaṅgraha](../texts/tarkasangraha.md) — ref: kāraṇa
### [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md)
From the absence of the cause follows the absence of the effect, not the reverse; a thing before it is produced is non-existent (prior absence) — the effect is a newly made whole.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vaiśeṣika Sūtra](../texts/vaisesika-sutra.md) — ref: 1.2.1-2; 9.1.1-12
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The effect is non-different from its cause, 'a verbal handle, a name' — as pots are only clay (CU 6.1.4; BS 2.1.14). Brahman is both material and efficient cause; the world is its apparent transformation (vivarta), not a real modification (vikāra): the snake is only the rope. From the highest standpoint nothing whatever is born.
**Texts:** 
  - [Chāndogya Upaniṣad](../texts/chandogya-upanisad.md) — ref: 6.1.4-6
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 1.4.23; 2.1.14
  - [Vedāntasāra](../texts/vedantasara.md) — ref: apavāda
  - [Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (Gauḍapāda Kārikā, Āgamaśāstra)](../texts/mandukya-karika.md) — ref: 3.48; 4.22
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
Brahman is both the material and the efficient cause; the world is non-different from Brahman as pots from clay, but the effect is a real state of Brahman, whose body is the sentient and non-sentient; real transformation (pariṇāma) touches Brahman's body, not its essence.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 1.4.23; 2.1.15
### [Bhedābheda Vedānta (Bhāskara's school and the early difference-and-non-difference Vedāntins)](../lineages/bhedabheda.md)
Brahman makes itself into the world by real transformation of its power (ātmakṛteḥ pariṇāmāt); the effect is real, both different and non-different from its cause (Bhāskara; Nimbārka).
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Bhāskara (Bhāskarabhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-bhaskara.md) — ref: 1.4.26; 2.1.14
  - [Vedāntapārijātasaurabha](../texts/vedanta-parijata-saurabha.md) — ref: 1.4.26
### [Puṣṭimārga (Vallabha Sampradāya; Śuddhādvaita Brahmavāda)](../lineages/pustimarga.md)
Brahman, transforming itself, is the material cause of the world, yet this transformation involves no change in Brahman (avikṛta-pariṇāma).
**Texts:** 
  - [Aṇubhāṣya (Brahmasūtrāṇubhāṣya)](../texts/anubhasya.md) — ref: 1.4.26
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
Viṣṇu is the independent cause who creates, maintains, dissolves and controls; the world is real and its material is prakṛti, dependent on him; the doctrine of apparent transformation is refuted, since the witness establishes what is real.
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya (Madhva)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-madhva.md) — ref: 1.1.2
  - [Anuvyākhyāna](../texts/anuvyakhyana.md) — ref: 1.4.95-98
  - [Tattvasaṅkhyāna](../texts/tattvasankhyana.md) — ref: 10-11
### [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
Things that appear in the present as external are possible only if they already exist within consciousness; the Lord, by his will and infinite powers, makes them appear (ābhāsa) — this is his creatorhood. Consciousness, free, is the cause of the universe, which she unfolds on her own screen: a real self-manifestation, not an illusion.
**Texts:** 
  - [Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā](../texts/isvarapratyabhijna-karika.md) — ref: 1.5.1; 2.4.1
  - [Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya](../texts/pratyabhijnahrdayam.md) — ref: sūtras 1-2
### [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md)
The world, spoken of as 'he, she and it', undergoes origination, maintenance and dissolution, so it has an agent; it resolves into and re-emerges from its cause — Śiva as efficient cause working through real māyā as material.
**Texts:** 
  - [Civañāṉa Pōtam](../texts/sivananabodham.md) — ref: sūtra 1
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
Formations occur through ignorance and the other conditions; the wheel of becoming has no maker such as a Brahmā: things arise dependently, neither from a self-made nor an other-made cause.
**Texts:** 
  - [Visuddhimagga](../texts/visuddhimagga.md) — ref: ch. 17
  - [Kaccānagotta Sutta](../texts/kaccanagotta-sutta.md) — ref: SN 12.15
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
No existent whatever is ever found arisen from itself, from another, from both, or without a cause (MMK 1.1); the effect is neither made of the conditions nor of non-conditions; dependent arising is itself emptiness, and emptiness, dependent origination and the middle way have one meaning.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mūlamadhyamakakārikā](../texts/mulamadhyamakakarika.md) — ref: 1.1-14; 20.24
  - [Buddhapālita-Mūlamadhyamakavṛtti](../texts/buddhapalita-vrtti.md) — ref: 1.1
  - [Vigrahavyāvartanī](../texts/vigrahavyavartani.md) — ref: 22; 71
  - [Madhyamakahṛdayakārikā](../texts/madhyamakahrdaya.md) — ref: ch. 6 (against Sāṃkhya)
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The existent is endowed at once with origination, cessation and persistence: a thing is in some respect existent and in some respect non-existent before its production. Time, own-nature, destiny, former deeds and human effort, each held one-sidedly as the cause, are wrong views; taken together they are right view.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvārthasūtra](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md) — ref: 5.30
  - [Āptamīmāṃsā](../texts/aptamimamsa.md) — ref: 14
  - [Sanmatitarka (Sammaisutta)](../texts/sanmati-tarka.md) — ref: 3.53
### [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md)
Brahman, without beginning or end, whose essence is the Word, appears (vivartate) as objects; the world is the Word-principle's appearing (Bhartṛhari — the earliest use of vivarta as a technical term recorded here).
**Texts:** 
  - [Vākyapadīya](../texts/vakyapadiya.md) — ref: 1.1
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Time, own-nature, fixed order, chance, the elements, the womb and the person are weighed as causes and none suffices; it is the greatness of God in the world by which the wheel of Brahman turns.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad](../texts/svetasvatara-upanisad.md) — ref: 1.2; 6.1-2

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level, P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Partly reconciled by the traditions' own devices: Advaita and Madhyamaka accept causal change or dependent arising at the conventional level while denying real origination ultimately (P1), and the Jains hold the pre-existence and the novelty of the effect true from the substance and mode standpoints (P2); the Sāṃkhya–Nyāya and vivarta–ābhāsa disagreements are not yet reconciled.
P1: Śaṅkara uses the language of transformation for the empirical world and non-difference/vivarta for the ultimate (BSBh 2.1.14; Vedāntasāra's vikāra–vivarta verse); Nāgārjuna negates the four modes of arising while affirming dependent arising as emptiness (MMK 1.1; VV 71). P2: the Jain triad of origination, cessation and persistence (TS 5.30) and Siddhasena's rule that each single cause held one-sidedly is wrong view (Sanmati 3.53) are the Jains' own reconciliation. What remains: whether the effect's pre-existence is literal (Sāṃkhya) or incoherent (Nyāya); whether the world's appearance is real manifestation of consciousness (Kashmir Śaivism) or indeterminable superimposition (Advaita); whether Brahman's transformation is real (Viśiṣṭādvaita, Bhedābheda, Vallabha) or apparent.
**The traditions' own objections:** Nyāya rejects satkārya outright (NS 4.1.48-50) and Sāṃkhya asatkārya (SK 9); Viśiṣṭādvaita, Bhedābheda, Vallabha and Dvaita deny that the world's transformation is merely apparent (Śrībhāṣya 2.1.15; Anuvyākhyāna 1.4.95-98); Kashmir Śaivism holds manifestation real, not vivarta; Madhyamaka rejects the Jain 'both' as a mode of arising already negated (MMK 1.1).
