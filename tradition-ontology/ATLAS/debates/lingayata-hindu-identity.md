# Is the Liṅgāyata/Vīraśaiva tradition a religion distinct from 'Hinduism' and the Vedic fold, or a Śaiva sampradāya within it — and are 'Liṅgāyata' and 'Vīraśaiva' one tradition or two?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Śaraṇa–vacana tradition](../lineages/sarana-vacana.md)
Liṅgāyata is a distinct religion founded by Basava; it rejects the Veda, caste, temple worship and brāhmaṇical ritual; 'Vīraśaiva' names a Vedic-Āgamic Śaiva sect that is not the same as Basava's Liṅgāyata (the position of many Basava-centred writers and some Virakta maṭhas).
- Basava puts the Veda to the touchstone and the vacanas reject caste and sacrifice.
- The vacanas, not Veda or Āgama, are the scripture.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vacanas of Basavaṇṇa (Basavaṇṇanavara vacanagaḷu)](../texts/basavanna-vacanas.md) — ref: 'Vēdakke oregalla kaṭṭuve…'
### [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md)
Vīraśaiva and Liṅgāyata are two names of one tradition, which has its own identity and scriptures (a position taken by many who reject the split of the names, whether or not they regard the tradition as outside Hinduism).
- Both strands wear the iṣṭaliṅga, follow the ṣaṭsthala and honour Basava.
**Texts:** 
  - [Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi](../texts/siddhantasikhamani.md) — ref: 5.15–23
### [Pañcācārya (Pañcapīṭha) tradition](../lineages/pancacarya.md)
Vīraśaivism is a Śaiva sampradāya within Sanātana dharma, agreeing with the Veda and Vedānta, whose scripture includes the Āgamas, the SSM and the Śrīkarabhāṣya (the Pañcācārya position as generally stated).
- The SSM declares the Śaiva Siddhānta Veda-consonant and its doctrine agreeing with Veda and Vedānta.
- Early-20th-c. Sanskrit prints present Vīraśaiva dharma as that of 'liṅga-bearing brāhmaṇas'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi](../texts/siddhantasikhamani.md) — ref: 3.83–87; 5.3–13
  - [Kaivalyasāra (Toṇṭadārya)](../texts/kaivalyasara-tontadarya.md) — ref: series title

**Historical debate — Campaign for recognition of Liṅgāyata as a separate religion** (2017–2018): In 2017–18 a public movement sought legal recognition of the Liṅgāyata (or Vīraśaiva-Liṅgāyata) community as a separate religion; the Government of Karnataka recommended minority-religion status in 2018 and the central government did not grant it (moderate confidence on details). Disagreement persisted between the positions recorded above.
