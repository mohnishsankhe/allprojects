# Do deeds have fruit, and does effort matter — or are experiences fated, caused by God, uncaused, or ended at death?

`dsp:kiriyavada-akiriyavada` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Ājīvika](../lineages/ajivika.md)
Makkhali Gosāla: there is no cause for the defilement or purification of beings; all are purified by wandering through saṃsāra by fate (niyati); effort does not affect it.
- As reported by Ajātasattu in DN 2.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāmaññaphala Sutta](../texts/samannaphala-sutta.md) — ref: DN 2 (19–21)
### [The śramaṇa movement](../lineages/sramana.md)
Pūraṇa Kassapa: there is no evil in killing nor merit in giving (akiriya); Ajita Kesakambalī: nothing survives death, there is no fruit of deeds (uccheda).
- As reported in DN 2 and MN 60.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāmaññaphala Sutta](../texts/samannaphala-sutta.md) — ref: DN 2 (16–24)
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
The Buddha teaches the doctrine of kamma and of effort: kamma is intention, deeds bear fruit, and striving is fruitful; the tenets that all is due to past deeds, to God's creation, or to chance lead to inaction.
- AN 3.61 refutes the three sectarian tenets.
- AN 6.63: kamma is intention.
- MN 101: how striving is fruitful.
**Texts:** 
  - [Titthāyatana Sutta](../texts/titthayatana-sutta.md) — ref: AN 3.61
  - [Nibbedhika Sutta](../texts/nibbedhika-sutta.md) — ref: AN 6.63
  - [Devadaha Sutta](../texts/devadaha-sutta.md) — ref: MN 101
