# Is awakening reached by the effortful arousal of kuṇḍalinī, or does it lie in the innate (sahaja), which such practices do not reach?

`dsp:natha-effort-or-sahaja` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md)
Kuṇḍalinī, lying coiled at the door of Brahman, must be awakened by the fire of yoga with mind and breath and the five seals; then she rises through suṣumṇā and time is cheated (the haṭha position of the Gorakṣaśataka; the Haṭhapradīpikā adds that she wakes 'by the guru's grace').
- GŚ 30–32: awakened by vahni-yoga; seals make one fit for success.
- HYP 3.2–3: when kuṇḍalī wakes by the guru's grace the knots are pierced.
**Texts:** 
  - [Gorakṣaśataka](../texts/goraksasataka.md) — ref: 30-37
  - [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) — ref: 3.2-3
### [Yoginī Kaula (Yoginīkaula) of Matsyendra](../lineages/yogini-kaula.md)
The Kaula path is twofold: the kuṇḍalī impelled by seals and mantras is 'made' (kṛtaka); the innate (sahaja) abides in samarasa; the Akulavīra is not in any support, channel or breath-practice.
- Akulavīra B.43–45: kṛtaka and sahaja.
- Akulavīra A.15–23: not in pūraka, kumbhaka, bindu-piercing...
**Texts:** 
  - [Akulavīratantra](../texts/akulavira-tantra.md) — ref: b.43-45; a.15-23
### [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md)
The Amaraughaśāsana (also ascribed to Gorakṣa) denies that liberation is the movement of kuṇḍalinī raised from the root: liberation is where the mind sees the mind in sahaja-samādhi.
- AmŚ 52–53: kuṇḍalinī's movement is a cultivated notion, not liberation.
- AmŚ 54: definition of mokṣa.
**Texts:** 
  - [Amaraughaśāsana](../texts/amaraughasasana.md) — ref: 46-54

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The haṭha texts themselves present effortful practice as a stairway to rājayoga/sahaja (HYP 1.1, 4.3–4, 4.114), so arousal of kuṇḍalinī belongs to the stage of practice and sahaja to the goal.
The Akulavīra still counts the 'made' path as a Kaula path (B.43) and the Amaraughaśāsana denies only that kuṇḍalinī's movement is itself liberation (AmŚ 52), not that it has a place; the Haṭhapradīpikā names the goal sahajā and calls talk of knowledge before sahaja hypocrisy. Read by stage, effort is the means and sahaja the end.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Akulavīra's apophatic verses (A.15–23) say the Akulavīra is not found in any practice at all, which resists a merely sequential reading; the haṭha texts would reject any suggestion that the practices are dispensable before sahaja arises.
