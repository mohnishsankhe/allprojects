# Is the ultimate one, or divisible (e.g. into a concordant/nominal and an actual ultimate)?

`dsp:paryaya-paramartha-division` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Kadam](../lineages/kadam.md)
The ultimate is only one; dharmatā, established as nothing at all, cannot be two or three (Atiśa).
- What is not established as anything cannot be counted.
**Texts:** 
  - [Entry into the Two Truths (Satyadvayāvatāra; bden pa gnyis la 'jug pa)](../texts/satyadvayavatara.md) — ref: v4
### [Svātantrika-Madhyamaka](../lineages/svatantrika.md)
The ultimate is taught as twofold: the actual ultimate beyond elaboration, and the concordant (nominal) ultimate — the reasoned negation of arising — which accords with it (Bhāviveka, Jñānagarbha).
- The reasoning consciousness's object is called ultimate because it accords with the ultimate.
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyamakahṛdayakārikā](../texts/madhyamakahrdaya.md) — ref: 3
  - [Satyadvayavibhaṅga](../texts/satyadvayavibhanga.md) — 

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level
**Explanation:** The 'concordant ultimate' is a conventional designation for what accords with the ultimate; Atiśa's 'one' concerns dharmatā itself, which the Svātantrikas also hold to be undivided.
**The traditions' own objections:** None recorded in the texts of this unit; U40 records the Svātantrika side fully.
