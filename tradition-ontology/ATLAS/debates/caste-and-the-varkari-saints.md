# May people of low birth know and expound the meaning of scripture and approach God on equal terms?

`dsp:caste-and-the-varkari-saints` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Vedic study, the exposition of the Veda's meaning and certain rights of worship belong to the twice-born by birth (adhikāra); the children of a sannyāsin who returned to household life are outcaste; a śūdra must not teach the Veda's meaning.
- Varṇa duties and rights are set by śruti and smṛti.
- Purity rules protect the rites and the temple.
**Texts:** 
  - [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) — ref: passim (rules of adhikāra; not cited verse by verse here)
### [Vārkarī sampradāya](../lineages/varkari.md)
Before God birth counts for nothing: the name is open to all; the devotee, whatever his birth, knows the Veda's meaning; the low-born saints are revered as equals; the Gītā itself promises the highest to women, vaiśyas and śūdras who take refuge.
- BhG 9.32 as expounded by Jñāneśvar: rivers lose their names in the ocean.
- The sugarcane is crooked, its juice is not (Cokhāmeḷā).
- 'We alone know the Veda's meaning; the others carry its burden' (Tukārām).
- The buffalo that recited the Veda shows one Self in all.
**Texts:** 
  - [Jñāneśvarī (Bhāvārthadīpikā)](../texts/jnanesvari.md) — ref: ch.9
  - [Cokhāmeḷā's abhaṅgas](../texts/cokhamela-abhangas.md) — ref: ūsa ḍoṅgā

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path
**Explanation:** The orthodox rules govern entitlement to Vedic ritual and study; the saints' claim concerns the path of the name and devotion, which scripture itself (BhG 9.32) opens to all.
Recorded as a partial reconciliation of scope only: the two sides legislate for different paths. It does not settle who may expound scripture, temple access, or social rank.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Vārkarī saints deny that birth confers any spiritual rank at all, including in understanding the Veda; orthodox authorities historically rejected low-caste exposition and temple entry. Neither side is recorded as accepting this reconciliation.
