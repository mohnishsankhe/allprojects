# Does liberation contain bliss (and consciousness)?

`dsp:bliss-in-liberation` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Mainstream Nyāya: liberation is absolute release from pain; there is no proof of an eternal pleasure manifested in it, and one who strives for liberation out of attachment to pleasure is not freed; the liberated self has neither pleasure nor cognition. Pleasure bound up with pain is to be relinquished like food mixed with honey and poison.
- NBh 1.1.22 against 'some' who hold eternal pleasure is manifested in liberation.
- Fear of a liberation 'without pleasure and without consciousness' is listed among forms of false knowledge (NBh 1.1.2).
- Navya-Nyāya: liberation is a destruction of pain not contemporaneous with any prior absence of pain (Muktivāda).
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.22
  - [Nyāyabhāṣya](../texts/nyaya-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.2; 1.1.22
  - [Muktivāda (Raghudeva)](../texts/muktivada-raghudeva.md) — ref: opening
### [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md)
Liberation is the absence of contact with a body and of new appearance when adṛṣṭa is exhausted; a quiescence like fire whose fuel is burnt (no special qualities of the self remain).
- VS 5.2.20 (Candrānanda); Praśastapāda.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vaiśeṣika Sūtra](../texts/vaisesika-sutra.md) — ref: 5.2.19–20
  - [Padārthadharmasaṅgraha](../texts/padarthadharmasangraha.md) — ref: dharma section
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Bhāsarvajña (a minority within Nyāya): liberation is the absolute cessation of pain qualified by the experience of eternal pleasure.
- Nyāyasāra and Nyāyabhūṣaṇa.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāyasāra](../texts/nyayasara.md) — ref: āgama chapter
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Liberation is the realization of the self as Brahman, being-consciousness-bliss; a liberation without consciousness or bliss would be no better than a stone's state — a charge pressed against Nyāya (e.g. in Śrīharṣa's Naiṣadhīyacarita, as usually cited).
- Polemical verse on Gautama and 'stone-hood' (ref from memory).
**Texts:** 
  - `src:naisadhiyacarita` — ref: 17.75 (from memory)
### [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md)
Pārthasārathi: no — release has no cognition and no bliss; it is like dreamless sleep without return, a human goal because pain ceases; Kumārila: release is not an enjoyment of pleasure, which would be a perishable heaven.
- Śāstradīpikā mokṣavāda; ŚV sambandhākṣepaparihāra 105–107.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śāstradīpikā](../texts/sastradipika.md) — ref: 1.1.5
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: sambandhākṣepaparihāra 105–107
### [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md)
Some Bhāṭṭas (reported in the Śāstradīpikā): yes — the self's innate bliss, overpowered in saṃsāra, becomes manifest and is enjoyed through the mind in release; Kumārila's verse states another's view.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śāstradīpikā](../texts/sastradipika.md) — ref: 1.1.5 (ānandamokṣavāda)
### [Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/prabhakara-mimamsa.md)
No — scripture does not make known accomplished things, and no supreme bliss is experienced by the embodied; release is valuable as the end of sorrow.
**Texts:** 
  - [Prakaraṇapañcikā](../texts/prakaranapancika.md) — ref: ch. 8
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Yes — liberation is the realization of Brahman, whose nature is bliss.

**Historical debate — Vātsyāyana against 'some' who hold eternal pleasure in liberation** (c. 5th c.): NBh 1.1.22 Nyāya: no means of knowledge proves eternal pleasure.

**Historical debate — Bhāsarvajña's dissent** (c. 10th c.): Nyāyasāra; Nyāyabhūṣaṇa remained a minority view in Nyāya

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled.
**The traditions' own objections:** Mainstream Nyāya explicitly rejects that the self is by nature conscious or blissful and rejects Bhāsarvajña's view; Advaita rejects a liberation without consciousness. The reconciliation is not accepted by either.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint / terminological: Nyāya denies pleasure as an adventitious quality produced by contact with objects; Advaita affirms bliss as the self's nature, not a produced quality — the two may partly talk past each other.; P1-level: Advaita could read Nyāya's account as describing liberation negatively from the empirical standpoint.; The Śāstradīpikā itself notes that on either view release is a human goal and differs from saṃsāra by the absence of pain.; P1-level: Advaita's bliss belongs to the absolute self, not to an experience of a knower — a reading the Mīmāṃsakas reject.
