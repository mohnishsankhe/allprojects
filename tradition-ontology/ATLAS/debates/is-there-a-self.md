# Is there a self (ātman, puruṣa, jīva) — an enduring conscious subject distinct from body and changing cognitions — or only a stream of impersonal factors designated 'person'?

`dsp:is-there-a-self` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G1

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
There is a self: an eternal, all-pervading substance distinct from body, senses, mind and cognitions; cognition, desire, aversion, effort, pleasure and pain are its qualities and the marks by which it is known. To think 'the self does not exist' is itself a form of the false knowledge that binds.
- Desire, aversion, effort, pleasure, pain and cognition need a substrate; they are the marks (liṅga) of the self (NS 1.1.10).
- One object is grasped by sight and by touch, so the grasper is other than either sense (NS 3.1.1); what the left eye saw the other recognizes (NS 3.1.7).
- Memory and recognition require the same knower at two times; a series of momentary cognitions could not remember what another cognized (NS 3.1.13-14; Uddyotakara; Udayana's Ātmatattvaviveka refutes momentariness first).
- A newborn's joy, fear and grief follow on memory of a former life (NS 3.1.18).
- If the self were the body there would be no sin in burning a body (NS 3.1.4).
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.10; 3.1.1-26
  - [Nyāyabhāṣya](../texts/nyaya-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.2; 3.1
  - [Nyāyavārttika](../texts/nyaya-varttika.md) — ref: 3.1.1
  - [Ātmatattvaviveka](../texts/atmatattvaviveka.md) — ref: whole
### [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md)
The self is not perceived by ordinary perception but inferred: breathing in and out, the closing and opening of the eyes, life, the movement of the mind, the functioning of the other senses, pleasure, pain, desire, aversion and effort are its marks; the word 'I' applies to it, not to the body; selves are many, since their states differ.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vaiśeṣika Sūtra](../texts/vaisesika-sutra.md) — ref: 3.1-3.2
  - [Padārthadharmasaṅgraha](../texts/padarthadharmasangraha.md) — ref: ātman section
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
A self distinct from body and cognition exists — the agent who enjoys the fruit of sacrifice in another world; without it the injunctions for heaven would be pointless. Kumārila: the self is known in the notion 'I'; Prabhākara: it shines forth in every cognition as its knower, never as its object.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śābarabhāṣya](../texts/sabara-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.5 (Vṛttikāra passage)
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: ātmavāda
  - [Prakaraṇapañcikā](../texts/prakaranapancika.md) — ref: ch. 8
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Puruṣa exists: aggregates exist for the sake of another; there must be something opposite to what has the three guṇas; there must be a controller, an enjoyer, and one for whose isolation activity takes place. Puruṣas are many, since birth, death and the instruments are allotted to each separately.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — ref: 17-18
  - [Sāṃkhya Sūtra](../texts/samkhya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.139-146; 6.1-3
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
The seer is seeing alone, which appears to conform to the mind's cognitions; the mind is not self-illuminating, because it is seen; if one cognition were grasped by another there would be infinite regress and confusion of memories; a mind that were only momentary cognitions could not be made one-pointed.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali](../texts/yoga-sutra.md) — ref: 2.20; 4.19-21
  - [Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra)](../texts/yoga-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.32; 4.21
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The self is the self-evident witness (sākṣin) of all cognitions, never an object and never deniable — no one thinks 'I am not'. Body, senses, breath, mind and intellect, and the agent-enjoyer, are not-self, superimposed on it. Against the Buddhists: memory and recognition ('I saw that, I see this') show one enduring knower; a momentary store-consciousness cannot hold impressions; the witness is not a momentary cognition.
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 1.1.1; 2.2.25-31; 3.3.53-54
  - [Dṛg-Dṛśya Viveka (Vākyasudhā)](../texts/drg-drsya-viveka.md) — ref: 1
  - [Vedāntasāra](../texts/vedantasara.md) — ref: refutation of mistaken views of the self
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
The self is a distinct, eternal, atomic knower, established in the awareness 'I', self-luminous; consciousness is its attribute, which belongs to someone and is of something — not the self itself; each self is a mode of Brahman.
**Texts:** 
  - [Siddhitraya](../texts/siddhitraya.md) — ref: Ātmasiddhi
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1 (mahāsiddhānta)
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The soul (jīva) exists; its defining mark is cognitive activity (upayoga); it is the doer and enjoyer of its karma, can be liberated, and is eternal as substance but non-eternal as mode (its states and births). One-sided permanence (as of the Naiyāyikas) and one-sided momentariness (as of the Buddhists) are both refuted.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvārthasūtra](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md) — ref: 2.8
  - [Bhagavatī Sūtra (Vyākhyāprajñapti)](../texts/bhagavati-sutra.md) — ref: 7.2
  - [Viśeṣāvaśyaka Bhāṣya](../texts/visesavasyaka-bhasya.md) — ref: gaṇadharavāda
  - [Āptamīmāṃsā](../texts/aptamimamsa.md) — ref: pariccheda 3
  - [Ātmasiddhi Śāstra](../texts/atmasiddhi.md) — ref: 43
### [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md)
There is a soul within the body and distinct from it: it says 'not this' of each thing, speaks of 'my body', knows through the senses, and knows on waking that it slept; souls are many, their experience, bondage and release differing.
**Texts:** 
  - [Civañāṉa Pōtam](../texts/sivananabodham.md) — ref: sūtra 3
  - [Nareśvaraparīkṣā](../texts/naresvarapariksa.md) — ref: ch. 1-2
### [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md)
Against the Buddhist that only momentary cognitions appear and no permanent seer: memory is impossible if the remembering cognition is separate from the experience; the unity of cognitions at different times is the one conscious subject, Maheśvara, endowed with the powers of knowing, remembering and excluding.
**Texts:** 
  - [Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā](../texts/isvarapratyabhijna-karika.md) — ref: 1.2-1.4
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
Each of the five aggregates is not-self: were it self it would not lead to affliction and one could say 'let it be thus'; what is impermanent and painful is not fit to be regarded 'this is mine, this I am, this is my self'. 'Being' is a designation, as 'chariot' is for assembled parts. Asked 'is there a self? is there no self?' the Buddha was silent: either answer would side with eternalism or annihilationism.
**Texts:** 
  - [Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta](../texts/anattalakkhana-sutta.md) — ref: SN 22.59
  - [Saṃyutta Nikāya](../texts/samyutta-nikaya.md) — ref: SN 5.10; SN 44.10
  - [Majjhima Nikāya](../texts/majjhima-nikaya.md) — ref: MN 22; MN 38
  - [Dhammapada](../texts/dhammapada.md) — ref: 277-279
### [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
The person is not found as a real and ultimate fact; 'Nāgasena' and 'chariot' are designations; as the chariot is not found when each part is examined, there is no being in the ultimate sense, only mentality-materiality.
**Texts:** 
  - [Milindapañha](../texts/milindapanha.md) — ref: the chariot dialogue (locator as in tea:milindapanha:3.1.1)
  - [Visuddhimagga](../texts/visuddhimagga.md) — ref: ch. 18
  - [Kathāvatthu](../texts/kathavatthu.md) — ref: 1.1
### [Sautrāntika](../lineages/sautrantika.md)
Apart from the series of aggregates there is no self; 'self' designates the series. Those outside this teaching are not liberated because they hold 'self' to be a separate entity rather than a designation for the series (Vasubandhu, AKBh ch. 9); only aggregates, conditioned by defilement and karma, enter the womb through the intermediate existence, like a flame.
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhidharmakośabhāṣya](../texts/abhidharmakosabhasya.md) — ref: ch. 9 (Pudgalaviniścaya)
  - [Abhidharmakośa (Abhidharmakośakārikā)](../texts/abhidharmakosa.md) — ref: 3.18
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
One who sees a self loves it as 'I', thirsts for its happiness, and that thirst hides faults and drives rebirth: the view of self is the root of saṃsāra. The selves of the Naiyāyikas, Mīmāṃsakas, Sāṃkhyas, Jains, Upaniṣadic thinkers and Vātsīputrīyas are examined and refuted; whatever exists is momentary.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: 1.219-221 (svārthānumāna numbering as recorded)
  - [Tattvasaṅgraha of Śāntarakṣita](../texts/tattvasangraha.md) — ref: Ātmaparīkṣā
  - [Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi (Ratnakīrti)](../texts/ksanabhangasiddhi-ratnakirti.md) — ref: whole
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
If the self were the aggregates it would arise and perish; if other than them it would lack their marks. The self is neither the aggregates, nor other than them, nor their possessor — like the chariot sought in seven ways. The Buddhas have taught 'self', have taught 'no-self', and have taught neither self nor no-self; 'I exist' and 'mine exists' are false in the ultimate sense.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mūlamadhyamakakārikā](../texts/mulamadhyamakakarika.md) — ref: ch. 18; 27.8

### [Pudgalavāda](../lineages/pudgalavada.md)
A person (pudgala) exists, designated in dependence on the present aggregates, neither the same as nor different from them, inexpressible — as fire in dependence on fuel; it is the bearer of the burden of the aggregates (the middle position; see dsp:pudgala).
**Texts:** 
  - [Abhidharmakośabhāṣya](../texts/abhidharmakosabhasya.md) — ref: ch. 9 (as reported)

### [Tathāgatagarbha (Buddha-nature) tradition](../lineages/tathagatagarbha.md)
'Self' in the true sense means the tathāgatagarbha: all beings have buddha-nature; seeing the Tathāgata as impermanent, suffering, non-self and impure is inverted; the dharmakāya has the perfection of self. (Other Mahāyāna texts read this 'self' as a skilful name for emptiness — see dsp:buddha-nature-self-or-emptiness.)

**Historical debate — Kathāvatthu 1.1 (Puggalakathā)** (tradition: the Third Council at Pāṭaliputra under Aśoka): Kathāvatthu 1.1 and its commentary (opponents named as Vajjiputtakas and Sammitīyas) Theravāda: the person is not found as a real and ultimate fact; the personalists refuted.

**Historical debate — Mahāvīra and Indrabhūti Gautama (the first gaṇadharavāda)** (tradition: Mahāvīra's lifetime): Viśeṣāvaśyakabhāṣya, gaṇadharavāda (the tradition's account) Jain: Indrabhūti, who secretly doubted the soul's existence, was shown that it is known directly in the awareness 'I' and became the first gaṇadhara.

**Historical debate — Nāgasena and King Milinda: the chariot** (tradition: the reign of Milinda): Milindapañha (the tradition's account) Theravāda: the king accepts that 'Nāgasena', like 'chariot', is a designation.

**Historical debate — The Nyāya–Buddhist exchange on the self (textual)** (c. 5th–11th c. CE (scholarly, approximate)): Nyāyabhāṣya and Nyāyavārttika on NS 3.1; Pramāṇavārttika; Tattvasaṅgraha (Ātmaparīkṣā); Ātmatattvaviveka; Ratnakīrti's Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi Each tradition holds that its own arguments stand; the exchange continued until Buddhism waned in India.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: all parties agree that the body, senses and changing cognitions are not the true self, but they disagree whether anything beyond them — an enduring conscious subject — exists.
Partial convergences recorded without reconciling: (a) Advaita, Sāṃkhya-Yoga and the Buddhists all deny that the agent-ego or the aggregates are the self; (b) the Jains offer their own standpoint synthesis — the soul permanent as substance, changing as mode (Bhagavatī 7.2; Āptamīmāṃsā 3); (c) Kṣemarāja places every doctrine of the self as a role of the one consciousness at a particular level (Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya 8). None of these is accepted by the other parties.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Buddhist logicians refute every permanent self, the Upaniṣadic one included (Tattvasaṅgraha, Ātmaparīkṣā); Theravāda and Vasubandhu reject even a person neither same nor different from the aggregates; Nyāya counts denial of the self as false knowledge (NBh 1.1.2); Advaita denies that its witness is the Buddhist's momentary cognition (BSBh 2.2.28); Madhyamaka rejects both 'self' and 'no-self' as fixed views (MMK 18.6).
