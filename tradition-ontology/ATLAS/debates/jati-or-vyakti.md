# Does a word denote the universal (jāti, ākṛti) or the individual (vyakti, dravya)?

`dsp:jati-or-vyakti` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md)
Vājapyāyana: the universal form; Vyāḍi: the individual substance; Patañjali accepts both; Bhartṛhari: the universal is the true part, and ultimately all words denote Being (sattā) / the Real, as all ornament-words denote gold.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vyākaraṇa-Mahābhāṣya](../texts/mahabhasya.md) — ref: on P 1.2.64
  - [Vākyapadīya](../texts/vakyapadiya.md) — ref: 3.1-3.2
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
The word denotes the universal (ākṛti), which is real and eternal; the individual is understood through it.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mīmāṃsā Sūtra](../texts/mimamsa-sutra.md) — ref: 1.3.33
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
The meaning of a word is the individual, the form and the universal together.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 2.2.68
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
There are no real universals: a word signifies by excluding what is other (apoha).
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇasamuccaya](../texts/pramanasamuccaya.md) — ref: ch. 5

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Patañjali himself treats universal and individual as two grammatical standpoints, and Nyāya combines them; the realist schools are reconciled as standpoints.
The reconciliation covers the realist schools only; the Buddhist apoha theory denies the reality of the universal and is not reconciled.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Buddhists reject any real universal; Mīmāṃsā insists that the universal alone is primarily denoted.
