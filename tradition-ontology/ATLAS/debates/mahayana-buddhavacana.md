# Are the Mahāyāna sūtras the word of the Buddha?

`dsp:mahayana-buddhavacana` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md)
Yes: they were taught by the Buddha to bodhisattva assemblies (and some kept by nāgas until beings could receive them); rejecting them is grave karma.
- Rejecting the perfection of wisdom leads to the hells (Aṣṭa 7).
- One who rejects the bodhisattva collection as not conducive to liberation becomes an icchantika (Laṅkāvatāra p. 28).
- Arguments of the Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra ch. 1 (U41).
**Texts:** 
  - [Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā](../texts/astasahasrika-prajnaparamita.md) — ref: ch. 7
  - [Laṅkāvatāra-sūtra](../texts/lankavatara-sutra.md) — ref: Vaidya p. 28
  - [Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra](../texts/mahayanasutralamkara.md) — ref: ch. 1
### [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
No: the Buddha's word is what was recited at the councils; the 'Vetulla' (Vaitulya) teachings are not the Buddha's word.
- The Kathāvatthu refutes Vetullaka views (e.g. that the Buddha did not himself teach the dharma) (U37).
- The Sri Lankan chronicles report kings suppressing the Vetullavāda at the Abhayagiri (from memory).
**Texts:** 
  - [Kathāvatthu](../texts/kathavatthu.md) — ref: Vetullaka sections
  - [Mahāvaṃsa](../texts/mahavamsa.md) — ref: ch. 36-37 (from memory)
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
Yes, by seven reasons: the Buddha did not warn of it as a future danger; it arose together with the disciples' vehicle; its content is beyond the reach of reasoners; it is established (by its results); whether or not a Mahāyāna exists, the conclusion follows; it is the antidote to afflictions; its meaning is not its letter (Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra 1.7); much of the Buddha's word has been lost (Vyākhyāyukti).
- MSA 1.7–21
- Vyākhyāyukti ch. 4
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra](../texts/mahayanasutralamkara.md) — ref: 1.7
  - [Vyākhyāyukti](../texts/vyakhyayukti.md) — ref: ch. 4
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
No: the Buddha's word is what is found in the sūtras and vinaya as recited; the Mahāyāna teachings are not found there and contradict them (as the objector is presented in these texts).
- Objections reported in MSA 1 and Vyākhyāyukti 4
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra](../texts/mahayanasutralamkara.md) — ref: 1
  - [Vyākhyāyukti](../texts/vyakhyayukti.md) — ref: 4

**Historical debate — Suppression of the Vetullavāda in Anurādhapura** (3rd-4th c. CE (chronicle account)): The Mahāvaṃsa reports that kings (Vohārika Tissa; later Goṭhābhaya) suppressed the Vetullavāda associated with the Abhayagiri monastery. Theravāda (Mahāvihāra) orthodoxy restored, per the chronicle; details from memory, low confidence.

**Historical debate — Xuanzang's assembly at Kanyākubja** (642–643 CE): Chinese biographies: before King Harṣa, Xuanzang set out a Mahāyāna treatise (against Sammitīya and Hīnayāna objections) and for eighteen days no one refuted it. Chinese account: Xuanzang victorious

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: an authenticity question that none of the eight principles decides.
**The traditions' own objections:** Theravāda rejects the Mahāyāna sūtras as buddhavacana; the Mahāyāna rejects the council criterion as the only test.
**Candidate readings:** P4: sūtras addressed to disciples of higher capacity, not recited in the śrāvaka councils (the Mahāyāna's own account).; P5: the Mahāyāna sūtras as the definitive intent of what the earlier sūtras taught provisionally (Mahāyāna reading) — rejected by Theravāda.
