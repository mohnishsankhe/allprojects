# Should Arjuna fight the battle in which his elders, teachers and kinsmen stand on the other side?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
He should not fight: killing his own people brings no good and only sin; he would rather live on alms, or be killed unresisting.
- He sees adverse omens and no good in killing his own people; he wants neither victory, kingship nor pleasures, and would not kill them even for rule over the three worlds (1.31–35).
- Sin would come upon them even for killing these aggressors (ātatāyin), and no happiness could follow; seeing the fault, they should turn back (1.36–39, 1.45).
- Destroying a family destroys its eternal family dharmas; adharma then prevails, the women are corrupted, the varṇas are mixed, the ancestors fall deprived of their offerings of rice-balls and water, and those whose family dharmas are uprooted dwell in hell — 'so we have heard' (1.40–44).
- It would be better to be killed unarmed and unresisting (1.46), or to live on alms than to kill venerable gurus like Bhīṣma and Droṇa and enjoy blood-smeared pleasures (2.4–5).
- It is not known which outcome is better, and they would not wish to live after killing them (2.6); no kingdom, even lordship over the gods, would dispel his grief (2.8); 'I will not fight' (2.9).

### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
He should stand up and fight, without grief, as his own dharma, with equanimity and without attachment to the fruit, finally resigning all actions to Kṛṣṇa.
- His faintness and unmanliness are unworthy of the noble, do not lead to heaven and bring disgrace (2.2–3).
- The wise do not grieve: the embodied one is eternal and neither slays nor is slain; only its bodies have an end, therefore fight (2.11–25, 2.30; esp. 2.18).
- Even if the self were born and died again and again, death is certain for the born, so grief would still be unfit (2.26–28).
- For a kṣatriya nothing is better than a battle in accordance with dharma; refusing it brings sin and a disgrace worse than death, fighting brings heaven or the earth (2.31–37).
- Fighting with pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat made the same incurs no sin (2.38); action done with the right only to the act, not to its fruit, does not bind (2.47–51).
- No one can remain without action; allotted action is better than inaction (3.5, 3.8); one should act for the holding together of the world, as Janaka did and as Kṛṣṇa himself does (3.20–24).
- Resigning all actions in Kṛṣṇa, free of expectation and of 'mine', fight (3.30); one's own dharma, even deficient, is better than another's well performed (3.35).

### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Renouncing all actions to Kṛṣṇa in thought, with thought on him, Arjuna should act; his resolve not to fight is vain; he should take refuge in the Lord.
- With thought on Kṛṣṇa he will cross all difficulties by his grace; from the sense of 'I' he would perish (18.57–58).
- His nature will compel him; bound by nature-born action he will do even unwillingly what he does not wish (18.59–60).
- One whose disposition is not shaped by the sense of 'I' and whose understanding is not stained does not slay though slaying, and is not bound (18.17).
- The Lord in the heart moves all beings by his māyā; take refuge in him (18.61–62).
- Not fleeing in battle is a kṣatriya's nature-born action (18.43).
- Having reflected fully, do as you wish (18.63); relinquishing all dharmas, come to me for refuge (18.66).
