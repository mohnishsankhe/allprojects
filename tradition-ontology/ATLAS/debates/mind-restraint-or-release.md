# Should the mind be restrained, or — once the truth is known — left unrestrained?

`dsp:mind-restraint-or-release` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Yoga is the restraint (nirodha) of the fluctuations of the mind.
- YS 1.2.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali](../texts/yoga-sutra.md) — ref: 1.2
### [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md)
The breath must be bound, for when the breath moves the mind moves and when it is still the yogī becomes like a post (GŚ 38–39).
- GŚ 38–39.
**Texts:** 
  - [Gorakṣaśataka](../texts/goraksasataka.md) — ref: 38-39
### [Yoginī Kaula (Yoginīkaula) of Matsyendra](../lineages/yogini-kaula.md)
Once the Kaula is known, restraint of the mind is not to be done; scripture, guru, mantra and Śiva are all free of elaboration (KJN 7.26–27); the mind is not to be grasped nor concentration held — the mind beyond mind is liberated (KJN 13.5).
- KJN 7.26–27.
- KJN 13.5.
**Texts:** 
  - [Kaulajñānanirṇaya](../texts/kaulajnananirnaya.md) — ref: 7.26-27; 13.2-7

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The Kaulajñānanirṇaya itself conditions its rule on the stage — 'when the Kaula is known' — so restraint belongs to the practitioner and release to the knower.
KJN 7.26 reads 'mano-rodhaṃ na kartavyaṃ yadā jñātaṃ hi kaulikam'; the Gorakṣaśataka and the Yoga Sūtra address the one still practising.
**The traditions' own objections:** Not recorded; the Pātañjala tradition would require that release not be claimed before the mind is actually still.
