# Is knowledge the means of devotion, is devotion a means to knowledge, or is devotion its own fruit?

`dsp:is-knowledge-the-means-of-bhakti` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G9

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md)
Devotion is its own fruit and higher than action, knowledge and yoga; it is not knowledge, since even one who hates the Lord may know him; knowing about a king or food neither pleases the king nor satisfies hunger.
- devotion is phala-rūpa (NBS 26, 30)
- knowledge exists in haters without devotion (ŚBS)
- the king/house/food examples (NBS 31–32)
- the Lord loves humility, not the pride of the knower (NBS 27)

  - [Nārada Bhakti Sūtra](../texts/narada-bhakti-sutra.md) — ref: 25-33
  - [Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra](../texts/sandilya-bhakti-sutra.md) — ref: 4
### [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md)
Two views the Nārada Sūtra reports without naming holders: 'some' say knowledge alone is its means; 'others' that knowledge and devotion depend on each other.
- reported in NBS 28–29
**Texts:** 
  - [Nārada Bhakti Sūtra](../texts/narada-bhakti-sutra.md) — ref: 28-29
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Knowledge of the self alone removes ignorance and liberates; devotion purifies the mind and prepares for knowledge, and in its highest sense is the constant search into one's own nature.
- among the means of liberation devotion is supreme — and devotion is self-inquiry (Vivekacūḍāmaṇi 31)
- the mind melted by the Lord's qualities flows to him (Madhusūdana) — devotion as a rasa accompanying knowledge
**Texts:** 
  - [Vivekacūḍāmaṇi](../texts/vivekacudamani.md) — ref: 31
  - [Bhaktirasāyana](../texts/bhaktirasayana.md) — ref: 1.3
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
The knowledge the Upaniṣads enjoin for liberation is itself devotion — steady remembrance vivid as perception, knowledge that has become love, won by grace; knowledge and devotion are not two.
- dhruvānusmṛti = bhakti (Śrībhāṣya 1.1.1)
- bhakti is knowledge in the form of love (Vedārthasaṅgraha)
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1
  - [Vedārthasaṅgraha](../texts/vedarthasangraha.md) — ref: bhakti section
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
One who has become Brahman attains supreme devotion; by devotion he knows the Lord in truth and enters him; by undivided devotion he is known, seen and entered.
- BhG 18.54–55
- BhG 11.54
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) — ref: 18.54-55; 11.54

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path, P4-stage
**Explanation:** Read as differences of path and stage: the Gītā places supreme devotion both after becoming Brahman and before knowing the Lord in truth, so knowledge and love can each precede the other according to the seeker's path and stage.
Under P3 the knowledge-path makes devotion a preparation or companion of knowledge, the devotion-path makes love the goal; under P4 the Nārada Sūtra's own grading (secondary devotion by motive, NBS 56–57) allows knowledge-seeking devotion as a lower stage of love. This reading does not claim the schools agree on what liberates.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Nārada Sūtra insists devotion is never merely a means (NBS 30–33); Advaita holds that only knowledge removes ignorance, so love cannot be the final means; Rāmānuja rejects the separation of knowledge from devotion that both of the others assume.
