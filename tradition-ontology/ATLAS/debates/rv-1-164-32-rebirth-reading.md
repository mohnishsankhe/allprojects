# Does RV 1.164.32 ('having many offspring he has entered Nirṛti') speak of repeated births?

`dsp:rv-1-164-32-rebirth-reading` · `sourced` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
The wandering ascetics (parivrājakāḥ): 'one with many births comes to misery' — the verse speaks of the embodied being entering misery through many births.
- The verse's subject is enveloped in the mother's womb and 'has many offspring' (bahuprajāḥ), read as 'many births'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 2.8
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
The etymologists (nairuktāḥ): the verse refers to the action of rain — the cloud (born of the mother, the atmosphere), 'rich in offspring', comes down to the earth (nirṛti read as bhūmi).
- Yāska glosses word by word: 'mother' is the atmosphere, 'enveloped' by wind, 'nirṛti' the earth.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 2.8

**Historical debate — Yāska's report of two schools on RV 1.164.32** (late Vedic (Nirukta)): Nirukta 2.8 both readings recorded

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The Vedic interpretive tradition itself reads verses on several planes (of the rite, of the deities, of the self); the rain reading is the adhidaivata plane, the many-births reading the adhyātma plane.
Yāska records both readings without forbidding either. Scholarly metadata: modern scholars generally find no explicit rebirth doctrine in the Saṃhitās (it becomes explicit in the Upaniṣads); this is noted, not used.
**The traditions' own objections:** None recorded within the Nirukta; Mīmāṃsā would subordinate both to the verse's ritual use.
