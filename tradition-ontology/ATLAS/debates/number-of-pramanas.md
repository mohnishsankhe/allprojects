# How many means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa) are there?

`dsp:number-of-pramanas` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G15

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
Two: perception and inference, because the object is twofold — the particular, capable of causal efficacy, grasped by perception, and the universal, grasped by inference; testimony and the rest are inference or not means of knowledge.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇasamuccaya](../texts/pramanasamuccaya.md) — ref: 1.2
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: 2.1-3 (numbering as recorded)
  - [Nyāyabindu](../texts/nyayabindu.md) — ref: 1.2-3
### [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md)
Two: perception and inference; verbal testimony, comparison, presumption and the other proposed means are included in inference, since they work in the same way.
**Texts:** 
  - [Padārthadharmasaṅgraha](../texts/padarthadharmasangraha.md) — ref: anumāna section
  - [Vaiśeṣika Sūtra](../texts/vaisesika-sutra.md) — ref: 9.18-19
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Three: perception, inference and reliable testimony, since all other means are included in these (presumption and inclusion in inference, absence in perception; tradition is testimony only if its speaker is reliable).
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — ref: 4
  - [Sāṃkhyatattvakaumudī](../texts/tattvakaumudi.md) — ref: on 5
### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
Three: perception, inference and testimony are the means of valid cognition.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali](../texts/yoga-sutra.md) — ref: 1.7
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
Valid cognition is knowledge that illumines itself and its object; it is of two kinds, direct (pratyakṣa) and indirect (parokṣa): of the five kinds of knowledge, sensory and scriptural knowledge are indirect, clairvoyance, mind-reading and omniscience direct; memory, recognition, reasoning and inference are forms of sensory knowledge.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvārthasūtra](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md) — ref: 1.9-13
  - [Nyāyāvatāra](../texts/nyayavatara.md) — ref: 1
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Four: perception, inference, comparison and verbal testimony; tradition (aitihya) is not different from testimony, and presumption, inclusion (sambhava) and absence are not different from inference. (Bhāsarvajña's Nyāyasāra, within Nyāya, counts three, comparison not being separate.)
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.3; 2.2.1-2
  - [Nyāyasāra](../texts/nyayasara.md) — ref: pramāṇa section
### [Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/prabhakara-mimamsa.md)
Five: perception, inference, verbal testimony, comparison and presumption — neither fewer nor more; absence is not a separate means.
**Texts:** 
  - [Prakaraṇapañcikā](../texts/prakaranapancika.md) — ref: ch. 6
### [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md)
Six: the five, and non-apprehension (abhāva/anupalabdhi), which knows the non-existence of a thing where the five positive means do not arise; the Vṛttikāra's list in Śabara already names six.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: abhāva 1-4
  - [Śābarabhāṣya](../texts/sabara-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.5 (Vṛttikāra)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Six, following the Bhāṭṭas in empirical matters: perception, inference, comparison, verbal testimony, presumption and non-cognition; valid cognition is of an object not already known and not contradicted. Śrīharṣa: the Advaitin may debate on the practices both parties accept without committing to the reality of the means of knowledge.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vedāntaparibhāṣā](../texts/vedantaparibhasa.md) — ref: ch. 1
  - [Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya](../texts/khandanakhandakhadya.md) — ref: opening
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
Three: perception, inference and scripture; comparison and the rest are included in these.
**Texts:** 
  - [Yatīndramatadīpikā](../texts/yatindramatadipika.md) — ref: 1-3
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
Valid knowledge accords with its object: knowledge itself (kevala) and its means (anupramāṇa); the means are three — perception (sevenfold, with the witness), inference and authoritative word; presumption and comparison are kinds of inference.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇalakṣaṇa](../texts/pramanalaksana-madhva.md) — ref: whole
### [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md)
Three: perception (of the senses and of the self), inference, and scripture (āgama) handed down from Maheśvara.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pañcārthabhāṣya](../texts/pancarthabhasya.md) — ref: on 1.1
### [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md)
Examination is fourfold: authoritative testimony, perception, inference and yukti (reasoning that sees effects produced by many combined causes).
**Texts:** 
  - [Caraka Saṃhitā](../texts/caraka-samhita.md) — ref: Sūtrasthāna 11.17-26
### [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md)
Eight (as the Naiyāyika doxography reports): the Paurāṇikas add inclusion (sambhava) and tradition (aitihya) to the six.
**Texts:** 
  - [Dinakarī (Muktāvalīprakāśa)](../texts/dinakari.md) — ref: on Kārikāvalī 51-52
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 2.2.1 (the candidates named)
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
In the matter of the transcendent, perception and inference are fallible; revelation (śabda) alone is decisive, and the Purāṇas complete the Veda.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvasandarbha](../texts/tattva-sandarbha.md) — ref: pramāṇa section
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
If the means of knowledge establish their objects, what establishes the means? Neither by other means (regress) nor by themselves (fire does not illumine itself) nor by their objects; 'if I apprehended anything by perception and the rest I would affirm or deny' — the Mādhyamika fixes no count.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vigrahavyāvartanī](../texts/vigrahavyavartani.md) — ref: 30-51
### [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](../lineages/carvaka.md)
(As reported) Perception alone — inference fails because pervasion cannot be known.
- no means establishes vyāpti; upādhi cannot be excluded
**Texts:** 
  - [Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha](../texts/sarvadarsanasangraha.md) — ref: ch. 1
### [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](../lineages/carvaka.md)
(Purandara) Inference as known in the world is valid; only inference to the transcendent is rejected.
- worldly inference vs inference beyond the worldly path
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvasaṅgraha-pañjikā](../texts/tattvasangraha-panjika.md) — ref: on 1481-82
### [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](../lineages/carvaka.md)
(Suśikṣita Cārvākas; Jayarāśi) The number cannot be fixed; no definition of any means of knowledge can be made good.
- circularity of every definition of perception and inference
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāyamañjarī](../texts/nyayamanjari.md) — ref: āhnika 1
  - [Tattvopaplavasiṃha](../texts/tattvopaplavasimha.md) — ref: 0.3–5
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
Perception and inference; one who denies inference uses it to convey his intention, and the world itself infers causes from effects.
- TS 1456, 1481–82
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvasaṅgraha of Śāntarakṣita](../texts/tattvasangraha.md) — ref: 1456; 1481-82

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Largely reconciled by the schools' own device of inclusion (antarbhāva): the Nyāya Sūtra folds tradition into testimony and presumption, inclusion and absence into inference; Vaiśeṣika, Sāṃkhya, Madhva and the Viśiṣṭādvaitins fold the extra means into fewer — differences of classification from different analytic standpoints (P2); the Cārvāka denial of inference, the status of testimony for the supersensible, and Madhyamaka's refusal to fix any means remain not yet reconciled.
What the schools agree is known (a fire from smoke, an absence of a pot, a word's meaning) they classify under different heads; the disagreement that matters doctrinally is over whether verbal testimony (the Veda, the Āgama, the Buddha's word) is an independent source for the supersensible (Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta, Nyāya, Jain, Gauḍīya) or reducible to inference (Buddhist, Vaiśeṣika).
**The traditions' own objections:** Each school defends the irreducibility of its own count: the Bhāṭṭas and Advaitins insist non-apprehension is not perception; the Prābhākaras reject it; Nyāya insists comparison is irreducible; the Buddhists deny testimony independent standing; the Cārvākas (as reported) deny inference; Jayarāśi and Nāgārjuna refuse any fixed set.
