# Are Tsongkhapa's eight 'difficult points' genuinely Candrakīrti's Prāsaṅgika?

`dsp:eight-difficult-points` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
Prāsaṅgika uniquely rejects an ālaya distinct from the six consciousnesses and reflexive awareness even conventionally, rejects autonomous inference, accepts external objects conventionally, holds that śrāvakas realise the selflessness of phenomena, that grasping at the self of phenomena is an affliction, that disintegration is a functioning thing, and so presents the three times uniquely.
- Each point follows from accepting no inherent existence even conventionally (e.g. karma needs no ālaya because 'disintegratedness' can function).
- Candrakīrti's Madhyamakāvatāra and Prasannapadā are cited for each.
**Texts:** 
  - [Memorandum on the Eight Great Difficult Points of the Root Wisdom (dbu ma rtsa ba'i dka' gnas chen po brgyad kyi brjed byang)](../texts/eight-difficult-points-memorandum.md) — 
  - [Illumination of the Thought (dbu ma dgongs pa rab gsal)](../texts/illumination-of-the-thought.md) — ref: ch. 6
### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
Sakya critics (Taktsang Lotsawa, Gorampa, Shākya Chokden) held several of these to be Tsongkhapa's innovations, charging contradictions (Taktsang's 'eighteen great burdens'): e.g. accepting conventional valid cognition and external objects under the name of Prāsaṅgika, or making disintegration a thing.
- Candrakīrti accepts conventions only as the world accepts them, without analysis.
- Positing a disintegratedness that functions reintroduces a quasi-substance.
**Texts:** 
  - [Distinguishing the Views (lta ba'i shan 'byed theg mchog gnad kyi zla zer)](../texts/taway-shenje.md) —

**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the dispute concerns which positions are Candrakīrti's.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Gelug holds the eight points are Candrakīrti's explicit or implied view; the Sakya critics hold them to be additions.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: Gelug systematises what conventional designation requires once inherent existence is denied; the critics speak from the standpoint of the unexamined worldly convention.; P5-neyartha: the disagreement is over which passages of Candrakīrti are definitive.
