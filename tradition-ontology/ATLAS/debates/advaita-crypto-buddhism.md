# Is Advaita Vedānta — the doctrine of māyā — 'Buddhism in disguise' (pracchanna-bauddha)?

`dsp:advaita-crypto-buddhism` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Bhedābheda Vedānta (Bhāskara's school and the early difference-and-non-difference Vedāntins)](../lineages/bhedabheda.md)
Bhāskara: the doctrine of māyā — disputed, cut off from its root and 'sung by the Mahāyāna Buddhists' — leads people astray.
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Bhāskara (Bhāskarabhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-bhaskara.md) — ref: 1.4.25
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
Madhva and Jayatīrtha: the māyā-teacher and the Mādhyamika are both to be ignored — what is the void for the voidist is Brahman for the māyā-teacher; both ultimates are attributeless and defined only by exclusion; the voidists' saṃvṛti is the māyā-teachers' māyā, alike beginningless, indeterminable, concealing and projecting. Liberation cannot come from knowing the void.
**Texts:** 
  - [Anuvyākhyāna](../texts/anuvyakhyana.md) — ref: 2.2.241-242; 3.3.155; 4.2.40-48
  - [Nyāyasudhā](../texts/nyayasudha.md) — ref: on 4.2.46
### [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md)
The Padma Purāṇa (Uttarakhaṇḍa), in Śiva's list of heretical teachings: 'māyāvāda is a false teaching, called Buddhism in disguise; I taught it in the Kali age in the form of a brāhmaṇa'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Padma Purāṇa](../texts/padma-purana.md) — ref: Uttarakhaṇḍa (as quoted in Paramātmasandarbha 71)
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
Jīva Gosvāmī: the doctrine that the world is false and the Lord's lordship imagined by ignorance is the view the Gītā (16.8) calls godless; it conflicts with the Lord's word and is rightly called māyāvāda, which the Padma Purāṇa calls Buddhism in disguise. Caitanya (as the Gauḍīya texts present him) rejects Śaṅkara's commentary as hiding the sūtras' direct sense.
**Texts:** 
  - [Paramātmasandarbha](../texts/paramatma-sandarbha.md) — ref: anuccheda 71
  - [Caitanya Caritāmṛta](../texts/caitanya-caritamrta.md) — ref: Madhya 6
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
Vedānta Deśika opens the Śatadūṣaṇī urging followers of Rāmānuja's path to 'strive on every side for victory over the crypto-Buddhists (pracchanna-bauddha)'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Śatadūṣaṇī](../texts/satadusani.md) — ref: opening verse(s)
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Vijñānabhikṣu: the māyā doctrine of the 'modern' Vedāntins is not the Brahmasūtra's teaching; with the Padma Purāṇa he counts it among heterodox (crypto-Buddhist) teachings.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhyapravacanabhāṣya](../texts/samkhya-pravacana-bhasya.md) — ref: introduction (low confidence)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Advaita is not Buddhism: Śaṅkara refutes all three Buddhist doctrines — the realist, the idealist and the void — and dismisses the void as opposed by every means of knowledge; against the idealist he upholds external objects and the undeniable, self-established witness; memory proves one enduring knower. The non-dual reality free of knower, known and knowing 'was not spoken by the Buddha': the Buddhist denial of external objects is only near to non-duality, which is to be known from the Upaniṣads alone. Brahman is being (sat) and consciousness, the self that no one can deny — not a void and not a momentary cognition.
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 2.2.18-32
  - [Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (Gauḍapāda Kārikā, Āgamaśāstra)](../texts/mandukya-karika.md) — ref: 4.99
  - [Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-Gauḍapādīyakārikā-bhāṣya (ascribed to Śaṅkara)](../texts/mandukya-karika-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 4.99
  - [Chāndogya Upaniṣad](../texts/chandogya-upanisad.md) — ref: 6.2.1-2

**Historical debate — Caitanya and Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya at Purī** (tradition: early 16th c. CE): Caitanya-caritāmṛta Madhya 6 (the Gauḍīya tradition's account) Gauḍīya: Sārvabhauma, after expounding Śaṅkara's reading for seven days, accepted Caitanya's and became his devotee.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the critics hold that Advaita's attributeless Brahman and māyā equal the Buddhists' void and saṃvṛti; Advaita holds that Brahman as being-consciousness and the undeniable witness are exactly what the Buddhists deny.
Both parties agree on some facts — Advaita and Madhyamaka each use two levels of truth, and Gauḍapāda uses Buddhist-like language — but disagree whether the difference in what is called ultimate (sat-cit vs śūnyatā) is decisive (Advaita: MK-bhāṣya 4.99; BSBh 2.2.31) or merely verbal (Jayatīrtha, Nyāyasudhā 4.2.46). The Buddhists' own view of the Upaniṣadic self is recorded in dsp:is-there-a-self (Madhyamakahṛdaya 8; Tattvasaṅgraha).
**The traditions' own objections:** Advaita rejects the charge (BSBh 2.2.18-32; MK-bhāṣya 4.99: 'this was not spoken by the Buddha'). The critics reject the reply: Jayatīrtha says the marks of māyā and saṃvṛti are the same; Jīva Gosvāmī reads Gītā 16.8 as describing māyāvāda. Buddhist authors (Bhāviveka, Śāntarakṣita) themselves refute the Vedāntic self and would not accept Advaita as Buddhist either.
