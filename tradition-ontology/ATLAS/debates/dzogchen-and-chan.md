# Is the Great Perfection the 'sudden' Chan of Moheyan, rejected at Samye, in another guise?

`dsp:dzogchen-and-chan` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G6

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
Critics (notably Sakya Paṇḍita in his Clear Differentiation of the Three Vows) held that teachings claiming liberation by recognizing the mind's nature without accumulations or analysis — the 'white panacea' and a 'Chinese-style Great Perfection' — revive the view of the Chinese master defeated at Samye.
- The Samye verdict (as told in the Testament of Ba) ruled for Kamalaśīla's gradual path against Moheyan's non-thinking.
- Kamalaśīla's Bhāvanākrama argues that mere absence of thought cannot uproot ignorance without discriminating insight.
- A realization said to need no merit, analysis or effort cannot be the Buddha's path.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhāvanākrama](../texts/bhavanakrama.md) — ref: third Bhāvanākrama
  - [Bashe (sba bzhed / dba' bzhed, the Testament of Ba)](../texts/bashe.md) — ref: samye-debate
### [Nyingma (the Ancient / Old Translation school)](../lineages/nyingma.md)
The Nyingma deny the identification: Nupchen's Lamp for the Eye in Contemplation already ranks the simultaneous approach of Chan below Mahāyoga and Atiyoga; the Great Perfection is recognition of awareness, not the suppression of thought; its lineage is Indian (Garab Dorje, Mañjuśrīmitra, Śrī Siṃha, Vimalamitra).
- The Samten Migdrön distinguishes four approaches and places Atiyoga above the simultaneous approach.
- The Kunjed Gyalpo itself directs those without fortune to the vehicles of cause and result.
- Longchenpa and Jigme Lingpa require the preliminaries and the accumulations and reject a blank thoughtless state (the all-base) as a deviation.
- Vimalamitra's Madhyamaka manual on simultaneous entry is preserved in the Tengyur.
**Texts:** 
  - [Samten Migdrön (bsam gtan mig sgron, the Lamp for the Eye in Contemplation)](../texts/samten-migdron.md) — ref: sections on the four approaches
  - [Kunjed Gyalpo (chos thams cad rdzogs pa chen po byang chub kyi sems kun byed rgyal po, the All-Creating King)](../texts/kunjed-gyalpo.md) — ref: ch.44
  - [Cig car 'jug pa rnam par mi rtog pa'i bsgom don (The Meaning of Meditation on Non-conceptuality: the Simultaneous Entry), Tōh 3910](../texts/cigcar-jugpa-vimalamitra.md) — ref: colophon

**Historical debate — The Samye debate (as background)** (late 8th c.): Testament of Ba and later histories; recorded in full under dsp:sudden-or-gradual (U50). The Tibetan histories report a ruling for the gradual Indian path; Chinese sources report otherwise (see U50).

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The 'effortless' teaching and the gradual path are addressed to different capacities, as the Kunjed Gyalpo itself states (ch.44); the question of historical derivation is not reconciled.
Under P4 the Nyingma's non-meditation is not a rejection of accumulation for those who need it. Whether Dzogchen's view is the view condemned at Samye remains not yet reconciled.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Nyingma reject any identification with Moheyan's teaching; the critics would say a stage-reconciliation does not answer their objection to the view itself.
**Candidate readings:** Dzogchen and Chan are independent 'sudden' teachings with different views of non-conceptuality (Nyingma position).; Dzogchen absorbed Chan elements in the 8th-10th c. (critics' position).
