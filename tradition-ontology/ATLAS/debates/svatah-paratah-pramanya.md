# Is the validity of a cognition intrinsic (svataḥ) or extrinsic (parataḥ)?

`dsp:svatah-paratah-pramanya` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A5

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Validity is extrinsic: it is produced by an excellence in the causes of a cognition and is known not with the cognition itself but by inference — from the success of the activity it prompts or other marks; invalidity likewise comes from defects and is known extrinsically.
- If validity were grasped together with a cognition, doubt about a cognition's validity could not arise, yet it does (Gaṅgeśa).
- A means of knowledge is 'purposeful' because action based on it succeeds (NBh 1.1.1).
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvacintāmaṇi](../texts/tattvacintamani.md) — ref: Pratyakṣa-khaṇḍa, Prāmāṇyavāda
  - [Nyāyabhāṣya](../texts/nyaya-bhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1
### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
Every cognition is valid by itself: validity arises from the very conditions that produce the cognition and is apprehended with it; invalidity is extrinsic, known from a defect in the causes or a later sublating cognition. A power not present in a thing by itself cannot be produced by another.
- Extrinsic validation would need further validation without end.
- The authorless Veda, having no author whose defects could vitiate it, is valid by itself.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ślokavārttika](../texts/slokavarttika.md) — ref: Codanāsūtra, v. 47 (number from memory)
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Both validity and invalidity are intrinsic — as reported in later doxography.
- Reported, not argued here; e.g. in Mādhava's Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha (Jaimini chapter).
**Texts:** 
  - [Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha](../texts/sarvadarsanasangraha.md) — ref: Jaimini chapter
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
Invalidity is intrinsic and validity extrinsic — as reported in the same doxographic scheme (Buddhist sources themselves are more differentiated).
- Reported; to be checked against Śāntarakṣita's Tattvasaṅgraha.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha](../texts/sarvadarsanasangraha.md) — ref: Jaimini chapter
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
Validity is known through practical activity (vyavahāra) — whether the cognition leads to the object's efficacy (Dharmakīrti, PV Pramāṇasiddhi 5, conventional).
- non-deception
**Texts:** 
  - [Pramāṇavārttika](../texts/pramanavarttika.md) — ref: 1.7 (e-text)
### [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md)
There is no single rule: some cognitions (self-awareness, inference, habituated perception, cognitions of efficacy) are valid by themselves, others only extrinsically (Śāntarakṣita and Kamalaśīla, as recalled).
- Tattvasaṃgraha, Svataḥprāmāṇyaparīkṣā (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvasaṅgraha of Śāntarakṣita](../texts/tattvasangraha.md) — ref: Svataḥprāmāṇyaparīkṣā

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the schools disagree about how validity is established.
**The traditions' own objections:** Both traditions insist on the difference: Mīmāṃsā needs intrinsic validity for an authorless Veda; Nyāya grounds the Veda's validity in its reliable author and cites doubt as proof that validity is not self-evident.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the Mīmāṃsaka thesis may describe the default acceptance of a cognition when no doubt has arisen, the Naiyāyika thesis the justification of validity once doubt arises.; P2-standpoint: both distinguish the arising (utpatti) from the ascertainment (jñapti) of validity; agreement may be possible on the first and not the second.

_Notes: The four-way doxographic schema (Sāṃkhya: both intrinsic; Nyāya: both extrinsic; Buddhists: invalidity intrinsic; Mīmāṃsā: validity intrinsic) is recalled from later doxography._
