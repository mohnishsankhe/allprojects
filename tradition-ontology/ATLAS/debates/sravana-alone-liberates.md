# Does the hearing of the Upaniṣadic sentence itself produce immediate knowledge (so that hearing is the principal means), or does the mind perfected by contemplation produce it?

`dsp:sravana-alone-liberates` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A6

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vivaraṇa school (Vivaraṇa-prasthāna)](../lineages/vivarana.md)
Hearing is principal (aṅgin), enjoined by a restrictive injunction; the mahāvākya directly gives immediate knowledge, as 'you are the tenth' does.
- Verbal testimony gives immediate knowledge when its object is immediate (the tenth man).
- Manana and nididhyāsana remove obstacles only.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pañcapādikāvivaraṇa](../texts/pancapadika-vivarana.md) — ref: śravaṇa
  - [Pañcadaśī](../texts/pancadasi.md) — ref: 7
### [Bhāmatī school (Bhāmatī-prasthāna)](../lineages/bhamati.md)
Contemplation is principal; verbal knowledge is mediate; the mind, perfected by contemplation, is the instrument of direct realization ('to be seen by the mind alone').
- Words produce only relational, mediate knowledge.
- Scripture names the mind as the means of seeing (BĀU 4.4.19; KU 1.3.12).
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhāmatī](../texts/bhamati.md) — ref: manas

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level, P6-upaya
**Explanation:** The tradition's own compendium (Siddhāntaleśasaṅgraha) records both as methods leading to the same knowledge; both agree hearing, reflection and contemplation are all to be practised.
Grounded in Sureśvara's principle that any method leading to knowledge of the inner self is valid (BĀU-Vārttika 1.4.402) and Appayya's statement that the ancients, intent on the oneness of the self, were indifferent to how the empirical is explained (SLS opening).
**The traditions' own objections:** The two lines disagree on which means is principal and on whether words can yield immediacy; neither concedes that this is merely a matter of method.
