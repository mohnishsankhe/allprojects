# Is surrender (prapatti) a means (upāya) to liberation?

`dsp:is-prapatti-an-upaya` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A6; G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vaṭakalai (northern school of Śrīvaiṣṇavism)](../lineages/vadakalai.md)
Yes: prapatti is an independent means, performed once with its five limbs and the principal act of self-offering, equal in fruit to bhakti-yoga; the Lord is the accomplished means and prapatti the means to be performed, which occasions his grace.
- scripture enjoins surrender in the imperative ('take refuge')
- the six-limbed śaraṇāgati of the Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā
- without an act on the soul's side, why are not all saved?
**Texts:** 
  - [Rahasyatrayasāra](../texts/rahasyatrayasara.md) — ref: Upāyavibhāga; Parikaravibhāga; Caramaśloka
  - [Nikṣeparakṣā](../texts/niksepa-raksa.md) — ref: passim
### [Teṅkalai (southern school of Śrīvaiṣṇavism)](../lineages/tenkalai.md)
No: the Lord himself is the only means; surrender is the soul's acceptance of that fact, befitting its dependent nature; to treat it as a means is to rely on one's own act and is a fault.
- 'me alone' (mām ekam) in the carama-śloka
- the soul's nature is pāratantrya
- the Lord's grace is causeless
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrīvacanabhūṣaṇam](../texts/srivacana-bhusanam.md) — ref: 2
  - [Mumukṣuppaṭi](../texts/mumuksuppadi.md) — ref: 3
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
Rāmānuja reads Gītā 18.66 both as renunciation of agency within the yogas and as surrender in place of expiation for those unable to begin bhakti; the Gadyas present surrender as his own act of refuge.
- Gītābhāṣya 18.66
- Śaraṇāgati Gadya
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Rāmānuja](../texts/gita-bhasya-ramanuja.md) — ref: 18.66
  - [Śaraṇāgati Gadya](../texts/saranagati-gadya.md) — ref: 1

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Both hold the Lord to be the accomplished means and surrender to be done once; they differ in whether the soul's act is called a means — a difference of standpoint (seeker's act vs the Lord's agency).
Reconciled only as to shared ground; whether surrender itself is a means remains not yet reconciled.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Teṅkalai reject any sense in which the soul's act is a means; the Vaṭakalai reject reducing surrender to a mere recognition, which would make the scriptural injunction pointless.
