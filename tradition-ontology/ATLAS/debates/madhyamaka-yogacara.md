# Is consciousness ultimately real while external objects are not (mind-only), or is mind too empty?

`dsp:madhyamaka-yogacara` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
Only consciousness exists; the external objects it appears as do not. The dependent nature (the constructing mind) is real and empty of the imagined subject–object duality; the perfected nature is that emptiness. The Saṃdhinirmocana's third turning, which distinguishes what exists from what does not, is the definitive meaning, while 'all dharmas lack own-nature' needs interpretation.
- Dream and other examples show cognition without external objects.
- Something must exist for emptiness to be the emptiness of something.
- The three natures and three non-natures interpret the Perfection of Wisdom.
**Texts:** 
  - [Viṃśatikā (Viṃśatikāvijñaptimātratāsiddhi)](../texts/vimsatika.md) — 
  - [Triṃśikā (Triṃśikāvijñaptikārikā)](../texts/trimsika.md) — 
  - [Madhyāntavibhāga](../texts/madhyantavibhaga.md) — ref: 1.1-2
  - [Saṃdhinirmocana-sūtra](../texts/samdhinirmocana-sutra.md) — ref: ch.7
### [Prāsaṅgika-Madhyamaka](../lineages/prasangika.md)
Consciousness is no more real than its objects: both are conventionally accepted and ultimately empty. Reflexive awareness and the storehouse are not established; 'the three worlds are mind only' denies a creator other than mind and is of provisional meaning.
- Mind and object stand or fall together (MA 6.71ff).
- Mind does not see itself (BCA 9.17).
- Scriptures of mind-only are provisional (MA 6.94ff).
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyamakāvatāra](../texts/madhyamakavatara.md) — ref: 6.45-97
  - [Bodhicaryāvatāra](../texts/bodhicaryavatara.md) — ref: 9.15-29
### [Svātantrika-Madhyamaka](../lineages/svatantrika.md)
Bhāviveka rejects the Yogācāra's real dependent nature and accepts external objects conventionally.
- The dependent nature cannot be ultimately real if it arises from conditions.
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyamakahṛdayakārikā](../texts/madhyamakahrdaya.md) — ref: ch.5
### [Yogācāra-Madhyamaka](../lineages/yogacara-madhyamaka.md)
Mind-only is correct as the account of conventional reality; ultimately even mind lacks own-nature. Riding both chariots one is a true Mahāyānist.
- Relying on mind-only external objects are understood not to exist; relying on Madhyamaka mind is understood to be selfless (MAL 92–93).
**Texts:** 
  - [Madhyamakālaṃkāra](../texts/madhyamakalamkara.md) — ref: 92-93

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level, P5-neyartha
**Explanation:** Śāntarakṣita's synthesis reconciles the two under P1-level: mind-only holds at the conventional level, emptiness of mind at the ultimate.
Each side reads the other's key scripture as provisional (P5): the Yogācāra the Perfection of Wisdom's 'no own-nature', the Madhyamaka the Laṅkāvatāra/Daśabhūmika 'mind only'.
**The traditions' own objections:** Candrakīrti rejects mind-only even conventionally, so the Prāsaṅgika does not accept the synthesis; Yogācāra masters hold that the dependent nature is ultimately real and would reject its demotion to the conventional.
**Candidate readings:** P4-stage: mind-only as a step for students before the full teaching of emptiness (MAL 92).; P5-neyartha: the provisional/definitive division applied in opposite directions by each school.
