# Must jhāna precede insight, or can insight proceed on momentary concentration (dry insight)?

`dsp:dry-insight-or-jhana-first` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Mahāsi Sayadaw tradition (the 'new Burmese method')](../lineages/mahasi.md)
Continuous noting develops momentary concentration strong enough to suppress the hindrances; the pure-insight vehicle (sukkhavipassaka) reaches the paths without prior jhāna.
- The Visuddhimagga names the pure-insight vehicle and the dry-insight worker.
- The sub-commentary makes momentary unification the concentration of the insight practitioner.
**Texts:** 
  - [Visuddhimagga](../texts/visuddhimagga.md) — ref: XVIII; XXI
  - [Practical Insight Meditation: Basic and Progressive Stages](../texts/practical-insight-meditation.md) — ref: concentration
### [Pa-Auk tradition (Pa-Auk Tawya)](../lineages/pa-auk.md)
At least access concentration, preferably jhāna, is needed; its light lets the meditator see ultimate materiality and mentality, without which insight is into concepts.
- The Visuddhimagga's pure-insight vehicle begins with four-elements meditation to access.
- The Buddha praised concentration as the basis of knowing things as they are.
**Texts:** 
  - [Knowing and Seeing](../texts/knowing-and-seeing.md) — ref: intro
  - [Visuddhimagga](../texts/visuddhimagga.md) — ref: XI; XVIII
### [Thai Forest tradition (Kammaṭṭhāna forest tradition)](../lineages/thai-forest.md)
Calm and insight are developed together; the citta must converge in samādhi to have the strength for wisdom, but wisdom can also lead to samādhi.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path, P4-stage
**Explanation:** The Visuddhimagga itself recognizes two vehicles — calm-first and pure insight — suited to different practitioners.
The paths themselves are supramundane jhāna for both vehicles; disagreement remains on how strong pre-path concentration must be.
**The traditions' own objections:** Pa-Auk teachers hold that momentary concentration of noting practice does not suffice to see ultimate realities; Mahāsi teachers deny that jhāna is required.
