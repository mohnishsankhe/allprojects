# Can hymns in Tamil, composed by human poets — one of them born outside the brahmin class, one a woman — have the authority of the Veda?

`dsp:tamil-prabandham-as-veda` · `skeleton` · confidence low

**Coverage:** G11

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
Yes: the Tiruvāymoḻi is the essence of the four Vedas in Tamil, the 'Tamil Upaniṣad', equal in authority to the Sanskrit and revealing its heart; Nammāḻvār's four works are the four Vedas, Tirumaṅkai's six the aṅgas; the Prabandham is recited in temples as Veda.
- Ācārya Hṛdayam
- Yāmuna's reverence for Nammāḻvār as 'first head of our clan'
- the ubhaya-vedānta of the ācāryas
- same purport as the Upaniṣads

### [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md)
The Veda is authorless and eternal in its word–meaning relation; human compositions (smṛti) are authoritative only as inferring a Vedic source and are set aside where they conflict with it.
- autpattika relation of word and meaning (MS 1.1.5)
- smṛti authoritative only by inference of Veda (MS 1.3.2–3)
**Texts:** 
  - [Mīmāṃsā Sūtra](../texts/mimamsa-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.5; 1.3.2-3
