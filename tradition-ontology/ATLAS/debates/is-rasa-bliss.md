# Is every rasa blissful, even compassion and terror?

`dsp:is-rasa-bliss` · `skeleton` · confidence low

**Coverage:** A12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
Yes: in compassion and the other rasas supreme happiness arises; the experience of the sensitive is the proof — otherwise no one would turn to the Rāmāyaṇa (Viśvanātha, following Abhinavagupta).
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāhityadarpaṇa of Viśvanātha Kavirāja](../texts/sahityadarpana.md) — ref: 3.4-5
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
No: rasa is of the nature of both pleasure and pain; the compassionate, furious, odious and terrible are painful, though spectators delight in the poet's and actors' skill (Rāmacandra and Guṇacandra).
**Texts:** 
  - [Nāṭyadarpaṇa of Rāmacandra and Guṇacandra](../texts/natyadarpana.md) — ref: 3

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The Nāṭyadarpaṇa grants delight in the artistry while calling the content painful; the other side speaks of the relish as a whole — two standpoints on one experience (content vs relish).
**The traditions' own objections:** The Nāṭyadarpaṇa's authors insist the painful rasas are painful in themselves; Viśvanātha insists all rasa is bliss.
