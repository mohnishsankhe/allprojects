# If the chart shows fate, what room is left for human effort and for remedial rites?

`dsp:jyotisa-fate-and-effort` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md)
Varāhamihira: fate is karma made in another birth; it alone gives birth in a good family and the like, but after childhood success needs fate and effort together, like farming; neither favourable planets alone (else the astrologer would be king) nor rites alone (else the priest would be king) suffice. Yet timing matters: an act begun under ill-placed planets destroys its doer, one begun well succeeds with slight effort.
- the farming analogy: seed, soil, rain and labour
- a single defect ruins all, like a leaking skin bag
- the astrologer-king and priest-king reductio
**Texts:** 
  - [Yogayātrā](../texts/yogayatra.md) — ref: 1.3-6
  - [Bṛhat Saṃhitā](../texts/brhat-samhita.md) — ref: 103.59-60
### [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
Yājñavalkya: success rests on fate and human effort; fate is only the human effort of a former body made manifest; as a chariot does not move on one wheel, fate does not succeed without effort.
- fate is past effort
- the chariot with one wheel
**Texts:** 
  - [Yājñavalkyasmṛti](../texts/yajnavalkyasmrti.md) — ref: 1.349-351
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
The Yoga Vāsiṣṭha: effort is everything; fate has no form, act or power and is only a name for the fruit of one's own former effort, which present effort overcomes; reliance on fate is delusion.
- fate is never perceived
- the believer in fate should sit in a fire and see whether fate burns him
**Texts:** 
  - [Mokṣopāya](../texts/moksopaya.md) — ref: 2.4-2.9
  - [Yoga Vāsiṣṭha](../texts/yoga-vasistha.md) — ref: 2.4-9
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
The Nakkhatta Jātaka: the fool who waits on the lucky star misses his goal; the goal is the goal's own star — what can the stars do?
- delay for the right star loses the opportunity
**Texts:** 
  - [Jātaka](../texts/jataka.md) — ref: 49

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Varāhamihira, Yājñavalkya and the Yoga Vāsiṣṭha all define fate as one's own former action; they view one karmic process from the past (fate) and the present (effort), differing in the weight given to each.
Jyotiṣa's reading of the chart is the reading of past action ripening; effort and rites are present action. The Yoga Vāsiṣṭha's teaching is owned by U06 and recorded there as dsp:daiva-or-paurusa; this dispute adds the jyotiṣa and smṛti sides.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Yoga Vāsiṣṭha denies fate any power of its own and would reject an astrology that predicts fixed outcomes; the jyotiṣa authors insist that the fruit shown in the chart does come and that timing counts; the Jātaka denies the stars all efficacy. The Ājīvika doctrine of niyati (known only from Buddhist and Jain reports) would deny effort altogether and is not covered by this reconciliation.
