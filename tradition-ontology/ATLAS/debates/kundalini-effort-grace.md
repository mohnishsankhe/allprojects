# Is kuṇḍalinī — and the absorption it leads to — awakened by the yogin's effortful practice or by grace: the guru's, or the Lord's descent of power (śaktipāta)?

`dsp:kundalini-effort-grace` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G14

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
Kuṇḍalinī sleeps at the mouth of the door of Brahman; to awaken her the yogin should practise the mudrās 'with every effort'; success comes only to one who practises — not from reading or wearing the garb. Yet the same text says the sleeping kuṇḍalī awakens 'by the grace of the guru', and the Gheraṇḍa has samādhi come through the guru's grace and one's own devotion. (Methods are restricted; summary only.)

### [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md)
Kuṇḍalinī, coiled above the bulb and closing the door of Brahman, is to be awakened with the fire of yoga by mind and breath (Gorakṣaśataka); but the Amaraughaśāsana, also ascribed to Gorakṣa, says liberation marked by the raising of kuṇḍalinī from the root is not liberation — it lies in innate (sahaja) absorption.
**Texts:** 
  - [Gorakṣaśataka](../texts/goraksasataka.md) — ref: 30-31
  - [Amaraughaśāsana](../texts/amaraughasasana.md) — ref: 46-54
### [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
The Amanaska: breath-control, mudrās, bandhas, mantra and meditation on objects are laborious and do not bring the mindless state; that arises without effort through the guru's grace and a gaze resting on nothing.
**Texts:** 
  - [Amanaska (Amanaskayoga)](../texts/amanaska.md) — ref: ch. 2
### [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
Grace (śaktipāta) depends on the Lord's freedom alone — not on a balance of karma or the ripening of impurity — and is intense, middling or mild; for the highest grace a single hearing of the teacher's word suffices (anupāya); the śāmbhava, śākta and āṇava means correspond to lessening grace, the āṇava working through breath, bodily seals and places; grace may come by degrees or suddenly. 'The upsurge (udyama) is Bhairava'; 'the teacher is the means'. The signs of immersion are bliss, leaping up, trembling, sleep and whirling.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tantrāloka](../texts/tantraloka.md) — ref: 1.168-170; ch. 2; 5.101-111; ch. 13
  - [Śivasūtra](../texts/siva-sutra.md) — ref: 1.5; 2.6

### [Kaula (the Kula tradition)](../lineages/kaula.md)
In the Kaula path there are two: the made (kṛtaka) kuṇḍalī, impelled by seals and mantras, and the innate (sahaja), abiding in sameness; the guru's initiations by touch, sight and — rarest — by piercing (vedha) awaken the disciple; the yogin rouses kuṇḍalinī 'by the method learned from the guru'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Akulavīratantra](../texts/akulavira-tantra.md) — ref: B 43-45
  - [Kulārṇava Tantra](../texts/kularnava-tantra.md) — ref: 14.53-66

### [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md)
Grace descends when the soul's two deeds come into balance and its mala has ripened; the Lord, not the mala, is the cause, and he acts through the guru in initiation.
**Texts:** 
  - [Kiraṇatantra](../texts/kirana-tantra.md) — ref: 5.1-20
  - [Mataṅgavṛtti](../texts/matangavrtti.md) — ref: vidyāpāda

### [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md)
The mind's activities are stilled by practice and dispassion; practice is effort toward stability, firm when long, uninterrupted and devoted; for those of intense ardour samādhi is near — or through devotion to Īśvara, who favours the yogin.
**Texts:**

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P3-path
**Explanation:** The texts themselves pair effort and grace — the Haṭhapradīpikā has kuṇḍalinī wake 'by the guru's grace' and prescribes the mudrās 'with every effort' — and Abhinavagupta orders the means by the intensity of grace, from the means-less for the highest to bodily practice for the lesser (P4-stage; P3-path); whether effortful arousal is itself the way to liberation or is surpassed by the innate, effortless state remains not yet reconciled.
P4: Tantrāloka 1.168-170, ch. 2 and 13.300-301; Kulārṇava's graded initiations; the Akulavīra's made and innate kuṇḍalī. P3: the Yoga Sūtra's practice-and-dispassion and its alternative of devotion to Īśvara. Not reconciled: the Amaraughaśāsana's and the Amanaska's denial that kuṇḍalinī-raising is liberation against the haṭha texts' claim; the Trika's grace independent of karma against the Siddhānta's grace upon ripened mala (dsp:cause-of-saktipata).
**The traditions' own objections:** The Amaraughaśāsana: liberation marked by kuṇḍalinī's movement is not liberation; the Amanaska: haṭha methods do not bring the mindless state; the Trika: grace is not caused by effort or by karma; the Haṭhapradīpikā: without practice there is no success.
