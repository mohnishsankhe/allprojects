# Is the perfected, indestructible body the goal of yoga, or is it surpassed by the body dissolved in the liṅga (the void)?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md)
U21 contribution (the Nāth side in its own texts): the yogī's body, filled with the moon's nectar and made adamantine, does not decay or die; the great siddhas 'broke the staff of Time and roam the universe'; yet the same texts place this within samādhi, the samarasa of breath and mind, and non-duality in the supreme state.
- The body filled with nectar is not abandoned by the embodied one (GŚ 66).
- By vajra-yoga the body stands like a thunderbolt and knows no dissolution (KJN 15.10, 15.25).
- The siddhas conquered Time by haṭha (HYP 1.9).
- In samādhi the yogī attains non-duality 'like milk in milk' (GŚ 100).

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** not yet reconciled; the Nāth texts join body-perfection to samādhi, while the Vīraśaiva account sets them against each other
**The traditions' own objections:** The Nāth tradition does not accept the Vīraśaiva portrayal of Gorakṣa as defeated; the Vīraśaivas reject any reading that makes Allama's void one stage among others.
**Candidate readings:** P4-stage: the perfected body as a stage and the dissolution of body-consciousness (samarasa / liṅgaikya) as the goal — the Nāth texts themselves place samādhi above the powers (GŚ 94–100; Akulavīra B.84), so the Nāth side might accept this reading of its own ladder, though not the Vīraśaiva narrative of defeat.; P2-standpoint: kāya-siddhi spoken from the standpoint of the body (mode), dissolution in the void from the standpoint of the ultimate (substance).; P4-stage: the perfected body as a stage below dissolution in the liṅga (the Vīraśaiva reading, which the Nāth tradition would reject).; The two traditions define the goal differently (kāya-siddhi vs liṅgaikya).
