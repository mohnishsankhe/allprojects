# Was being (sat) or non-being (asat) first, at the origin of the world?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Being alone was in the beginning, one without a second; being could not come from non-being (Uddālaka).
- 'How could being be born from non-being?' (ChU 6.2.2)
- being thought 'may I be many' and emitted heat, water and food (ChU 6.2.3)

### [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)
Non-being was in the beginning; from it being was born; it made itself (TU 2.7); non-being became being and an egg (ChU 3.19); one who knows brahman as non-being becomes non-being (TU 2.6).
- 'asad vā idam agra āsīt tato vai sad ajāyata' (TU 2.7.1)
- 'asad evedam agra āsīt tat sad āsīt' (ChU 3.19.1)
**Texts:**

**Historical debate — Uddālaka's rejection of the non-being view** (undated (Upaniṣadic period)): ChU 6.2.1-2 cites the view of 'some' and rejects it ChU affirms being; the TU and ChU 3.19 passages stand in the canon

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P8-six-marks
**Explanation:** The Brahma Sūtra (2.1.17) reads 'non-being' in these passages as the unmanifest state of name and form, as shown by the passages' own continuation ('it made itself', TU 2.7), so that the purport of both is being.
Candidate readings: (1) asat = the unmanifest (avyākṛta) before name and form (Vedānta, BS 2.1.17); (2) two older cosmogonic traditions preserved side by side; (3) asat as what is beyond the category of being (cf. RV 10.129.1 'neither non-being nor being'). The texts themselves state both; this entry does not decide between the readings.
**The traditions' own objections:** None recorded within the Upaniṣads; the reconciliation is the Vedānta schools', not the texts' own.
