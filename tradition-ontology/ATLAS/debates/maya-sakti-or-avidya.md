# Is māyā the Goddess's real power (śakti), or an indeterminable ignorance that knowledge sublates?

`dsp:maya-sakti-or-avidya` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Śākta traditions](../lineages/sakta.md)
Māyā is the Goddess's own power: she is Mahāmāyā, queen of the supreme Brahman, who transforms herself into mind and the elements, and is 'of the form of knowledge and ignorance'; even ignorance is knowledge, the world is the Lord (Kaula Upaniṣad).
**Texts:** 
  - [Saundaryalaharī](../texts/saundarya-lahari.md) — ref: 35, 98
  - [Lalitā Sahasranāma](../texts/lalita-sahasranama.md) — ref: 86-87
  - [Kaula Upaniṣad](../texts/kaula-upanisad.md) — ref: paradoxes
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Māyā is the Lord's power but is beginningless ignorance, neither real nor unreal, removed by knowledge of Brahman; duality is mere māyā.
**Texts:** 
  - [Vivekacūḍāmaṇi](../texts/vivekacudamani.md) — ref: 108
  - [Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (Gauḍapāda Kārikā, Āgamaśāstra)](../texts/mandukya-karika.md) — ref: 1.17
### [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md)
Within Śrīvidyā the Sahasranāma also calls the Goddess 'the substratum of the illusory world', a name read in Advaita terms by Advaita-inclined commentators.
**Texts:** 
  - [Lalitā Sahasranāma](../texts/lalita-sahasranama.md) — ref: 141-142

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level
**Explanation:** At the conventional level the world is the Goddess's real play and power; at the ultimate level only she (Brahman) is — the Sahasranāma itself holds both names.
Related to the registry dispute dsp:world-real-or-appearance.
**The traditions' own objections:** Realist Śākta and Kaula readings (the Kāmakalāvilāsa's real manifestation, the Kaula Upaniṣad's 'the world is the Lord') reject calling the world unreal; Advaita rejects treating māyā as ultimately real power.
