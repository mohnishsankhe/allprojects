# Was Kabīr a disciple of Rāmānanda?

`dsp:kabir-guru-ramananda` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Rāmānandī sampradāya](../lineages/ramanandi.md)
Yes: Kabīr was one of Rāmānanda's twelve disciples; lying on the steps of the Pañcagaṅgā ghāṭ he received the Name 'Rām' when Rāmānanda stepped on him; his Rām is Rāmānanda's.
- the Rāmānandī hagiographies (Anantadās, Nābhādās) say so
- the popular line 'kāśī meṃ ham pragaṭ bhae, rāmānand ceṭāe' (late, of doubtful authenticity)
**Texts:** 
  - [Kabīr Paracaī](../texts/kabir-paracai.md) — 
  - [Bhaktamāl](../texts/bhaktamal.md) — 
### [Kabīr Panth](../lineages/kabir-panth.md)
No human guru was needed: Kabīr is the Sat Puruṣ incarnate who came in every age; he accepted Rāmānanda outwardly only to establish the custom of taking a guru (some panth accounts even make Rāmānanda his disciple).
- Kabīr appeared on a lotus and was not born of a womb
- the Satguru of Kabīr's songs is the Lord, not a man
**Texts:** 
  - [Anurāg Sāgar](../texts/anurag-sagar.md) — 
  - [Kabīr Sāgar](../texts/kabir-sagar.md) — 

**Historical debate — Hagiographical claims** (late 16th c. onward): Anantadās's Kabīr Paracaī and Nābhādās's Bhaktamāl vs the Kabīr Panth literature each tradition maintains its account
