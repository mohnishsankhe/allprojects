# Is the Mahāyāna the word of the Buddha?

`dsp:mahayana-as-buddhavacana` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
(As reported by Mahāyāna authors:) the Mahāyāna is not the Buddha's word; it is not found in the recited collections, and it teaches non-arising where the Buddha taught extinction.
- Not included in the received canon.
- Its doctrines differ from the agreed teaching.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ratnāvalī](../texts/ratnavali.md) — ref: 4.67-98 (the objection as reported)
  - [Bodhicaryāvatāra](../texts/bodhicaryavatara.md) — ref: 9.42 (objection)
### [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md)
The Mahāyāna is the Buddha's word: the non-arising it teaches and the extinction taught to śrāvakas are one in meaning; the bodhisattva's aspirations and conduct are not taught in the śrāvaka scriptures; whatever reason establishes your scripture establishes the Mahāyāna; the Buddha taught according to each hearer's capacity.
- Non-arising and extinction are the same in meaning (RĀ 4.86).
- Parity of reasons for accepting scripture (BCA 9.43).
- Graded teaching (RĀ 4.94–96).
**Texts:** 
  - [Ratnāvalī](../texts/ratnavali.md) — ref: 4.67-98
  - [Bodhicaryāvatāra](../texts/bodhicaryavatara.md) — ref: 9.41-49
  - [Madhyamakahṛdayakārikā](../texts/madhyamakahrdaya.md) — ref: ch.4

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Mahāyāna's own principles (graded teaching, provisional meaning) include the śrāvaka teachings, but the śrāvaka traditions do not accept the Mahāyāna texts as canon.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Theravāda and other early schools do not accept the Mahāyāna sūtras as Buddha-word, so they reject the inclusion.
**Candidate readings:** P6-upaya / P4-stage: the Buddha taught different vehicles to hearers of different capacity (RĀ 4.94–96; the Mahāyāna reading).; P5-neyartha: the Mahāyāna treats the śrāvaka teaching of extinction as agreeing in meaning with non-arising (RĀ 4.86).
