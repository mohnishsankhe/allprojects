# Which worshippers know yoga best — the devotees who, ever yoked, worship the Lord, or those who worship 'the imperishable, the unmanifest' (BhG 12.1)? And what is 'the imperishable, the unmanifest' that the second group worships?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
'The imperishable, the unmanifest' is brahman free of all attributes and adjuncts, beyond the senses and speech; its worshippers are renunciants established in the knowledge of oneness. They 'reach me alone' because the knower is the Lord's very self (7.18), so the question whether they are 'the most yoked' does not arise for them. Those called 'most yoked' (12.2) are the devotees who worship the Lord in the form just shown; the path of the unmanifest is harder only for those who identify with the body (12.5); the graded means of 12.8–11 are for those not fit for it, 12.12's exaltation of relinquishing the fruit of actions is praise (stuti) meant to encourage them, and the marks of 12.13–19 describe the worshippers of the imperishable.
- 12.4 'te prāpnuvanti mām eva' read with 7.18 'jñānī tv ātmaiva me matam': the knowers are the Lord himself (recalled)
- 12.5 'dehavadbhiḥ': the hardship lies in identification with the body, not in a lesser goal (recalled)
- 12.13 'adveṣṭā sarvabhūtānām …': the marks that follow are those of the renunciant knowers of the imperishable (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Śaṅkara](../texts/gita-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: on 12.1–5, 12.12–13
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
'The imperishable, the unmanifest' is the individual self (pratyag-ātman) in its own nature, not perceptible to the senses. Those who meditate on it reach a self of the same form as the Lord, free from saṃsāra, slowly and with hardship; those who meditate on the Lord himself, with his auspicious qualities, are superior — 'the most yoked' — reach him swiftly and easily, and are themselves lifted out of saṃsāra by him (12.2, 12.6–7).
- the Lord's own verdict 'yuktatamāḥ' (12.2)
- the contrast between the greater hardship of 12.5 and the deliverance 'before long' of 12.7
- 12.4 'mām eva': what they reach is a self like the Lord's (recalled)
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Rāmānuja](../texts/gita-bhasya-ramanuja.md) — ref: on 12.1–7
