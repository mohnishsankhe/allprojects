# Is the knowledge that liberates a knowledge arising from the scriptural sentence, or a sustained meditation (upāsana) that is bhakti?

`dsp:is-liberating-knowledge-meditation` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A6; G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
It is meditation: the Upaniṣads use 'know', 'meditate' and 'worship' interchangeably; the knowledge enjoined is repeated, loving remembrance vivid as sight, i.e. bhakti, which pleases the Lord who grants release.
- Chāndogya 7.26.2 ('steady remembrance')
- BS 4.1.1 (repetition)
- Kaṭha 1.2.23 (whom he chooses)
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1; 4.1.1
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
It is the direct knowledge of identity produced by the great sayings; meditation (nididhyāsana) only removes obstacles to it; knowledge is not an act to be enjoined.
- knowledge depends on its object, not on will
- 'tat tvam asi'
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: 1.1.4
