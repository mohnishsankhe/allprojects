# Is liberation only the cessation of suffering, or also the attainment of lordship?

`dsp:duhkhanta-cessation-or-lordship` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md)
It is twofold: cessation and lordship; elsewhere liberation is only cessation or isolation, but even those liberated by Sāṃkhya-Yoga remain paśus.
- SDS ch. 6; PABh on PS 1.1 and 5.46.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha](../texts/sarvadarsanasangraha.md) — ref: ch. 6
  - [Pañcārthabhāṣya](../texts/pancarthabhasya.md) — ref: on PS 1.1; 5.46
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Liberation is isolation (kaivalya) of the person from matter (position as characterised by the Pāśupatas; the Sāṃkhya unit holds the texts).
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Liberation (apavarga) is absolute release from pain; Yāmuna even reports the Pāśupatas' end of suffering in these terms, as the destruction of the soul's special qualities.
- Yāmuna's report.
**Texts:** 
  - [Āgamaprāmāṇya](../texts/agamapramanya.md) — ref: Māheśvara section

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the Pāśupatas insist their goal exceeds cessation and isolation.
**The traditions' own objections:** Kauṇḍinya calls even the Sāṃkhya-Yoga liberated paśus.
**Candidate readings:** P2-standpoint: the impersonal aspect (cessation) is common ground; the personal aspect (lordship) is the Pāśupata's distinctive claim.; No reconciliation: the Pāśupatas deny that the Sāṃkhya-liberated are free.
