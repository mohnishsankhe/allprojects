# How does the one Brahman appear as many jīvas (and as Īśvara): by limitation, reflection or semblance?

`dsp:how-the-one-appears-as-many` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A6

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Bhāmatī school (Bhāmatī-prasthāna)](../lineages/bhamati.md)
Limitation (avaccheda): the jīva is Brahman delimited by the inner organ, like pot-space.
- No reflection of the formless is possible (BS 3.2.19 objection).
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhāmatī](../texts/bhamati.md) — ref: avaccheda
  - [Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (Gauḍapāda Kārikā, Āgamaśāstra)](../texts/mandukya-karika.md) — ref: 3.3
### [Vivaraṇa school (Vivaraṇa-prasthāna)](../lineages/vivarana.md)
Reflection (pratibimba): the jīva is Brahman's reflection, real and identical with the prototype; Īśvara the prototype.
- BS 2.3.50 and 3.2.18 speak of reflection; the reflection's identity with the original secures the mahāvākya.
**Texts:** 
  - [Pañcapādikāvivaraṇa](../texts/pancapadika-vivarana.md) — ref: reflection
  - [Brahma Sūtra](../texts/brahma-sutra.md) — ref: 3.2.18
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Semblance (ābhāsa, Sureśvara): the jīva is a mere semblance, neither real nor the original; once known as semblance only consciousness remains.
- A reflection is not real; what is real is only the original consciousness.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bṛhadāraṇyakopaniṣadbhāṣyavārttika](../texts/brhadaranyaka-bhasya-varttika.md) — ref: ābhāsa

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P6-upaya, P1-level
**Explanation:** The tradition explicitly treats the three as alternative methods (prakriyā) for teaching the one self, all at the empirical level (Sureśvara's prakriyā verse; SLS opening).
Grounded in Sureśvara's principle that any method leading to knowledge of the inner self is valid (BĀU-Vārttika 1.4.402) and Appayya's statement that the ancients, intent on the oneness of the self, were indifferent to how the empirical is explained (SLS opening). Madhusūdana's Siddhāntabindu likewise surveys them as alternatives.
**The traditions' own objections:** Each sub-school defends its own theory as the more coherent; the reconciliation is the tradition's own, but individual authors still argue for one.
