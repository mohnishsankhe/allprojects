# Is the Lord's saving grace without any cause in the soul, or does it take the soul's surrender as its occasion?

`dsp:nirhetuka-or-sahetuka-krpa` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A6; G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Teṅkalai (southern school of Śrīvaiṣṇavism)](../lineages/tenkalai.md)
Grace is causeless (nirhetuka); nothing the soul does earns or occasions it ('mārjāra-nyāya').
- pāratantrya
- the Lord's svātantrya
**Texts:** 
  - [Śrīvacanabhūṣaṇam](../texts/srivacana-bhusanam.md) — ref: 2
### [Vaṭakalai (northern school of Śrīvaiṣṇavism)](../lineages/vadakalai.md)
Grace is free but takes surrender as its occasion (vyāja), lest the Lord be partial or cruel in not saving all ('markaṭa-nyāya').
- the problem of partiality
- scripture's injunction to surrender
**Texts:** 
  - [Rahasyatrayasāra](../texts/rahasyatrayasara.md) — ref: Upāyavibhāga

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Both deny that surrender earns grace; the Vaṭakalai 'occasion' is not a cause, so the difference concerns the seeker-side description of one divine act.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Teṅkalai reject even 'occasion' on the soul's side; the Vaṭakalai hold that without it the Lord's justice is compromised.
