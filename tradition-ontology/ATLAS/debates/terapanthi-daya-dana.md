# Are saving life and giving to the unrestrained part of spiritual dharma?

`dsp:terapanthi-daya-dana` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Śvetāmbara Terāpanth](../lineages/terapanthi.md)
No: dharma is only what stops karmic inflow and fosters self-restraint; saving a being's bodily life by force or feeding those without vows is worldly (laukika) duty, not spiritual (lokottara) dharma, and may even support further harm.
- The Jina's dharma lies in renunciation, not in preserving worldly life
- Spiritual compassion leads a soul to restraint
**Texts:** 
  - [Bhikṣu Granth Ratnākar (collected works of Ācārya Bhikṣu)](../texts/bhiksu-granth-ratnakar.md) — ref: anukampa
### [Sthānakavāsī](../lineages/sthanakavasi.md)
Yes: compassion (anukampā, dayā) that protects life and giving to the needy are religious merit and belong to dharma; the Jina's first teaching is non-harm and care for beings.
- Compassion is a mark of right faith
- Megha's compassion for the hare was praised by Mahāvīra
**Texts:** 
  - [Jñātādharmakathā](../texts/jnatadharmakatha.md) — ref: 1.1
  - [Daśavaikālika Sūtra](../texts/dasavaikalika-sutra.md) — ref: 4.10
### [Śvetāmbara Mūrtipūjaka](../lineages/murtipujaka.md)
Yes, with the Sthānakavāsīs: compassion and giving are meritorious.
- As above
**Texts:** 
  - [Jñātādharmakathā](../texts/jnatadharmakatha.md) — ref: 1.1

**Historical debate — Bhikṣu's separation from Raghunāth** (1760): Terāpanthī accounts; Sthānakavāsī counter-accounts. Foundation of the Terāpanth.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level
**Explanation:** Bhikṣu's own distinction between worldly and supramundane dharma is a distinction of level: his opponents affirm compassion as merit (puṇya) and good conduct, which he does not forbid as social duty; he denies only that it is the spiritual dharma that stops karma.
Both sides accept that merit (puṇya) differs from the shedding of karma (nirjarā) and that non-harm is the highest dharma.
**The traditions' own objections:** Opponents deny that life-saving compassion is merely worldly; Terāpanthīs deny that it is spiritual dharma. The reconciliation claims only that they speak of different levels, not that either side accepts the other's classification.
