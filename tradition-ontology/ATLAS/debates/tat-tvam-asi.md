# What does 'tat tvam asi' (Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7) teach — identity of soul and Brahman, or something else?

`dsp:tat-tvam-asi` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
It teaches identity: 'that' (Brahman) and 'you' (the soul) are one consciousness; the words, stripped of their incompatible parts (by implication that drops part and keeps part), point to pure consciousness, as 'this is that Devadatta' identifies one person.
- the ninefold repetition marks it as the Upaniṣad's purport
- identity is the fruit that ends sorrow
**Texts:** 
  - [Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Śaṅkara (Śārīrakamīmāṃsābhāṣya)](../texts/brahma-sutra-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: passim
  - [Vedāntasāra](../texts/vedantasara.md) — ref: on the mahāvākya
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
It teaches co-ordinate predication: both words denote Brahman — 'that' as the cause of the world, 'you' as having the individual soul as its body; the soul is a mode of Brahman, one with it as body with embodied self, yet distinct.
- sāmānādhikaraṇya of two words with different grounds referring to one thing
- the soul is Brahman's body (śarīra-śarīri-bhāva)
**Texts:** 
  - [Vedārthasaṅgraha](../texts/vedarthasangraha.md) — ref: passim
  - [Śrībhāṣya](../texts/sribhasya.md) — ref: 1.1.1
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
It teaches difference: read 'sa ātmā atat tvam asi', 'you are not that', repeated nine times with examples that show difference (the bird tied to its post, honey from many trees, rivers in the sea); or 'aitadātmya' as being under the Lord's control and 'tat tvam asi' as dependence or likeness. Identity contradicts experience ('no one experiences I am omniscient, lord of all') and the conceit of it is the asuras' error.
- experience contradicts identity
- no scripture teaches non-difference; all teach difference
- the examples illustrate difference
- the Nyāyāmṛta refutes the 'impartite meaning' of the sentence
**Texts:** 
  - [Viṣṇutattvavinirṇaya](../texts/visnutattvavinirnaya.md) — ref: 1
  - [Chāndogyopaniṣad-bhāṣya (Madhva)](../texts/chandogya-upanisad-bhasya-madhva.md) — ref: 6.8.7
  - [Nyāyāmṛta](../texts/nyayamrta.md) — ref: 2

**Historical debate — Akṣobhya Tīrtha and Vidyāraṇya at Mulbagal** (14th c. CE (tradition)): The Mādhva tradition records a debate on 'tat tvam asi' between Akṣobhya Tīrtha and the Advaitin Vidyāraṇya, with Vedānta Deśika as arbiter, and a verse it ascribes to Deśika: that with the sword of 'tat tvam asi', which separates the Supreme from the soul, Akṣobhya cut down the great forest of Vidyāraṇya. Mādhva tradition: Akṣobhya prevailed. The Advaita tradition does not accept this account, and the ascription of the verse to Vedānta Deśika is questioned outside the Mādhva tradition.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: all three sides read the same passage with the same exegetical rules and reach opposite purports.

**The traditions' own objections:** Dvaita rejects any reading that makes difference a partial standpoint: the chapter teaches difference and only difference (VTN 1). Advaita holds identity is the sole purport; Viśiṣṭādvaita rejects both unqualified identity and Dvaita's separation of the soul from Brahman's body.
