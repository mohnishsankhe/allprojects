# Is the renunciation of action or the yoga of action better? (BhG 3.1–8, 5.1–6, 6.1–4, 18.1–12)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
If understanding (buddhi) is held superior to action (cf. 2.49), there is no reason to engage in terrible action; Kṛṣṇa's words seem mixed, and Arjuna asks for the one thing by which he may reach the good.
- Kṛṣṇa himself ranked action far below the yoga of understanding (2.49), yet urges Arjuna to fight (3.1).
- The teaching seems mixed and confuses the understanding (3.2).
- The view that freedom from action (naiṣkarmya) is reached by not undertaking actions, and perfection by renunciation alone, is mentioned in order to be denied (3.4).

### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
There is a twofold commitment, jñāna-yoga for the sāṃkhyas and karma-yoga for the yogins; freedom from action is not reached by not acting, nor perfection by renunciation alone; one must act, for sacrifice and for holding the world together, without attachment.
- A twofold commitment (niṣṭhā) was declared by Kṛṣṇa of old (3.3).
- No one attains naiṣkarmya by not undertaking actions, nor perfection by mere renunciation (3.4).
- No one can remain even a moment without acting; the guṇas born of prakṛti make all act (3.5).
- Outward restraint while the mind dwells on objects is hypocrisy (3.6); action with the organs of action, the senses controlled and without attachment, excels (3.7); action is better than inaction and even bodily life needs it (3.8).
- Action for sacrifice done unattached does not bind (3.9).
- For one who delights in the self there is nothing to be done (3.17–18); yet act unattached, for by unattached action one attains the highest (3.19).
- Janaka and others reached perfection by action; act for lokasaṃgraha, as Kṛṣṇa himself does, without unsettling the understanding of the ignorant (3.20–26).

### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Renunciation: action should be abandoned as an evil (the view the Gītā reports at 18.3); the path of knowledge of the Sāṃkhyas; mere abstention appeals to Arjuna, who asks why he is urged to terrible action if understanding is superior (3.1).
- Arjuna's question (3.1–2; 5.1).
- 'Some wise men say action should be abandoned as an evil' (18.3).

### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
Action: no one can remain without acting (3.5); mere renunciation does not bring perfection (3.4); the yoga of action excels renunciation of action (5.2); acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity are not to be abandoned (18.5).
- The embodied cannot abandon action entirely, so the one who relinquishes the fruit is the relinquisher (18.11).
- Janaka attained perfection by action (3.20).

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage, P3-path
**Explanation:** The Gītā reconciles the two itself: action is the means for one ascending and quiescence for one ascended (6.3), true renunciation is acting without depending on the fruit (6.1), saṃnyāsa and tyāga are redefined (18.2), and Sāṃkhya and Yoga are one in result (5.4–5).
Śaṅkara applies the stage reading: the preference for karma-yoga (5.2) holds for those without self-knowledge, renunciation for the knower. Tilak (recent) holds that even the knower should act.
**The traditions' own objections:** The renunciant reading (Advaita) holds formal renunciation to be the knower's proper state; the activist reading denies this; neither accepts that the text leaves the knower's case open.
