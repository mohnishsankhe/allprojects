# The rivalry of the priestly houses of Viśvāmitra and Vasiṣṭha

`dsp:visvamitra-vasistha-rivalry` · `skeleton` · confidence low


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Viśvāmitra's house: Viśvāmitra is priest of the Bharatas and of Sudās (RV 3.33, 3.53); the closing imprecations of 3.53 are traditionally taken as aimed at Vasiṣṭha, and Vasiṣṭha's descendants do not recite them.
- The Bharatas' crossing of the rivers by Viśvāmitra's word; the imprecation verses.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 3.33; 3.53.21–24
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Vasiṣṭha's house: Vasiṣṭha is Sudās's priest in the Battle of the Ten Kings (7.18, 7.33) and swears 'may I die today if I am a sorcerer', against one who falsely called him so (7.104.15).
- Victory of Sudās through the Vasiṣṭhas' prayers.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 7.18; 7.33; 7.104.15

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)

**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: a rivalry of lineages, remembered differently by each house and by the epics.
**Candidate readings:** The epic account (Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla-kāṇḍa) resolves it by Viśvāmitra's tapas and Vasiṣṭha's final acknowledgement of him as brahmarṣi — a P4-stage reading (change of standing through attainment).; The two houses served one king at different times (a historical, non-doctrinal reading).; The attributions of the imprecations and of the sorcery charge are commentarial and may not concern Vasiṣṭha at all.
