# Is suggestion (dhvani, vyañjanā) a distinct power of words, and is it the soul of poetry?

`dsp:dhvani-vyanjana` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** A12

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
Yes: beyond denotation and indication there is an implied meaning — fact, figure or rasa — conveyed by suggestion, which is the soul of poetry (Ānandavardhana, Abhinavagupta, Mammaṭa).
**Texts:** 
  - [Dhvanyāloka of Ānandavardhana](../texts/dhvanyaloka.md) — ref: 1.1; 1.4; 1.13
  - [Kāvyaprakāśa of Mammaṭa](../texts/kavyaprakasa.md) — ref: 2-5
### [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md)
No: what is called suggestion is inference from the expressed meaning; words have one power, denotation (Mahimabhaṭṭa); or rasa is conveyed by the purport of the sentence (Dhanika); or it is included in striking expression (Kuntaka).
**Texts:** 
  - [Vyaktiviveka of Mahimabhaṭṭa](../texts/vyaktiviveka.md) — ref: 1
  - [Avaloka of Dhanika on the Daśarūpaka](../texts/dasarupavaloka.md) — ref: 4
  - [Vakroktijīvita of Kuntaka](../texts/vakroktijivita.md) — ref: 1
### [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md)
Nāgeśa accepts suggestion (vyañjanā) as a third power of words beside denotation and indication.
**Texts:** 
  - [Paramalaghumañjūṣā of Nāgeśa](../texts/paramalaghumanjusa.md) — ref: (on the powers of words)

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Both sides grant that poems convey implied meanings and rasa; they differ on the cognitive operation (suggestion or inference/purport) — an analytic standpoint difference about one experience.
The reconciliation does not decide whether the operation is inference; Ānandavardhana argues that suggestion cannot be inference because the suggested sense is not invariably concomitant.
**The traditions' own objections:** Mahimabhaṭṭa insists that suggestion is nothing but inference; the dhvani school insists it is irreducible.
