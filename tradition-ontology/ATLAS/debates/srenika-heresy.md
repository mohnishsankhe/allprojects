# Is there a permanent mind-nature that leaves the perishing body at death?

`dsp:srenika-heresy` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G1

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Chan (Chinese Meditation school)](../lineages/chan.md)
(Reported view of certain 'southern' teachers) The knowing nature is permanent and has never arisen or perished; the body perishes like a snake's shed skin while the nature departs.
- Seeing, hearing and knowing pervade the body — that is the buddha.
**Texts:** 
  - [Record of the Transmission of the Lamp compiled in the Jingde era (Jingde chuandeng lu)](../texts/jingde-chuandeng-lu.md) — ref: juan 28 (reported by Huizhong)
### [Chan (Chinese Meditation school)](../lineages/chan.md)
Nanyang Huizhong: this is no different from the heretic Śreṇika, who holds that a spirit leaves the house of the body when it burns.
**Texts:** 
  - [Record of the Transmission of the Lamp compiled in the Jingde era (Jingde chuandeng lu)](../texts/jingde-chuandeng-lu.md) — ref: juan 28
### [Sōtō Zen](../lineages/soto.md)
Dōgen: body and mind are one, nature and form are not two; the Śreṇika view is not the buddha-dharma.
**Texts:** 
  - [Bendōwa (Talk on Wholehearted Practice)](../texts/bendowa.md) —
