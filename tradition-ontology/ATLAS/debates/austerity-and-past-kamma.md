# Is suffering ended by burning off past kamma through austerity, and which action is most blameworthy?

`dsp:austerity-and-past-kamma` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G9

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
All experience is due to past deeds; by austerity past kamma is worn away and by not doing new kamma nothing flows in; bodily action ('rod') is most blameworthy.
- As reported in MN 101 and MN 56 (Nigaṇṭhas).
- In Jain sources: austerity (tapas) both stops and sheds karma (Tattvārtha Sūtra 9.3).
**Texts:** 
  - [Devadaha Sutta](../texts/devadaha-sutta.md) — ref: MN 101 (2)
  - [Upāli Sutta](../texts/upali-sutta.md) — ref: MN 56 (3)
  - [Tattvārthasūtra](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md) — ref: 9.3
### [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
Not everything felt is due to past deeds; self-mortification is an ignoble extreme which did not lead the bodhisatta to awakening; kamma is intention, so mental action is most blameworthy; striving is fruitful by abandoning craving, not by torment.
- MN 101: the Nigaṇṭhas cannot know how much kamma remains.
- MN 56: mental action is most blameworthy.
- MN 36; MN 12: the bodhisatta's austerities.
- SN 56.11: the middle way.
**Texts:** 
  - [Devadaha Sutta](../texts/devadaha-sutta.md) — ref: MN 101
  - [Upāli Sutta](../texts/upali-sutta.md) — ref: MN 56
  - [Mahāsaccaka Sutta](../texts/mahasaccaka-sutta.md) — ref: MN 36
  - [Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta](../texts/dhammacakkappavattana-sutta.md) — ref: SN 56.11

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** not yet reconciled (queued)
**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled: the two traditions differ on the nature of karma (matter shed by tapas vs intention) and the value of austerity.
**The traditions' own objections:** Jain sources hold tapas indispensable for nirjarā; the suttas call self-mortification ignoble. The Jain side here is partly from Buddhist reports (MN 101, MN 56).
**Candidate readings:** P3-path: both value restraint (the suttas call restraint 'tapa', Snp 2.4) and differ in method; P2-standpoint: the Jain analysis of karma as substance versus the Buddhist analysis of karma as intention
