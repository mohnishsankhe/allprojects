# Are the three vows (individual liberation, bodhisattva, mantra) one in essence, or distinct systems to be kept separately?

`dsp:three-vows-one-essence` · `skeleton` · confidence low


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Drikung Kagyu](../lineages/drikung-kagyu.md)
The three vows are one in essence and differ only in aspect; they are kept together as one discipline, the higher including the lower (the 'single intent').
- All the Buddha's teachings share a single intent (Gongchig).
**Texts:** 
  - [The Single Intent (Gongchig)](../texts/gongchig.md) — 
### [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md)
The three vows differ in how they are taken, kept and restored and must not be confused; Sakya Paṇḍita's Discrimination of the Three Vows sets out their distinct rules and criticizes teachings that blur them.
- Confusing the systems leads to faulty practice (as reported).
**Texts:** 
  - [Discrimination of the Three Vows (sdom pa gsum gyi rab tu dbye ba)](../texts/domsum-rabye.md) — 

**Historical debate — The Gongchig and the Discrimination of the Three Vows** (13th c.): Sakya Paṇḍita's work is traditionally read as answering, among others, Drikung positions of the Gongchig (low confidence). each tradition maintains its view

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** From the standpoint of essence (the motivation and view that the vows serve) they are one; from the standpoint of mode (ritual, rules, restoration) they are distinct — the Gongchig itself grants distinct aspects.
The reconciliation claims only that the two statements answer different questions; it does not claim either school accepts the other's practice.
**The traditions' own objections:** Sakya Paṇḍita's objection is precisely to the blurring of rules; Drikung holds its formulation already preserves the distinctions.
