# Is release won by Śiva's grace and worship, or by the Jina's path of self-discipline? (the Tamil Śaiva–Jain contests)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [The Nāyaṉmārs and the Tirumuṟai (Tamil Śaiva bhakti)](../lineages/nayanmar.md)
Śiva alone saves by grace; the Jain monks' austerities, nakedness and denial of the Lord and the Veda are fruitless; the Lord's name, ash and temples protect the devotee.
- The tenth verse of Campantar's patikams denounces Jains and Buddhists.
- Appar's return from Jainism and survival of persecution by grace.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tēvāram](../texts/tevaram.md) — ref: 1-3.10
  - [Periya Purāṇam](../texts/periya-puranam.md) — ref: tirunanacampantar-puranam
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
Release is gained by right faith, knowledge and conduct, by one's own effort and austerity, without a creator's grace; non-violence is the highest dharma (position as held in Jain texts; in the Śaiva sources it is reported by opponents).
- Reported here chiefly through Śaiva sources (reported by opponent).
**Texts:** 
  - [Tēvāram](../texts/tevaram.md) — ref: 1-3.10

**Historical debate — The contest at Madurai** (7th c. (tradition)): Periya Purāṇam and Tiruviḷaiyāṭal Purāṇam: Campantar cured the king's fever with sacred ash; in the fire ordeal his palm-leaf hymn did not burn, in the water ordeal it floated upstream on the Vaikai; the Jains, having wagered their lives, were impaled (the tradition's account; no Jain account of the event is known, and its historicity is doubted by scholars). Śaiva sources: victory of Śaivism and conversion of the king.

**Historical debate — Appar's trials under the Pallava king** (7th c. (tradition)): Lime-kiln, poison, elephant and the sea; he survived by singing Śiva's names and the king (identified with Mahendravarman I) became a Śaiva. Śaiva sources: the king's conversion.

**Historical debate — Māṇikkavācakar's debate with the Buddhists of Laṅkā** (c. 9th c. (tradition places it earlier)): At Chidambaram he defeated Buddhist teachers and made the Laṅkan king's mute daughter speak (Tiruviḷaiyāṭal and later accounts). Śaiva sources: the Buddhists' defeat.
