# Were the hymns 'seen' by the seers or 'fashioned' by poets?

`dsp:seen-or-made-hymns` · `sourced` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Seen: the seers had direct vision of dharma and handed on the mantras (Nirukta 1.20); 'a ṛṣi from seeing — he saw the hymns' (2.11); speech was found entered into the seers (RV 10.71.3); the Veda is authorless (Sāyaṇa).
- The seer receives what he did not make; names of śākhās record transmitters, not authors.
**Texts:** 
  - [Nirukta](../texts/nirukta.md) — ref: 1.20; 2.11
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 10.71.3
  - [Mīmāṃsā Sūtra](../texts/mimamsa-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.27–32
### [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
Fashioned: the poets say they 'fashioned' (takṣ) their praise 'as a skilled craftsman a chariot' (RV 5.2.11), offer 'new' hymns, and speak of making them with mind and heart.
- The hymns' own language of craft and novelty.
**Texts:** 
  - [Ṛgveda Saṃhitā](../texts/rgveda.md) — ref: 5.2.11; 10.71.2

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** The hymns themselves hold both: speech is found entered into the seers (source, 10.71.3) and the wise fashion it with mind like grain sifted in a sieve (utterance, 10.71.2) — seen as to its origin, fashioned as to its form.
**The traditions' own objections:** Mīmāṃsā admits no authorship at any level (the Veda is eternal word); scholarly metadata (not used): modern scholarship treats the hymns as compositions of poet families.
