# Do the planets cause a person's fortune, or only reveal the karma that causes it?

`dsp:grahas-cause-or-sign` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md)
The planets are signs: the horā reveals (abhivyanakti) the ripening of karma earned in a former birth, as a lamp shows objects already in the dark; portents 'announce' (saṃsūcayanti) calamity that men's sins have prepared.
- the cause is one's own former karma, not the planet
- the lamp shows what is already there
- portents follow from men's misconduct
**Texts:** 
  - [Bṛhajjātaka](../texts/brhat-jataka.md) — ref: 1.3
  - [Laghujātaka](../texts/laghu-jataka.md) — ref: 1.3
  - [Bṛhat Saṃhitā](../texts/brhat-samhita.md) — ref: 45.1-3
### [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md)
The planets are divine agents: Janārdana in the form of the planets gives beings the fruits of their karma; the happiness and sorrow of all creatures depend on them, and the rise and fall of kings; therefore they are to be worshipped and pacified.
- the planets are avatāras and dispensers of fruit
- Brahmā's boon makes the worshipped planets favour the worshipper
- pacification works because the planets are persons
**Texts:** 
  - [Bṛhat Parāśara Horā Śāstra](../texts/brhat-parasara-hora-sastra.md) — ref: 2.3; 84.2-3
  - [Yājñavalkyasmṛti](../texts/yajnavalkyasmrti.md) — ref: 1.307-308

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Both accounts make one's own karma the ground of fruit; 'sign' is the causal-epistemic standpoint (what the astrologer reads), 'dispenser' the divine standpoint (who delivers it).
BPHS 2.3 itself joins the two: the planets are Janārdana giving the fruits of karma — the karma is the cause, the planet its divine agent, the chart its sign. The difference that remains is practical: on the 'sign' view remedies act on karma, on the 'agent' view they propitiate a person.
**The traditions' own objections:** Varāhamihira also warns that rites alone do not secure success (Yogayātrā 1.6), which limits the agent view; the Buddhist Nakkhatta Jātaka denies the stars any power ('what can the stars do?').

_Notes: Not a single recorded debate but two positions stated within the jyotiṣa corpus; recorded because later practice divides on it._
