# Is Chöd — a practice first taught by a Tibetan woman and concerned with spirits and the gift of the body — authentic Buddhadharma with an Indian source?

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Chöd (gcod), 'Cutting'](../lineages/chod.md)
Chöd is the practical application of the Perfection of Wisdom: its view is that of the sūtras, its instructions come in part from the Indian siddha Padampa Sangye and from Āryadeva the Brahmin's Grand Poem, and Machig is the emanation of the Great Mother. Indian scholars who came to test her accepted it, and it is the one Dharma that went from Tibet to India.
- The root texts are titled instructions of the Perfection of Wisdom and apply its view of emptiness.
- The father lineage runs from Padampa Sangye.
- The tradition's account of the Indian scholars' test and acceptance.

### None
A practice composed in Tibet, lacking an Indian tantra or sūtra as its source and centred on dealings with spirits, cannot be accepted as the Buddha's teaching merely on the authority of a Tibetan founder.
- The general Tibetan criterion that doctrines must be traced to an Indian source.
- Suspicion of practices associated with spirit-dealing and wandering yogins.

**Historical debate — The Indian scholars' test of Machig (tradition's account)** (late 11th – early 12th c.): Recorded in the Chöd tradition's own biographies of Machig. The Chöd tradition: the Indians accepted her teaching and took it to India.
