# Are the cleansing acts necessary before breath-control, and how many are there?

`dsp:necessity-of-satkarma` · `sourced` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
The HYP says the six acts are for one with excess fat or phlegm, and that another with balanced humours should not do them (2.21); the acts are to be kept secret (2.23).
**Texts:** 
  - [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) — ref: 2.21-2.23
### [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
Some ācāryas hold that all impurities dry up by breath-control alone, and do not approve any other act (2.37).
**Texts:** 
  - [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) — ref: 2.37
### [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
(Haṭhapradīpikā) Only those with excess fat or phlegm need the six acts; others should not do them.
- HYP 2.21, 2.36
**Texts:**

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** reconciled
**Principles:** P4-stage
**Explanation:** The HYP itself makes the acts conditional on the student's constitution (adhikāra by doṣa), which accommodates both the 'all' and 'none' positions.
The number of acts (six or eight) is a matter of enumeration of variants, not doctrine.
**The traditions' own objections:** The 'some teachers' of HYP 2.37 reject the acts outright; the reconciliation records that they would not accept them even conditionally.

_Notes: Both positions are stated in the HYP itself; the text gives no ruling between them._
