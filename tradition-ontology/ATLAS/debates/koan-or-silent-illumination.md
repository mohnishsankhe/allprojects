# Kōan (critical-phrase) introspection or silent illumination?

`dsp:koan-or-silent-illumination` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B4

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Yangqi branch (of the Linji house)](../lineages/yangqi.md)
Awakening is the standard; one must raise great doubt on the critical phrase ('wu') throughout daily life until it shatters; 'silent-illumination Chan' that teaches people to sit like cold ash and dead wood, and teachers who deny awakening, cut off the life of buddha-wisdom.
- Quiet alone cannot kill 'the monkey' of discriminating mind.
- Taking stillness as ultimate is to be captured by heretical silent illumination.
- Many teachers now deny that there is awakening.
**Texts:** 
  - [Recorded Sayings of Dahui (Dahui Pujue chanshi yulu)](../texts/dahui-yulu.md) — ref: 884c25; 891b05; 910c22; 921c05
### [Caodong house](../lineages/caodong.md)
In silence the words are forgotten and illumination is clear; silence and illumination are one, knowing without touching things; the original field is already present and is revealed by letting go, not produced by striving.
- If illumination loses silence there is aggression; if silence loses illumination there is murky superfluous dharma — so Hongzhi himself rejects mere quietism.
- Buddhas and patriarchs' essential function knows without objects.
**Texts:** 
  - [Inscription on Silent Illumination (Mozhao ming)](../texts/mozhao-ming.md) — ref: 100a26
  - [Admonitions on Seated Meditation (Zuochan zhen) of Hongzhi](../texts/zuochan-zhen-hongzhi.md) — ref: 98a29
  - [Extensive Record of Hongzhi (Hongzhi guanglu)](../texts/hongzhi-guanglu.md) — ref: 73c05
### [Yangqi branch (of the Linji house)](../lineages/yangqi.md)
Wumen too lists 'keeping the mind clear and still' as heretical silent-illumination Chan among the faults of practice.
**Texts:** 
  - [Gateless Barrier (Wumen guan)](../texts/wumenguan.md) — ref: Chanzhen

**Historical debate — Dahui's campaign against 'heretical silent illumination'** (1130s–1150s (in Fujian from c. 1134)): Dahui's sermons and letters name no target explicitly; scholars have suggested Zhenxie Qingliao or other Caodong teachers rather than Hongzhi himself. Tradition records a cordial friendship between Dahui and Hongzhi (Hongzhi is said to have entrusted his funeral arrangements to Dahui). Linji tradition: kanhua became the mainstream; Caodong/Sōtō tradition: silent illumination/just sitting remained its practice.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path, P2-standpoint
**Explanation:** Both sides reject quietist 'silence without illumination'; they differ as methods suited to different temperaments and in whether awakening is framed as a breakthrough event.
Hongzhi's own inscription condemns silence that loses illumination, which is what Dahui attacks; what remains unreconciled is the use of the kōan and the status of the awakening experience.
**The traditions' own objections:** Rinzai tradition holds kōan practice to be necessary for genuine kenshō; Sōtō tradition (Dōgen) holds that aiming at a future awakening misunderstands practice-realization and criticised Dahui.
