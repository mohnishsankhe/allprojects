# Is buddha-nature a true self (permanent, blissful, pure, not empty of qualities) or another name for emptiness?

`dsp:buddha-nature-self-or-emptiness` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Tathāgatagarbha (Buddha-nature) tradition](../lineages/tathagatagarbha.md)
Buddha-nature is the true self: 'self means the tathāgatagarbha'; the dharmakāya has the perfection of self; the garbha is not empty of the inseparable buddha-qualities.
- 'No-self is saṃsāra, self is the Tathāgata' (Mahāparinirvāṇa juan 2).
- 'Self means the tathāgatagarbha' (juan 7).
- Śrīmālā 9 and 12; RGV 1.35.
- Aṅgulimālīya.

### [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md)
The tathāgatagarbha is not the self of the tīrthikas: the buddhas teach it meaning emptiness, the reality-limit, nirvāṇa, the unborn, so that the foolish may not fear no-self; its purpose is to remove five faults.
- Laṅkāvatāra p. 33.
- RGV 1.156-157: taught to remove discouragement, contempt, etc.
**Texts:** 
  - [Laṅkāvatāra-sūtra](../texts/lankavatara-sutra.md) — ref: Vaidya p. 33
  - [Ratnagotravibhāga (Mahāyānottaratantraśāstra)](../texts/ratnagotravibhaga.md) — ref: 1.156-157
### [Tathāgatagarbha (Buddha-nature) tradition](../lineages/tathagatagarbha.md)
(East Asian reading from the same sūtra) Buddha-nature is the emptiness of the highest meaning, which sees both empty and non-empty — the middle way.
- Mahāparinirvāṇa juan 27.
**Texts:** 
  - [Mahāyāna Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra](../texts/mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana.md) — ref: T374 juan 27

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level, P6-upaya
**Explanation:** The texts themselves distinguish levels — the Mahāparinirvāṇa separates worldly and supramundane 'self', and the Laṅkāvatāra and RGV state the teaching's purpose — so 'self' and 'no-self' refer to different objects (the dharmakāya's qualities vs the imagined self of the aggregates).
Under P1 the 'self' of the garbha is the ultimate-level perfection of the dharmakāya and 'no-self' denies the self imagined in the aggregates; under P6 the Laṅkāvatāra says the garbha-teaching is skillful means. What remains open is whether the garbha's 'self' is itself definitive (P5) — see dsp:which-turning-is-definitive.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Jonang and many East Asian readers hold the self-language definitive, not skillful means; Gelug holds buddha-nature to be the mind's emptiness and rejects any non-empty absolute; Theravāda rejects any self at all (dsp:is-there-a-self).
