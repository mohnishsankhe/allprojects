# Is knowledge of the pure self by the ultimate standpoint the whole path, so that outward rites, worship and monastic authority are not needed?

`dsp:adhyatma-niscaya-and-ritual` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** B2 / G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Adhyātma movement (Adhyātma-mata)](../lineages/adhyatma-jain.md)
The soul's own pure nature, known by the ultimate standpoint and self-experience, is the path; ritual formalism, flower offerings and the authority of bhaṭṭārakas and monks are not the path. (In his early phase Banārsīdās abandoned conduct altogether; his mature view holds both standpoints with the guṇasthānas.)
- Samayasāra 11: the soul that takes refuge in the real is of right view.
- Ardhakathānaka on the early and mature phases.
- Samayasāra Nāṭaka's closing chapter on the guṇasthānas.
**Texts:** 
  - [Samayasāra](../texts/samayasara.md) — ref: 11
  - [Ardhakathānaka](../texts/ardhakathanaka.md) — 
  - [Samayasāra Nāṭaka](../texts/samayasara-nataka.md) — 
### [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
The pure self is the goal, but conduct, image worship, the monastic order and scripture are its indispensable means; the Adhyātmīs err in rejecting them (and in Digambara doctrines such as the kevalin's not eating).
- Yaśovijaya: action-yoga and knowledge-yoga together; Adhyātmamataparīkṣā.
- Meghavijaya's Yuktiprabodha against the 'Vāṇārasīya' view.
**Texts:** 
  - [Adhyātmamataparīkṣā](../texts/adhyatmamatapariksa.md) — 
  - [Yuktiprabodha](../texts/yuktiprabodha.md) — 
  - [Adhyātmasāra](../texts/adhyatmasara.md) — 
### [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
Both one-sided niścaya and one-sided vyavahāra are wrong views; the disciple who understands both becomes impartial and gains the full fruit of the teaching.
- Puruṣārthasiddhyupāya 8.
- Ṭoḍarmal's Mokṣamārga Prakāśaka ch. 7 (niścayābhāsī, vyavahārābhāsī).
**Texts:** 
  - [Puruṣārthasiddhyupāya](../texts/purusarthasiddhyupaya.md) — ref: 8
  - [Mokṣamārga Prakāśaka](../texts/moksamarga-prakasaka.md) — ref: ch. 7

**Historical debate — Adhyātmī circle in Agra and its critics** (c. 1630s–1700): Banārsīdās's autobiography; Yaśovijaya's and Meghavijaya's refutations. The Adhyātma current continued in the Digambara Terāpantha; Śvetāmbara authors regarded it as refuted.

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P1-level, P4-stage
**Explanation:** Within the tradition the two standpoints are held together: niścaya names the real goal, vyavahāra the means proper to each guṇasthāna (PSU 8; Samayasāra Nāṭaka).
P1: niścaya and vyavahāra are the Jain form of the ultimate/conventional distinction; P4: outward practice is prescribed for the stages before the self is fully realised. This matches the mature Adhyātma position and Ṭoḍarmal's.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Śvetāmbara critics do not accept the Adhyātmī rejection of image rites and of monastic authority as a valid vyavahāra; the sectarian questions (kevalin's eating, clothing) remain separate and unreconciled (see dsp:kevali-bhukti).
