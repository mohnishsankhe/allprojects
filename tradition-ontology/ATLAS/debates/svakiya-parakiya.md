# Is the gopīs' (and Rādhā's) love for Kṛṣṇa paramour love (parakīyā) or wedded love (svakīyā) in truth?

`dsp:svakiya-parakiya` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate


## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
Rūpa (Ujjvalanīlamaṇi) and Viśvanātha Cakravartin (Ānandacandrikā): paramour love in Vraja is the summit of amorous rasa, uniquely permitted there because Kṛṣṇa brought the gopīs down to taste rasa; Viśvanātha holds it eternal.
- obstacles to union intensify love
- the Vraja līlā is beyond worldly morality

### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
Jīva Gosvāmī: the gopīs are Kṛṣṇa's own internal potencies (Brahma Saṃhitā 5.37), hence svakīyā in truth; their apparent marriage to others is Yogamāyā's arrangement in the manifest play; the Gopālacampū ends with their marriage.
- the Lord's śaktis are eternally his own
- an eternal paramour relation would be improper

### [Dvaitādvaita (Nimbārka Sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaitadvaita.md)
Nimbārka tradition: Rādhā is Kṛṣṇa's eternal consort worshipped on his left (commonly described as svakīyā).
**Texts:** 
  - [Daśaślokī (Siddhāntaratna; Vedāntakāmadhenu)](../texts/dasasloki.md) — ref: 5

**Historical debate — Bengal svakīyā–parakīyā debates** (early 18th c. CE): Gauḍīya accounts describe formal debates (e.g. at the court of the Nawab of Murshidabad, c. 1717–18, with Rādhāmohan Ṭhākur defending parakīyā) the parakīyā side is said to have prevailed (low confidence; to verify)
