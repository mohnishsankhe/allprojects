# What is liberation?

`dsp:nature-of-liberation` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G (Sāṃkhya against rival views)

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
Isolation (kaivalya): the complete cessation of the threefold suffering; puruṣa has no properties, so liberation is not bliss, motion, annihilation or powers.
- SK 68; SS 1.1, 3.85; liberation is only the destruction of obstacles (SS 6.20); not the manifestation of bliss, destruction of specific qualities, going to a place, total destruction, emptiness, gaining powers or Indra's rank (SS 5.74–83); the praise of liberation as bliss is for the dull (SS 5.68).
**Texts:** 
  - [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](../texts/samkhya-karika.md) — ref: 68
  - [Sāṃkhya Sūtra](../texts/samkhya-sutra.md) — ref: 5.74-83; 6.20; 5.67-68
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Liberation is realizing one's nature as Brahman, which is bliss.
- Advaita texts to be cited by U13 (e.g., Taittirīya Up. on Brahman as bliss).
**Texts:** 
  - [Taittirīya Upaniṣad](../texts/taittiriya-upanisad.md) — ref: 2-3
### [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md)
Liberation is absolute release from pain (Nyāya Sūtra 1.1.22), with the cessation of the self's specific qualities (Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika).
- Nyāya Sūtra 1.1.22 (recalled).
**Texts:** 
  - [Nyāya Sūtra](../texts/nyaya-sutra.md) — ref: 1.1.22
### [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md)
The liberated soul rises to the summit of the world (Tattvārtha Sūtra 10.5, recalled).
- Rejected by SS 5.76: the inactive does not go anywhere.
**Texts:** 
  - [Tattvārthasūtra](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md) — ref: 10.5

**Explanation:** Not yet reconciled.
**The traditions' own objections:** Sāṃkhya denies that puruṣa has bliss as a property; Advaita holds bliss is Brahman's nature, not a property.
**Candidate readings:** P1-level / P2-standpoint: descriptions by negation (Sāṃkhya, Nyāya: cessation of suffering) vs by affirmation (Advaita: bliss) of a state all agree is beyond saṃsāra.; P7-arthavāda: Sāṃkhya itself reads 'bliss' as figurative praise (SS 5.67–68).
