# What is the Gītā's principal teaching — knowledge, action, devotion, or their combination?

`dsp:gita-primary-teaching` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G9

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Liberation comes from knowledge of the self alone, steadfastness in knowledge preceded by renunciation of all works; action done as an offering purifies the mind for that knowledge; devotion leads to knowledge; the combination of knowledge and works is not the means.
- The teaching opens with the self (2.11) and closes with 'abandoning all dharmas' (18.66), read as renunciation of works resting in the Lord as the self.
- All action culminates in knowledge (4.33) and the fire of knowledge burns all actions (4.37).
- Madhusūdana's three hexads: action (1–6), devotion (7–12), knowledge (13–18).
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Śaṅkara](../texts/gita-bhasya-sankara.md) — ref: introduction; on 2.10–11, 3.1, 18.66
  - [Gūḍhārthadīpikā](../texts/gudharthadipika.md) — ref: introduction
### [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
The Gītā teaches Nārāyaṇa, the supreme Brahman, reached by bhakti-yoga, which is produced by one's own dharma (karma-yoga), knowledge and detachment; the first hexad teaches the yogas of action and knowledge leading to vision of the self, the middle hexad bhakti-yoga; 18.66 teaches surrender.
- Yāmuna's summary verse 1; the hexad scheme of verses 2–4.
- By devotion alone the Lord is known, seen and entered (11.54); supreme devotion is the fruit of becoming Brahman (18.54).
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītārthasaṅgraha of Yāmuna](../texts/gitarthasangraha-yamuna.md) — ref: 1–4
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Rāmānuja](../texts/gita-bhasya-ramanuja.md) — ref: introduction; on 18.66
### [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md)
The Gītā teaches the supremacy of Viṣṇu and the eternal difference of the selves; liberation is by his grace through devotion grounded in knowledge of his supremacy, with desireless action as worship.
- The highest person is beyond the perishable and the imperishable (15.16–18).
- One attains perfection by worshipping him with one's own work (18.46).
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya (Madhva)](../texts/gita-bhasya-madhva.md) — ref: passim
  - [Gītātātparyanirṇaya](../texts/gita-tatparya-nirnaya.md) — ref: passim
### [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
Knowledge and action go together: the one established in knowledge acts and is not bound (reported for Abhinavagupta's Gītārthasaṅgraha).
- One without the sense of doership does not slay even if he slays (18.17).
- One who sees inaction in action is a doer of all action (4.18).
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītārthasaṅgraha of Abhinavagupta](../texts/gitarthasangraha-abhinavagupta.md) — ref: passim
### [Bhedābheda Vedānta (Bhāskara's school and the early difference-and-non-difference Vedāntins)](../lineages/bhedabheda.md)
Knowledge combined with works (jñāna-karma-samuccaya) is the means to liberation.
- Janaka and others attained perfection by action (3.20).
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītābhāṣya of Bhāskara](../texts/gita-bhasya-bhaskara.md) — ref: passim
### [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md)
The most secret teaching is pure devotion to Kṛṣṇa, the culmination of the Gītā (9.34, 18.64–66).
- 18.64–65 is called 'the most secret of all'.
**Texts:** 
  - [Sārārthavarṣiṇī of Viśvanātha Cakravartin](../texts/sararthavarsini.md) — ref: on 18.64–66
  - [Gītābhūṣaṇa of Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa](../texts/gita-bhusana.md) — ref: on 18.66
### [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
(Recent) The Gītā's principal teaching is energetic action (karma-yoga) by the one who knows, for the welfare of the world, rather than renunciation.
- Kṛṣṇa's own conclusion that the yoga of action excels renunciation (5.2) and that the wise act for lokasaṃgraha (3.25).
**Texts:** 
  - [Gītā Rahasya (Karmayogaśāstra) of Bāl Gaṅgādhar Tilak](../texts/gita-rahasya.md) — ref: passim

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path, P4-stage, P8-six-marks
**Explanation:** The Gītā itself names plural paths by temperament (3.3; 13.24–25) and grades them by capacity (6.3; 12.8–11), so all schools agree the four yogas are taught; which is the final, direct means of liberation is not yet reconciled.
Each school applies the six marks of purport — above all opening and close (upakrama–upasaṃhāra: 2.11 and 18.66) and repetition — to argue for its own primacy. The reconciliation claims only that the text addresses different temperaments and stages; it does not claim the schools agree on the ultimate means.
**The traditions' own objections:** Śaṅkara explicitly rejects the combination of knowledge and works as the means; Rāmānuja and Madhva reject the Advaita reading that knowledge of identity alone liberates; each school holds its reading to be the Gītā's single purport, not one option among several.
**Candidate readings:** knowledge as the direct means, action and devotion preparatory (Advaita); bhakti/prapatti as the means, action and knowledge preparatory (Viśiṣṭādvaita); devotion grounded in knowledge of Viṣṇu's supremacy, by grace (Dvaita); knowledge combined with action (Bhāskara; Abhinavagupta as reported); pure devotion as the most secret teaching (Gauḍīya); action by the enlightened for the world's welfare (Tilak, recent)
