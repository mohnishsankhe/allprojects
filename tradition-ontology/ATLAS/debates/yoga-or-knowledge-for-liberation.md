# Is knowledge alone sufficient for liberation, or must bodily yoga accompany it?

`dsp:yoga-or-knowledge-for-liberation` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Coverage:** G

## Sides (recorded before any reconciliation)
### [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
Yoga and knowledge together: knowledge without yoga cannot liberate, and the mere knower is reborn until his body is 'ripened' by yoga.
- Yogatattva 14–15
- Yogaśikhā 1.12–14, 1.40–53
**Texts:** 
  - [Yogatattva Upaniṣad](../texts/yogatattva-upanisad.md) — ref: 14-15
  - [Yogaśikhā Upaniṣad](../texts/yogasikha-upanisad.md) — ref: 1.12-14
### [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
Knowledge alone gives kaivalya; bodily yoga is for the unawakened; realization is not samādhi or yogic attainment but oneness with Brahman.
- Muktikā prose: not by works, Sāṃkhya, yoga or worship
- Tejobindu 1.33: nose-pressing for the ignorant
- Mahāvākya 5
**Texts:** 
  - [Muktikā Upaniṣad](../texts/muktika-upanisad.md) — ref: 1 prose
  - [Tejobindu Upaniṣad](../texts/tejobindu-upanisad.md) — ref: 1.33
  - [Mahāvākya Upaniṣad](../texts/mahavakya-upanisad.md) — ref: 5

## Reconciliation (interpretation layer)
**Status:** partially-reconciled
**Principles:** P3-path, P4-stage
**Explanation:** The Śāṇḍilya names two ways of ending mind — yoga and knowledge — suited to different people, and the Varāha's bird and ant paths both reach Brahman.
Śāṇḍilya 1.41 (P3) and Varāha 4.34–42 (P4) treat yoga and knowledge as routes for different temperaments and stages; the Tejobindu keeps the limbs but reads them as knowledge.
**The traditions' own objections:** The Yogaśikhā explicitly rejects 'knowledge alone'; the Muktikā explicitly excludes yoga as a means of kaivalya.
