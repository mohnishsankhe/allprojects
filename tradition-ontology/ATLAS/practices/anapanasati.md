# Mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānassati)

Place and posture: gone to the forest, the root of a tree or an empty hut, the monk sits with legs crossed, body upright, mindfulness established in front, and mindfully breathes in and mindfully breathes out (mn118:17). Then sixteen steps in four tetrads: (1) knowing a long breath, (2) a short breath, (3) training in experiencing the whole body, (4) calming the bodily formation; (5) experiencing rapture, (6) pleasure, (7) the mental formation, (8) calming the mental formation; (9) experiencing the mind, (10) gladdening the mind, (11) concentrating the mind, (12) liberating the mind; (13) contemplating impermanence, (14) fading away, (15) cessation, (16) relinquishment (mn118:18-21). The sutta identifies the tetrads with contemplation of the body, feelings, mind and dhammas (mn118:24-27). Stated fruit: great fruit and benefit; it fulfils the four establishments, which fulfil the seven awakening factors, which fulfil knowledge and liberation (mn118:15).
**Stage:** unmarked
**Prerequisites:** The text's own condition: 'Nāhaṁ, bhikkhave, muṭṭhassatissa asampajānassa ānāpānassatiṁ vadāmi' - it does not speak of mindfulness of breathing for one of muddled mindfulness and without clear comprehension (mn118:26). Place and posture as at mn118:17.
**Signs of progress:** The text states no bodily or mental signs of its own beyond the arising of the awakening factors in sequence (mn118:30-36).

## The texts' own warnings
- I do not say there is development of mindfulness of breathing for one who is muddled and not clearly comprehending. — [Ānāpānasati Sutta](../texts/anapanasati-sutta.md) MN 118 (26.6)
- Mindfulness of breathing is a subject for great persons, difficult to develop; the breath becomes very subtle, and the meditator must not give up but seek it where it touches. — [Visuddhimagga](../texts/visuddhimagga.md) VIII

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- contested: [Breath-control (prāṇāyāma) in haṭha](pranayama.md) — both take the breath as object, but ānāpānasati observes the natural breath without retention; not the same practice
