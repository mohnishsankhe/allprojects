# Loving-kindness (mettā-bhāvanā)

**Category:** meditation
**Convergence:** 2 independent lineage(s): [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
**Taught in:** [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Mahāvihāra fraternity (Mahāvihāra-vāsins)](../lineages/mahavihara.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md), [U Ba Khin – S. N. Goenka tradition (Sayagyi U Ba Khin lineage)](../lineages/u-ba-khin-goenka.md)

Vism IX, from the opening to the breaking down of the barriers: the beginner, having cut impediments and taken the subject, sits at ease after the meal in a secluded place and first reflects on the danger in hate and the benefit of patience. Loving-kindness is developed first toward oneself ('may I be happy and free from suffering'), as one's own witness; then toward a dear and respected person; then, to break down the barriers, toward a very dear friend, a neutral person and an enemy, making the mind soft and workable at each step. It is not developed first toward a disliked person, a very dear friend, a neutral person or an enemy, not specifically toward one of the opposite sex, and not at all toward the dead. If resentment arises, the text gives an ordered set of remedies (prc:patigha-vinodana-vism). The barrier is broken when the mind is even toward oneself and the three others (the bandits test); then the sign and access arise, and absorption follows (the first jhāna accompanied by loving-kindness, and the higher ones), after which the directions are pervaded (Vism IX p. 307-308).
**Stage:** beginner
**Signs of progress:** The text's mark that the barrier is broken: the mind is equal toward oneself, the dear, the neutral and the enemy, so that if bandits demanded one of the four no one is chosen (Vism IX p. 307); at that moment the sign and access are gained.
