# The Gītā's meditation (dhyāna-yoga, BhG 6.10–32)

`prc:dhyana-yoga-gita` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Category:** meditation
**Convergence:** 1 independent lineage(s): [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
**Taught in:** [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)

In solitude, alone, with mind and body controlled and without possessions, sit on a firm seat in a clean place; with body, head and neck erect and still and the gaze as if at the tip of the nose, serene and fearless, keeping the vow of celibacy, make the mind one-pointed, fixed on the self or on the Lord; gradually still the mind by a steady intellect and think of nothing else; whenever the restless mind wanders, bring it back; be moderate in food, sleep and effort.
**Stage:** the one who has ascended (6.3–4); practised with determination and without despair (6.23)
**Prerequisites:** renunciation of intention (6.2); self-control; moderation (6.16–17)
**Duration:** constantly (satatam 6.10; sadā 6.15, 6.28); perfection may take many births (6.45)
**Signs of progress:** the mind steady like a lamp in a windless place (6.19); contentment in the self and happiness beyond the senses (6.20–21); the happiness of contact with Brahman (6.28); seeing the self in all and all in the self (6.29)

- The mind is restless, turbulent and as hard to restrain as the wind (6.34); it is held only by practice and dispassion (6.35). — [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) 6.34-35
- Yoga is hard to attain for one whose self is unrestrained (6.36). — [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) 6.36

_Notes: Yoga is defined here as 'the unyoking from union with sorrow', to be practised with resolve and an undejected mind (6.23). One who falls away is not lost: he is reborn and resumes (6.40–45)._
