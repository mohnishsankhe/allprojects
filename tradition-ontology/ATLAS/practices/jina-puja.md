# Worship of the Jina image (jina-pūjā)

`prc:jina-puja` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Category:** ritual
**Convergence:** 2 independent lineage(s): [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md), [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md)
**Taught in:** [Bīsapantha (Digambara)](../lineages/bisapantha.md), [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md), [Śvetāmbara Mūrtipūjaka](../lineages/murtipujaka.md), [Terāpantha (Digambara)](../lineages/terapantha-digambara.md)

The householder bathes the consecrated image and offers eight substances — water, sandalwood paste, flowers, incense, lamp, unbroken rice, sweets and fruit (the aṣṭaprakārī pūjā) — with verses recalling the Jina's qualities, followed by inner worship (caityavandana, praise, meditation). The Digambara Terāpantha offers only non-living, dry substances; mendicants perform only inner worship. The Jina, being liberated, does not respond; the worshipper is purified by recalling his qualities.

## The texts' own warnings
- Sthānakavāsīs and Terāpanthīs reject material worship because it harms one-sensed beings (water, flowers, fire); Mūrtipūjaka teachers restrict material worship to householders. — [Ācārāṅga Sūtra](../texts/acaranga-sutra.md) 1.1.2-7

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- analogous: [Ritual worship of the deity (pūjā, arcana)](puja.md) — outwardly similar image worship, but the Jina is not a grace-giving deity and receives nothing

_Notes: The Sthānakavāsī and Śvetāmbara Terāpanthī traditions reject image worship; the Digambara Terāpantha rejects offerings of flowers and fresh produce and the bhaṭṭārakas' ritual authority (recalled)._
