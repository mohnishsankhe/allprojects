# Renunciation (saṃnyāsa) in the Dharmaśāstra

**Taught in:** [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md), [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md), [Saṃnyāsa — the renunciate ideal of the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads](../lineages/sannyasa.md)

After the Prājāpatya sacrifice with all possessions given away, the renouncer deposits the fires in himself and goes forth giving safety to all beings; he lives alone, without fire or house, begging once a day, purifying step, water, speech and act, delighting in the self, practising breath-control and meditation, neither welcoming death nor life (MDh 6.33–85); he must not renounce the Veda (VDh 10.4).
**Stage:** after the debts are paid (or, in other views, from any stage)

## The texts' own warnings
- Seeking liberation without paying the three debts leads downward (MDh 6.35–37). — [Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)](../texts/manusmrti.md) 6.35-37
- Renunciation is hard to attain without yoga (5.6). — [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) 5.6
