# Restraint and withdrawal of the senses (indriya-saṃyama)

`prc:indriya-samyama` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Category:** sense-withdrawal-concentration
**Convergence:** 1 independent lineage(s): [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
**Taught in:** [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)

Withdraw the senses from their objects on all sides as a tortoise draws in its limbs (2.58); restraining them all, sit yoked, intent on Kṛṣṇa (2.61); keep the senses withheld from their objects on all sides (2.68); move among objects with senses free of attraction and aversion and under the self's control (2.64); control the senses by the mind while acting (3.7); restrain the senses first in order to slay desire (3.41).
**Stage:** all
**Signs of progress:** ['wisdom firmly established (2.58, 2.61, 2.68)', 'serenity (prasāda), the end of all sorrows and steady understanding (2.64–65)', 'even the inner taste for objects turns away on seeing the highest (2.59)']

## The texts' own warnings
- For one who abstains, the objects turn away but the taste (rasa) remains; it turns away only on seeing the highest. — [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) 2.59
- The turbulent senses forcibly carry off the mind even of a discerning person who strives. — [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) 2.60
- The mind that follows the roving senses carries off wisdom as the wind a ship on the water. — [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) 2.67
- Restraining the organs of action while dwelling on objects in the mind is hypocrisy (mithyācāra). — [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) 3.6
- Beings follow their nature; 'what will restraint accomplish?' — [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) 3.33
- Attraction and aversion lie in the object of each sense; do not come under their sway, for they are waylayers. — [Bhagavad Gītā](../texts/bhagavad-gita.md) 3.34
