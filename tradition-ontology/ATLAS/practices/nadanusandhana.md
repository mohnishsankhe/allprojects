# Attention to the inner sound (nādānusandhāna) in the haṭha texts

**Category:** mantra-sound
**Convergence:** 3 independent lineage(s): [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md), [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md), [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
**Taught in:** [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md), [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md), [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)

Attention to the inner sound (nāda): seated, with the ears closed by the hands, the mind is fixed on the sound heard, and on the subtler sound as it appears, until it dissolves in it (HYP 4.65-68, 4.82-99); the text says it is accepted even for the dull (4.65) and calls it the chief of the ways of laya (4.66). The instruction of 4.68 that touches restricted practice is not reproduced.
**Stage:** advanced
**Signs of progress:** ['the four stages with their sounds — tinkling (ārambha), kettle-drum (ghaṭa), mardala (paricaya), flute and vīṇā (niṣpatti) (HYP 4.70-77)', 'outer sound is covered; in a fortnight distraction is overcome (HYP 4.83)', 'the body becomes like a log; conch and drum are not heard (HYP 4.106)', 'a light within the sound, and the mind dissolving in it (GS 5.82-83; HYP 4.100)', 'body like a log, unaware of heat and cold, gaze, breath and mind steady (Nādabindu 52–56)']

## The texts' own warnings
- On whatever sound the mind first fastens it should be kept there; the mind, even if delighted or distracted, is not to be let move elsewhere. — [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) 4.87-89
