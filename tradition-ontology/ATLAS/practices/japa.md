The repetition of a mantra received from a teacher — a seed syllable, the praṇava, a divine name, a Vedic verse or a longer formula — with attention to its sound and meaning. Before repetition the practitioner states the mantra's seer, metre and deity (viniyoga) and places its parts on the body (nyāsa); the count is kept on a rosary (akṣamālā) or the finger-joints. It is done aloud (vācika), whispered (upāṃśu) or mentally (mānasa), each far superior to the previous; Patañjali joins repetition to contemplation of its meaning (YS 1.28). A course of fixed counts with supplementary rites (puraścaraṇa) 'masters' a mantra for use.

## The texts' own warnings
- A mantra wrong in tone or letter does not convey the meaning intended; as a verbal thunderbolt it harms the sacrificer. — [Pāṇinīya Śikṣā](../texts/paniniya-siksa.md) 52
- Mantras with defects such as being 'cut' do not protect the practitioner; they must first be purified. — [Śāradātilaka](../texts/saradatilaka.md) 2.64-70
- The mantra being repeated is to be kept secret (gopana). — [Śāradātilaka](../texts/saradatilaka.md) 2.122
- In mental japa one should not move head, neck, tongue or lips. — [Padārthādarśa of Rāghavabhaṭṭa](../texts/padarthadarsa.md) on 16 (quotation)
- Recitation done with desire, pride or wrongly leads to lesser worlds, not to Brahman (as taught in the Jāpaka story). — [Mokṣadharma (Mokṣadharmaparvan)](../texts/moksadharma.md) 12.189–193
