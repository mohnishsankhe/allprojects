# Development of the four divine abidings (brahmavihāra)

**Category:** meditation
**Convergence:** 2 independent lineage(s): [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
**Taught in:** [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)

Vism IX p. 318-319: the four divine abidings are loving-kindness, compassion, gladness and equanimity; the text gives each a mark, function, manifestation, proximate cause, success and failure, a common purpose (pleasant abiding in insight and success in existence) and a special purpose (loving-kindness against ill will, compassion against cruelty, gladness against discontent, equanimity against lust), and a near and a far enemy for each: for loving-kindness lust is near and ill will far; for compassion household-based displeasure near and cruelty far; for gladness household-based gladness near and discontent far; for equanimity the household-based equanimity of ignorance near, lust and resentment far. In Vism III they are listed among the forty: the first three bring three jhānas (fourfold count); for the fourth divine abiding the edition prints "catukkajjhānikā" (read by the extractor, in the notes of tea:visuddhimagga:3.p111/2, as the fourth jhāna; the page does not say so in those words); they suit the hating temperament.
