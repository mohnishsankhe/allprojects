# Cutting the aśvattha with the weapon of non-attachment (BhG 15.3–5)

Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: The text gives the method as an image: the firmly rooted aśvattha (roots above, branches below, swollen by the guṇas, sense-objects its shoots, its roots below bound up with action) is to be cut with the strong weapon of non-attachment (asaṅga-śastra); then the place from which there is no return is to be sought, in the words 'I take refuge in that very primal person from whom the ancient activity flowed forth'. No further technique is given.
**Signs of progress:** ['Free from pride and delusion, the fault of attachment conquered, constantly intent on what pertains to the self, desires turned away, released from the pairs of pleasure and pain — such undeluded ones go to the imperishable place (15.5).']
