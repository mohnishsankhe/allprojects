# Bhastrikā ('the bellows')

`prc:bhastrika` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Category:** breath
**Convergence:** 2 independent lineage(s): [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md), [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md)
**Taught in:** [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md), [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md)

Seated in padmāsana, the breath is expelled and drawn in forcefully through the nose again and again like a blacksmith's bellows, sounding in heart, throat and skull; when tired, one fills through the right nostril, holds with the nostrils closed and releases through the left (HYP 2.59-67; GS 5.76-78). The numbers of strokes and rounds given in the Gheraṇḍa are not reproduced.
**Stage:** advanced
**Signs of progress:** ['removes vāta, pitta and kapha, increases the bodily fire, quickly awakens kuṇḍalinī, destroys the phlegm barring the mouth of the brahma-channel, pierces the three knots (HYP 2.65-67)']

## The texts' own warnings
- As a lion, an elephant or a tiger is tamed only slowly, so the breath must be brought under control gradually; otherwise it kills the practitioner. — [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) 2.15
- Proper breath-control destroys all disease; improper practice produces all disease — hiccup, asthma, cough and pains in the head, ears and eyes arise from the disturbed breath. — [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) 2.16-17
- Draw the breath in slowly and release it slowly; do not hold it excessively nor release it quickly. — [Gorakṣaśataka](../texts/goraksasataka.md) 51
- When fatigue arises in the body the bellows-breathing is to give way to filling through the sun (right) nostril. — [Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)](../texts/hatha-yoga-pradipika.md) 2.63
