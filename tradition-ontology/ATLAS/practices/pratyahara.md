# Sense-withdrawal (pratyāhāra) in the haṭha texts

The haṭha texts teach withdrawal in several ways: bringing the wandering mind back from each kind of object — sight, praise and blame, sounds, heat and cold, smells, tastes — and subduing it in the self (GS 4.1-7); withdrawing the senses from their objects while holding the breath, and regarding whatever is seen, smelt, heard as the self (DYŚ 84-88; ŚS 3.57-58); moving the breath and attention through the eighteen vital points (Yoga Yājñavalkya; Vasiṣṭha Saṃhitā); and, in the Gorakṣaśataka and Vivekamārtaṇḍa, the 'withdrawal' of the moon's nectar from the sun by viparītakaraṇī (GŚ 55-59). It is the fourth of the Gheraṇḍa's seven means, giving calm (GS 1.10).
**Stage:** intermediate
**Signs of progress:** ['far-hearing, far-seeing, instant travel, perfection of speech, invisibility and other powers (DYŚ 89-90)', 'desire and the other enemies are destroyed (GS 4.1)']
