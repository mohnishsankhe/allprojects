# The five hindrances (nīvaraṇa)

`obs:five-hindrances` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Category:** hindrance
**Convergence:** 3 independent lineage(s): [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
**Taught in:** [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)

In Vism IV (commentary on 'quite secluded from sensual desires, secluded from unwholesome states'), the 'unwholesome states' from which the first jhāna is secluded are the hindrances, because they are the adversaries and opposites of the jhāna factors: concentration is the opposite of sensual desire, rapture of ill will, applied thought of sloth and torpor, happiness of restlessness and remorse, sustained thought of doubt (cited from the Peṭaka). The jhāna does not arise while they are unabandoned, so they are called the factors of abandoning (five factors abandoned). They are abandoned by suppression (vikkhambhana), and 'seclusion' (viveka) is the going away of the hindrances. Ch. XXII lists them among the states to be abandoned, so called by way of covering, hindering and concealing consciousness.
**Antidotes:** concentration, rapture, applied thought, happiness and sustained thought, as the opposed jhāna factors (Vism IV p. 141), [Contemplation of the foul (asubha-bhāvanā): summary only](../practices/asubha-bhavana.md), [Loving-kindness (mettā-bhāvanā)](../practices/metta-bhavana.md), [Perception of light](../practices/aloka-sanna.md), [Wise attention](../practices/yoniso-manasikara.md), wise attention to their denourishment (SN 46.51), [The four dhyānas](../practices/four-dhyanas.md), [The four immeasurables (apramāṇa)](../practices/four-immeasurables.md)
