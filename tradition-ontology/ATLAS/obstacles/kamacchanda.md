# Sensual desire (kāmacchanda)

`obs:kamacchanda` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Category:** hindrance
**Convergence:** 2 independent lineage(s): [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
**Taught in:** [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)

First hindrance. In Vism IV it is the desire that the jhāna opposes: concentration is its opposite (p. 141), and 'quite secluded from sensual desires' declares the seclusion of suppression of sensual desire. The text distinguishes objective sensual desires (vatthukāma: agreeable sights, sounds etc.) and sensual desires as defilement (kilesakāma: zeal, lust, zealous lust, intention, lust for sense objects). How it obstructs, in the text's words: consciousness enticed by objects of many kinds is not concentrated on a unified object, and, overcome by sensual desire, does not follow the way to abandon the sense sphere.
**Antidotes:** concentration (samādhi) as its opposite in the jhāna factors (p. 141); the first jhāna as the escape from sensual desires, i.e. renunciation (p. 139), [Contemplation of the foul (asubha-bhāvanā): summary only](../practices/asubha-bhavana.md), [Attention to the repulsiveness of the body (paṭikūlamanasikāra)](../practices/patikulamanasikara.md)
