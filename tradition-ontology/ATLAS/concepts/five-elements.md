# The five elements (mahābhūta)

## Names
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): mahābhūta

## Definitions
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): Space, air, fire, water and earth, arising from the five subtle elements; specific, and calm, turbulent or dull (SK 22, 38).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Lineage contribution (Upaniṣadic): from the self arose space, from space wind, from wind fire, from fire water, from water earth (TU 2.1); earth, wind, space, water and light as the five great elements (AU 3.1.3); the fivefold quality of yoga — earth, water, fire, wind, space (ŚU 2.12); ChU 6 has a threefold scheme (heat, water, food).
- [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md): Lineage contribution (Āyurveda): space, air, fire, water and earth with their qualities (sound, touch, form, taste, smell), each succeeding one having one more quality (Ca Śā 1.27-28); the person's six dhātus are the five elements and consciousness (Śā 1.16; 5.4); in Suśruta space is rich in sattva, air in rajas, fire in sattva and rajas, water in sattva and tamas, earth in tamas (Su Śā 1.20).
- [Siddha medicine (cittā maruttuvam)](../lineages/siddha-medicine.md): Nilam (earth), nīr (water), tī (fire), vaḷi (air), viṇ / ākāyam (ether): the body and every drug are made of them; the humours are compounded of them — vaḷi of air (and ether), aḻal of fire, aiyam of earth and water.
