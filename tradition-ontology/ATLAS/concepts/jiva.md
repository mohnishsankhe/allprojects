# The individual living self (jīva)

`cpt:jiva` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Category:** self

## Names
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): jīva

## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Lineage contribution (Upaniṣadic): being enters the elements 'with this living self' (ChU 6.3.2); life does not die (ChU 6.11.3); the bird that eats the fruit and grieves beside the Lord (MuU 3.1.1-2; ŚU 4.6-7); the jīva as a hair-tip part, fit for infinity (ŚU 5.9); the elemental self bound by karma (MaiU 3.1-2).
- [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md): Eternal, atomic, self-luminous as 'I', a knower with attributive consciousness, an agent dependent on the Lord, an enjoyer, essentially his servant (śeṣa), a mode of Brahman; countless, and remaining distinct in liberation.
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Dvaita: each soul is eternal, sentient, atomic, dependent, a reflection of God, different from God, from matter and from every other soul, with an innate and unalterable fitness; its identity with Brahman is denied as contrary to experience and scripture.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7.5: the Lord's higher nature 'has become the living being' (jīvabhūtā) and upholds the world.
