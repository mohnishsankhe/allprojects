# Perfection of the body and immortality (kāya-siddhi, amaratva)

**Category:** karma-rebirth

## Definitions
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): The body filled with the moon's nectar does not leave the embodied one (GŚ 66); the great siddhas broke the staff of Time and roam the universe (HYP 1.9); 'the swallowing of the body by the body' (Jñāneśvarī 6.291).
- [Yoginī Kaula (Yoginīkaula) of Matsyendra](../lineages/yogini-kaula.md): Immortality by devotion to guru and yoginīs (KJN 5.33); the five jewels ending in the destruction of old age and death (17.12–13); the body standing like a thunderbolt with no dissolution (15.25).
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): Without the body life perishes and true knowledge cannot be reached; knowing the means to nourish the body, one nourishes the life (TM 724); the Lord has made the body his temple (TM 725). Kāya citti removes karma and opens higher powers (Tiruvaḷḷuvar ñāṉam 14); vāci yoga makes it free of obstacles (Caṭṭaimuṉi 4).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): In the haṭha texts bodily perfection (dehasiddhi, HYP 3.103) and immortality (amaratva, a synonym of samādhi, 4.3) are fruits of retaining bindu and nectar; the DYŚ's perfected yogin may keep his body and roam the worlds at will (115–118); the Yogabīja's 'ripe body'.
