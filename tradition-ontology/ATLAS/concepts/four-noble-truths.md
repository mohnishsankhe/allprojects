## Definitions
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): DN 22:17-21: as the fifth field of dhamma-contemplation the four truths are known 'as they really are', and each is expanded: suffering as birth, ageing, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, despair, the disliked, the lost, the unobtained, in brief the five aggregates of clinging; origin as threefold craving arising and settling in the dear-looking and pleasant-looking; cessation as the complete fading and ceasing of that craving, in the same places; the way as the noble eightfold path.
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): Suffering, its origin, its cessation, the way to its cessation; the fifth field of dhamma-contemplation, each known 'as it really is' (MN 10:44).
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): Suffering, its origin (craving), its cessation, and the path (the noble eightfold path) (SN 56.11); to be fully understood, abandoned, realised and developed; what the Buddha declares because it is beneficial (MN 63; SN 56.31); all wholesome states are included in them as footprints in an elephant's (MN 28).
- [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md): Taught in the order of realization; all that has outflows is suffering; realized in sixteen moments.
- [Lokottaravāda](../lineages/lokottaravada.md): Taught at Ṛṣivadana with the two extremes and three turnings.
- [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md): In emptiness there are no suffering, origin, cessation or path (Heart); of the four only cessation is permanent, the one truth and refuge (Śrīmālā).
