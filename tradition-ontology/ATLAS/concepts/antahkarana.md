# The internal instrument (antaḥkaraṇa)

`cpt:antahkarana` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Category:** mind
**Members:** buddhi, ahaṃkāra, manas, citta, ahaṅkāra

## Names
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): antaḥkaraṇa

## Definitions
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): In Sāṃkhya threefold: buddhi, ahaṃkāra and manas, each with its own function, sharing the five vital winds as their common function (SK 29, 33).
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): One inner organ functioning as mind (doubt), intellect (decision), citta (memory) and ego (I-sense); the adjunct of the jīva, not the self.
- [Bhedābheda Vedānta (Bhāskara's school and the early difference-and-non-difference Vedāntins)](../lineages/bhedabheda.md): Bhāskara: the intellect and mind are real limiting adjuncts (upādhi) of the soul, part of what makes it distinct from Brahman until death.
- [Puṣṭimārga (Vallabha Sampradāya; Śuddhādvaita Brahmavāda)](../lineages/pustimarga.md): Vallabha addresses his own mind in the Antaḥkaraṇaprabodha; the mind's flowing toward the Lord is itself service (mānasī sevā), the highest form; identification with the inner organ is one of the five joints of ignorance.
- [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md): The mind is to be engaged in remembrance while the tongue sings (Upadeśāmṛta 8) and instructed (Raghunātha Dāsa's Manaḥśikṣā); bhāva 'softens' the heart (citta-māsṛṇya).
