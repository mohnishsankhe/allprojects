## Definitions
- [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md): All philosophies are roles of the one conscious Lord: Buddhists rest in the intellect-principle, Jains in the qualities, Vedic knowers in the Person, Pāñcarātrikas in the unmanifest; the Trika alone sees the self as both transcendent and immanent.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): Mādhava's compendium arranges sixteen systems from the Cārvāka upward ending in Advaita; Madhusūdana holds that all sages intend the one Lord and their differing paths suit differing capacities.
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): Haribhadra sets out six darśanas without polemic; Siddhasena holds that there are as many doctrines as ways of speaking, and that the Jina's word is the aggregate of all the one-sided views; the Sūtrakṛtāṅga tradition classes rival doctrines as 363 kinds of kriyāvāda, akriyāvāda, ajñāna and vinaya.
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): The Brahmajāla surveys the ascetics' and brahmins' theories about past and future on sixty-two grounds, all arising from contact and feeling, which the Tathāgata knows and goes beyond.
- [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md): Bhāviveka examines the Sāṃkhya, Vaiśeṣika, Vedānta and Mīmāṃsā and grants what is acceptable in the Vedānta only as the emptiness the Buddha taught.
