## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The embodied self (ātman, dehin) is unborn, eternal, not slain when the body is slain, passing into new bodies as into new clothes, unmanifest and unthinkable (BhG 2.11–30); the knower of the field (13.1–2); an eternal portion of the Lord (15.7); Bhṛgu defends the existence of a living self in the elemental body (MBh 12.175–185).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: The self in which, by knowledge, all beings are seen, and then in the Lord (4.35); the yoked yogin sees the self abiding in all beings and all beings in the self (6.29), his self having become the self of all beings (5.7); restrained thought (citta) rests in the self alone (6.18) and, seeing the self by the self, is content in the self (6.20). The same word is used reflexively: one raises oneself by oneself, and the self is one's own friend or enemy (6.5–6). In 4.35 and 6.29 'ātmani' can also be read reflexively ('in oneself').
