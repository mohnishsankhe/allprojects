# Monastic nudity and clothing

## Definitions
- [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md): Complete nudity ('the form of the newborn') is required of the fully renounced; clothing, however small, is possession and bars liberation even for a Tīrthaṅkara.
- [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md): Possession is attachment; clothes and bowls kept for restraint are not possessions. The Ācārāṅga allows three, two, one or no garments; Mahāvīra went naked, but the naked Jinakalpa mode lapsed after Jambū, and the Keśin–Gautama dialogue shows the outward sign is secondary to knowledge, faith and conduct.
- [Yāpanīya](../lineages/yapaniya.md): Nudity is the normal sign, but a garment may be kept by exception, and at death may be given up; women and those with just cause are not excluded from liberation.
