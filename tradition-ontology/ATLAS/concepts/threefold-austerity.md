## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Austerity of body (worship of gods, the twice-born, teachers and the wise; purity, uprightness, celibacy, non-harm), of speech (non-agitating, truthful, pleasant, beneficial speech and recitation) and of mind (serenity, gentleness, silence, self-restraint, purity of being); each again sāttvic, rājasic or tāmasic (BhG 17.14–19); self-tormenting austerity not enjoined by scripture is demonic (17.5–6).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: Austerity of the body: worship of gods, twice-born, teachers and the wise; purity, straightforwardness, celibacy, non-harming (17.14). Of speech: non-agitating, true, pleasant and beneficial speech and the practice of recitation (17.15). Of the mind: clarity of mind, gentleness, silence, self-restraint, purity of disposition (17.16). This threefold austerity with supreme faith and without desire for fruit is sāttvic (17.17); done for respect and with hypocrisy, rājasic, unstable and impermanent (17.18); done from a deluded notion with self-torment or to ruin another, tāmasic (17.19).
