## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The afflicted, the seeker of knowledge, the seeker of wealth and the knower worship the Lord; the knower is the best and is the Lord's very self (BhG 7.16–18).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7.16–19: four kinds of people of good deeds worship the Lord — the afflicted (ārta), the one who seeks to know (jijñāsu), the seeker of an object/wealth (arthārthin) and the knower (jñānin). All are noble, but the knower, ever yoked and of single devotion (eka-bhakti), is distinguished: he is exceedingly dear to the Lord and the Lord to him, and is held to be the Lord's very self; at the end of many births he takes refuge knowing 'Vāsudeva is all' — a great soul very hard to find.
- [Vārkarī sampradāya](../lineages/varkari.md): Jñāneśvar calls the knower's devotion the 'fourth' devotion, beyond those of the afflicted, the seeker and the one who wants gain.
