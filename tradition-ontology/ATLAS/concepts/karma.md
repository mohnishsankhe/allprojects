## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Lineage contribution (Upaniṣadic): first taught in secret — 'one becomes good by good action, bad by bad' (BAU 3.2.13); 'as one acts, as one behaves, so one becomes'; desire → resolve → action → result (BAU 4.4.5); rebirth 'according to action and knowledge' (KU 2.2.7; KauU 1.2); conduct determines the womb (ChU 5.10.7); action does not cling to the knower (ChU 4.14.3; Īśa 2); the knots cut, actions are exhausted (MuU 2.2.8).
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): In Sāṃkhya virtue and non-virtue are dispositions of buddhi, not of puruṣa (SK 23; SS 5.25); they carry the subtle body upward or downward (SK 44) and ripen as experience; the fruit of action comes from karma, not from a governing Lord (SS 5.2); with knowledge they cease to act as causes — future karma burnt like seeds, present karma exhausted (SK 67; Gauḍapāda).
- [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md): Karma is primarily the enjoined act; its fruit comes through the apūrva; dharma and adharma produce bodies and experiences until exhausted.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: The course of action is deep (4.17); action binds when done from desire with attachment to fruit (5.12), but not when done without attachment, for the sake of sacrifice, with actions placed in Brahman (4.20–23, 5.10); knowledge burns all actions to ashes (4.19, 4.37); all sacrifices are born of action (4.32) and all action culminates in knowledge (4.33); the Lord is not stained by actions and whoever knows this is not bound (4.14).
