## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Varāha 1's enumeration of 96 tattvas: the 24 Sāṃkhya principles plus elements, bodies, states, six modifications, six waves, six sheaths, six enemies, three jīvas, guṇas, karmas, actions, inner functions, four sublime attitudes and fourteen deities; the Pāśupatabrahma makes the 96 the haṃsa's sacred thread.
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): The Siddha and Siddha-medical enumeration of the embodied person (toṇṇūṟṟāṟu tattuvam), in the commonly given form: elements 5, sense-organs 5, sense-objects 5, organs of action 5, their functions 5, inner faculties 4, knowing (aṟivu) 1, channels (nāṭi) 10, winds (vāyu) 10, receptacles (ācayam) 5, sheaths (kōcam) 5, supports (ātāram) 6, circles (maṇṭalam) 3, impurities (malam) 3, humours (tōṣam) 3, desires (ēṭaṇai) 3, qualities (kuṇam) 3, deeds (viṉai) 2, passions (irākam) 8, states (avattai) 5 = 96. Aḻukaṇṇi speaks of 'the ninety and six' (19) and of all the principles burnt in realization (36).
- [Siddha medicine (cittā maruttuvam)](../lineages/siddha-medicine.md): The same count is the anatomical base of Siddha medicine: health and disease are read against the ninety-six.

## Relations (interpretation layer)
- contrasts-with → [The thirty-six principles (ṣaṭtriṃśat-tattva)](thirty-six-tattvas.md): Śaiva Siddhānta's 36 are the cosmic principles; the 96 enumerate the embodied person
- corresponds-to-in-map → [The twenty-five principles (pañcaviṃśati-tattva)](twenty-five-tattvas.md): the first 24–25 of the 96 overlap the Sāṃkhya principles

_Notes: The overall count is certain; the membership given is the one most often printed, recalled at low confidence — some lists include the nine openings (vācal 9) and differ elsewhere. The Varāha Upaniṣad has its own 96._
