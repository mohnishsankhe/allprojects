## Definitions
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The twenty-four teachers from whose ways the avadhūta brāhmaṇa learned freedom, told to King Yadu: earth (forbearance; its mountains and trees: living for others), air (content with bare sustenance; unattached among objects), sky (the self pervasive and untouched), water (purity that purifies), fire (taking all without taint; the Lord in all forms), moon (changes belong to the body), sun (taking and giving without attachment; one appearing many), pigeon (ruin by excessive attachment), python (accepting what comes), ocean (unmoved by gain and loss), moth (lured by forms to ruin), honeybee (begging a little from many; taking the essence of all scriptures; not hoarding), elephant (bound by touch of the female), honey-gatherer (hoarded wealth enjoyed by others), deer (lured by song), fish (the tongue), Piṅgalā (hope is misery, hopelessness bliss), osprey (possession invites attack), child (carefree bliss), maiden with bangles (live alone), arrow-maker (one-pointed absorption), serpent (homeless solitude), spider (the Lord emits and withdraws the world), wasp (one becomes what one dwells on). His own body is a further teacher of dispassion; knowledge from one teacher alone is not firm, for Brahman is sung in many ways.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bodhya names six teachers — Piṅgalā, the osprey, the snake, the bee, the arrow-maker and the maiden (Mahābhārata 12.171.61).
