## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 8.23–27: the two perennial courses of departing yogins — the bright (śukla): fire, light, day, the bright fortnight, the six months of the northern course, by which knowers of brahman go to brahman and do not return; and the dark (kṛṣṇa): smoke, night, the dark fortnight, the six months of the southern course, by which the yogin reaches the lunar light and returns. Knowing the two paths, the yogin is not deluded.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The bright path (fire, light, day, bright fortnight, northern course) leads knowers of Brahman to non-return; the dark path (smoke, night, dark fortnight, southern course) to the lunar light and return (BhG 8.23–26); Bhīṣma waits for the northern course to die (MBh 6.114).
