# Liberation while living (jīvanmukti)

`cpt:jivanmukti` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Category:** karma-rebirth

## Names
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): jīvanmukta

## Definitions
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): After perfect knowledge, virtue and the rest cease to cause, but the knower remains embodied through the momentum of impressions, like a potter's wheel (SK 67); knowledge burns future karma like burnt seeds (Gauḍapāda). The Sāṃkhya Sūtra names the one liberated while living and grounds it in the need for liberated teachers — otherwise the blind would lead the blind (3.78–84).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Vyāsa: with the cloud of dharma the afflictions are uprooted and karmic residues destroyed; 'when afflictions and karma cease, the wise one is liberated while living', since error is the cause of becoming (YBh 4.30); one whose afflictions are exhausted has no karma for a future birth (YBh 2.12) and is 'in his last body' (YBh 2.4).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Lineage contribution (Upaniṣadic): the word is later, but the texts say the mortal 'attains brahman here' when all desires in the heart are released (BAU 4.4.7; KU 2.3.14), that the knots are cut 'here' (KU 2.3.15; MuU 2.2.8), that the best knower of brahman delights in the self yet is active (MuU 3.1.4), and 'if one has known it here, there is truth' (Kena 2.5).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): In the minor Upaniṣads: the state of one who, knowing the Self, is freed while the body continues on the momentum of prārabdha; marked by freedom from 'I' and 'mine', from vāsanā and from the mind's properties (Muktikā 2; Varāha 4; Tejobindu 4); the dissolution of mind 'with form'.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): In the Yoga Vāsiṣṭha: the seventh stage; outwardly engaged, inwardly cool; mind 'dead' (manonāśa); one acts as custom requires, like one half-asleep. In the Aṣṭāvakra Gītā: the liberated is neither distracted nor absorbed, the same wherever he is. In the Rāma Gītā: living out prārabdha free of ego until merging.
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The perfected one does not notice the body, which lives on while its karma lasts; though in the body he is not in it.
- [Śākta traditions](../lineages/sakta.md): The destruction of ignorance, completion of human aims, is the state of liberation while living.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): On knowledge, past karma is destroyed and future karma does not cling, but the body continues till prārabdha is exhausted; the knower is free while living.
- [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md): Rejected: while the body remains, karma remains; the knower or surrendered is assured of release but released only at death.
- [Bhedābheda Vedānta (Bhāskara's school and the early difference-and-non-difference Vedāntins)](../lineages/bhedabheda.md): Denied by Bhāskara.
- [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md): Redefined as the state of one who serves Hari in all conditions.
- [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md): After grace and knowledge the body continues by prārabdha; the jīvanmukta acts as Śiva's servant among devotees, untouched by deeds.
- [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md): The initiate liberated by nirvāṇa-dīkṣā is released at death; the body continues until prārabdha is exhausted.
