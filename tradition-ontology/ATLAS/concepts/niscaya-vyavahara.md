# The ultimate and conventional standpoints (niścaya and vyavahāra)

## Definitions
- [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md): Kundakunda's pair: the ultimate (pure) standpoint sees the soul as unbound, one and made of knowledge; the conventional describes it through its relations with karma, body and practice; the conventional is needed to teach the ultimate but is not its real meaning (Samayasāra 8, 11, 14); the wise disciple understands both and becomes impartial (Puruṣārthasiddhyupāya 8).
- [Adhyātma movement (Adhyātma-mata)](../lineages/adhyatma-jain.md): For the Adhyātma movement the ultimate standpoint and self-experience are the heart of the path; one-sided adherence to either standpoint is a wrong view (niścayābhāsa, vyavahārābhāsa) (Mokṣamārga Prakāśaka; Ardhakathānaka).
- [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md): Haribhadra: in the ultimate sense yoga is the three jewels in the soul, in the conventional sense their causes such as serving the teacher (Yogaśataka 2–4); Yaśovijaya joins knowledge-yoga and action-yoga.
- [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md): The standpoint of ultimate reality (niścaya), the 'real', shows the soul as pure knower beyond all stages and distinctions; the conventional standpoint (vyavahāra), the 'unreal', speaks of knowledge, faith, conduct, bondage and stages and is indispensable for teaching those at the lower stage, as one must speak to a foreigner in his language.
- [Terāpantha (Digambara)](../lineages/terapantha-digambara.md): Ṭoḍarmal warns against both one-sided errors — clinging to niścaya talk while neglecting conduct, and clinging to vyavahāra rites without knowing the self.
