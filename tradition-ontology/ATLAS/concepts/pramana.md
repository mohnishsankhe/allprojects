## Definitions
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): Sāṃkhya accepts three: perception (determination concerning each object), inference (threefold; from general correlation for things beyond the senses) and reliable testimony (for what neither establishes) (SK 4–6); other proposed means are included in these (SK 4; Vācaspati). The Sāṃkhya Sūtra defines perception as cognition that, in contact, reflects its object (1.89).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Yoga accepts three means of valid cognition — perception, inference and testimony (YS 1.7) — defined in YBh 1.7; the insight of samādhi has the particular as object, beyond testimony and inference (1.49), and the perception of nirvitarkā samāpatti is the 'highest perception' (YBh 1.42).
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): Six means, valid for the empirical and following Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā; all within the sphere of ignorance; scripture alone reveals Brahman; perception grasps being, not difference.
- [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md): Three: perception, inference, scripture; all have qualified objects; the Lord is known from scripture alone.
