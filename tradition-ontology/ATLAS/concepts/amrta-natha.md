## Definitions
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): Nectar rains from the moon at the palate and is consumed by the sun; the locks and the reversed practice save it; the body filled with it does not decay (GŚ 36, 55–66); in the sky-circle is an upside-down well of nectar that only the guru-ful drink (Gorakh Bānī).
- [Yoginī Kaula (Yoginīkaula) of Matsyendra](../lineages/yogini-kaula.md): Contemplated as white, cool, fragrant nectar pouring from the sky, it conquers death and old age (KJN 5.6–11); without nectar no immortality (14.94).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): In the haṭha texts the nectar dripping from the moon in the head is saved from the sun's fire by jālandhara, viparītakaraṇī and khecarī (HYP 3.70–79; DYŚ 126–128) and drunk by the yogin with the tongue turned back; the body filled with it is not abandoned by the soul (HYP 3.46; GŚ 66); its 'liquor' (amaravāruṇī) is the true meaning of transgressive drinking (HYP 3.47–49).
