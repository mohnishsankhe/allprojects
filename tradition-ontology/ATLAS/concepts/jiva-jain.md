## Definitions
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): The soul is an eternal, formless, conscious substance marked by cognitive activity (upayoga); agent and enjoyer of its karma; co-extensive with its body, contracting and expanding like a lamp's light; souls are innumerable and each distinct; worldly or liberated (TS 2.8–10, 5.3, 5.16; Dravyasaṃgraha 2).
- [Adhyātma movement (Adhyātma-mata)](../lineages/adhyatma-jain.md): By the ultimate standpoint the soul is the pure knower, not the doer of material karma; 'the soul is other, matter is other' is the whole of reality (Iṣṭopadeśa 50; Samayasāra 38).
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): The Ācārāṅga's starting point: one who knows 'my soul is reborn' believes in soul, world, karma and action; the soul is eternal as substance and non-eternal as modes; the self is the doer and undoer of its own happiness and suffering, its own friend and foe.

## Relations (interpretation layer)
- contrasts-with → [Puruṣa (the conscious self)](purusa.md): Unlike the Sāṃkhya-Yoga puruṣa, the Jain soul is an agent, changes in its modes and is body-sized. — rests on [5.16](../texts/tattvartha-sutra.md#tea-tattvartha-sutra-5-16), [2](../texts/dravyasangraha.md#tea-dravyasangraha-2)
