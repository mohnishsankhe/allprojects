# The three malas (āṇava, karma, māyā)

**Category:** obstacles
**Members:** āṇava, kārma, māyīya

## Names
- [Pañcācārya (Pañcapīṭha) tradition](../lineages/pancacarya.md): malatraya

## Definitions
- [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md): Āṇava (innate, one, beginningless), kārma (the stream of deeds) and māyīya (the bond of māyā's products); each a real substance.
- [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md): Mummalam: āṇavam, kaṉmam, māyai — eternal; in release their power is quelled, not their existence ended.
- [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md): Āṇava: loss of freedom or awareness; māyīya: the appearance of separate knowables; kārma: agency in an unconscious agent; all through the power of māyā (ĪPK 3.2.4-5).
- [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md): The root impurity is ignorance, not a substance; knowledge alone liberates (TĀ 1.22-26).
- [Pañcācārya (Pañcapīṭha) tradition](../lineages/pancacarya.md): Śiva is free of the three impurities; the soul is atomic through impurity and is freed when the bond of impurity is shaken off.
