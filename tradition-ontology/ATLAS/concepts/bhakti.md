## Definitions
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Dvaita: firm love for Viṣṇu, greater than love for anything else, preceded by knowledge of his greatness; the sole means of liberation to which gifts, pilgrimage, austerity and sacrifice are subsidiary; it continues in liberation as eternal bliss.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Devotion to the Lord: accepting a leaf or flower offered with love (BhG 9.26), bearing the devotee's welfare (9.22), making even the wicked righteous (9.30), the means by which he is seen and entered (11.54), preferred over worship of the unmanifest as easier (12.2–5), attained in its supreme form by one who has become Brahman (18.54).
- [Early Bhāgavata devotion (the Nārāyaṇīya / Sātvata–Ekāntika dharma)](../lineages/bhagavata-early.md): Exclusive devotion (ekānta-bhakti) to Nārāyaṇa, the highest dharma, by which the ekāntins enter Vāsudeva directly (MBh 12.336.3–4, 12.332.18).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Lineage contribution (Upaniṣadic): 'to one who has the highest devotion to God and to his teacher as to God, these matters shine forth' (ŚU 6.23), the earliest Upaniṣadic use of the word in this sense; prayers and refuge in ŚU 3-6.
- [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md): Love, longing and a melting heart for Tirumāl, given by his grace, lived as service, refuge and praise at his temples; Tamil pattu/aṉpu.
- [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md): Supreme love of the nature of nectar, higher than action, knowledge and yoga, being the fruit itself; primary (one, though elevenfold) and secondary (by guṇas or motive).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: The one who has become Brahman attains supreme devotion to the Lord (18.54); by devotion he knows the Lord in truth and then enters him (18.55); 'be my devotee, sacrifice to me, bow to me; you will come to me' (18.65); the teaching is withheld from one without devotion (18.67) and declared among devotees with supreme devotion (18.68).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: The noun bhakti does not occur, but Arjuna is taught as the Lord's devotee (bhakta) and friend (4.3); the Lord responds to people in the way they resort to him (4.11); many absorbed in him and taking refuge in him reached his state (4.10); the meditator sits with thought on him and intent on him (6.14); the yogin established in oneness worships him as abiding in all beings (6.31); and of all yogins the one who worships him with faith, inner self gone to him, is the most yoked (6.47).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Knowing the Lord as the origin of all, the wise worship him, their thought on him, awakening one another and speaking of him, content and delighting (10.8–9); by undivided devotion alone he can be known, seen and entered in truth (11.54); whoever does his work, holds him supreme, is devoted, unattached and without enmity toward any being comes to him (11.55); those who fix their mind on him and worship him with supreme faith are 'the most yoked' (12.2); the devotee dear to him bears the marks of 12.13–19, and those who follow the teaching with faith are exceedingly dear (12.20).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: unswerving devotion to Kṛṣṇa by a yoga with no other is counted among the dispositions called knowledge (13.11); his devotee, knowing field, knowledge and knowable, becomes fit for his state (13.19); one who serves him with the unswerving yoga of devotion goes beyond the guṇas and becomes fit for becoming brahman (14.26); one who knows him as puruṣottama worships him with his whole being (15.19).
