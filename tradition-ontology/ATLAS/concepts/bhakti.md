# Devotion (bhakti)

## Names

## Definitions
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Dvaita: firm love for Viṣṇu, greater than love for anything else, preceded by knowledge of his greatness; the sole means of liberation to which gifts, pilgrimage, austerity and sacrifice are subsidiary; it continues in liberation as eternal bliss.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Devotion to the Lord: accepting a leaf or flower offered with love (BhG 9.26), bearing the devotee's welfare (9.22), making even the wicked righteous (9.30), the means by which he is seen and entered (11.54), preferred over worship of the unmanifest as easier (12.2–5), attained in its supreme form by one who has become Brahman (18.54).
- [Early Bhāgavata devotion (the Nārāyaṇīya / Sātvata–Ekāntika dharma)](../lineages/bhagavata-early.md): Exclusive devotion (ekānta-bhakti) to Nārāyaṇa, the highest dharma, by which the ekāntins enter Vāsudeva directly (MBh 12.336.3–4, 12.332.18).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Lineage contribution (Upaniṣadic): 'to one who has the highest devotion to God and to his teacher as to God, these matters shine forth' (ŚU 6.23), the earliest Upaniṣadic use of the word in this sense; prayers and refuge in ŚU 3-6.
- [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md): Love, longing and a melting heart for Tirumāl, given by his grace, lived as service, refuge and praise at his temples; Tamil pattu/aṉpu.
- [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md): Supreme love of the nature of nectar, higher than action, knowledge and yoga, being the fruit itself; primary (one, though elevenfold) and secondary (by guṇas or motive).
