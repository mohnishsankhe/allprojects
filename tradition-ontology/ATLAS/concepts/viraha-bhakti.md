## Definitions
- [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md): Separation (vipralambha) intensifies love; Caitanya's last years and Śikṣāṣṭaka 7–8.
- [Puṣṭimārga (Vallabha Sampradāya; Śuddhādvaita Brahmavāda)](../lineages/pustimarga.md): Love in separation is the highest state; renunciation is fitting only when viraha makes home impossible.
- [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md): The love-sick girl who does not sleep, bails out her tears, joins her palms crying 'conch and discus!' (TVM 7.2.1); Āṇṭāḷ begging for his garment or garland to cool her burning; the grieving father Daśaratha; ended at last in union — Nammāḻvār's longing 'ended' in release.
- [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md): Supreme anguish at forgetting the Lord is Nārada's own definition of devotion; supreme separation is the last of love's eleven forms.
- `lin:jagannatha`: Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa's mutual longing in separation, carried by the friend, fills most of the Gīta Govinda before union.
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The gopīs' longing when Kṛṣṇa vanishes or departs, which Kṛṣṇa says he causes so that their minds dwell on him; Uddhava wishes to be the dust of their feet.
- [Mīrābāī's devotion (the Mīrā song-tradition)](../lineages/mirabai.md): The madness and wound of love that only the Lord as physician can heal.
- [Unaffiliated regional bhakti poets (classificatory grouping)](../lineages/regional-bhakti-poets.md): Rādhā's laments in the rains and at the flute's call (Vidyāpati, Caṇḍīdās).
- [Rādhāvallabha sampradāya](../lineages/radhavallabha.md): Not real in the eternal union; felt only within it.
- [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md): Separation is the sultan; the one who has it wakes and weeps while the world eats and sleeps; the arrow of separation is the Beloved's gift.
- [Bāul](../lineages/baul.md): Longing for the Man of the Heart who comes near and cannot be held.
