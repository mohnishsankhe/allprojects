## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The perishable (all beings), the imperishable (the unchanging) and the highest person (Puruṣottama) beyond both, who sustains the three worlds (BhG 15.16–18).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: There are two persons in the world, the perishable — all beings — and the imperishable, called the one standing firm (kūṭastha) (15.16); the highest person is another, called the supreme self, the imperishable lord who enters and sustains the three worlds (15.17); since the Lord is beyond the perishable and higher than the imperishable he is celebrated as puruṣottama (15.18); whoever knows him thus worships him with his whole being (15.19). The commentators divide on what the imperishable is.
