## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Prāṇa, apāna, samāna, udāna, vyāna and the five minor — nāga (belching), kūrma (blinking), kṛkara (sneezing, hunger), devadatta (yawning, sleep), dhanañjaya (remains after death); each has a seat and function.
- [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md): Prāṇa, apāna, vyāna, udāna, samāna, nāga, kūrma, kṛkara, devadatta and dhanañjaya are the goddesses of the outer ten triangles of the Śrīcakra (Bhāvanā Upaniṣad).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): Five principal breaths (prāṇa in the heart, apāna at the anus, samāna at the navel, udāna in the throat, vyāna throughout) and five minor ones (nāga, kūrma, kṛkara, devadatta, dhanañjaya) causing belching, blinking, hunger, yawning and hiccup (SS 3.4–8); they move in the channels as the living soul (GŚ 24–25).
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): Ten winds among the ninety-six: pirāṇaṉ, apāṉaṉ, viyāṉaṉ, utāṉaṉ, camāṉaṉ, nākaṉ, kūrmaṉ, kirukaraṉ, tēvatattaṉ, taṉañcayaṉ; in medicine they are the ten forms of the vātam humour.
- [Siddha medicine (cittā maruttuvam)](../lineages/siddha-medicine.md): Siddha medicine treats the ten vāyus as the subdivisions of vaḷi (vātam).
