## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The world-tree with roots above and branches below, whose leaves are the Vedic hymns, nourished by the guṇas, binding through action, to be cut with the axe of non-attachment (BhG 15.1–4); the Anugītā's tree of Brahman (14.47).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 15.1–5: 'they speak of' an imperishable aśvattha with roots above and branches below, its leaves the metres; knowing it one knows the Veda; its branches spread below and above, swollen by the guṇas, with sense-objects as shoots, and its roots below are bound up with action in the human world; its form, end, beginning and foundation are not perceived here; having cut it with the strong weapon of non-attachment one is to seek the place of no return, taking refuge in the primal person from whom the ancient activity flowed; the undeluded, free of pride, delusion, attachment and desire, reach that imperishable place. (The verses do not themselves name the tree saṃsāra.)
