## Definitions
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): The moon at the palate rains nectar, the sun at the navel devours it; the three channels have moon, sun and fire as deities; the reversed practice turns sun above and moon below (GŚ 23, 55–59; AmŚ 71).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The moon in the head (or at the palate) rains nectar; the sun/fire at the navel swallows it, and so the body ages and dies (HYP 3.77; GŚ 55–58; SS 2.6–12; Amṛtasiddhi); iḍā is the moon and left, piṅgalā the sun and right; sun and moon create time, which suṣumnā devours (HYP 4.17); haṭha is the union of ha (sun) and ṭha (moon) (Yogabīja; Jyotsnā). The Śiva Svarodaya reads the alternation of the nostrils as the flow of moon and sun.
