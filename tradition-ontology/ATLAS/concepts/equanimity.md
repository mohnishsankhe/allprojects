# Evenness of mind (samatva)

`cpt:equanimity` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Category:** consciousness-states

## Names
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): samadarśana / sāmya

## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Evenness in success and failure is yoga (BhG 2.48); the wise see the same in all (5.18) and 'even here' conquer creation (5.19); the yogin sees the self in all (6.29) and others' pleasure and pain as his own (6.32); Jaigīṣavya is unmoved by praise and blame (MBh 12.222).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Being the same in success and failure (4.22); neither hating nor craving (5.3); seeing a learned brahmin, cow, elephant, dog and dog-eater with an equal eye (5.18); the mind established in sameness, as Brahman is the same (5.19); neither rejoicing at the pleasant nor agitated by the unpleasant (5.20); composure amid cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour (6.7); clod, stone and gold alike (6.8); equal regard toward friend, foe, neutral, good and wicked (6.9); seeing the same everywhere (6.29), by comparison with oneself (6.32). Arjuna calls the yoga taught 'yoga as sāmya' (6.33).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Evenness (samatā) is among the states that arise from the Lord (10.5); the worshippers of the imperishable are even-minded everywhere (12.4); the devotee dear to the Lord is the same in pain and pleasure (12.13), to foe and friend, in honour and dishonour, cold and heat, and holds blame and praise equal (12.18–19).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: Constant evenness of thought (sama-cittatva) when the wanted or unwanted befalls is among the dispositions called knowledge (13.10); one who sees the supreme lord standing the same (samam) in all beings sees, and seeing him equally present everywhere does not harm himself and goes to the highest goal (13.28–29); the one gone beyond the guṇas is the same in pain and pleasure, toward clod, stone and gold, the pleasant and the unpleasant, blame and praise, honour and dishonour, friend and foe (14.24–25).
