# Evenness of mind (samatva)

## Names

## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Evenness in success and failure is yoga (BhG 2.48); the wise see the same in all (5.18) and 'even here' conquer creation (5.19); the yogin sees the self in all (6.29) and others' pleasure and pain as his own (6.32); Jaigīṣavya is unmoved by praise and blame (MBh 12.222).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Being the same in success and failure (4.22); neither hating nor craving (5.3); seeing a learned brahmin, cow, elephant, dog and dog-eater with an equal eye (5.18); the mind established in sameness, as Brahman is the same (5.19); neither rejoicing at the pleasant nor agitated by the unpleasant (5.20); composure amid cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour (6.7); clod, stone and gold alike (6.8); equal regard toward friend, foe, neutral, good and wicked (6.9); seeing the same everywhere (6.29), by comparison with oneself (6.32). Arjuna calls the yoga taught 'yoga as sāmya' (6.33).
