## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: The devotee dear to the Lord hates no being, is friendly and compassionate, without 'mine' and without the sense of 'I', the same in pain and pleasure, forbearing, ever content, a yogin, self-restrained, firm in resolve, with mind and understanding offered to the Lord (12.13–14); neither disturbs the world nor is disturbed by it, free of elation, intolerance, fear and agitation (12.15); without expectation, pure, capable, impartial, rid of distress, relinquishing every undertaking (12.16); neither rejoices nor hates, grieves nor craves, having given up good and bad (12.17); the same toward enemy and friend, in honour and dishonour, cold and heat, pleasure and pain, free of attachment, the same in blame and praise, silent, content with whatever comes, homeless, steady in judgement (12.18–19). Each group ends with 'he is dear to me'; those who with faith follow this 'nectar of dharma' are exceedingly dear (12.20).
- [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md): Humility (the Lord dislikes pride), non-violence, truth, purity, compassion and faith; no disputation; no attention to talk of lust, wealth, unbelievers and enemies; all conduct offered to the Lord; among devotees no distinction of caste, learning, beauty, family, wealth or occupation; the one-pointed are chief and purify the world.
- [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md): Having nothing of one's own and relying on the Lord alone (ākiñcanya); being the servant of the Lord's servants; the observances of the vow — no harsh words, no evil tales, alms and help.

Several marks recur in the Gītā's other portraits: nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ (2.71), na dveṣṭi (2.57, 5.3), na śocati na kāṅkṣati (18.54), mānāpamāna / mitrāri / sarvārambhaparityāgī / tulyanindā… (14.24–25), śītoṣṇasukhaduḥkheṣu tathā mānāpamānayoḥ (6.7).
