## Definitions
- [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md): The Goddess's tantra alone accomplishes all the aims of life (SL 31); her worshipper enjoys Sarasvatī and Lakṣmī and, freed of bonds, tastes the supreme Brahman (SL 99); saubhāgya names this double blessing.
- [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md): The Mantramārga distinguishes the initiate who seeks powers and enjoyments in higher worlds (bubhukṣu, the sādhaka) from the one who seeks liberation only (mumukṣu); both are legitimate and receive different initiations.
- [Kaula (the Kula tradition)](../lineages/kaula.md): In the Kula way enjoyment itself becomes yoga: the yogin is not an enjoyer and the enjoyer does not know yoga, but the Kaula is both.
- [Vajrayāna (Mantranaya) in India](../lineages/vajrayana.md): Common attainments (the eight great siddhis, the six rites) and the supreme attainment (mahāmudrā, buddhahood) are both fruits of mantra practice; the common are to be sought for the sake of beings.
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): Mantra rites for protection and worldly ends are practised under devotion to the Jina, while liberation comes only from the three jewels and the destruction of karma.
- [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md): Śiva's scriptures in all four pādas are means to enjoyment (powers, higher worlds) and to liberation; initiates are bubhukṣu or mumukṣu accordingly.
