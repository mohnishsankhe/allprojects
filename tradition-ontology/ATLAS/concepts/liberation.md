## Definitions
- [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md): Release comes by the exhaustion of karma: avoiding desire-prompted and prohibited acts, performing obligatory and occasional ones, knowing the self, and exhausting past karma by experience, so that no new body arises; release is an absence, hence permanent, not an enjoyment of pleasure (Kumārila); the absolute dissolution of the threefold bond, without bliss (Pārthasārathi); some Bhāṭṭas held that innate bliss manifests.
- [Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/prabhakara-mimamsa.md): The absolute end of embodiment through complete exhaustion of dharma and adharma, reached by abstention, experience and self-knowledge with calm and restraint; the self remains in mere being.
- [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md): The sūtra and Śabara speak of heaven as the fruit of rites; release as a goal is added by the later teachers; later Bhāṭṭas teach dharma offered to Īśvara as the cause of the highest good.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Chapters 1–3 name the goal in several ways without systematising them: fitness for immortality (amṛtatva, 2.15); freedom from the bondage of birth and going to the state free from ill (padam anāmayam, 2.51); brahma-nirvāṇa (2.72); attaining the highest (param, 3.19); perfection (saṃsiddhi, 3.20); release from actions (3.31).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): In the Gītā: brahmanirvāṇa (2.72, 5.24–26), non-return (8.15–16), entering the Lord after knowing him by devotion (18.55), freedom from birth, death and old age beyond the guṇas (14.20); in the Mokṣadharma: the twenty-fifth becoming alone (kevala) on awakening and attaining the state of the twenty-sixth (12.294, 12.296).
- [Early Bhāgavata devotion (the Nārāyaṇīya / Sātvata–Ekāntika dharma)](../lineages/bhagavata-early.md): Entry through the sun and the vyūhas into Vāsudeva, the ekāntins entering him directly (MBh 12.332.13–18).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7–9: those who take refuge in the Lord and strive for release (mokṣa) from old age and death know Brahman, the adhyātma and action completely (7.29); the great souls who come to him, having reached the highest perfection, do not come to rebirth, whereas all worlds up to Brahmā's are subject to return (8.15–16); the imperishable unmanifest, the supreme goal and the Lord's supreme abode, is that from which those who reach it do not return (8.21); departing by the bright course, knowers of Brahman go to Brahman — the course of non-return (anāvṛtti) (8.23–24, 8.26); offering every act to the Lord, one is freed (vimukta) from the bonds of action and comes to him (9.28).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: chs. 4–6 name the goal in several ways: not being born again and coming to the Lord (4.9); release from the inauspicious (4.16); going to the eternal Brahman (4.31, 5.6); supreme peace (4.39, 5.12, 5.29); non-return (apunarāvṛtti, 5.17); brahma-nirvāṇa (5.24–26); being forever free (sadā mukta) while intent on mokṣa (5.28); peace culminating in nirvāṇa (6.15); the highest course (parā gati, 6.45).
