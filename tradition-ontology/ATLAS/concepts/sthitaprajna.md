## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Asked for the marks of one of steady wisdom established in samādhi (2.54), Kṛṣṇa describes one who gives up all desires and is content in the self by the self (2.55); is unshaken by sorrow, without craving in pleasure, free from passion, fear and anger (2.56); unattached, neither rejoicing nor hating (2.57); withdraws the senses like a tortoise (2.58), the taste itself ceasing on seeing the highest (2.59); sits yoked, intent on Kṛṣṇa, senses mastered (2.61, 2.68); moves among objects with mastered senses and attains serenity (2.64–65); is awake where beings sleep (2.69); is filled by desires like the ocean yet unmoved (2.70); without 'mine' and 'I' attains peace (2.71). This is the brahmic state, reached which even at death one attains brahma-nirvāṇa (2.72).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): One who has given up the desires of the mind and is content in the self, unagitated in sorrow and pleasure, senses withdrawn, awake where beings sleep, unmoved as the ocean, in the brāhmī state (BhG 2.54–72); with close parallels in the devotee dear to the Lord (12.13–20) and the one beyond the guṇas (14.22–25).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 5.20 describes the knower of Brahman in the same terms as the steady-wise of 2.54–72: not elated by the pleasant nor agitated by the unpleasant, steady in intellect (sthira-buddhi), undeluded, established in Brahman.
