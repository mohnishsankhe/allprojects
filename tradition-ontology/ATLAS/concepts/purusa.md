## Definitions
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): The conscious principle, neither producer nor product (SK 3), the opposite of the guṇa-made (SK 11): witness, isolated, neutral, seer, non-agent (SK 19), unattached and by nature ever free (SS 1.15–19), light itself, without consciousness as a property (SS 1.145–146). Its existence is proved because aggregates are for another, because something must stand opposite the guṇas, because of control, experience and the striving for isolation (SK 17). It is many (SK 18). It is not truly bound or released (SK 62).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): The puruṣa is seeing alone, pure, yet conforming to cognitions (2.20); unchanging, it always knows the mind's activities (4.18); consciousness that does not pass over into objects (4.22); utterly unlike sattva (3.35); neither the same as the buddhi nor entirely different (YBh 2.20); pure, alone and liberated when established in itself (YBh 1.51). Knowledge of the puruṣa comes from saṃyama on what exists for itself (3.35).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Lineage contribution (Upaniṣadic): the self in the form of a person at the beginning (BAU 1.4.1); the cosmic person with a thousand heads (ŚU 3.14); the highest principle beyond the unmanifest (KU 1.3.11); the divine formless person (MuU 2.1.2); the person in the eye (ChU 4.15.1).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: Beginningless like prakṛti (13.20), puruṣa is the cause in experiencing pleasure and pain (13.21); abiding in prakṛti it experiences the guṇas, and attachment to them is the cause of its births in good and bad wombs (13.22). The highest person in this body is onlooker, consenter, sustainer, experiencer, great lord, and is called the supreme self (13.23); whoever knows puruṣa and prakṛti with the guṇas thus is not born again (13.24). Chapter 15 speaks of two persons, perishable and imperishable, and the highest person beyond both (15.16-15.18).
