## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: The self neither slays nor is slain, and the one who knows it cannot be said to slay or cause to slay (2.19–21); actions are done by the guṇas of prakṛti, and only one deluded by the sense of 'I' thinks 'I am the doer', while the knower of the truth sees that the guṇas move among the guṇas and is not attached (3.27–28).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: having abandoned attachment to the fruit, though fully engaged in action one does nothing at all (4.20); the yoked knower of reality thinks 'I do nothing at all' while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping, breathing, speaking, releasing, grasping and opening and closing the eyes, holding that the senses move among the objects of the senses (5.8–9); having renounced all actions with the mind, the embodied one dwells at ease in the city of nine gates, neither acting nor causing action (5.13); the master (prabhu) creates neither agency nor actions nor the link of action with its fruit — inherent nature (svabhāva) is at work — and the pervading, mighty one (vibhu) takes on no one's sin or good deed (5.14–15). Whether prabhu and vibhu name the embodied self or the Lord is disputed by the commentators.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: prakṛti is the cause in agency (13.21); one who sees actions done by prakṛti alone and his self as not the doer sees (13.30); the supreme self, beginningless and without guṇas, though in the body neither acts nor is tainted (13.32), as space is not tainted (13.33); the seer who beholds no agent other than the guṇas and knows what is higher than them attains Kṛṣṇa's state (14.19).
