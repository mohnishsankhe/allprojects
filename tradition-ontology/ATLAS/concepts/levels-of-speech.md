## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Speech sprouts in parā, leafs in paśyantī, buds in madhyamā and blossoms in vaikharī; reversed, sound dissolves (Yogakuṇḍalī 3.18–19); from the power at the base arise all letters, mantras, scriptures and the seven notes (Yogaśikhā 3).
- [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md): Parā (supreme speech = reflexive awareness itself, ĪPK 1.5.13), paśyantī, madhyamā and vaikharī; the Kashmir Śaivas add parā above the grammarians' three and deny that paśyantī is ultimate (Śivadṛṣṭi 2.1).
- [Kaula Śrīvidyā (the Vāmakeśvara–Yoginīhṛdaya exegetical line)](../lineages/kaula-srividya.md): The Yoginīhṛdaya's question-and-answer is Śiva himself, by his vimarśa aspect, descending through paśyantī to vaikharī (Dīpikā on YH 1.1).
- [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md): Bhartṛhari names three — vaikharī (articulated), madhyamā (mental, sequential), paśyantī (undivided, sequence withdrawn) — and calls the Veda their supreme station (VP 1.142 with Vṛtti); Nāgeśa adds parā and locates the four in the root centre, navel, heart and throat.
- [Mantraśāstra (the science of mantras: the smārta-tantric mantra manuals)](../lineages/mantrasastra.md): Four levels — parā, paśyantī, madhyamā, vaikharī — as the stages by which śabda-brahman, dwelling as kuṇḍalinī, becomes the letters (ŚT 1.14).
- [Saṅgītaśāstra (the science of music: song, instrument and dance)](../lineages/sangita.md): Nāda rises from the knot of Brahmā through navel, heart, throat and head to the mouth and has five names by these places (ŚR 1.3.3–5; Bṛhaddeśī 23–25).
