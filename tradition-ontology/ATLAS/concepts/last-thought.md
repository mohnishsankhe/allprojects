## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Lineage contribution (Upaniṣadic): 'as his resolve is in this world, so he becomes on departing' (ChU 3.14.1); 'with whatever thought one has, one enters breath' (PrU 3.10); 'mind, remember what was done' (Īśa 17); meditation on Om until death determines one's world (PrU 5); the refuge in three sayings at death (ChU 3.17.6).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): What one remembers at death shapes what follows: Bharata dying absorbed in a fawn became a deer; Ajāmila calling 'Nārāyaṇa' was rescued; remembrance of Nārāyaṇa at the end is the highest gain of life (BhP 2.1.6); the dying should meditate on the Lord (12.3.50); the yogin whose karma remains should recall yoga at departure (MkP 42.16-17); Kāśī tradition speaks of Śiva's instruction at death.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): What one thinks at death determines what one becomes (Yogaśikhā 1.31; Nāradaparivrājaka 5).
- [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md): Remembering or uttering the name at death saves even a sinner (Ajāmila); Haridāsa died with the name and Caitanya's form before him.
- [Puṣṭimārga (Vallabha Sampradāya; Śuddhādvaita Brahmavāda)](../lineages/pustimarga.md): Tradition's account: Vallabha took renunciation at the end of life, left a final instruction (Śikṣāślokī) and entered the Ganges at Kāśī.
- [Dvaitādvaita (Nimbārka Sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaitadvaita.md): Liberation after death: the devotee attains the Lord's nature by his grace (Daśaślokī 2, 9).
