## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The unstruck sound heard within when breath and mind are stilled; listened to in stages from loud to subtle it absorbs the mind 'like milk in water'; its end is the soundless Brahman and the state beyond mind; in the Yogaśikhā nāda is bindu and bindu is mind, and no mantra is higher than nāda.
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The inner (unstruck) sound heard in the purified suṣumnā; the HYP makes attention to it the chief of the layas (4.66), maps its sounds onto the four stages (4.69–77) and says whatever is heard as nāda is śakti and the soundless is the supreme Brahman (4.101–102); Śiva is 'of the nature of nāda, bindu and kalā' (4.1).
- [Unaffiliated regional bhakti poets (classificatory grouping)](../lineages/regional-bhakti-poets.md): Śiva is embodied in sound; the seven notes arise from his five faces; music joined to devotion is worship (Tyāgarāja).
