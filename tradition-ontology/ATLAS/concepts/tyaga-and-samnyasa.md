## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Saṃnyāsa is laying aside desire-prompted actions, tyāga relinquishing the fruit of all actions (BhG 18.2); obligatory acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity are not to be abandoned (18.5); relinquishment is threefold by the guṇas (18.7–9); the true renouncer is the one who acts without depending on the fruit (6.1).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: Arjuna asks the truth of each (18.1). Saṃnyāsa is laying aside desire-prompted acts, tyāga giving up the fruit of all acts (18.2). Some say action is to be abandoned as faulty, others that sacrifice, giving and austerity are not (18.3); Kṛṣṇa's settled view: these are to be done, giving up attachment and fruit (18.4–6). Relinquishment is threefold: abandoning allotted action from delusion is tāmasic (18.7), from fear of hardship rājasic (18.8), doing it as what ought to be done without attachment or fruit sāttvic (18.9). The relinquisher neither hates nor clings (18.10); the embodied cannot give up action entirely, so the relinquisher of fruit is the tyāgin (18.11); the fruit of action accrues after death to non-relinquishers, not to renouncers (18.12). Through renunciation one attains the supreme perfection of freedom from action (18.49).
