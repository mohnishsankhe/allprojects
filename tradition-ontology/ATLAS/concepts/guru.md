## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The guru removes darkness ('gu' darkness, 'ru' its remover) and is the supreme Brahman (Advayatāraka); he is Hari (Brahmavidyā), Brahmā-Viṣṇu-Sadāśiva (Yogaśikhā 5); his word breaks the pot that hides the lamp; without his grace the natural state is hard to reach (Varāha 2.76).
- [Vārkarī sampradāya](../lineages/varkari.md): Without a guru even Viṭṭhala's companion Nāmdev was 'unbaked'; the guru (Nivṛtti, Visobā, Janārdana) shows God everywhere; the saint is mother (māulī).
- [Rāmānandī sampradāya](../lineages/ramanandi.md): The guru's foot-dust cleans the mirror of the mind; the true guru is the helmsman of the human boat.
- [Rāmdāsī sampradāya (Samartha sampradāya)](../lineages/ramdasi.md): The Dāsbodh sets out the marks of the true guru and disciple.
- [Ekaśaraṇa Dharma](../lineages/ekasarana.md): The guru is one of the four real things, with the name, God and the devotees.
