## Definitions
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): In the Kārikā: viśva (waking-station, outward-knowing, enjoyer of the gross, right eye), taijasa (inward, enjoyer of the separated (pravivikta), mind), prājña (mass of cognition, enjoyer of bliss, space of the heart) — one alone threefold; the fourth is not bound by cause or effect and has no seed-sleep, sleep or dream; it is reached when the two errors are exhausted.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The self has four quarters: vaiśvānara (waking, outward-knowing, enjoyer of the gross), taijasa (dream, inward-knowing, enjoyer of the separated), prājña (deep sleep, become one, mass of cognition, enjoyer of bliss) and the fourth, described by negation and as the stilling of the world, auspicious and non-dual.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): Waking (viśva), dream (taijasa) and deep sleep (prājña) come and go; the self as the fourth (turīya) pervades and transcends them, non-dual; analysis of the three states is a means to know the self.
