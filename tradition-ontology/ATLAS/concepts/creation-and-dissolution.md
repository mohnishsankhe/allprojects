## Definitions
- [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md): There is no proof of a first creation or of a dissolution destroying everything; beings arise and perish continually, 'as today' (ŚV sambandhākṣepaparihāra 66–68, 113).
- [Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/prabhakara-mimamsa.md): Things arise and perish gradually; people were always born of parents (Prakaraṇapañcikā ch. 7).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7–9: the Lord is the origin and dissolution of the whole world (7.6; 9.18); at the close of a kalpa beings go into his prakṛti and at its beginning he sends them forth again, taking hold of his prakṛti (9.7–8); prakṛti brings forth the moving and unmoving with him as overseer (9.10); manifest things arise from and dissolve into the unmanifest with Brahmā's day and night (8.18–19). These acts do not bind him (9.9).
