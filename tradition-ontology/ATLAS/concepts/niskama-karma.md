## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Action performed with a right only to the act and not to its fruit, without attachment, the same in success and failure, 'established in yoga' (2.47–48); yoked with understanding it leaves behind good and evil deeds and frees from the bondage of birth (2.50–51); undertaken with the organs of action by one whose senses are controlled by the mind (3.7); done for sacrifice (3.9), for the holding together of the world (3.20, 3.25), and finally resigned to Kṛṣṇa (3.30).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Acting as duty, with evenness, abandoning attachment and the fruit, offering the act to the Lord; action so done does not bind (BhG 2.47–48, 3.19, 4.20, 9.27–28, 18.6).
- [Early Bhāgavata devotion (the Nārāyaṇīya / Sātvata–Ekāntika dharma)](../lineages/bhagavata-early.md): Yudhiṣṭhira's dharma practised 'not for the fruit of dharma' (MBh 3.32.4).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: undertakings free of desire and intention (4.19); abandoning attachment to the fruit, ever content, dependent on nothing (4.20); acting for the sake of sacrifice, action dissolves (4.23); acting with actions placed in Brahman, abandoning attachment, one is unstained like a lotus leaf (5.10); yogins act for self-purification (5.11); the yoked one abandons the fruit and gains peace, the unyoked is bound by attachment to it (5.12); the true renunciant and yogin does the required action without depending on its fruit (6.1).
