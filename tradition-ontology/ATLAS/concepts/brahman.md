## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): In the Upaniṣads brahman is that from which beings arise, by which they live and into which they return (TU 3.1.1); being, one without a second (ChU 6.2.1); truth, knowledge, infinite (TU 2.1.1); understanding and bliss (BAU 3.9.28); plenitude (ChU 7.23-24); the imperishable (BAU 3.8.8; MuU 1.1.5). It has two forms, formed and formless (BAU 2.3.1), and is also given only by negation — 'not so, not so' (BAU 2.3.6).
- [Vedānta (Uttara Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/vedanta.md): The cause of the world's origin, sustenance and dissolution, known from scripture, the purport of all the Upaniṣads.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): The one non-dual reality, existence–consciousness–bliss, identical with the inner self; nirguṇa in itself, saguṇa (Īśvara) through māyā.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: Brahman's threefold designation is '[oṃ] tat sat', by which brāhmaṇas, Vedas and sacrifices were ordained (17.23); one who has attained perfection attains Brahman, the highest culmination of knowledge (18.50), becoming fit for it through the discipline of 18.51–53; the one become Brahman attains supreme devotion (18.54).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Brahman is every factor of the sacrifice and is what is to be reached (4.24); it is the fire into which sacrifice is offered (4.25); the eternal Brahman is reached by those who eat the remainder of sacrifice (4.31); the yoked sage soon reaches it (5.6); actions are placed in it (5.10); it is flawless and the same, so that those whose mind rests in sameness are established in it (5.19–20); the yogin becomes Brahman (brahma-bhūta) and attains brahma-nirvāṇa and contact with Brahman (5.24, 6.27–28).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Arjuna acknowledges Kṛṣṇa as the supreme brahman, the supreme abode, the supreme purifier, the eternal divine Person, as all the seers declare (10.12–13).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: the knowable is brahman, beginningless and supreme, neither sat nor asat (13.13); it has hands, feet, eyes and ears everywhere and stands enveloping all (13.14); appears through the sense-qualities yet is without senses, is unattached yet supports all, without guṇas yet their experiencer (13.15); is inside and outside beings, moving and unmoving, not to be known through subtlety, far and near (13.16); undivided yet as if divided, sustainer, devourer and generator of beings (13.17); light of lights beyond darkness, knowledge, knowable and reached by knowledge, set in the heart of all (13.18); seeing the many as resting in the one one attains brahman (13.31); by unswerving devotion one becomes fit for becoming brahman (14.26); Kṛṣṇa is the foundation of brahman (14.27).
