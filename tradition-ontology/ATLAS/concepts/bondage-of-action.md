## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Action binds: the world is bound by action except action done for sacrifice (3.9); one yoked with the understanding of yoga casts off this bondage (2.39), leaves behind good and evil deeds (2.50) and, abandoning the fruit, is freed from the bondage of birth (2.51); those who follow the teaching with faith are released from actions (3.31).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7–9: the Lord's acts of sending forth beings do not bind (nibadhnanti) him, seated as one indifferent and unattached to them (9.9); whatever one does, eats, offers, gives or practises as austerity, done as an offering to the Lord, frees one from the bonds of action (karma-bandhana) with their good and bad fruits, and, yoked in the yoga of renunciation, freed, one comes to him (9.27–28).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: whoever knows the Lord as unstained by actions is not bound by actions (4.14); the one content with what comes unsought, beyond the pairs of opposites, the same in success and failure, is not bound even when acting (4.22); actions do not bind the self-possessed one who has renounced actions by yoga and cut doubt by knowledge (4.41); the constant renouncer, free of the pairs, is easily released from bondage (5.3); the unyoked one, attached to the fruit through desire, is bound (5.12).
