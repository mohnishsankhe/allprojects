# Māyā in the principal Upaniṣads

`cpt:maya` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Category:** ultimate

## Names
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): māyā

## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The word appears as Indra's many-formed powers (BAU 2.5.19, quoting RV 6.47.18), as deceit (PrU 1.16), and in the Śvetāśvatara as the Lord's power: 'know prakṛti to be māyā and the great Lord the māyin' (4.10), from which the māyin projects the world and in which the other is confined (4.9); knowing him there is 'the cessation of all māyā' (1.10). Whether māyā here means the world's unreality is not stated by the texts (see dsp:world-real-or-appearance).
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): The Lord's beginningless indescribable power of three guṇas, inferred from its effects, by which Brahman appears as the world; neither being nor non-being; removable by knowledge.
- [Rāmānandī sampradāya](../lineages/ramanandi.md): 'I and mine, you and yours' is māyā; the world is a colourless painting on the void, neither simply true nor false; worship of Hari alone is real, the world a dream.
