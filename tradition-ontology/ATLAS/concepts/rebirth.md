## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7–9: all beings fall into bewilderment at birth through the delusion of the pairs (7.27); the knower takes refuge in the Lord at the end of many births (7.19); one goes to whatever state one remembers on leaving the body (8.6); rebirth is the impermanent abode of sorrow, and all worlds up to Brahmā's are subject to return, but those who come to the Lord are not reborn (8.15–16); the same multitude of beings comes to be again and again, helplessly, with Brahmā's day and night (8.19); of the two everlasting courses of the departed, the bright leads to non-return (anāvṛtti) and the dark to return (8.23–26); those without faith in this dharma return to the path of death and saṃsāra (9.3); Vedic sacrificers who reach heaven re-enter the world of mortals when their merit is spent — 'going and coming' (9.21).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The Upaniṣads describe return to this world: like grain a mortal is born again (KU 1.1.6); the path of the fathers returns through rain and food (ChU 5.10.3-6; BAU 6.2.16); the self takes another body as a caterpillar or goldsmith (BAU 4.4.3-4); the third birth (AU 2.1.4); tiny creatures that are born and die (ChU 5.10.8).
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): Beings pass away and reappear according to their deeds, seen with the divine eye (DN 2); conception requires the gandhabba (MN 38); the other world cannot be seen with the fleshly eye (DN 23); no self transmigrates — consciousness is dependently arisen (MN 38).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: The embodied one obtains another body as it passes through childhood, youth and old age (2.13); it casts off worn-out bodies and enters new ones as a man changes clothes (2.22); death is certain for the born and birth for the dead (2.27); freedom from the bondage of birth is gained by abandoning the fruit of action (2.51).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna have both passed through many births, which Kṛṣṇa knows and Arjuna does not (4.5); whoever knows the Lord's divine birth and action does not go to rebirth on leaving the body (4.9); the one fallen from yoga dwells in the worlds of the meritorious and is reborn, carrying the understanding and practice of his former body (6.41–44), perfected through many births (6.45).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: attachment to the guṇas causes puruṣa's births in good and bad wombs (13.22); one who knows puruṣa and prakṛti is not born again (13.24); dying in sattva, rajas or tamas leads to the stainless worlds, to birth among those attached to action, or to deluded wombs (14.14–15, 14.18); the living being takes the senses and mind with it from body to body as the wind carries scents (15.8).
- [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md): Bodies arise for the experience of the fruits of karma; while dharma and adharma remain, a new body arises; when they are exhausted it does not.
