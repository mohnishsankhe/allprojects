## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4.24–33 list the sacrifices offered by different practitioners, all born of action and all purifying: every element of the offering seen as Brahman; sacrifice to the gods; offering sacrifice by sacrifice into the fire of Brahman; offering the senses into the fires of restraint; offering sense objects into the fires of the senses; offering the activities of the senses and of breath into the fire of the yoga of self-restraint; sacrifice with material things, with austerity, with yoga, with study and with knowledge; offering prāṇa into apāna and apāna into prāṇa, with breath-regulation; offering the breaths into the breaths with regulated food. The sacrifice of knowledge is the best, since all action culminates in knowledge.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The Gītā keeps the wheel of sacrifice between gods and men (3.10–16) but widens sacrifice to austerity, yoga, study, sense-restraint, breath and knowledge (4.25–30), declares the sacrifice of knowledge highest (4.33) and sees offering, oblation, fire and offerer as Brahman (4.24); sacrifice is to be done without desire for fruit (17.11; 18.5); the Anugītā describes an inner sacrifice of the senses (14.20).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 9.15–16, 9.24, 9.26–27: some worship the Lord by the sacrifice of knowledge; he is the rite, the sacrifice, the ancestral offering, the herb, the mantra, the butter, the fire and the oblation; he is the enjoyer and lord of all sacrifices; a leaf, flower, fruit or water offered in devotion he accepts; every act, eating, oblation, gift and austerity is to be done as an offering to him.
