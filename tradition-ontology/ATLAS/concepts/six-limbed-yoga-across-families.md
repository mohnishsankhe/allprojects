## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Maitrī Upaniṣad: breath-control, withdrawal, meditation, concentration, reasoning (tarka) and absorption; the Amṛtanāda lists the same six in another order.
- [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md): The Śaiva Mantramārga teaches a six-limbed yoga with reasoning (tarka) as a limb, called the highest limb (Mālinīvijayottara, as quoted in the Tantrāloka — recalled); the Mṛgendra's yoga section gives breath-control, withdrawal, concentration, meditation and absorption with recitation (japa) and examination (ūha) of what is to be rejected and accepted.
- [Vajrayāna (Mantranaya) in India](../lineages/vajrayana.md): Guhyasamāja and Kālacakra: withdrawal, meditation, breath-control, retention, recollection (anusmṛti) and absorption — the completion-stage yoga of the Kālacakra.
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): Gorakṣaśataka and Vivekamārtaṇḍa: posture, breath-control, withdrawal, concentration, meditation, absorption (the yamas and niyamas not counted).
