# Pati, paśu, pāśa — the Lord, the bound soul, the bonds

## Definitions
- [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md): The three eternal, irreducible categories (padārtha): Śiva, the countless souls, and the bonds (mala, karma, māyā). None is created; souls are not parts or appearances of Śiva; bonds are real.
- [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md): Mupporuḷ ('the three realities') — pati, pacu, pācam; the Civañāṉa Pōtam's twelve sūtras prove and define each, then give the means and fruit.
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): All selves in saṃsāra are paśus; Śiva is their lord (Paśupati), who binds them with the noose of māyā and alone frees them; he himself is bondage, binder, noose and bound (Īśvara Gītā).
- [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md): The Kiraṇa teaches the knowledge of paśu, pāśa and pati; the soul is eternal, pervasive, ignorant and inactive, lodged within māyā and bound through kalā, vidyā, rāga, prakṛti and the instruments.
- [Śiva-viśiṣṭādvaita (Śaiva Vedānta of Śrīkaṇṭha)](../lineages/siva-visistadvaita.md): Śrīkaṇṭha uses the Śaiva triad within a Vedānta frame: Śiva the Lord, souls bound by the impurity, karma and māyā, freed by his grace to equality with him.

## Relations (interpretation layer)
- contrasts-with → [The thirty-six principles (ṣaṭtriṃśat-tattva)](thirty-six-tattvas.md): Kashmir non-dualism derives all categories from Śiva's consciousness; the Siddhānta keeps three
- opposes → [Liberation (mokṣa) in Advaita](liberation-advaita.md): the Siddhānta denies that soul and bonds are superimposed on one reality — rests on [parapakkam](../texts/sivananasiddhiyar.md#tea-sivananasiddhiyar-parapakkam)
