# Renunciation (saṃnyāsa) in the principal Upaniṣads

`cpt:sannyasa` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Category:** ethics

## Names
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): saṃnyāsa

## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Knowers of the self rise above the desires for sons, wealth and worlds and live as mendicants (BAU 3.5.1, 4.4.22); wanderers wander forth desiring the self as their world (4.4.22); ascetics purified by the yoga of renunciation are released (MuU 3.2.6); forest-dwellers living on alms go by the door of the sun (MuU 1.2.11); Śvetāśvatara teaches those beyond the stages of life (ŚU 6.21).
- [Saṃnyāsa — the renunciate ideal of the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads](../lineages/sannyasa.md): Leaving the ritual world and household to wander as a mendicant; in the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads true renunciation is the union of jīva and supreme Self (Maitreya 2.17), undertaken 'the very day one becomes dispassionate' (Jābāla 4); 'by renunciation alone some reached immortality' (Kaivalya 1.3).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Asked whether renunciation of actions or yoga is better (5.1), Kṛṣṇa says both bring the highest good but the yoga of action is superior (5.2); the constant renouncer is one who neither hates nor craves (5.3); Sāṃkhya and Yoga are one in result (5.4–5); renunciation is hard to attain without yoga (5.6); renouncing all actions by the mind the embodied one dwells at ease (5.13); the renouncer and yogin is one who does required action without depending on its fruit, not one without sacred fires or rites (6.1); what is called renunciation is yoga, for no one becomes a yogin without renouncing intention (6.2).
