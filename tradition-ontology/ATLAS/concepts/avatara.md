# The Lord's descent (avatāra / prādurbhāva)

`cpt:avatara` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Category:** ultimate

## Names
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): avatāra
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): divyaṃ janma karma ca (divine birth and action)

## Definitions
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Though unborn, the Lord comes into being by his own māyā whenever dharma declines, to protect the good, destroy evil-doers and establish dharma (BhG 4.6–8); knowing his divine birth frees one (4.9).
- [Early Bhāgavata devotion (the Nārāyaṇīya / Sātvata–Ekāntika dharma)](../lineages/bhagavata-early.md): Nārāyaṇa's successive manifestations: Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana, Rāma Bhārgava, Rāma Dāśarathi, the appearance at Mathurā; earlier the Swan and Hayaśiras (MBh 12.326.72–94).
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Dvaita: the avatāras are Viṣṇu himself manifesting his eternal body of consciousness and bliss, flawless and without internal difference; apparent human weakness is displayed to delude the unfit. One must also know which beings are merely indwelt (āveśa).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Though unborn, of imperishable self and lord of beings, Kṛṣṇa comes into being (saṃbhavāmi) through his own māyā, presiding over his own prakṛti (4.6); whenever dharma declines and adharma rises he sends himself forth (4.7), age after age, to protect the good, destroy evil-doers and establish dharma (4.8); he has had many births, all of which he knows (4.5); whoever knows his birth and action as divine is not reborn but comes to him (4.9). The word avatāra itself does not occur.
- [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md): The Lord's descents are real, taken by his will in bodies of pure sattva, not from karma, for protecting the good, destroying evil and above all for being accessible; knowing their divinity liberates. Principal (mukhya) descents are the Lord himself; secondary (gauṇa) are empowered souls.
- [Rāmānandī sampradāya](../lineages/ramanandi.md): Whenever dharma declines the compassionate Lord takes bodies to relieve the good.
- [Mahānubhāva panth](../lineages/mahanubhava.md): Parameśvara incarnates as the five Kṛṣṇas; the incarnations, not the devatās, are to be worshipped.
- [Ekaśaraṇa Dharma](../lineages/ekasarana.md): Nārāyaṇa is the cause of all the avatāras, whom the Kīrtana-ghoṣā praises.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 9.11 with 7.24–25 (the word avatāra is not used): the deluded disregard the Lord who has taken on a human body, not knowing his supreme state as great lord of beings; he is unborn and imperishable, veiled by his yoga-māyā and not manifest to all.
