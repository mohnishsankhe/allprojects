# The two birds on one tree

**Category:** self

## Definitions
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): Two birds, companions, on one tree: one eats the sweet fig, the other looks on without eating (RV 1.164.20); the lord, guardian of the world, has entered 'me the simple one' (1.164.21).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Two birds, companions, cling to the same tree; one eats the sweet fruit, the other looks on without eating; the one who grieves, seeing the other, the Lord, and his greatness, is freed from sorrow (MuU 3.1.1-2; ŚU 4.6-7, quoting RV 1.164.20); KU 1.3.1 speaks of two, 'shadow and light', drinking in the cave. Later schools read the two birds as jīva and Lord distinct (Dvaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita) or as the reflected and the witness self (Advaita).
