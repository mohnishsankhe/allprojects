## Definitions
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Five selves, each "made of" (-maya) something, each person-shaped, each within the previous and filling it, with the refrain "tasyaiṣa eva śārīra ātmā yaḥ pūrvasya" (its embodied self is this very one, the one of the former; the construal is disputed): the self made of the essence of food (2.1.1); of breath (head prāṇa, wings vyāna and apāna, self ākāśa, support earth; 2.2.1); of mind (head yajus, wings ṛc and sāman, self ādeśa, support Atharvāṅgiras; 2.3.1); of understanding (head śraddhā, wings ṛta and satya, self yoga, support mahas; 2.4.1); of bliss (head priya, wings moda and pramoda, self ānanda, support brahman; 2.5.1). The knower, departing, approaches all five (2.8.5, 3.10.5). The word kośa does not occur in the text.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): In the Kārikā 3.11: the sheaths of the Taittirīya, beginning with rasa, whose self is the supreme jīva shown like space.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): TU 2.1-5 teaches five selves, each within and filling the one before and each person-shaped: made of the essence of food, of breath, of mind, of understanding and of bliss, with brahman as the tail and foundation of the last; TU 3 reaches the same five as successive understandings of brahman. The TU calls them selves 'made of' (-maya); the name 'sheath' (kośa) for them is later Vedānta usage.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): Food, vital air, mind, intellect and bliss sheaths, which seem to cover the self; taught progressively so that the self is known as distinct from each; the bliss-sheath is not Brahman (Śaṅkara).
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): Five kōcams among the ninety-six: aṉṉamayam, pirāṇamayam, maṉōmayam, viññāṉamayam, āṉantamayam.
