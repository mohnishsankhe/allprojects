## Definitions
- [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md): Nārada Bhakti Sūtra: supreme love (parama-prema) for the Lord, of the nature of nectar; not desire but relinquishment; its own fruit.
- [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md): Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra: supreme attachment (parānurakti) to Īśvara — a form of rāga, not knowledge, not an act.
- [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md): NBS 16–19's doxography: attachment to worship (Vyāsa), to hearing his stories (Garga), love not opposed to delight in the self (Śāṇḍilya), offering all acts and anguish at forgetting him (Nārada).
- [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md): The Āḻvārs: love (aṉpu), longing (ārvam) and a melting heart lit as a lamp for Nāraṇaṉ; 'knowledge-and-love' (matinalam) given by the Lord's grace.
- [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md): Rāmānuja: knowledge that has become love (prīti) — steady remembrance (dhruvānusmṛti) of the Lord, vivid as perception, won by his grace.
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Madhva: very firm love (sneha), greater than love for anything else, preceded by knowledge of the Lord's greatness.
- [Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya Sampradāya; Acintya Bhedābheda)](../lineages/gaudiya-vaisnava.md): Rūpa Gosvāmī: pure devotion (uttamā bhakti) is favourable cultivation of Kṛṣṇa, empty of other desires and unobscured by knowledge and works.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): Madhusūdana Sarasvatī: the mind melted by the Lord's qualities flowing continuously toward him; the Vivekacūḍāmaṇi: devotion is the constant search into one's own true nature.
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The Bhāgavata: devotion free of the guṇas is the mind's unbroken movement toward the Lord, like the Gaṅgā to the sea, unmotivated and uninterrupted.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The Gītā: by undivided devotion the Lord is known, seen and entered; supreme devotion is reached by one who has become Brahman.

_Notes: Graded equivalences between the defining terms are recorded on the terms (trm:parama-prema, trm:paranurakti, trm:matinalam, trm:kanta-bhava) with rests_on. The schools do not accept each other's definitions: Advaita reads devotion as a means to or form of self-knowledge; the Nārada Sūtra holds it is the fruit; Rāmānuja makes it a form of knowledge; the Śāṇḍilya Sūtra denies that it is knowledge._
