# Māyā as the Goddess's power

## Definitions
- [Śākta traditions](../lineages/sakta.md): Māyā is not an independent illusion but the Goddess's own power: she is Mahāmāyā, queen of the supreme Brahman, who makes the universe whirl (SL 98), 'of the form of both knowledge and ignorance' (LSN 87).
- [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md): She herself becomes mind, space and the elements; nothing is beyond her transformation (SL 35); yet Bhāskararāya's tradition also names her 'substratum of the illusory world' (LSN 142).
- [Kaula Śrīvidyā (the Vāmakeśvara–Yoginīhṛdaya exegetical line)](../lineages/kaula-srividya.md): The Kaula Upaniṣad: ignorance is knowledge, the world is the Lord, the impermanent is permanent — nothing is other than Śakti (with Bhāskararāya's gloss).

## Relations (interpretation layer)
- contrasts-with → [Māyā in the principal Upaniṣads](maya.md): Advaita's māyā is indeterminable and sublated by knowledge; the Śākta māyā is the real power of the Goddess — rests on [98](../texts/saundarya-lahari.md#tea-saundarya-lahari-98), [35](../texts/saundarya-lahari.md#tea-saundarya-lahari-35)
