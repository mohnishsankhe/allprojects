# Īśvara (the Lord)

## Definitions
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): Classical Sāṃkhya posits no established Īśvara: the SK does not mention one; Gauḍapāda argues that the attributeless Īśvara could not produce what has guṇas (on SK 61); the Sāṃkhya Sūtra holds that Īśvara is not established (1.92–99), that karma, not a Lord, yields fruit (5.2–12), and that effects depend on ahaṃkāra, not Īśvara (6.64) — yet it admits emergent lords: the one absorbed in prakṛti who becomes all-knowing and all-doing (3.55–57). Vijñānabhikṣu salutes the Lord (Sāṃkhyasāra 1.1.1).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Īśvara is a special puruṣa untouched by afflictions, karma, fruition and residues (1.24), in whom the seed of omniscience is unsurpassed (1.25), the teacher of the ancients (1.26), designated by the praṇava (1.27). He is ever free and ever Lord, his pre-eminence resting on excellent sattva, without equal or superior; his purpose is compassion for beings (YBh 1.24–25). Devotion to him brings samādhi near (1.23, 2.45); he favours the devotee by mere intention (YBh 1.23). He is not the material of the world. Vijñānabhikṣu makes knowledge of the Supreme Lord the swift cause of liberation (YV 1.51).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Lineage contribution (Upaniṣadic): 'all this is to be dwelt in by the Lord' (Īśa 1); the lord of all, controller of all (BAU 4.4.22; MāU 6); the one God, Rudra-Śiva, the māyin, lord of lords, whose power is innate, who gives grace and frees from fetters (ŚU 1.10, 3.2, 4.10, 6.7-9); Indra/prāṇa as lord of the world who makes one do good or bad (KauU 3.9).
- [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md): Kumārila rejects a creator Prajāpati: no means of knowledge reaches him, a bodiless being cannot will creation, compassion would create only happiness, and purposeless action is unintelligible; later Bhāṭṭas dedicate dharma to Īśvara/Govinda.
- [Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/prabhakara-mimamsa.md): No Īśvara directs dharma and adharma or moves atoms; fruits arise from dharma and adharma with their circumstances.
- [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md): The Brahma Sūtra reports Jaimini's view that dharma itself gives the fruit.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Kṛṣṇa is unborn, of imperishable self and lord of beings, coming into being by his own māyā (4.6); he responds to people as they resort to him (4.11); he made the fourfold class-order yet is the imperishable non-doer (4.13); actions do not stain him and he has no longing for their fruit (4.14); he is the enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities, great lord of all worlds and friend of all beings (5.29); he is seen everywhere and everything in him (6.30). Whether the 'lord' (prabhu, vibhu) of 5.14–15, who creates no agency and takes on no one's merit or sin, is the supreme Lord or the embodied self is disputed by commentators.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: The Lord is unborn, without beginning, the great lord of the world (10.3); the beginning of the gods and great seers, whose origin they do not know (10.2); from him alone arise all the states of beings (10.4–5), and the seven great seers and the Manus, sharing his being (mad-bhāva), were born of mind (10.6); he is the origin of all, from whom everything proceeds (10.8), the seed of all beings without whom nothing moving or unmoving could exist (10.39); he stands supporting the whole world with a single portion (10.42); he alone knows himself by himself (10.15); Arjuna praises him as father, object of worship and weightiest teacher of the world, with none equal (11.43), who pervades all and is therefore all (11.40).
