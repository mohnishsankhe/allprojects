## Definitions
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The haṭha texts' classification of yoga into mantrayoga (lowest), layayoga, haṭhayoga and rājayoga (highest) — first in the Dattātreyayogaśāstra (9), then the Amaraughaprabodha, Śiva Saṃhitā (5.9) and others; the Śiva Saṃhitā assigns them to four grades of aspirant; the Yogabīja and Haṭharatnāvalī call them one 'great yoga'. The DYŚ also says the eight-limbed yoga of Yājñavalkya and the haṭha of Kapila differ in practice, not in fruit.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): One yoga divided in practice into four (Yogatattva 19); for practitioners of dull, middling and superior capacity (Varāha 5.10); in the Yogaśikhā four inner stages of one great yoga, each reinterpreted (mantra as reversed haṃsa, haṭha as union of sun and moon, laya as union of knower and supreme, rāja as union of rajas and retas).
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): Yoga is fourfold — mantra, laya, haṭha and rāja — with haṭha as the means to rājayoga (Amaraughaprabodha).
