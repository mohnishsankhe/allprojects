## Definitions
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): The unconscious, productive root (mūlaprakṛti, pradhāna, the unmanifest): uncaused, one, common, of three guṇas in equilibrium (SK 3, 10–11; SS 1.61). Not perceived because subtle, it is inferred from its effects (SK 8): from the limited measure of things, their homogeneity, activity through potency, the distinction of cause and effect and the merging of the manifold (SK 15–16). It acts for puruṣa like milk for the calf (SK 57), withdraws like a dancer once seen (SK 59), and is what is truly bound and released (SK 62). Gauḍapāda lists its synonyms pradhāna, brahman, avyakta, bahudhānaka, māyā; there is only one prakṛti (Gauḍapāda on SK 66).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): The seen has the nature of illumination, activity and inertia, consists of elements and senses, and exists for experience and liberation (2.18); the guṇas are called pradhāna (YBh 2.18); it is real and common to all puruṣas (2.22).
- [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md): Eternal, real, insentient matter with three guṇas (mixed sattva), the Lord's body; called māyā for its wonderful effects, not because it is illusory.
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Dvaita: an eternal-and-non-eternal insentient reality, the material cause of the world, dependent on and moved by Viṣṇu, presided over by Lakṣmī; its products are the principles from mahat to the elements. God is not the material cause.
