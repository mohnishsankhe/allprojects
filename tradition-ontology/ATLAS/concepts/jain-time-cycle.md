## Definitions
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): Time is beginningless; in Bharata and Airāvata a descending half-cycle (avasarpiṇī) and an ascending one (utsarpiṇī) of six periods each follow one another endlessly; twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras appear in the third and fourth periods; we live in the fifth period of the descending half (TS 3.27–28). Durations are measured in pit- and ocean-measures down to the instant (samaya).
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): Time turns without beginning in descending (avasarpiṇī) and ascending (utsarpiṇī) halves of six ages each; at the end of the third age of the descending half the wish-fulfilling trees fail, the patriarchs (kulakara) and then Ṛṣabha teach the arts of civilization, and twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras appear in the third and fourth ages.
