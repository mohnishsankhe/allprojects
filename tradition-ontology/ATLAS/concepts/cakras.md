## Definitions
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The haṭha texts give different body-maps: six centres (ādhāra/mūlādhāra, svādhiṣṭhāna, maṇipūra, anāhata, viśuddha, ājñā) with the thousand-petalled lotus above (GŚ 10–15, 60–62, 78–85; ṢCN); the Nāth scheme of six (or nine) centres, sixteen supports, three targets and five voids (Vivekamārtaṇḍa; Siddhasiddhāntapaddhati); the Śiva Saṃhitā's centres with Kailāsa, the sahasrāra, 'outside the body called brahmāṇḍa' (5.151); the GS's twelve-petalled lotus within the thousand-petalled where the guru is meditated on. Numbers, petals, letters and deities differ between texts.
- [Śākta traditions](../lineages/sakta.md): In Pūrṇānanda's Ṣaṭcakranirūpaṇa (1577) the six centres are strung on the brahmanāḍī within citriṇī, vajrā and suṣumnā: mūlādhāra (4 petals, earth, LAṂ, Brahmā, Ḍākinī), svādhiṣṭhāna (6, water, VAṂ, Viṣṇu), maṇipūra (10, fire, RAṂ, Rudra), anāhata (12, air, YAṂ, Īśa), viśuddha (16, space, HAṂ, Sadāśiva), ājñā (2, mind, praṇava, Śambhu), and the thousand-petalled lotus with Paramaśiva above.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Six centres (mūlādhāra, svādhiṣṭhāna, maṇipūra, anāhata, viśuddhi, ājñā) in most Yoga Upaniṣads, with the thousand-petalled lotus above; the Maṇḍalabrāhmaṇa counts nine cakras, sixteen ādhāras; the Yogaśikhā adds four pīṭhas (Kāmarūpa, Pūrṇagiri, Jālandhara, Uḍyāna); counts and attributes differ between texts.
- [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md): Six centres below the thousand-petalled lotus; the Saundaryalaharī places earth in the mūlādhāra, water in the maṇipūra, fire in the svādhiṣṭhāna, air in the heart, space in the throat and mind between the brows (SL 9), and worships a Śiva-Śakti pair in each (SL 36-41).
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): The six supports (āṟu ātāram) — mūlātāram, cuvātiṭṭāṉam, maṇipūrakam, anākatam, vicutti, ākkiñai — with the crown-space above; the goddess and the fire at the root; the guru's grace burns them away with the fifty-one letters (Aḻukaṇṇi 35).
