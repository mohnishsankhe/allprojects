# paśu

`trm:pasu` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** पशु
**Literal:** bound beast

## Definitions by tradition
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): All selves wandering in saṃsāra, the Lord's 'cattle'.
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): Every conscious being other than the Lord - with body and organs (sāñjana) or without (nirañjana), including the gods from Brahmā down and those liberated by Sāṃkhya-Yoga - bound by the kalās; so called from 'seeing' (only the body) and 'being bound'.
- [Kāpālika (Somasiddhānta)](../lineages/kapalika.md): Used in the same sense in the Śaiva sources that report on the Kāpālikas; no independent Kāpālika definition survives.
- [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md): The soul dependent on the Lord, its servant at the stage of practice (SSM 10.40); 'who is paśu, who pati?' when māyā dissolves (14.8–9).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The jīva bound by ego and the bonds.
- [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md): The soul: eternal, formless, ignorant, inactive, without qualities or lordship, pervasive, lodged in māyā and bound through kalā, vidyā, rāga, prakṛti and the instruments.
- [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md): The bound soul: eternal, countless, by nature a conscious knower and agent (cit), all-pervading, whose powers are covered by beginningless mala; 'tethered animal' of the Lord.
- [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md): The soul (Tamil uyir, āṉmā, pacu): sadasat, knowing only when made to know, taking on the nature of what it is joined with.
- [Spanda (the doctrine of vibration)](../lineages/spanda.md): The bound soul, deprived of his glory by the kalās and made the object of the powers of the mass of sounds (SK 3.13).

## Forms in other languages
- Tamil: pacu / uyir / āṉmā  — exact

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- partial: [jīva](jiva.md) — both name the individual embodied soul; for the Pāśupata the paśu is eternal, pervasive and never becomes the Lord
