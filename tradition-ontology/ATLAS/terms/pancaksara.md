# pañcākṣara

`trm:pancaksara` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** पञ्चाक्षर
**Literal:** the five syllables

## Definitions by tradition
- [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md): The mantra 'namaḥ śivāya', designation of Śiva and root of all mantras, at the head of all the śrutis; there is no mantra greater (SSM 8.2–7); whispered into the disciple's right ear at initiation (6.19–21).
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): The formula 'namaḥ śivāya', which the Śaiva traditions trace to the eighth section of the Śatarudrīya (TS 4.5.8).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): Śiva's mantra 'namaḥ śivāya' (six syllables with Oṃ), praised as the essence of the Veda.
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): Tamil añceḻuttu: the five letters (na-ma-ci-vā-ya); chanting them yields birthless release (Paṭṭiṉattār, Tiruttillai 17).
- [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md): The five letters na-ma-śi-vā-ya (Tamil namaccivāya): concealment, mala, Śiva, grace, soul; gross form 'namaḥ śivāya', subtle 'śivāya namaḥ'.
- [The Nāyaṉmārs and the Tirumuṟai (Tamil Śaiva bhakti)](../lineages/nayanmar.md): Namaccivāya: the devotee's refuge in every danger.
