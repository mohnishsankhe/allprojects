# rajas

`trm:rajas` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** रजस्
**Literal:** the guṇa of passion, activity

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Desire and anger arise from the guṇa rajas (3.37).
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): A region of space, especially the atmosphere; 'there was no rajas' before creation (RV 10.129.1); the six rajāṃsi propped apart (1.164.6).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The guṇa of passion, arising from thirst and attachment, binding by attachment to action (BhG 14.7); desire and anger are born of it (3.37).
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): The guṇa that is stimulating and mobile (SK 13), of the nature of pain (SK 12); predominant in the middle world (SK 54); suffering is a modification of rajas (Vācaspati).
- [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md): Lineage contribution (Āyurveda): with tamas one of the two doṣas of the mind (Ca Sū 1.57; AHS Sū 1.21); the rājasa mind is faulty, having the part of anger (Śā 4.36); rajas and tamas bind the person to the wheel of births (Śā 1.36, 1.68); their absence is a condition of liberation (Śā 1.142).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: The yogin to whom supreme happiness comes has his rajas stilled (śānta-rajas) (6.27).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: rajas is of the nature of passion, bound up with the arising of thirst and attachment; it binds by attachment to action (14.7) and attaches to action (14.9); when it increases, greed, activity, undertaking of actions, restlessness and craving arise (14.12); dying in it one is born among those attached to action (14.15); its fruit is pain (14.16); greed arises from it (14.17); the rājasa remain in the middle (14.18).
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): The guṇa characterized as attraction and aversion; those in it go to the human state (MDh 12.26, 12.40).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: The guṇa by which faith, food, sacrifice, austerity, giving, relinquishment, knowledge, action, agent, understanding, steadiness and happiness are called rājasic — marked by desire for fruit or return, display, strain, craving, elation and grief (17.9, 17.12, 17.18, 17.21, 18.8, 18.21, 18.24, 18.27, 18.31, 18.34, 18.38).
