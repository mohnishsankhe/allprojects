# advaita

`trm:advaita` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** अद्वैत
**Literal:** non-duality

## Definitions by tradition
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): In the Kārikā ch.1: the fourth is non-dual among all entities (1.10); non-duality is the ultimate (1.17); Oṃkāra is the stilling of duality (1.29). | In the Kārikā ch.2: non-dual: the rope alone (2.18); the non-dual as auspicious (2.33); the memory to be fixed on it (2.36). | In the Kārikā ch.3: non-duality is the ultimate; the mind is non-dual though appearing dual. | In ch.4: consciousness is peaceful and non-dual (4.45); duality is the vibration of mind (4.72); the unborn sameness non-dual (4.80).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): In this text: non-dual, said of the fourth.
- [Pañcācārya (Pañcapīṭha) tradition](../lineages/pancacarya.md): Rejected as a practice for the worshipper at the māheśvara stage, since worship rests on difference (SSM 10.37–43), and affirmed at the aikya stage, where difference is seen to be imagined by māyā (14.8–14).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Without a second, non-dual: 'he becomes the ocean, the one seer without duality' (BAU 4.3.32); the fourth is 'peaceful, auspicious, non-dual' (MāU 7, 12).
