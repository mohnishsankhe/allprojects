# paramātman

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** परमात्मन्
**Literal:** the supreme self

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: one of the names of 'the highest puruṣa in this body' (13.23); this supreme self, beginningless, without guṇas and imperishable, though dwelling in the body neither acts nor is tainted (13.32); the highest person, other than the perishable and the imperishable, is called the supreme self, the imperishable lord who enters and sustains the three worlds (15.17).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The supreme person in the body — witness, permitter, supporter, experiencer, great Lord (BhG 13.22); the highest puruṣa (15.17); beginningless and without guṇas, it neither acts nor is tainted (13.31).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): In the Bhāgavata one of the three names of the non-dual reality (with Brahman and Bhagavān).
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): The supreme self whose subtlety the renouncer discerns by yoga (MDh 6.65).
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): The supreme self, i.e. Brahman as the self of all; not different from the inner self.
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): In Jainism the soul in its perfected state — the embodied omniscient (arhat) and the bodiless Siddha; by the ultimate standpoint every soul's own nature (Paramātmaprakāśa; Yogasāra 22).
- [Navya-Nyāya](../lineages/navya-nyaya.md): The Lord, one and omniscient, as one of the two kinds of self (Tarkasaṅgraha).
- [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md): 'This supreme self is examined here' (NK 1.2).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: For one who has conquered himself and is at peace, the paramātman is steadily composed amid cold and heat, pleasure and pain, honour and dishonour (6.7); the reading (the supreme self established in him, or his self supremely composed) is disputed.
- [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md): The supreme Self, whose sovereignty is the reflexive awareness that is supreme speech (ĪPK 1.5.13).
