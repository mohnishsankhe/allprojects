# oṃ

`trm:om` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** ओं
**Literal:** the sacred syllable

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The one-syllabled Brahman to be uttered at death while remembering the Lord (BhG 8.13); the praṇava in all the Vedas (7.8); the first of the threefold designation 'oṃ tat sat' with which the expounders of Brahman begin their rites (17.23–24); 'among words I am the one syllable' (10.25).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7.8, 8.13, 9.17: the Lord is the praṇava in all the Vedas and the oṃkāra; Oṃ is 'the one-syllable brahman', to be uttered while remembering the Lord at departure.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: 'Of utterances I am the one syllable (ekam akṣaram)' (10.25) — a syllable the Gītā names as oṃ at 8.13.
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): The syllable made by Prajāpati from the sounds a, u, m drawn out of the three utterances (AB 5.32); expounded at length in the Gopatha Brāhmaṇa.
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): The imperishable syllable, Brahman and Prajāpati, recited with the utterances and the Sāvitrī (MDh 2.76–84; VDh 10.5).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The syllable Om: the udgītha (ChU 1.1.1); brahman and all this (TU 1.8; MāU 1); the word all Vedas proclaim (KU 1.2.15); the bow (MuU 2.2.4); the higher and lower brahman (PrU 5.2); its measures A, U, M and the measureless (MāU 8-12).
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): The sacred syllable, which in the Saṃhitā layer appears at the close of the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā ('Oṃ, kratu, remember'; 'Oṃ, the void is brahman', VS 40.15–17) and as the first of the four quarters of speech in one reading (Nirukta 13.9).
- [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md): Uttered and prolonged, with the void contemplated at its end (VBT 39).
