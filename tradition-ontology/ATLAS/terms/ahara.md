# āhāra

`trm:ahara` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** आहार
**Literal:** food; taking in

## Definitions by tradition
- [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md): The first support of life; wholesome food alone makes the person grow and unwholesome food causes disease (Ca Sū 25.31); its rule has eight factors (Vi 1.21); food is the root of living beings, of strength, complexion and ojas (Su Sū 1.28).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Food, which is threefold by the guṇas: life-giving, savoury and wholesome for the sāttvic; bitter, sour and burning for the rājasic; stale and impure for the tāmasic (BhG 17.7–10).
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): The four nutriments that maintain beings: edible food, contact, mental volition and consciousness (MN 9; SN 12.63); also the conditions that feed or starve the hindrances and awakening factors (SN 46.51).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: Food, threefold as dear to each type: foods increasing life, sattva, strength, health, happiness and delight, savoury, smooth, substantial and agreeable are dear to the sāttvic; pungent, sour, salty, very hot, sharp, dry and burning foods, bringing pain, grief and disease, to the rājasic; stale, tasteless, putrid, overnight, leftover and impure food to the tāmasic (17.7–10).
