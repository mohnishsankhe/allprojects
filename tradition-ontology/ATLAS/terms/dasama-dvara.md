# daśama-dvāra

`trm:dasama-dvara` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Literal:** the tenth door

## Definitions by tradition
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): The tenth door of the body: in the Amaraughaśāsana the opening of the śaṅkhinī between the royal teeth through which nectar flows, twofold as the path of nectar and the path of death (AmŚ 32, 70); in Hindi Nāth and Sant usage the opening at the crown.
- [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md): The 'tenth door' at the crown of the head beyond the nine bodily openings, through which the soul goes out to the Lord; Nāth-derived image in Kabīr and Dādū.
- [Sant Mat (Radhasoami and its successor lines)](../lineages/sant-mat.md): Daswān Dwār: the third region above Trikuṭī (also called Sunn), where the soul, free of mind and matter, sees itself as pure spirit.

## Forms in other languages
- Hindi: dasvāṁ dvār  — exact
- Hindi: dasvāṃ dvār / daswan dwar  — exact

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- contested: [brahmarandhra](brahmarandhra.md) — Hindi usage places the tenth door at the crown; the Amaraughaśāsana locates it at the śaṅkhinī-mouth in the palate
- partial: [brahmarandhra](brahmarandhra.md) — Both name an opening at the crown; the Sant Mat Daswān Dwār is a region, not only an aperture.
