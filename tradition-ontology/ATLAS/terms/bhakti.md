# bhakti

`trm:bhakti` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** भक्ति
**Literal:** devotion, loving participation

## Definitions by tradition
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Firm love for Viṣṇu exceeding all other love, preceded by knowledge of his greatness; the sole means of liberation, to which all other practices are subsidiary, and itself eternal bliss in liberation.
- [The Āḻvārs (Tamil Vaiṣṇava saint-poets) and their hymn tradition](../lineages/alvar.md): Devotion as the Āḻvārs sing it (Tamil pattu/patti): love (aṉpu), longing (ārvam) and a melting heart offered to Tirumāl, given by his grace as 'knowledge-and-love', lived as service and refuge.
- [Bhakti-śāstra: the sūtra tradition of devotional theory (Nārada, Śāṇḍilya)](../lineages/bhakti-sastra.md): Supreme love (parama-prema) for the Lord, of the nature of nectar (NBS 2–3); supreme attachment (parānurakti) to Īśvara (ŚBS 2); not desire, not knowledge, not an act — the fruit itself.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Devotion to the Lord, by which he is known, seen and entered (BhG 11.54); supreme devotion is attained by one who has become Brahman (18.54), and by devotion one knows him in truth (18.55).
- [Early Bhāgavata devotion (the Nārāyaṇīya / Sātvata–Ekāntika dharma)](../lineages/bhagavata-early.md): One-pointed devotion (ekānta-bhakti) to Nārāyaṇa, 'the one devoted solely, intent on Nārāyaṇa' (MBh 12.336).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): Devotion: the natural, motiveless inclination of the senses and mind toward the Lord, greater than liberation; by it alone the Lord is attained; it purifies even outcastes.
- [Rāmānandī sampradāya](../lineages/ramanandi.md): Loving devotion to Rāma, the lamp without which knowledge does not shine and the cause of liberation; open to men, women and even animals.
- [Śākta traditions](../lineages/sakta.md): Devotion to the Goddess, the easiest of the three paths, graded by the guṇas and culminating in supreme devotion.
- [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md): Devotion to Śiva, ninefold (SSM 9.2–3), outer and inner (9.6–9), arising from grace and giving rise to grace (9.11–12); its presence, not birth, makes one a bhakta (9.4–5); in the six-bhakti scheme it rises from faith to samarasa.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: The noun does not occur in chs. 4–6, but the verb bhaj- does: the Lord 'responds to' (bhajāmi) people in the way they approach him (4.11); the yogin established in oneness worships (bhajati) him as abiding in all beings (6.31); the one who worships (bhajate) him with faith and inner self fixed on him is the most yoked (6.47).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Devotion: the wise, knowing the Lord as the origin of all, worship (bhajante) him (10.8); to those ever yoked who worship him with love he gives the yoga of understanding (10.10); by undivided devotion alone he can be known, seen and entered in truth (11.54).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): Unmotivated, uninterrupted love and service directed to the Lord, the highest dharma (BhP 1.2.6); graded by the guṇas and beyond them (3.29); ninefold in practice (7.5.23-24); the one means that 'wins' the Lord (11.14.20-21).
- [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md): Meditation that has become love: knowledge in the form of supreme affection (prīti) for the Lord, the means to liberation prescribed by the Upaniṣads.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7–9: four kinds of people of good deeds worship (bhaj) the Lord, the knower of single devotion (eka-bhakti) being foremost (7.16–17); devotion joins yoga-power at departure (8.10); the supreme Person is gained by undivided devotion (8.22); the great-souled bow to him with devotion (9.14); he partakes of a leaf, flower, fruit or water offered with devotion (9.26); those who worship him with devotion are in him and he in them (9.29); his devotee does not perish (9.31); be my devotee (9.34).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Devotion: 'to one who has the highest devotion to God and to his teacher as to God, these matters shine forth' (ŚU 6.23).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: Unswerving devotion to the Lord is counted among the items called knowledge (13.11); unswerving bhakti-yoga carries one beyond the guṇas (14.26); whoever knows the Lord as the highest person worships (bhajati) him with his whole being (15.19).
- [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md): Devotion as the relish of identity with Śiva; for the one rich in devotion Śiva manifests without meditation or procedure (Śivastotrāvalī 1.1); the intense devotion of the dispassionate is Śaṅkara's power (VBT 121).
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): Tamil patti: 'devotion is the highest thing on earth' (Kaṭuveḷi 18); the Siddhars' devotion is to the Lord within and to the guru, not to temple images.
