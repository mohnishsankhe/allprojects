# haṃsa

`trm:hamsa` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** हंस
**Literal:** swan/goose

## Definitions by tradition
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): The swan seated in the bright, in men, in ṛta, in the sky (RV 4.40.5); in the Atharvaveda the swan rising from the waters that never lifts one foot is Prāṇa (AVŚ 11.4.21).
- [Yoginī Kaula (Yoginīkaula) of Matsyendra](../lineages/yogini-kaula.md): The jīva as the breath-syllable 'haṃsa', the 'person' of Śakti, moving up and down and dissolving at the dvādaśānta (KJN 6.5; 13.2; 17.18).
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): The word dwelling in the bodies of breathing beings, repeated naturally 21,600 times a day and called 'so 'ham' (Yogaviṣaya 28–29).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Goose, swan: the self or God as the goose seated in the bright (KU 2.2.2); the goose that wanders in the wheel (ŚU 1.6); flutters outward in the city of nine gates (ŚU 3.18); the one goose in the midst of the world (6.15).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The self moving with the breath (ha out, sa in), meditated as a bird and repeated naturally 21,600 times a day; also the third kind of renouncer.
- [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md): The mantra the living being repeats with each breath, going out with sa and in with ha (VBT 155b).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The breath, which goes out thirty-six finger-breadths (GŚ 40); the haṃsa in the thousand-petalled lotus (GS 6.10; Pādukāpañcaka).
- [Kubjikā tradition (Paścimāmnāya, the 'Western Transmission')](../lineages/kubjika.md): The haṃsa upon which the self rests, itself within the breath (Kubjikāmata 11.14).
- [Bengali and Assamese Śākta tantra](../lineages/bengal-assam-sakta.md): One of the names of the final abode in the crown (Ṣaṭcakranirūpaṇa 50).
- [Kabīr Panth](../lineages/kabir-panth.md): The freed soul, a swan that returns to Satlok when it receives the true Name; Kāl's prey is the crow, the Name makes it a swan.
- [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md): The soul as a swan that feeds on pearls (the Name) at the Mānasarovar within.
