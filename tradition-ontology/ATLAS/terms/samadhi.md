# samādhi

`trm:samadhi` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** समाधि
**Literal:** putting together

## Definitions by tradition
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): In the Kārikā 3.37: the samādhi described as motionless and fearless, free of speech and thought.
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): That same meditation, shining forth as the object alone and as if empty of its own form (3.3).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): As salt dissolves in water, the union of self and mind; the sameness when breath is exhausted and mind dissolved; the union of individual and supreme self with all intention ceased (HYP 4.5–7); sixfold in the GS (7.5–6).
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): Unification of mind (MN 44); right concentration is the four jhānas (SN 45.8); noble right concentration is supported by the other seven path factors (MN 117); four developments of concentration (AN 4.41).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Yoga itself (YBh 1.1); the eighth limb — meditation shining only as its object, as if empty of its own form (3.3); of two kinds, samprajñāta and asamprajñāta; with seed or seedless.
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): VP 6.7.92: the grasping of the very nature of the object free of imagination, produced by meditation.
- [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md): The attainment of meditation on Śiva as the self through inner worship; undivided attention on the haṃsa-formed supreme self contemplated as 'I am He' (SSM 12.21–24).
- [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md): Wholesome one-pointedness of mind, placing consciousness and concomitants evenly on one object; threefold as momentary, access and absorption.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Absorption, collectedness: the one 'not collected' cannot attain it (KU 1.2.24, asamāhita); 'samāhita' among the virtues of BAU 4.4.23; the sixth limb (MaiU 6.18); the happiness of a mind washed by samādhi (MaiU 6.34).
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): Absorption: the samarasa of breath and mind (GŚ 94); also, among Nāths, the tomb in which a yogī's body is buried seated.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: In 4.24 'brahma-karma-samādhi' — concentration on (or absorption in) action as Brahman; the later technical yoga sense is not given in chs. 4–6 (cf. samāhita, 'composed', 6.7).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: In which the resolute understanding of the ritual-minded is not established (2.44); in which understanding must stand immovable for one to attain yoga (2.53); the one of steady wisdom is 'established in samādhi' (2.54). Commentators gloss the word variously in these verses.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): In the Yoga Upaniṣads: the union of jīvātman and paramātman; the forgetting of meditation; distinguished from deep sleep; for the Mahāvākya, realization is not mere samādhi.
- [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md): The practice of a particular samādhi yields knowledge of truth (NS 4.2.38); obstacles to it are overcome by the continuing effect of prior practice (NS 4.2.41); Īśvara possesses concentration (NBh 4.1.21).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The settled state in which the resolute understanding stands unmoving (BhG 2.44, 2.53), not attained by those attached to enjoyment and power.
- [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md): In Jainism (1) the limb of Haribhadra's parā view; (2) composure of the ascetic tended by others (sādhu-samādhi, TS 6.24); (3) peaceful death (samādhi-maraṇa).
- [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md): Absorption that, cultivated in emergence, becomes ever-present (nityodita, PH 19); the bliss of the world is its joy (Śiva Sūtra 1.18).
