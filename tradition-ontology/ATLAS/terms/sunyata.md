# śūnyatā

`trm:sunyata` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** शून्यता
**Literal:** emptiness, voidness

## Definitions by tradition
- [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md): The lack of own-being (svabhāva) of all dharmas — persons, aggregates, the path and nirvāṇa alike; not nothingness and not an entity: 'form is emptiness, emptiness is form'. Emptiness does not make dharmas empty; they are empty. It is to be realized without being grasped as a view.
- [Tathāgatagarbha (Buddha-nature) tradition](../lineages/tathagatagarbha.md): The tathāgatagarbha is empty of the adventitious, separable defilements and not empty of the inseparable buddha-qualities; emptiness misread as mere absence is criticized.
- [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md): The absence of own-nature (svabhāva) in every dharma, which is the same as its dependent origination; it is a dependent designation and the middle way (MMK 24.18), is itself empty (13.7) and is not a view to be held (13.8).
- [Prāsaṅgika-Madhyamaka](../lineages/prasangika.md): The mere negation of own-nature, implying nothing positive in its place; taught as sixteen or twenty emptinesses (MA 6); realized also by śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas (Candrakīrti).
- [Yogācāra-Madhyamaka](../lineages/yogacara-madhyamaka.md): The lack of own-nature of all things, established because nothing is truly one or truly many (MAL 1).
- [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md): The non-existence of duality and the existence of that non-existence, present within unreal imagination; neither defiled nor undefiled because mind is luminous; the emptiness of person and dharmas.
- [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md): Seeing emptiness (selflessness) is what liberates; the other contemplations serve it.
