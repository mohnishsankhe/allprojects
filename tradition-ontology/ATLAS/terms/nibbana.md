# nibbāna

`trm:nibbana` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Pali
**Literal:** extinguishing, going out

## Definitions by tradition
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): The destruction of greed, hatred and delusion (SN 38.1); the unconditioned (SN 43.12), the cessation of becoming (SN 12.68), the highest happiness (Dhp 203–204); described negatively as the sphere where there is no earth, water, fire, air … no coming or going (Ud 8.1) and as the unborn, unmade, unconditioned (Ud 8.3); named by many epithets (SN 43.44).
- [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md): The one unconditioned dhamma, real and not mere absence, outside the aggregates; not a self (commentarial Theravāda).
- [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md): The unconditioned element, the fourth ultimate reality: cessation of craving and of the round, object of the path and fruition knowledges; one in nature, twofold as with and without residue, threefold as void, signless and desireless; not a self.
- [Suan Mokkh (Buddhadāsa's tradition)](../lineages/suan-mokkh.md): Coolness: the extinction of the fires of defilement, tasted here and now, momentarily or completely (Buddhadāsa).
- [Dhammakāya meditation tradition (Vijjā Dhammakāya; Wat Paknam)](../lineages/dhammakaya.md): Taught by the Dhammakāya tradition as a realm and a true self (attā) where the Dhammakāyas of the Buddhas abide — contested by other Theravāda scholars.
- [U Ba Khin – S. N. Goenka tradition (Sayagyi U Ba Khin lineage)](../lineages/u-ba-khin-goenka.md): The experience beyond the field of mind and matter, reached when all saṅkhāras are eradicated.

## Forms in other languages
- Sanskrit: nirvāṇa  — exact

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- exact: [nirvāṇa](nirvana.md) — same word; whether anything is attained beyond the extinguishing (Brahman, self) is where the traditions differ; Theravāda rejects a self or ground
- partial: [nirvāṇa](nirvana.md) — the same Buddhist term in Sanskrit; the Gītā's brahma-nirvāṇa and other Hindu usages differ
