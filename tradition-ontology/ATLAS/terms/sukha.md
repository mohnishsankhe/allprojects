# sukha

`trm:sukha` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** सुख
**Literal:** pleasure, happiness

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Pleasure and pain come from sense-contacts (2.14) and are to be made the same (2.38); for one without peace there is no happiness (2.66).
- [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md): What all experience as agreeable; a quality of the self; bound up with pain and so to be relinquished like food mixed with poison (NBh 1.1.2).
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): The bliss of the first three jhānas (DN 2); the pleasure the bodhisatta saw no reason to fear (MN 36); nibbāna is the highest happiness (Dhp 203–204).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 5–6: the happiness in the self, found by one unattached to external contacts, imperishable (5.21); enjoyments born of contact are sources of suffering (5.22); happiness, delight and light within (5.24); boundless happiness grasped by the intellect and beyond the senses (6.21); the highest happiness and the boundless happiness of contact with Brahman (6.27–28).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Pleasure, paired with pain among the states from the Lord (10.4); the dear devotee is the same in both (12.13, 12.18).
- [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md): Pleasant feeling; a jhāna factor, distinguished from rapture as the enjoyment is from the thirst for water.
