# mahāvākya

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** महावाक्य
**Literal:** great sentence

## Definitions by tradition
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): The great saying ('that thou art' and the like) that teaches the identity of the delimited consciousness with the whole; understood by partial implication.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): 'Great saying': a later (Advaita) name for four Upaniṣadic statements of identity, one from each Veda — prajñānaṃ brahma (AU 3.1.3), ahaṃ brahmāsmi (BAU 1.4.10), tat tvam asi (ChU 6.8.7), ayam ātmā brahma (MāU 2; BAU 2.5.19). The word does not occur in these texts.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): A 'great saying' of the Upaniṣads teaching the identity of self and Brahman: prajñānaṃ brahma (ṚV/Aitareya), ahaṃ brahmāsmi (Yajur/BĀU), tat tvam asi (Sāma/ChU), ayam ātmā brahma (Atharva/Māṇḍūkya); each is assigned to one of the four āmnāya maṭhas.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): U04 usage: the four sayings given as secret teaching (Śukarahasya) and at renunciation; read as the Goddess in the Bahvṛca.
