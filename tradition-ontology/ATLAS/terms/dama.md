## Definitions by tradition
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): In 4.86: self-control, from being tamed by nature.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Self-restraint, a mark of the divine endowment (BhG 16.1) and one of the three 'horses of Brahman' (MBh 11.7.19); taught by the Haṃsa (12.288).
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): Self-control: restraining the outer organs and turning them back to their sources.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Restraint, among the states of beings that arise from the Lord (10.4).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: Self-restraint: a mark of the divine endowment (16.1) and part of the brāhmaṇa's work born of his nature (18.42).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Self-restraint: the gods' 'da' (BAU 5.2); among the virtues of BAU 4.4.23 (dānta); foundation of the upaniṣad with austerity and work (Kena 4.8).
