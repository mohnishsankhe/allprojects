# mantra

`trm:mantra` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** मन्त्र
**Literal:** instrument of thought; formula

## Definitions by tradition
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): A Vedic verse or formula used in rite and recitation; its meaningfulness was defended by Yāska against Kautsa (Nirukta 1.15–16); in the Saṃhitā 'common be your mantra (counsel)' (RV 10.191.3).
- [Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/mimamsa.md): The portion of the Veda recited during the rite as prompting its performance; the rest is Brāhmaṇa (MS 2.1.32–33). Mantras have meaning like ordinary sentences (MS 1.2.40).
- [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md): Mantras are meaningful by reminding the performer of items connected with the performance; an unseen purpose for their recitation is assumed only where no seen purpose is possible.
- [Vedāṅga (the six limbs of the Veda)](../lineages/vedanga.md): The Saṃhitā portion of the Veda — verses, formulas and chants — which, with the Brāhmaṇa, makes up the Veda (ĀpŚS 24.1.31); to be recited with exact accent (PŚ 52) and understood (Nirukta 1.15–18).
- [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md): Śiva's powers present as sound-forms through which ritual acts; the perfected mantras paralyse and destroy the soul's bonds in initiation.
- [Spanda (the doctrine of vibration)](../lineages/spanda.md): The mind is mantra (Śiva Sūtra 2.1); mantras rest on the strength of spanda and dissolve in it with the worshipper's mind (SK 2.1-2).
