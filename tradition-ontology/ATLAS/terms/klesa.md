# kleśa

`trm:klesa` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** क्लेश
**Literal:** affliction

## Definitions by tradition
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): The five afflictions — ignorance, I-am-ness, attachment, aversion, clinging to life (2.3) — the five errors (YBh 2.3), rooted in ignorance (2.4), root of the karmic residue (2.12); thinned by kriyā-yoga, abandoned by meditation and involution (2.2, 2.10–11), ceasing through the cloud of dharma (4.30).
- [Jōdo Shinshū (the True Pure Land school of Shinran)](../lineages/jodo-shinshu.md): Bonnō, blind passions: the foolish being is 'fully possessed of blind passions' (bonnō gusoku) to the end of life; the vow is for such a being; lack of joy in the nenbutsu is their working.
- [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md): The six root afflictions — greed, aversion, delusion, conceit, (wrong) views and doubt — which with their secondary afflictions constitute the afflictive obscuration; the afflicted mind has its own four afflictions of self.
- [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md): Defilement; what calms them is the discernment of dharmas; a defilement arises complete from an unabandoned anuśaya, a present object and improper attention.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Hardship, toil: 'greater is the kleśa of those whose awareness (cetas) is attached to the unmanifest' (12.5) — used here in its general sense, not as the Yoga system's technical 'afflictions'.
- [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md): Desire, aversion and delusion arise from conceptualization in dependence on the errors about the pleasant and unpleasant; they do not exist by own-nature and so are not real (MMK 23.1–2); if they existed by own-nature they could not be abandoned (23.24).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The afflictions, whose destruction comes by yoga alone (VP 6.7.25).
