# karma

`trm:karma` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** कर्म
**Literal:** action; ritual act; the deed and its result

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Action, which no embodied being can abandon (BhG 3.5, 18.11) and which binds only through attachment to its fruit (2.47); also the ritual act (3.14) and the deed that follows its doer 'as a calf finds its mother' (MBh 12.174.16).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Action: one's entitlement is to it, never to its fruits (2.47); mere action is far inferior to buddhiyoga (2.49); no one remains without acting even for a moment (3.5); action is better than inaction (3.8); it binds except when done for sacrifice (3.9); sacrifice arises from action and action from brahman (3.14–15); done unattached it leads to the highest (3.19); Janaka and others reached perfection by it (3.20); all actions are done by the guṇas (3.27) and are to be renounced in Kṛṣṇa (3.30).
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): Ritual action, whose fruit is secured by correct performance and knowledge.
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): Action of mind, speech and body, bearing good or bad fruit in high, middle or low births (MDh 12.3–9); its residue shapes the next birth (ĀpDh 2.1.2.3).
- [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md): Motion: residing in one substance, without qualities, the independent cause of conjunction and disjunction (VS 1.1.16 C); five kinds (VS 1.1.6).
- [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md): Action whose results bind; the body arises from the fruits of past actions (NS 3.2.60); 'activity' (pravṛtti) of speech, mind and body (NS 1.1.17).
- [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md): Lineage contribution (Āyurveda): (1) action as a category — movement, the cause of conjunction and disjunction (Ca Sū 1.49, 1.52); (2) the deed of a former body, called daiva, whose fruit is this life (Sū 11.31; Vi 3.30); karma-born diseases subside only with its exhaustion (Śā 1.116-117); (3) the five purificatory acts.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Action and its result: 'one becomes good by good action, bad by bad action' (BAU 3.2.13); 'as one acts, so one becomes' (BAU 4.4.5); rebirth 'according to action and learning' (KU 2.2.7; KauU 1.2); action does not cling to one who knows (ChU 4.14.3; Īśa 2).
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Action and its residue; it cannot be exhausted by experience because new karma is always made, so liberation comes by grace through knowledge, while ritual action at best wins the lowest grace (heaven).
- [Sant Mat (Radhasoami and its successor lines)](../lineages/sant-mat.md): Of three kinds: pralabdh (destiny, lived out in this body), sinchit (stored, burnt by the Master and by practice) and kriyaman (new, avoided by the disciple's vows).
