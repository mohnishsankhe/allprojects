# laya

`trm:laya` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** लय
**Literal:** dissolution, absorption

## Definitions by tradition
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): Dissolution of mind (with breath): 'the forgetting of objects because latent impressions do not arise again' (HYP 4.34); the lord of the breath, resting on nāda (4.29); Ādinātha taught a crore and a quarter kinds, of which nāda is chief (4.66).
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): In the Aṣṭāvakra Gītā, 'dissolution' of the world into the Self by four recognitions (space and pot, ocean and waves, shell and silver, all in me), after which there is nothing to renounce, accept or dissolve.
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): Torpor in meditation, from which one must turn away, 'say the teachers' (SS 6.30).
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): Dissolution of mind and breath into the supreme; one of the four yogas and a synonym of samādhi (HYP 4.3).
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): Dullness: the mind's lapse into sleep for want of support, first obstacle to nirvikalpa samādhi.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Dissolution of mind; laya-yoga meditates on the Lord in all activity; 'laya is lord of the breath'; also an obstacle in samādhi (torpor).
- [Saṅgītaśāstra (the science of music: song, instrument and dance)](../lineages/sangita.md): Tempo: the rest between the actions of tāla, slow, middle and fast.
