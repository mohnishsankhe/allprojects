# moha

`trm:moha` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** मोह
**Literal:** delusion, bewilderment

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: The steadfast are not bewildered by the passage to another body (2.13); understanding must cross the thicket of delusion (mohakalila, 2.52); delusion (saṃmoha) arises from anger and leads to loss of memory (2.63); one who attains the brāhmī sthiti is not deluded (2.72); desire deludes the embodied one (3.40).
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): The second error, eightfold (SK 48): attachment to or self-assertion for the eight powers, through which even gods like Indra fall back (Gauḍapāda; Kramadīpikā).
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): The third unwholesome root (MN 9), one of the three fires (SN 35.28).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7.13, 7.27; 8.27; 9.12: the world, deluded by the guṇa-made states, does not know the Lord; the delusion of the pairs; knowing the two paths the yogin is not deluded; the deluding (mohinī) nature of rākṣasas and asuras.
- [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md): The worst of the faults, since the others arise only in the deluded (NS 4.1.6).
- [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md): Delusion, the unwholesome root present in all unwholesome consciousness; characteristic blindness to the object's true nature; synonymous with ignorance.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4.35: knowing which one does not again fall into delusion; 5.15: creatures are deluded because knowledge is covered by ignorance; 5.20: the knower of Brahman is undeluded.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Delusion born of anger (BhG 2.63) and of the pairs of opposites (7.27); destroyed by the teaching, as Arjuna declares (18.73).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Delusion: whoever knows the Lord as unborn and beginningless is undeluded (asaṃmūḍha) (10.3); Arjuna says his delusion is gone through the teaching (11.1); the Lord tells him not to be bewildered (vimūḍhabhāva) by the terrible form (11.49).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: Delusion: tamas deludes all embodied beings (14.8); delusion is a sign and product of tamas (14.13, 14.17); the one beyond the guṇas does not hate it when present (14.22); the undeluded reach the imperishable place and know the Lord as the highest person (15.5, 15.19); the deluded do not see the living being (15.10).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: Delusion: the demonic take up false notions out of delusion (16.10) and are enveloped in its net (16.16); abandoning allotted action out of delusion is tāmasic (18.7), as is action begun from delusion (18.25); Arjuna refuses from delusion what his nature will make him do (18.60); asked whether his confusion born of ignorance is destroyed (18.72), he answers 'delusion is destroyed' (18.73).
