# rasa

`trm:rasa` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** रस
**Literal:** sap; taste; essence; (in Rasa Śāstra) mercury

## Definitions by tradition
- [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md): Lineage contribution (Āyurveda): (1) taste, the object of the tongue, sixfold — sweet, sour, salt, pungent, bitter, astringent (Ca Sū 1.64-65; 26.9); (2) the first tissue (AHS Sū 1.13).
- [Rasa Śāstra (the science of mercury) and the Raseśvara-darśana](../lineages/rasa-sastra.md): In Rasa Śāstra, mercury, 'the rasa of my body' (Rasārṇava 1.36); rasa as the means of crossing saṃsāra, identified with brahman ('raso vai saḥ', SDS Raseśvara 49).
- [Alaṅkāraśāstra and Nāṭyaśāstra (poetics, dramaturgy and the theory of rasa)](../lineages/alankara.md): Aesthetic relish: the stable emotion made relishable by the union of determinants, consequents and transient states, tasted by the sensitive spectator as food is tasted (NŚ 6 rasa-sūtra); eight in drama (NŚ 6.15), nine with śānta; 'twin of tasting Brahman' (SD 3.2).
- [Saṅgītaśāstra (the science of music: song, instrument and dance)](../lineages/sangita.md): The emotional flavour that notes, rāgas and dance are to evoke; the music treatises assign notes and rāgas to rasas.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: The inner taste for sense-objects that remains when one abstains from them and turns away only on seeing the highest (2.59).
- [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md): Quality grasped by the tongue; six tastes; in earth and water.
- [Vaiṣṇava Sahajiyā](../lineages/vaisnava-sahajiya.md): The essence of love tasted in the union of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, located in the Sahajiyā body; the Sahajiyā reads Rūpa Gosvāmī's rasa theory as a guide to embodied practice.
