# yama

`trm:yama` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** यम
**Literal:** twin; restrainer

## Definitions by tradition
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): Yama son of Vivasvant, first mortal to die, who found the path for many and is king of the dead in the highest heaven (RV 10.14).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): The five restraints: non-harming, truthfulness, non-stealing, celibacy, non-possessiveness (2.30); universal, the great vow (2.31).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The first limb: in the VP and ŚiP non-harming, truth, non-stealing, celibacy, non-possession; in MkP 41.16 the ascetic's five vows (non-stealing, celibacy, renunciation, non-greed, non-harming).
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): Non-harming, celibacy, truthfulness, non-dealing and non-stealing (with the five niyamas also counted as yamas).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Ten restraints (non-injury … purity), each with an outer and an inner meaning; moderate diet is chief.
- [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md): The means of dharma common to all life-stages (NBh 4.2.46).
- [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md): Without restraint, pure food does not bring prosperity (VS 6.2.9 C).
