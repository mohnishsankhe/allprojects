# amṛtatva

`trm:amrtatva` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** अमृतत्व
**Literal:** immortality, deathlessness

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: The goal for which the steadfast person, unmoved by the contacts of the senses and the same in pleasure and pain, is fit (2.15).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Immortality: the goal named by the early Upaniṣads — 'there is no hope of immortality through wealth' (BAU 2.4.2); 'so much is immortality' (4.5.15); 'the mortal becomes immortal' (4.4.7; KU 2.3.14); 'lead me from death to immortality' (BAU 1.3.28).

## Forms in other languages
- Pali: amata  — partial — Buddhist epithet of nibbāna

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- partial: [mokṣa](moksa.md) — the early texts' 'immortality' is what the later texts (ŚU 6.16, MaiU 6.20) call mokṣa; the early texts do not themselves equate the two words
