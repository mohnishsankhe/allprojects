# kṣetra

**Language:** Sanskrit

**Literal:** field

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The body and the whole of prakṛti with its modifications — elements, ego, intellect, the unmanifest, the organs, the objects, desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, the aggregate, consciousness and steadfastness (BhG 13.1, 13.5–6).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: the body (śarīra) is called the field (13.2). In brief it consists of the great elements, ahaṅkāra, buddhi, the unmanifest, the ten senses and the one, the five ranges of the senses, desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, the aggregate, sentience (cetanā) and steadiness, together with its modifications (13.6–7). Every being is born from the union of field and knower (13.27); the possessor of the field illumines the whole field as the sun the world (13.34); knowing the difference between field and knower leads to the supreme (13.35).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): A sacred territory such as Kāśī or Puruṣottama; seven cities are liberating (GP 2.38.5).
