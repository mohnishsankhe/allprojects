# nirvāṇa

`trm:nirvana` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** निर्वाण
**Literal:** extinction, blowing out

## Definitions by tradition
- [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md): Not abandoned, not attained, neither annihilated nor eternal, neither ceased nor arisen (MMK 25.3); neither an existent nor a non-existent (25.4–10); not the slightest different from saṃsāra (25.19–20); the ending of the conceptions of existence and non-existence (RĀ 1.42).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: The peace that culminates in nirvāṇa (nirvāṇa-paramā) and abides in the Lord, attained by the constantly yoked yogin (6.15); see brahma-nirvāṇa (5.24–26).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): In the Purāṇas the calm of the mind freed of object like a flame going out (BhP 3.28.35), the end of the delusion of object-knowledge-action (11.14.46), Maheśa's state (LiP 1.8.5), and the self's nature (VP 6.7.22).
- [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md): For the bodhisattva not a final rest to be entered early: even nirvāṇa is like an illusion (Aṣṭa 2); the arhats' nirvāṇa is a resting place conjured by skillful means (Lotus 7); the Tathāgata's great nirvāṇa is permanent, blissful, self and pure (Mahāparinirvāṇa).
- [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md): Cessation through discernment — a real, unconditioned, eternal dharma, the disjunction from all sāsrava dharmas; the object of refuge together with the Buddha and Saṅgha.
- [Sautrāntika](../lineages/sautrantika.md): A mere absence: the non-arising of future defilements and births; not an entity (like the extinction of a flame).
- [Pudgalavāda](../lineages/pudgalavada.md): The unconditioned, the fourth of the five knowables; the relation of the person to nirvāṇa is inexpressible (low confidence).
