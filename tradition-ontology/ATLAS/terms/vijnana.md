# vijñāna

`trm:vijnana` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** विज्ञान
**Literal:** discernment, realised or particular knowledge

## Definitions by tradition
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Understanding: a rung in Sanatkumāra's ladder (ChU 7.7, 7.17); the self made of understanding (TU 2.4; BAU 4.3.7); brahman is understanding and bliss (BAU 3.9.28).
- [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md): Consciousness, the bare representation (prativijñapti) of each object; six kinds; citta, manas and vijñāna have one meaning.
- [Mahāsāṃghika](../lineages/mahasanghika.md): A root consciousness (mūlavijñāna) underlying the six is ascribed to the Mahāsāṃghikas by Asaṅga (low confidence).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: With jñāna, what desire destroys (3.41); commentators differ on the distinction.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7–9: Paired with jñāna as its complement in the knowledge Kṛṣṇa declares 'with vijñāna' (jñānaṃ savijñānam, 7.2; jñānaṃ vijñānasahitam, 9.1). The text does not define the difference; commentators take it as realization of what is taught, or as knowledge of the Lord's distinguishing particulars.
- [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md): Consciousness arises in dependence on the eye and visible form, as a son from his parents (MMK 3.7).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Paired with knowledge: the yogin whose self is satisfied with jñāna and vijñāna is called yoked (6.8); chs. 4–6 do not distinguish the two (commentators take vijñāna as realised or specific knowledge).
- [Kagyu (the Kagyu transmissions: Marpa / Dakpo Kagyu, with the Shangpa grouped by name)](../lineages/kagyu.md): rnam shes: dualistic consciousness in its eight kinds, which when recognized are the five wisdoms (3rd Karmapa's Distinguishing Consciousness and Wisdom).
