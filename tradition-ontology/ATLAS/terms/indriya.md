# indriya

`trm:indriya` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** इन्द्रिय
**Literal:** sense faculty

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: The senses are to be withdrawn from their objects like a tortoise's limbs (2.58); they are turbulent and carry off the mind even of the wise (2.60, 2.67); they are to be restrained (2.61, 2.68) and controlled first when fighting desire (3.41); rāga and dveṣa are set in each sense towards its object (3.34); they are one of the seats of desire (3.40); the senses are high, the mind higher (3.42).
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): The eleven capacities produced from ahaṃkāra: five sense capacities, five action capacities and manas (SK 24–27); not made of the elements (SS 2.20, 5.84).
- [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md): Smell, taste, sight, touch, hearing, made from the elements (NS 1.1.12).
- [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md): Each located in a bodily seat — nose-tip, tongue-tip, the black of the eye, the whole skin, the ear's ākāśa; manas is the inner sense.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The senses, which carry off the mind (BhG 2.60, 2.67), are withdrawn like a tortoise's limbs (2.58), and are high but lower than mind (3.42); in the Anugītā they are among the seven priests of the inner sacrifice (14.20).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): The senses (ten, with manas eleven, YBh 2.19), part of the seen (2.18); withdrawn in pratyāhāra (2.54) and mastered by saṃyama on their five forms (3.47).
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): The five spiritual faculties — faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom (SN 48.10); also the six sense faculties, to be developed not by blindness but by equanimity (MN 152).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: The senses are offered into the fires of restraint and their actions into the fire of self-restraint (4.26–27); they are to be restrained to gain knowledge (4.39) and in meditation (6.12, 6.24); the knower holds that 'the senses move among the sense-objects' (5.9).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Faculty / sense: of the indriyas the Lord is manas (10.22); the worshippers of the imperishable restrain the whole group of the senses (indriya-grāma) (12.4).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: the ten senses 'and the one' belong to the field (13.6); the knowable appears through the qualities of all the senses yet is devoid of them (13.15); the portion of Kṛṣṇa that has become the living being draws the senses with the mind as sixth, which abide in prakṛti, carries them from body to body, and presiding over ear, eye, touch, taste, smell and mind enjoys objects (15.7–9).
- [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md): The twenty-two faculties, including the sense faculties, life, the feelings, the five spiritual faculties and the three supramundane faculties.
- [Sarvāstivāda](../lineages/sarvastivada.md): Faculty, 'dominant': twenty-two — six sense faculties, female, male, life, five feelings, five spiritual faculties and three pure faculties.
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): In Jain analysis five senses, each twofold: physical (formation and instrument) and psychic (attainment and use) (TS 2.15–19).
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): The eleven faculties — five of knowledge, five of action and mind — to be restrained like horses (MDh 2.88–92).
- [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md): The doors of pleasure and pain, to be abandoned for abiding in the self (VBT 136); in the Śiva Sūtra, the spectators of the self's dance (3.11).
