# aśvattha

`trm:asvattha` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** अश्वत्थ
**Literal:** the sacred fig

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 15.1–3: the imperishable aśvattha is spoken of with roots above and branches below, its leaves the metres; one who knows it knows the Veda; its branches spread below and above, swollen by the guṇas, with the sense-objects as shoots, and its roots below are bound up with action in the human world; its form, end, beginning and foundation are not perceived here; it is to be cut with the weapon of non-attachment.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The imperishable tree with roots above and branches below, whose leaves are the Vedic hymns, to be cut with the axe of non-attachment (BhG 15.1–4); 'among trees I am the aśvattha' (10.26).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: 'of all trees (I am) the aśvattha' (10.26); here simply the chief of trees (contrast 15.1–3).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The eternal fig tree with roots above and branches below, which is brahman, the immortal, in which all worlds rest (KU 2.3.1).
