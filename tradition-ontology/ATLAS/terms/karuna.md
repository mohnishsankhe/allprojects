# karuṇā

`trm:karuna` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** करुणा
**Literal:** compassion

## Definitions by tradition
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Compassion cultivated toward the suffering, one of the four attitudes that clarify the mind (1.33, YBh 1.33). Saṃyama on it yields its strength (3.23).
- [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md): The second brahmavihāra, abandoning cruelty (MN 62; DN 13).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Compassionate (karuṇa): a mark of the devotee dear to the Lord (12.13).
- [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md): Compassion is the nurse of the buddha's children (RGV 1.34); love for beings seen as reflections and echoes (Vimalakīrti 6).
- [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md): The starting point of the Buddha's authority: cultivated over many lives, it grows by its own momentum to the inability to bear others' suffering.
- [The Mahāsiddhas (the eighty-four great adepts)](../lineages/mahasiddha.md): Compassion, which alone keeps one in saṃsāra; joined with emptiness it is the path (People Dohā v15–17); its flower and fruit are benefit for others.
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): In Jainism compassion toward the afflicted (TS 7.11: kāruṇya).
- [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md): Compassion, praised first as seed, water and ripening of the Victors' harvest; threefold as focused on beings, on dharmas (impermanence) and without object (MA 1.2–4); the root of buddhahood (Bhāvanākrama I); out of compassion the Buddha taught for the abandonment of all views (MMK 27.30).
