# yajña

`trm:yajna` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** यज्ञ
**Literal:** worship; sacrifice

## Definitions by tradition
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): The sacrifice: offering into fire to the gods with mantra, the rite that sustains gods and world; the gods first sacrificed the Puruṣa, 'sacrificing to the sacrifice with the sacrifice' (RV 10.90.16).
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): The Vedic offering-rite performed with fire, mantras and priests; in the Brāhmaṇas it is identified with the cosmic Person, with Prajāpati, with the year and with Viṣṇu ('puruṣo vai yajñaḥ', ŚB 1.3.2.1; 'yajño vai viṣṇuḥ').
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): Any of the prescribed offerings of the householder — the daily great sacrifices, the cooked-food, oblation and Soma sacrifices counted among the forty sacraments (GDh 8.14–21).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Action other than for the sake of sacrifice binds (3.9); Prajāpati created beings together with sacrifice, by which gods and humans nourish one another (3.10–12); rain arises from sacrifice and sacrifice from action (3.14); the all-pervading brahman is ever established in it (3.15).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Sacrifice, action done for whose sake does not bind (BhG 3.9); the wheel of sacrifice between gods and men (3.10–16); widened to many kinds — of material things, austerity, yoga, study, knowledge, breath (4.25–30) — the sacrifice of knowledge being highest (4.33); inwardly, the seven priests of the senses offering into the fire within (Anugītā 14.20).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Sacrifice is extended in ch. 4: acting for the sake of sacrifice dissolves action (4.23); every factor of the rite is Brahman (4.24); many kinds are listed — to the gods, into Brahman, senses into restraint, objects into senses, actions of senses and breath into self-restraint, material goods, austerity, yoga, recitation and knowledge, breath-regulation, regulated food (4.25–30); all are born of action and destroy impurity (4.30, 4.32); the sacrifice of knowledge is best (4.33); the non-sacrificer has neither this world nor the next (4.31); the Lord is the enjoyer of sacrifices (5.29).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Sacrifice: of sacrifices the Lord is the japa-yajña (10.25); not by sacrifices can he be seen in the form Arjuna saw (11.48, 11.53).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: Sacrifice, threefold: sāttvic when offered according to injunction by those not desiring fruit, the mind set on 'it simply ought to be offered'; rājasic when offered for fruit or display; tāmasic when without injunction, distributed food, mantras, fee or faith (17.11–13). The demonic sacrifice 'in name only', with hypocrisy and not by injunction (16.17). Sacrifice, giving and austerity purify the wise and are not to be abandoned (18.5); the rites of the expounders of Brahman begin with oṃ (17.24).
