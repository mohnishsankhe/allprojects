# yogi-pratyakṣa

`trm:yogipratyaksa` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** योगिप्रत्यक्ष
**Literal:** yogic perception

## Definitions by tradition
- [Buddhist epistemology and logic (pramāṇa school)](../lineages/pramana-buddhist.md): Perception born of the culmination of meditative cultivation of a real object — vivid, free of the net of construction; the foulness and earth-kasiṇa meditations show that cultivation yields vividness even of the unreal, but only cultivation of the real (the four truths) is a means of knowledge.
- [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md): Perception of the self and other substances from a special conjunction of self and mind (VS 9.13–14); of yogins in concentration (yukta) and out of it (viyukta) (Praśastapāda).
- [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md): The perception of one engaged in yoga arises from samādhi (NBh 1.1.3).
- [Navya-Nyāya](../lineages/navya-nyaya.md): Yogaja, one of the three extraordinary contacts.

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- analogous: [alaukika-pratyakṣa](alaukika-pratyaksa.md) — Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika also admit yogic perception (yogaja, a kind of extraordinary perception); the Buddhists ground it in cultivation of a real object, not in a special contact.
