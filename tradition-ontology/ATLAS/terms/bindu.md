# bindu

`trm:bindu` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** बिन्दु
**Literal:** drop, dot, point; semen

## Definitions by tradition
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The vital essence (semen; with rajas in women), identified with the moon (Amṛtasiddhi); 'death by the fall of bindu, life by holding bindu' (HYP 3.88); its control is a sign of success (2.78); mind, breath and bindu are made steady together (4.28). Cosmologically bindu is Śiva and rajas Śakti (SS 1.92); Śiva's nature is nāda, bindu and kalā (HYP 4.1).
- [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md): The central point of the Śrīcakra, abode of Kāmeśvara and Kāmeśvarī, the ocean of nectar (Lakṣmīdhara on SL 8); in the Kāmakalāvilāsa the great point in which Śiva's light is reflected in the mirror of self-awareness, and the pair of white and red points.
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): The vital drop (semen/seed) whose retention is life and whose fall is death (HYP 3.88); arising from the mind as ghee from milk (Candrāvalokana).
- [Mantraśāstra (the science of mantras: the smārta-tantric mantra manuals)](../lineages/mantrasastra.md): The 'point' arising from nāda (ŚT 1.7), which divides into bindu, nāda and bīja — Śiva, their union and Śakti (ŚT 1.8–9); in mantras the nasal dot (anusvāra).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The point of Oṃ; the seed-essence (white and red) whose retention the haṭha passages seek; identified with mind.
- [Kerala tantra (temple-tantra tradition of Kerala)](../lineages/kerala-tantra.md): The point arising from Nāda in the Śāradātilaka's account of emanation.
- [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md): The point of light seen or meditated (VBT 36-37) and a subtle phase of a mantra's utterance (VBT 42, 90).
- [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md): Pure māyā (śuddha-māyā, mahāmāyā, kuṭilā): the pure material cause of the pure universe, of the bodies of pure beings and of sound; distinct from Śiva, who does not change.
