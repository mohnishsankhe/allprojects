# prasāda

`trm:prasada` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** प्रसाद
**Literal:** grace, favour

## Definitions by tradition
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Viṣṇu's grace, without which there is no liberation; lowest when won by ritual (giving heaven), middling by hearing etc., highest by fullness of knowledge (giving liberation); it reaches lower beings through the higher.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Attained by the disciplined one who moves among objects with senses free of passion and aversion (2.64); in it all sorrows cease and understanding quickly becomes steady (2.65).
- [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md): Purity of mind achieved by Śiva's grace; whatever is offered to Śiva is his prasāda, and eating only what has been offered purifies the mind (SSM 11.2; 11.6–11); devotion and grace arise from each other like seed and sprout (9.11–12).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: (1) Grace: by the Lord's grace the one who takes refuge attains the eternal, imperishable state even while acting (18.56), crosses all difficulties (18.58), and attains supreme peace and the eternal abode (18.62); Arjuna regains memory by Kṛṣṇa's grace (18.73); Sañjaya heard the dialogue by Vyāsa's grace (18.75). (2) Clarity: clarity of mind (manaḥ-prasāda) is mental austerity (17.16); sāttvic happiness is born of the clarity of ātma-buddhi, one's own understanding or the understanding of the self (18.37).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Two senses: the serenity of mind of one who moves among objects free of attraction and aversion (BhG 2.64–65, 18.37); and the Lord's grace, by which one attains the eternal abode (18.56, 18.62), sees the universal form (11.47) and regains memory (18.73).
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): The Lord's grace (īśaprasāda), by which alone the end of suffering is reached; also listed as the fifth means. As 'clarity of mind' (mateḥ prasāda) it is one of the strengths.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Grace, favour: Arjuna repeatedly asks the Lord to be gracious (prasīda, 11.25, 11.31, 11.45) and seeks his grace (prasādaye, 11.44); the Lord, gracious (prasanna), showed him the supreme form (11.47).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Serenity; grace: 'through the serenity of the constituents' (dhātuprasāda, KU 1.2.20) or 'through the grace of the creator' (dhātuḥ prasāda, ŚU 3.20); by the serenity of knowledge one sees the partless (MuU 3.1.8); by God's grace Śvetāśvatara knew brahman (ŚU 6.21); by serenity of mind one destroys karma (MaiU 6.20, 6.34).
- [Dādū Panth](../lineages/dadu-panth.md): Grace received from the guru met in the unseen.
- [Sant Mat (Radhasoami and its successor lines)](../lineages/sant-mat.md): Parshād: food or grace blessed by the Master.
