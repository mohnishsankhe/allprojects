# japa

`trm:japa` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** जप
**Literal:** muttered repetition

## Definitions by tradition
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Repetition of the praṇava together with contemplation of Īśvara, making the mind one-pointed (1.28, YBh 1.28); self-study includes repetition of purifying formulas (YBh 2.1).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Muttered recitation, counted as a sacrifice: 'of sacrifices I am the japa-yajña' (10.25).
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): Repetition of a mantra; audible, whispered and mental japa are ten, a hundred and a thousand times better than ritual sacrifice (MDh 2.85–87).
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): Muttering of the five brahma-mantras (the 'five purifiers') and the Raudrī Gāyatrī; the last limb of the offering; mental for the Ratnaṭīkā.
- [Mantraśāstra (the science of mantras: the smārta-tantric mantra manuals)](../lineages/mantrasastra.md): Repetition of a mantra: loud (vācika), whispered (upāṃśu) or mental (mānasa), each superior to the previous (Vāyavīya Saṃhitā quoted in the Padārthādarśa).
- [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md): Repeated contemplation of the supreme state; the self-arising sound is what is repeated (VBT 144-145); the liberated's conversation is japa (Śiva Sūtra 3.27).
