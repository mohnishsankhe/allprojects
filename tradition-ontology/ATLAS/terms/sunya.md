# śūnya

`trm:sunya` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** शून्य
**Literal:** empty

## Definitions by tradition
- [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md): Empty of own-nature: said of every dharma because none is not dependently arisen (MMK 24.19); yet 'empty' itself is said only for the sake of designation and is not to be asserted as a thesis (22.11).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The void: a name of suṣumnā's path (śūnyapadavī, HYP 3.3–4); the breath dissolves in the void (4.10); the nāda stages pass through śūnya, atiśūnya and mahāśūnya (4.70–75); 'empty within, empty without' (4.56).
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): The void: the breath's path through the void becomes the royal road (HYP 3.3); the mind made 'void of void' in unmanī (KJN 14.84); in Hindi Nāth sayings 'sunn', the place of the supreme.
- [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md): Void as an object of contemplation leading beyond itself (VBT 32, 39-40, 43-48, 58): 'by the void power one goes to voidness'.
- [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md): The void as a limited subject-position (śūnya-pramātṛ), subordinate to consciousness (ĪPK 3.2.12-15).
- [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md): The void or empty sky (sunn, śūnya-maṇḍal) in the head where the Lord is met — a Nāth-derived name for the highest state, not a Buddhist emptiness.
- [Sant Mat (Radhasoami and its successor lines)](../lineages/sant-mat.md): Sunn (with Mahāsunn above it): the region of the soul's self-realization above Trikuṭī, where the sārangī is heard.
- [Śaraṇa–vacana tradition](../lineages/sarana-vacana.md): In Allama Prabhu's teaching the void in which liṅga, aṅga and all attainments are no longer two; the 'attainment of the void' gives the Śūnyasampādane its name.

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- contested: [śūnyatā](sunyata.md) — Allama's śūnya is Śiva's own void-fullness (liṅga as bayalu); interpreters in the tradition reject its identification with Buddhist emptiness, though the word is shared
