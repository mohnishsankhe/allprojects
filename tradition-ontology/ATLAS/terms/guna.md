# guṇa

`trm:guna` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** गुण
**Literal:** strand, constituent, quality

## Definitions by tradition
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): The three constituents of prakṛti — sattva, rajas, tamas — of the nature of pleasure, pain and delusion, serving illumination, activity and restraint, and functioning by mutual domination, support, production and pairing (SK 12).
- [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md): What resides in a substance, has no qualities and is not an independent cause of conjunction and disjunction (VS 1.1.15 C); twenty-four (Praśastapāda).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: The guṇas born of prakṛti make everyone act helplessly (3.5); actions are done by the guṇas, and the deluded think 'I am the doer' (3.27); the knower thinks 'the guṇas move among the guṇas' (3.28); the Vedas have the three guṇas as their domain, and Arjuna is to be free of them (2.45); desire and anger arise from the guṇa rajas (3.37).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7–9: The states (bhāva) of sattva, rajas and tamas come from the Lord, yet he is not in them (7.12); deluded by these three guṇa-made states the world does not recognize him, who is beyond them (7.13); his māyā is 'made of the guṇas' (7.14).
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): Strand: the nine-gated lotus is 'covered with three strands' (AVŚ 10.8.43).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The three strands born of prakṛti — sattva, rajas and tamas — which bind the embodied one (BhG 14.5), act upon one another as the true agents (3.27–28), and classify faith, food, austerity, giving, knowledge, action, agent, intellect, steadfastness and happiness (chs. 17–18).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): The three constituents of the seen — sattva (illumination), rajas (activity), tamas (inertia) (2.18); their conflicting activity makes all suffering for the discerning (2.15); everything is a particular arrangement of them (4.13); at kaivalya they return to their source (4.34).
- [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md): Lineage contribution (Āyurveda): the twenty qualities in ten opposed pairs — heavy/light, cold/hot, unctuous/dry, slow/sharp, stable/mobile, soft/hard, clear/slimy, smooth/rough, subtle/gross, dense/liquid (Ca Sū 25.36; AHS Sū 1.18); the qualities as a category (Ca Sū 1.49); and the three guṇas of the mind (Śā 4.36).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: The division of guṇas and actions according to which the Lord made the fourfold class-order (4.13); the guṇas are not further explained in chs. 4–6 (rajas is named in 6.27).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: the guṇas arise from prakṛti (13.20, 14.5); puruṣa experiences them and attachment to them causes birth (13.22); the knowable is without guṇas yet their experiencer (13.15). Chapter 14: the three guṇas — sattva, rajas and tamas — bind the embodied one (14.5–9), prevail by overpowering one another (14.10), show their signs (14.11–13), shape what follows death (14.14–15, 14.18) and the fruit of action (14.16–17); one who sees no agent but the guṇas and knows what is beyond them attains Kṛṣṇa's state (14.19); the embodied one who goes beyond them attains the immortal (14.20). The aśvattha's branches are swollen by the guṇas (15.2).
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): The three qualities of the self — sattva, rajas, tamas — determining destinies (MDh 12.24–51).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The three qualities belong to the intellect, not to the self; everything is classed by them with a fourth, attributeless class belonging to the Lord; one conquers rajas and tamas by sattva and sattva by desirelessness.
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The three qualities of Prakṛti (sattva, rajas, tamas) by which devotion (BhP 3.29), Purāṇas (Matsya 53.68-69) and the measures of Oṃ (MkP 42.4) are classified.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: Faith (17.2), food (17.7–10), sacrifice (17.11–13), austerity (17.17–19), giving (17.20–22), relinquishment (18.7–9), knowledge, action and agent (18.19–28), understanding and steadiness (18.29–35) and happiness (18.36–39) are each threefold by the guṇas; no being on earth or among the gods is free of the three guṇas born of prakṛti (18.40); the actions of the four varṇas are distributed by the guṇas arising from their nature (18.41).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Quality, strand: God's power hidden by its own guṇas (ŚU 1.3); the jīva is bound to the guṇas and has three guṇas (ŚU 5.7); tamas, rajas and sattva as successive states and as Rudra, Brahmā and Viṣṇu (MaiU 5.2).
- [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md): In horā, the planets carry the three qualities: Moon, Sun and Jupiter sattva; Mercury and Venus rajas; Mars and Saturn tamas (BJ 2.7); a sāttvika, rājasa or tāmasa person is recognized by his conduct (BS 68.8–9).
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): In Jain usage an inherent quality of a substance, present in all its modes and itself without qualities (TS 5.38, 5.41).
