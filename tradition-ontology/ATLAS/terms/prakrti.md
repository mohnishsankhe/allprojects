# prakṛti

`trm:prakrti` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** प्रकृति
**Literal:** that which brings forth; nature, source

## Definitions by tradition
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): The unconscious, productive principle consisting of the three guṇas in equilibrium (SS 1.61), from which mahat and the other principles emerge (SK 22); one and common to all puruṣas (SK 11); inferred from its effects (SK 8, 15–16); it acts for puruṣa's experience and liberation and binds and releases itself (SK 57–63). In SK 3 'prakṛti' also names any principle that produces another (the seven prakṛti-vikṛtis).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Material nature, the seen, of three guṇas; in YS 4.2–3 'prakṛti' (plural) are the material causes whose overflow transforms beings.
- [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md): Lineage contribution (Āyurveda): (1) bodily constitution — the doṣa predominating from conception, giving vāta-, pitta-, kapha-, mixed or balanced natures (Ca Sū 7.39-40; Vi 8.95; AHS Sū 1.9-10); (2) the normal state, i.e. balance of the dhātus (Ca Sū 9.4); (3) the eightfold prakṛti of the person's principles (Ca Śā 1.63); (4) for Suśruta 'in medicine' nature, the Lord, time, chance, destiny and transformation are held to be the source (Su Śā 1.11).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: The guṇas born of prakṛti make everyone act (3.5); actions are done by the guṇas of prakṛti (3.27); beings deluded by its guṇas are attached to their workings (3.29); one's own nature, which even the knower follows (3.33).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The Lord's nature: the lower, eightfold prakṛti and the higher life-element (BhG 7.4–5); beginningless, the cause of effect, instrument and agency, from which the guṇas arise (13.19–20); all action is done by its guṇas (3.27); under the Lord's oversight it brings forth the world (9.10).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The unmanifest pradhāna of three guṇas, cause and effect in one; in Kapila's teaching it is powerless before the self that sees with knowledge, dispassion and devotion.
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): Nature whose properties (suffering, ignorance) belong to it and not to the self (VP 6.7.22-24); the Goddess as the primordial unmanifest Prakṛti (DM 4).
- [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md): Mixed-sattva matter with the three guṇas, eternal and real, the Lord's body, transforming into the twenty-three evolutes; it is also called māyā because it produces wonderful effects.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Nature, primal matter: 'know prakṛti to be māyā' (ŚU 4.10).
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): The eternal material cause of the world, insentient and dependent, presided over by Lakṣmī (as Śrī, Bhū and Durgā); God is the efficient cause only.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: In 4.6 the Lord comes into being presiding over his own prakṛti (svāṃ prakṛtim adhiṣṭhāya).

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- contested: [māyā](maya.md) — Gauḍapāda lists māyā among the synonyms of prakṛti (on SK 22) and Śvetāśvatara Up. 4.10 equates them, but Sāṃkhya holds prakṛti real and independent, while Advaita holds māyā indeterminable and not a second reality.
- exact: [pradhāna](pradhana.md) — In the Yoga texts pradhāna names the unmanifest prakṛti (YBh 2.18–19, YS 3.48).
