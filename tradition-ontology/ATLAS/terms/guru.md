# guru

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** गुरु
**Literal:** heavy, weighty

## Definitions by tradition
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): Teacher: the self is its own chief teacher; yet ignorance-bound persons need a knower of the principles to give knowledge; the avadhūta takes twenty-four teachers from nature; the guru is the helmsman of the human boat.
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): In the Guru Gītā: 'gu' is darkness, 'ru' its remover; the guru is Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśvara and the supreme Brahman; the root of liberation is his grace.
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Īśvara is the teacher (guru) even of the ancients, unlimited by time (1.26); devotion offers all actions to the supreme teacher (paramaguru, YBh 2.1); Vyāsa: in the stages of saṃyama 'yoga itself is the teacher' (YBh 3.6).
- [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md): 'Gu' is what is beyond the qualities and 'ru' what is beyond form; the guru gives that (SSM 15.8–9); Śiva stands present as the guru and the two are not to be distinguished (11.19–21); without devotion to the guru no devotion to Śiva arises (11.22–24).
- [Spanda (the doctrine of vibration)](../lineages/spanda.md): 'The teacher is the means' (Śiva Sūtra 2.6); from the teacher comes awakening to the wheel of letters (2.7).
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): The teacher who is learned in scripture and established in Brahman, through whom alone the knowledge is to be sought (MU 1.2.12); approached with humility, service and questioning.
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): The one who knows the nine groups and consecrates the disciple; a mere knower of texts without faith is a semblance of a teacher.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Teacher: 'he should go to a teacher learned in the Veda and established in brahman' (MuU 1.2.12); devotion to the teacher as to God (ŚU 6.23).
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): The teacher from whose grace the qualified disciple gains the supreme knowledge; the lineage runs from Nārāyaṇa and Vyāsa through Madhva and the maṭha pontiffs; devotion to the teacher as to God is enjoined.
