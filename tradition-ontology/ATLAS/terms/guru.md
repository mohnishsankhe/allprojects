# guru

`trm:guru` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** गुरु
**Literal:** heavy, weighty

## Definitions by tradition
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): Teacher: the self is its own chief teacher; yet ignorance-bound persons need a knower of the principles to give knowledge; the avadhūta takes twenty-four teachers from nature; the guru is the helmsman of the human boat.
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): In the Guru Gītā: 'gu' is darkness, 'ru' its remover; the guru is Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśvara and the supreme Brahman; the root of liberation is his grace.
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Īśvara is the teacher (guru) even of the ancients, unlimited by time (1.26); devotion offers all actions to the supreme teacher (paramaguru, YBh 2.1); Vyāsa: in the stages of saṃyama 'yoga itself is the teacher' (YBh 3.6).
- [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md): 'Gu' is what is beyond the qualities and 'ru' what is beyond form; the guru gives that (SSM 15.8–9); Śiva stands present as the guru and the two are not to be distinguished (11.19–21); without devotion to the guru no devotion to Śiva arises (11.22–24).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Arjuna's venerable elders (Bhīṣma, Droṇa), whom he would rather not kill even if it meant living on alms (2.5).
- [Spanda (the doctrine of vibration)](../lineages/spanda.md): 'The teacher is the means' (Śiva Sūtra 2.6); from the teacher comes awakening to the wheel of letters (2.7).
- [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md): The spiritual teacher relied upon as the root of the path, seen in guru yoga as inseparable from the Buddha (Tsongkhapa as Lobsang Thubwang Dorje Chang).
- [Kadam](../lineages/kadam.md): The good teacher who confers the bodhisattva vow is skilled in the rite, abides in the vow, and has patience and compassion (Lamp 23–24); the tantric master is to be pleased with service and gifts (Lamp 64).
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): The teacher who is learned in scripture and established in Brahman, through whom alone the knowledge is to be sought (MU 1.2.12); approached with humility, service and questioning.
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): The one who knows the nine groups and consecrates the disciple; a mere knower of texts without faith is a semblance of a teacher.
- [Kaula (the Kula tradition)](../lineages/kaula.md): 'Gu' is darkness and 'ru' its remover (Kulārṇava 17.7); the guru is father, mother and Maheśvara (12.49); rare is the guru who removes the disciple's suffering (13.108).
- [Nyingma (the Ancient / Old Translation school)](../lineages/nyingma.md): The lama (bla ma), root of blessings and the first of the three roots, to be examined before and followed after accepting; visualized as Padmasambhava in guru yoga.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Arjuna calls the Lord the world's object of worship and its 'weightiest teacher' (guru garīyān) (11.43).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Teacher: 'he should go to a teacher learned in the Veda and established in brahman' (MuU 1.2.12); devotion to the teacher as to God (ŚU 6.23).
- [The Mahāsiddhas (the eighty-four great adepts)](../lineages/mahasiddha.md): The 'true guru' (bla ma dam pa), from whose mouth alone the innate is found (Saraha, Tōh 2292 no. 6; People Dohā v19), who points out mind's nature 'as a finger points at the sky'.
- [Vajrayāna (Mantranaya) in India](../lineages/vajrayana.md): The vajra master, to be examined before the bond and then regarded as inseparable from Vajradhara.
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): The teacher from whose grace the qualified disciple gains the supreme knowledge; the lineage runs from Nārāyaṇa and Vyāsa through Madhva and the maṭha pontiffs; devotion to the teacher as to God is enjoined.
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): Tamil kuru, ācāṉ, āriyaṉ: for the Siddhars the guru comes of his own accord, shows his own form and burns the bonds; guru and Lord are one (Tirumūla ñāṉam 6).
- [Kagyu (the Kagyu transmissions: Marpa / Dakpo Kagyu, with the Shangpa grouped by name)](../lineages/kagyu.md): bla ma: the root of blessing and the one who points out the mind; 'devotion is the head of meditation'; qualified by the three trainings and experience; seeing mind directly 'is the guru's kindness' (Tilopa).
- [Bāul](../lineages/baul.md): The living guru (also murśid, sāṃi) through whom alone the Man of the Heart is found; Bāuls distinguish the initiating guru (dīkṣā-guru) and the instructing guru (śikṣā-guru).
- [Kartābhajā](../lineages/kartabhaja.md): The Mahāśay, in whom the Kartā is present; 'the guru is truth'.
