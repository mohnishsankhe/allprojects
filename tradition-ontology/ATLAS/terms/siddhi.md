# siddhi

`trm:siddhi` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** सिद्धि
**Literal:** accomplishment

## Definitions by tradition
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): Yogic power: eighteen are listed (eight chief, ten from the guṇas) plus lesser ones; they are obstacles and a waste of time for one seeking the Lord.
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): In the Sāṃkhya Kārikā, the eight accomplishments conducive to knowledge: reasoning, words (hearing), study, the three destructions of suffering, gaining friends and giving (SK 51) — not supernormal powers, which Sāṃkhya calls aiśvarya (SK 23).
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): Powers arising from birth, herbs, mantras, austerity or samādhi (4.1); those from samādhi are obstacles in samādhi but powers in emergence (3.37).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): Success in yoga, and the powers that arise (distant hearing and seeing, speed, invisibility, turning metal to gold, flight — DYŚ 89–90) which the yogin must regard as obstacles and hide (DYŚ 91–94).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Success, as against failure, to which one is to be equal (2.48); perfection, not reached by renunciation alone (3.4) but reached by Janaka and others through action (saṃsiddhi, 3.20).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): Powers arising from dhāraṇā - eighteen in the Bhāgavata, eight aiśvaryas in MkP and LiP - which are obstacles to the highest goal.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7–9: Among thousands hardly one strives for perfection, and of the striving/perfected hardly one knows the Lord in truth (7.3); the great-souled reach the highest perfection (saṃsiddhi) and are not reborn (8.15).
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): The fifth gain; also the marks of perfection of the yogin (not subject to others, invulnerable, unaging, unobstructed etc.).
- [Vajrayāna (Mantranaya) in India](../lineages/vajrayana.md): Attainment: the common (mundane) attainments — powers such as flight, the sword, the elixir, invisibility, the eight great siddhis — and the supreme attainment, mahāmudrā / buddhahood.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: The success of actions sought by those who sacrifice to deities, which comes quickly in the human world (4.12); the unbound one is the same in success and failure (4.22).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Perfection, accomplishment of the goal, attained even by doing actions for the Lord's sake (12.10).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 13–15: Perfection, attainment: the highest perfection (parā siddhi) to which the sages went by the highest knowledge (14.1) — here the goal, not a yogic power.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): A power arising from practice; contrived or spontaneous; an obstacle to hide, or the test of a siddha.
- [Spanda (the doctrine of vibration)](../lineages/spanda.md): Powers arising in yoga (Śiva Sūtra 1.19-20; SK 3.1-7), which, gained under delusion, fall short of liberation (Śiva Sūtra 3.6).
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): Tamil citti: the powers won by yoga and kāya-citti — above all the eight great powers (aṭṭamā citti) of the Tirumantiram; Kaṭuveḷi says they come to anyone only by Śiva's action, and Tāyumāṉavar holds them easier than stilling the mind.
