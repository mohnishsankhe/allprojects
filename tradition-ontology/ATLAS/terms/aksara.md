# akṣara

`trm:aksara` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** अक्षर
**Literal:** imperishable

## Definitions by tradition
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): Lakṣmī, whose body is imperishable; beyond her is Hari (Puruṣottama).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 7–9: Brahman is the supreme imperishable (8.3); the imperishable that knowers of the Veda declare, which passionless ascetics enter and for which people practise brahmacarya (8.11); the unmanifest beyond the cosmic unmanifest is called the imperishable, the supreme goal from which there is no return, the Lord's supreme abode (8.20–21). Oṃ is the 'one-syllabled' (ekākṣara) Brahman (8.13).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: The imperishable: Arjuna names the Lord 'the imperishable, the supreme to be known' (11.18) and 'the imperishable — being and non-being and what is beyond' (11.37); in ch. 12 'the imperishable, the unmanifest' is the object of a worship distinct from devotion to the Lord, described as indefinable, unmanifest, all-pervading, unthinkable, unchanging, immovable and constant (12.1, 12.3). In 10.25 and 10.33 akṣara means 'syllable, letter'.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The imperishable: that on which space is woven, described by negations (BAU 3.8.8), by whose command sun and moon stand apart (3.8.9); the object of the higher knowledge (MuU 1.1.5); also 'syllable', especially Om (ChU 1.1.1; KU 1.2.16; MāU 1).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 15.16: of the two persons in the world, the one standing firm (kūṭastha) is called the imperishable; Kṛṣṇa is higher even than the imperishable (15.18). What the akṣara denotes here is read differently by the commentators.
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): The syllable in the highest heaven on which the verses rest and the gods sit (RV 1.164.39); the thousand-syllabled cow of speech (1.164.41).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The imperishable supreme Brahman (BhG 8.3), the goal entered by ascetics (8.11); the unchanging (kūṭastha) imperishable puruṣa (15.16), surpassed by the highest person (15.18); also the syllable (Oṃ, 'the one syllable', 8.13, 10.25).
- [Vedāṅga (the six limbs of the Veda)](../lineages/vedanga.md): A syllable or phoneme (akṣarasamāmnāya, the list of phonemes, PŚ 57).
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): The imperishable syllable Oṃ, which is Brahman and Prajāpati, while all rites pass away (MDh 2.84).
- [Kālacakra tradition in India](../lineages/kalacakra.md): The imperishable: the unchanging bliss and gnosis of the Kālacakra ('the supreme imperishable', Sekoddeśa 77).
- [Vajrayāna (Mantranaya) in India](../lineages/vajrayana.md): The letter/syllable: 'A, foremost of letters, the supreme imperishable' (Nāmasaṃgīti 28).
- [Vyākaraṇa (the grammarian-philosophers; śabdādvaita)](../lineages/vyakarana.md): The imperishable Word-Brahman (VP 1.1) and the syllable/letter.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: In 3.15 the imperishable from which brahman arises (in the chain: action from brahman, brahman from the imperishable); its identification is disputed among commentators.
