# amṛta

`trm:amrta` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** अमृत
**Literal:** not-dying; immortality; nectar

## Definitions by tradition
- [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md): Immortality and the immortal: of the gods, of the fathers' world, of soma; 'may I be freed from death, not from immortality' (RV 7.59.12).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The nectar of immortality dripping from the moon in the head, lost to the sun's fire unless saved by viparītakaraṇī, jālandhara and khecarī (HYP 3.72, 3.77–79; DYŚ 127–128); drinking it the yogin conquers death.
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): The nectar of immortality flowing from the moon at the head; retaining it the body does not decay (GŚ 57–66).
- [Yoginī Kaula (Yoginīkaula) of Matsyendra](../lineages/yogini-kaula.md): The nectar without which there is no immortality; 'the Kaula nature, of the nature of kāmakalā' (KJN 14.94).
- [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md): Tamil amirtam: the nectar of immortality; for the Siddhars the inner nectar that knowers drink (Tiruvaḷḷuvar ñāṉam 10) and that flows from the moon in the head.
- [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md): The nectar that rains in the sky-circle of the head in the encounter with the Lord; also the guru as 'mine of nectar'.

## Forms in other languages
- Pali: amata  — partial
- Tibetan: bdud rtsi  — partial — nectar in Vajrayāna usage
- Hindi: amī / amṛt  — exact
- Tamil: amirtam அமிர்தம் — exact

## Equivalents (interpretation layer)
- contested: [mokṣa](moksa.md) — The Saṃhitās' immortality is chiefly full life and the immortal world of light; the Upaniṣads and Vedānta distinguish mokṣa from heavenly immortality. Recorded as contested.
