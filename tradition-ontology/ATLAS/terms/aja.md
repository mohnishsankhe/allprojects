**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** अज
**Literal:** unborn

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Said of the embodied self: unborn, eternal, everlasting, primeval (2.20); knowing it as unborn one sees that it neither slays nor is slain (2.21).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 4–6: Said by Kṛṣṇa of himself: though unborn and of imperishable self, and lord of beings, he comes into being through his own māyā (4.6).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Unborn: the Lord is to be known as unborn and without beginning, the great lord of the world; whoever knows him so is undeluded and freed from all sins (10.3); Arjuna calls him unborn (10.12).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The unborn (she-goat): one unborn female, red, white and black, producing many like herself, and two unborn males who enjoy and leave her (ŚU 4.5; cf. 1.9).
