# piṇḍa

`trm:pinda` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** पिण्ड
**Literal:** lump, body

## Definitions by tradition
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): The body as the microcosm of the whole cosmos; the SSP's six upadeśas are organised around the piṇḍa (its origin, knowledge, vision, support and samarasa with the pada).
- [Yoginī Kaula (Yoginīkaula) of Matsyendra](../lineages/yogini-kaula.md): The 'place' among the four (piṇḍa, pada, rūpa, rūpātīta) of the Kula aim (KJN 3.4).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The body as microcosm containing Meru, the continents, stars and gods (SS 2.1–5).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Arjuna says that when classes are mixed the ancestors fall, deprived of the offerings of rice-balls and water (piṇḍodaka-kriyā, 1.42).
- [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md): The rice-ball offered to the dead and ancestors; the ten daily piṇḍas build the preta's body and the monthly ones feed him on the road.
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): The rice-ball offered to the father, grandfather and great-grandfather in the ancestral rites.
- [Saṅgītaśāstra (the science of music: song, instrument and dance)](../lineages/sangita.md): The body as the place where nāda becomes manifest; hence the Saṅgītaratnākara's section on 'the origin of the body' (piṇḍotpatti) (ŚR 1.2.3).
- [Pañcācārya (Pañcapīṭha) tradition](../lineages/pancacarya.md): The first sub-sthala: the embodied soul made fit for the path by the ripening of karma and Śiva's grace (SSM 5.27–30; cf. 5.34–36).
