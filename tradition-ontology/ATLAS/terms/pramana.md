# pramāṇa

`trm:pramana` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** प्रमाण
**Literal:** means of valid cognition

## Definitions by tradition
- [Pātañjala Yoga (the Yoga darśana)](../lineages/patanjala-yoga.md): The first activity of mind: valid cognition, of three kinds — perception, inference and testimony (YS 1.7).
- [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md): The instrument of valid cognition; four: perception, inference, comparison and verbal testimony (NS 1.1.3). That by which the knower knows; purposeful because it makes action succeed (NBh 1.1.1).
- [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md): Two only: perception and inference, verbal knowledge being explained as inference (VS 9.18–19 in Candrānanda's recension; Praśastapāda).
- [Navya-Nyāya](../lineages/navya-nyaya.md): The special cause (karaṇa) of a valid cognition; the four khaṇḍas of the Tattvacintāmaṇi follow the four means.
- [Dvaita Vedānta (Tattvavāda; the Mādhva sampradāya)](../lineages/dvaita.md): What accords with its object (yathārtha); twofold — valid knowledge itself (kevala) and its means (anupramāṇa).
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): Three are accepted — perception, inference and reliable testimony — since all others are included in them (SK 4); Vācaspati reduces postulation, absence, inclusion and tradition to these.
- [Pāśupata (Pāñcārthika Pāśupata)](../lineages/pasupata.md): Three: perception (sensory and of the self), inference (as-before, from-the-remainder, seen-in-general), and scripture.
- [Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/bhatta-mimamsa.md): A cognition that apprehends what was not apprehended before and is free from defects in its causes and from contradicting cognition (Śāstradīpikā); six in number.
- [Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā](../lineages/prabhakara-mimamsa.md): Every experience (anubhūti), as distinct from memory; five in number (Prakaraṇapañcikā).
- [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md): Three are accepted — perception, inference and scripture; all of them apprehend qualified objects, none an undifferentiated reality.
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): Means of valid knowledge: six in Advaita (perception, inference, comparison, testimony, presumption, non-cognition), all valid empirically and all within the sphere of ignorance; scripture alone reveals Brahman.
- [Cārvāka / Lokāyata](../lineages/carvaka.md): (Lokāyata, reported variously) One (perception); or not fixable in number (suśikṣita); or none establishable (Jayarāśi).
- [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md): In Jain epistemology valid knowledge itself — the five knowledges (TS 1.10); knowledge illuminating itself and its object, free from contradiction (Nyāyāvatāra 1); the right ascertainment of an object (Pramāṇamīmāṃsā 1.1.2).
