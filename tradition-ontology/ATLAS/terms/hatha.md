# haṭha

`trm:hatha` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** हठ
**Literal:** force, violence; obstinacy

## Definitions by tradition
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The yoga of force: the physical methods — postures, retentions, mudrās and bandhas, cleansing acts — that force the breath into the central channel and awaken kuṇḍalinī; taught 'solely for the sake of rājayoga' (HYP 1.2) and inseparable from it (HYP 2.76). The Yogabīja and the Jyotsnā gloss ha as the sun and ṭha as the moon, haṭha being their union, i.e. of prāṇa and apāna. The Śārṅgadhara Paddhati distinguishes Gorakṣa's haṭha from that of Mṛkaṇḍu's son.
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): 'Force': the yoga of the body and breath taught by Ādinātha as a stairway to rājayoga (HYP 1.1); later texts gloss ha and ṭha as sun and moon.
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): Haṭha-yoga: eight limbs with mudrās; in the Yogaśikhā, the union of ha (sun) and ṭha (moon).
