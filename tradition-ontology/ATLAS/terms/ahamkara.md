# ahaṃkāra

`trm:ahamkara` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** अहंकार
**Literal:** I-maker

## Definitions by tradition
- [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md): The evolute of mahat whose nature is self-assertion (abhimāna); from it proceed the eleven organs (from its vaikṛta, sāttvika form) and the five subtle elements (from its bhūtādi, tāmasa form), both with its taijasa form (SK 24–25). It, not puruṣa, is the agent (SS 6.54).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Ego, one of the eight constituents of the lower nature (BhG 7.4); the deluded by ego think 'I am the doer' (3.27); free of 'mine' and ego one attains peace (2.71); in the Nārāyaṇīya, Aniruddha is called ahaṃkāra (12.327.26).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The I-maker: the 'teaching of I' (ahaṃkārādeśa) precedes the teaching of the self (ChU 7.25.1); listed among the faculties resting in the self (PrU 4.8); the jīva is endowed with intention and ahaṃkāra (ŚU 5.8).
- [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md): The sense and grasping of 'I'; with 'mine'-making it ceases when self and mine are pacified (MMK 18.2–4); grasping at aggregates brings 'I'-making, action and birth (RĀ 1.35); the aggregates arise from it (RĀ 1.29).
- [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md): The notion 'I' and 'mine', at whose arising the mind is to go to the supportless (VBT 131); in the Krama it is dissolved in the phases of Kālāgnirudra and Mahākāla (TĀ 4.161-168).
