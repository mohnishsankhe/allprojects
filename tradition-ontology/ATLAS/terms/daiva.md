# daiva

`trm:daiva` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** दैव
**Literal:** divine, fate

## Definitions by tradition
- [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md): Fate: in the Yoga Vāsiṣṭha nothing but one's own former effort (prāk-pauruṣa); a name given to the fruit of one's deeds, without form, act or power of its own; relying on it is delusion.
- [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md): Lineage contribution (Āyurveda): one's own deed of a former body (Ca Vi 3.30; Śā 1.116; 2.44); balanced against present effort (Vi 3.29-36).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): The divine or fated factor, the fifth cause of action (BhG 18.14); which does not bear fruit without human effort, as a seed without a field (MBh 13.6.7).
- [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md): In jyotiṣa, fate: karma, good and bad, produced in another birth, which yields birth in a good family and the like, and bears fruit together with human effort, as in farming (Yogayātrā 1.3–4); it is what the horā reveals (BJ 1.3).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: The divine as the fifth factor of every action, beside seat, agent, instruments and activities (18.14); the verse does not explain it (commentators: the presiding deities, unseen merit, or the Lord within). Also 'divine' in 'the divine creation' of beings (16.6).
