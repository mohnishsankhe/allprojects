# dhātu

`trm:dhatu` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** धातु
**Literal:** that which upholds; constituent

## Definitions by tradition
- [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md): The seven bodily tissues — rasa, blood, flesh, fat, bone, marrow and semen — called 'the vitiable' (dūṣya) (AHS Sū 1.13); each is formed from the preceding by its own fire, with essence and waste (Ca Ci 15.15-16); in Caraka's wider usage every body-constituent whose balance is health (Sū 1.53; 9.4); in Śā 1.16 the six 'dhātus' of the person are the five elements and consciousness.
- [Vedāṅga (the six limbs of the Veda)](../lineages/vedanga.md): A verbal root, listed in the Dhātupāṭha (Aṣṭādhyāyī 1.3.1: 'bhū and the rest are roots').
- [Siddha medicine (cittā maruttuvam)](../lineages/siddha-medicine.md): In Siddha medicine the seven body-constituents (uṭal tātukkaḷ): cāram, ceṉnīr, ūṉ, koḻuppu, eṉpu, mūḷai, cukkilam/curōṇitam; in pharmacy tātu also names the mineral-metal class of drugs.
