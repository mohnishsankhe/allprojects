# saṃnyāsa

`trm:samnyasa` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** संन्यास
**Literal:** renunciation, laying down

## Definitions by tradition
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 1–3: Perfection is not reached by renunciation (saṃnyasana) alone (3.4); Arjuna is to renounce all actions in Kṛṣṇa and fight (3.30).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Laying aside actions prompted by desire (BhG 18.2); mere renunciation of action does not bring perfection (3.4), and renunciation is hard without yoga (5.6); the true renouncer neither hates nor desires (5.3; 6.1).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 5–6: renunciation of actions (karma-saṃnyāsa) and the yoga of action both bring the highest good, the latter being superior (5.2); the constant renunciant (nitya-saṃnyāsin) is the one who neither hates nor desires (5.3); renunciation is hard to attain without yoga (5.6); the self-controlled embodied one renounces all actions with the mind (5.13); the renunciant is the one who does the required action without depending on its fruit, not the one without sacred fire or rites (6.1); what is called renunciation is yoga (6.2). 4.41 speaks of actions renounced through yoga.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 9.28: 'saṃnyāsa-yoga': being yoked in oneself (-yuktātmā) by the yoga of renunciation — here the offering of all acts to the Lord of 9.27 — frees one from the bonds of action so that one comes to him.
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 16–18: Renunciation: the seers understand it as the laying aside of desire-prompted actions (18.2); renunciation of allotted action is not proper (18.7); through renunciation one with unattached understanding attains the supreme perfection of actionlessness (18.49); Arjuna is to renounce all actions in the Lord 'in thought' (cetasā, 18.57).
- [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md): Renunciation of rites and possessions after depositing the fires in oneself (MDh 6.38); the renouncer should never renounce the Veda (VDh 10.4).
- [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md): Bhagavad Gītā 10–12: Laying down: those who lay down (saṃnyasya) all actions in the Lord and worship him (12.6).
- [Saṃnyāsa — the renunciate ideal of the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads](../lineages/sannyasa.md): Renunciation of household and rites for the wandering life; truly, the union of jīva and Self.
