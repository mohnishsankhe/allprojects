# rājayoga

`trm:raja-yoga` · `sourced` · confidence moderate

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Native script:** राजयोग
**Literal:** royal yoga

## Definitions by tradition
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The aim for which haṭha is taught (1.2); the text lists it as one of the words for samādhi (4.3-4) and says that on the mind becoming one 'this is called rājayoga' (4.77); all means of haṭha and laya are for its accomplishment (4.103).
- [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md): The goal served by haṭha: samādhi, the mindless, supportless state in which mind and breath are dissolved; HYP 4.3–4 lists it first among the synonyms of samādhi (with unmanī, manonmanī, amanaska, sahaja, turyā…). The DYŚ calls it the best of the four yogas, the Śiva Saṃhitā 'free of duality'; the Jyotsnā identifies it with asamprajñāta yoga, the cessation of all mental activity.
- [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md): The royal yoga, the goal to which haṭha leads; synonymous with samādhi, unmanī, sahaja and the rest (HYP 4.3–4).
- [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md): The fourth of the four yogas; in the Yogaśikhā the union of rajas and retas.
