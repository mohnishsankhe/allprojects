# Lalitā Sahasranāma

`src:lalita-sahasranama` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Śrī Lalitā Sahasranāma Stotra, Rahasya-nāma-sāhasra
**Original title:** ललितासहस्रनाम
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md), [Śākta traditions](../lineages/sakta.md), [The Purāṇic tradition (paurāṇika)](../lineages/puranic.md)
**Genre:** stotra (sahasranāma)
**Part of:** [Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa](brahmanda-purana.md)
**Location in parent:** said to be in the Uttarakhaṇḍa of the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, in the Hayagrīva-Agastya dialogue (colophon)
**Authors:** 
  - [The eight Vāgdevatās (Vaśinī and the others)](../teachers/vagdevatas.md) — role: author; attribution: traditional
  - [Hayagrīva](../teachers/hayagriva.md) — role: revealer; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: Composed at Lalitā's command by the eight goddesses of speech (Vaśinī and the others), who knew the secret of her cakra; named the 'Rahasya-nāma-sāhasra'; taught by Hayagrīva to Agastya (pūrvabhāga 32-33 in Bhāskararāya's numbering).; scholarly: Not part of the main printed Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa; a Śrīvidyā composition of uncertain date, authoritative by the time of Bhāskararāya (18th c.).; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Scholarly account: date uncertain; before the 18th c.; (confidence low)
**Structure:** An introductory part (pūrvabhāga, 51 verses in Bhāskararāya's count), the thousand names in 182½ anuṣṭubh verses with no name repeated and no filler particles, and a concluding part (phalaśruti); 320 verses in all by Bhāskararāya's count. The stotra proper is usually numbered 1-183.
**Availability:** digitized-original

The thousand names of Lalitā Mahātripurasundarī, framed as Hayagrīva's teaching to Agastya. The names describe her form and weapons, her city and palace, her war on Bhaṇḍāsura, her subtle body as the three sections of the Śrīvidyā mantra, her ascent as kuṇḍalinī through the cakras and knots, her qualityless nature, the three states and the fourth, the five cosmic acts, the Śrīcakra and her oneness with Śiva. Recitation and worship with the names are central Śākta practices.

### purvabhaga.32-33 <a id="tea-lalita-sahasranama-purvabhaga-32-33"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Hayagrīva: commanded by Lalitā, the goddesses of speech composed a supreme hymn of divine secret names; hence it is famed as the 'thousand secret names' (rahasya-nāma-sāhasra).

_level: conventional · standpoint: divine · path: sound, devotion · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission, sound-language_

### uttarabhaga <a id="tea-lalita-sahasranama-uttarabhaga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The concluding section praises the recitation of the thousand names above other rites, lists its fruits, and limits its transmission to devotees of Lalitā.
