# Śrībhāṣya

`src:sribhasya` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Brahmasūtrabhāṣya of Rāmānuja
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta (Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya)](../lineages/visistadvaita.md)
**Genre:** bhāṣya
**Commentary on:** [Brahma Sūtra](brahma-sutra.md)
**Authors:** 
  - [Rāmānuja](../teachers/ramanuja.md) — role: commentator; attribution: accepted
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Rāmānuja, 1017–1137 CE (traditional); Scholarly account: 11th–12th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 4 adhyāyas × 4 pādas, following the sūtras (Rāmānuja's sūtra division differs slightly from Śaṅkara's)
**Availability:** digitized-original

Rāmānuja's commentary on the Brahma Sūtras following, he says, Bodhāyana's lost Vṛtti. Its first sūtra contains the 'great prior view' (mahāpūrvapakṣa) of Advaita and its refutation (mahāsiddhānta), including the seven untenables of avidyā; the whole establishes Brahman as the qualified cause of the world, the soul as its mode, bhakti-meditation as the means and liberation as reaching Brahman by the path of light.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Śrutaprakāśikā](srutaprakasika.md)

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-sribhasya-1-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'Then' (atha) means after the inquiry into ritual action (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā) has been completed, because the knowledge that the fruits of ritual are small and impermanent gives rise to the desire to know Brahman; the two Mīmāṃsās form a single science (śāstra).

### 1.1.1/2 <a id="tea-sribhasya-1-1-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The knowledge that the Upaniṣads enjoin for liberation is not the understanding of a sentence but meditation (dhyāna, upāsana): a steady, unbroken remembrance (dhruvānusmṛti) like a stream of oil, repeated until it becomes as vivid as direct perception (darśana-samānākāra); this remembrance, being supremely dear, is bhakti, and the Lord chooses and reveals himself to the one who is dearest to him ('whom he chooses, by him he is attained').

### 1.1.1/4 <a id="tea-sribhasya-1-1-1-4"></a>

### 1.1.1/5 <a id="tea-sribhasya-1-1-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

No means of knowledge establishes an undifferentiated object: perception, even at its first, non-conceptual (nirvikalpaka) moment, grasps an object with some distinguishing feature; inference rests on perception; and scripture, made of words with stems and suffixes, conveys only qualified meanings. ### 1.1.1/7 <a id="tea-sribhasya-1-1-1-7"></a>

When Brahman is attained through meditation, earlier sins are destroyed and later ones do not cling (uttara-pūrvāghayor aśleṣa-vināśau); merit is similarly shed; only the karma that has begun to bear fruit (prārabdha) remains until the body falls.

### 4.2 <a id="tea-sribhasya-4-2"></a>

At death the knower of Brahman departs through the channel in the head (the hundred-and-first), the heart's opening being lit up by the grace of the Lord dwelling there.

### 4.3 <a id="tea-sribhasya-4-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The liberated soul travels by the path beginning with light (arcirādi) — flame, day, the bright fortnight, the northern course, the year, wind, sun, moon, lightning — and is then led by superhuman escorts (ātivāhika) to Brahman; the path is for all who meditate on Brahman.

_level: conventional · standpoint: cosmic · path: devotion, meditation · stage: realized · types: death-dying, karma-liberation_

### 4.4 <a id="tea-sribhasya-4-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Reaching the highest light, the self manifests its own true nature (svena rūpeṇa abhiniṣpadyate), freed from karma; it experiences itself as inseparable from Brahman, being its mode, and enjoys Brahman with all his qualities.

### 4.4/2 <a id="tea-sribhasya-4-4-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The released self attains equality with Brahman in enjoyment and in all powers except the cosmic functions of creating, sustaining and ruling the world (jagad-vyāpāra-varjam), which belong to the Lord alone.

### introduction <a id="tea-sribhasya-intro"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The extensive commentary (vṛtti) on the Brahma Sūtras composed by the venerable Bodhāyana was abridged by earlier teachers; following their view, Rāmānuja will explain the words of the sūtras.

### maṅgala <a id="tea-sribhasya-mangala"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Rāmānuja prays that his understanding may take the form of devotion (bhakti) to the supreme Brahman, Śrīnivāsa (in whom Śrī dwells), whose play is the creation, maintenance and dissolution of all worlds, whose one vow is to protect the hosts of beings who bow to him, and who shines in the crown of the Veda (the Upaniṣads).
