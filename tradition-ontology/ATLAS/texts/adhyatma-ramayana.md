# Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic

**Attribution:** tradition: Śiva (Mahādeva) narrates Rāma's story to Pārvatī (umā-maheśvara-saṃvāda); as part of the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa its author is Vyāsa; scholarly: an independent Advaitic Rāma text, probably composed c. 14th-15th c. CE, perhaps in Vārāṇasī in a Rāmānandī milieu; a major source for Tulsīdās; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: narrated by Śiva; part of Vyāsa's Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa; Scholarly account: c. 14th-15th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 7 kāṇḍas, 65 sargas in the Gita Press edition (Bāla 7, Ayodhyā 9, Araṇya 10, Kiṣkindhā 9, Sundara 5, Yuddha 16, Uttara 9), preceded by a Māhātmya spoken by Brahmā to Nārada
**Availability:** digitized-original

Retells Rāma's story so that every episode teaches that Rāma is the supreme Brahman and Sītā his power, primal nature (mūlaprakṛti), who performs the events superimposed on him. It contains the Rāma-hṛdaya (1.1), Rāma's teaching to Lakṣmaṇa on māyā, knowledge and devotion (3.4), the nine means of devotion taught to Śabarī (3.10), many hymns, and the Rāma Gītā (7.5), and holds that devotion to Rāma is the means to knowledge and liberation.
