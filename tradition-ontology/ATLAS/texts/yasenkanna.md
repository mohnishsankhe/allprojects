Hakuin's account (1757) of the 'Zen sickness' he contracted from excessive practice and its cure learned from the hermit Hakuyū: introspection (naikan) concentrating the breath and energy in the lower abdomen (tanden, kikai), and the soft-butter method of visualised healing; warns that the method is for restoring health so that practice can continue.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### naikan <a id="tea-yasenkanna-naikan"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Hakuyū's cure: lying on the back, stretching the legs, concentrate the breath and energy in the lower abdomen (tanden, kikai) and the soles of the feet, and contemplate that this below-the-navel field is one's original face, one's own Pure Land, one's own Amitābha; the energy descends, the heart-fire cools, and health returns; practise this 'introspection' (naikan) diligently.

### soft-butter <a id="tea-yasenkanna-soft-butter"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The soft-butter method: imagine a lump of fragrant soft butter placed on the crown of the head, slowly melting and flowing down through the head, shoulders, chest, organs and legs, washing away all accumulated illness and pain, until the whole body is soaked as if in warm medicinal water.

### zen-sickness <a id="tea-yasenkanna-zen-sickness"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Hakuin describes how, after excessive striving, his heart-fire rose, his lungs dried, his legs felt ice-cold, his ears rang, he was fearful and exhausted, sweated and wept — an illness that doctors could not cure; this is 'Zen sickness' (zenbyō).
