## Teachings (20: skeleton 20)

### 1 <a id="tea-wumenguan-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Case 1, Zhaozhou's dog: a monk asked whether a dog has buddha-nature; Zhaozhou said 'wu' (no). Wumen: to study Chan you must pass the barrier of the patriarchs, and this one word 'wu' is it. With your 360 bones and 84,000 pores rouse a mass of doubt and investigate 'wu' day and night; do not take it as nothingness or as the 'no' of being and non-being; it is like swallowing a red-hot iron ball you cannot spit out; all former wrong knowledge is swept away, inside and outside become one piece, like a mute who dreams and knows it only himself; suddenly it bursts open, shaking heaven and earth, and you are free on the brink of birth and death.

> 將三百六十骨節八萬四千毫竅。通身起箇疑團。參箇無字。晝夜提撕。莫作虛無會。莫作有無會

### 2 <a id="tea-wumenguan-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Case 2, Baizhang's fox: an old man had once answered that a person of great practice 'does not fall into cause and effect' and was reborn five hundred times as a fox; Baizhang answered 'does not obscure cause and effect' and he was freed. Wumen: 'not falling' — why a fox? 'not obscuring' — why freed? One with an eye here knows Baizhang won five hundred lives of elegance.

> 不落因果。為甚墮野狐。不昧因果。為甚脫野狐

### 3 <a id="tea-wumenguan-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Case 3, Juzhi raises a finger: whenever asked, Juzhi raised one finger; when a boy imitated him, Juzhi cut off the boy's finger; as the boy ran off crying, Juzhi called him, raised his finger, and the boy awoke. Dying, Juzhi said he had used Tianlong's one-finger Chan all his life without exhausting it.

> 俱胝和尚。凡有詰問。唯舉一指

### 6 <a id="tea-wumenguan-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Case 6, the World-Honoured One holds up a flower: on Vulture Peak the Buddha held up a flower; all were silent, only Mahākāśyapa smiled. The Buddha said: 'I have the treasury of the true Dharma eye, the wondrous mind of nirvāṇa, the true form without form, the subtle Dharma gate, not established on words and letters, a special transmission outside the teachings; I entrust it to Mahākāśyapa.' Wumen: if the eye can be transmitted, the Buddha deceived people; if not, why approve Kāśyapa alone?

> 吾有正法眼藏涅槃妙心實相無相微妙法門。不立文字教外別傳。付囑摩訶迦葉

> 大眾道得即救。道不得即斬却也

### 19 <a id="tea-wumenguan-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Case 19, the ordinary mind is the Way: Zhaozhou asked Nanquan 'what is the Way?' — 'the ordinary mind is the Way' — 'can one aim at it?' — 'to aim is to deviate' — 'without aiming, how know it is the Way?' — 'the Way is not a matter of knowing or not knowing; knowing is delusion, not knowing is blankness; truly reaching the Way beyond doubt, it is vast like space.' Wumen's verse: spring has its hundred flowers, autumn its moon … if nothing idle weighs on the mind, that is the best season.

> 平常心是道。州云。還可趣向否。泉云。擬向即乖

### 47 <a id="tea-wumenguan-47"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Case 47, Doushuai's three barriers: going through the grass to study the mystery is only to see the nature — where is your nature now? Once you know your nature you are free of birth and death — when the light of your eyes falls, how will you be free? Once free of birth and death you know where you are going — when the four elements scatter, where do you go?

> 撥草參玄只圖見性。即今上人性在甚處

### preface <a id="tea-wumenguan-preface"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Wumen's preface: the Buddha's words take mind as the source and the gateless as the Dharma gate; if it is gateless, how will you pass through? What enters by the gate is not the family treasure; what is gained through conditions begins and ends. He used the old cases as 'tiles to knock on the gate'; one who goes straight in with a single sword, careless of danger, cannot be stopped.

> 佛語心為宗。無門為法門。既是無門。且作麼生透

### zenzhen <a id="tea-wumenguan-zenzhen"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Wumen's Admonitions on Chan (appended): following rules and measures is binding oneself without a rope; roaming freely without restraint is heretics and Māra's army; keeping the mind clear and still is the heretical Chan of silent illumination; letting the mind go and forgetting conditions is falling into a deep pit; being alert and unobscured is wearing chains and a cangue; thinking of good and evil is heaven and hell; views of buddha and Dharma are two iron mountains; being aware of thoughts as they arise is toying with spirits; sitting blankly in samādhi is making a living in the ghost cave; neither advancing nor retreating is a dead man breathing.

> 存心澄寂。 默照邪禪
