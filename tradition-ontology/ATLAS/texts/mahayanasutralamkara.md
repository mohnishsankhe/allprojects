A verse summa of the bodhisattva path in the light of Yogācāra: why the Mahāyāna is the Buddha's word, the lineages, the arising of the mind of awakening, reality as neither existent nor non-existent, awakening with its three bodies and four wisdoms, and the meditative stages by which the bodhisattva sees objects as mental speech, rests in mind-only and then abandons even that to reach the first ground.

## Teachings (14: skeleton 14)

### 1.7 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-1-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Reasons why the Mahāyāna is the Buddha's word: it was not predicted (as a danger) at the beginning; it arose together (with the disciples' vehicle); it is not the domain (of reasoners); it is established; if there is (a Mahāyāna) or is not, (either way it is authentic); it is the antidote (to afflictions); and its meaning differs from the letter.

### 3.1-11 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-3-1-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The spiritual lineage (gotra) is shown by its existence, superiority, nature, marks, divisions, dangers, benefits and two similes (gold mine, jewel mine): it is natural and developed, basis and based; its marks are compassion, faith, patience and good conduct; it is determined or undetermined; and some beings are without the lineage — those bent only on misconduct, those whose good qualities are cut off, those whose merit does not lead to liberation, those of weak good, and those lacking the cause.

> aikāntiko duścarite 'sti kaścit kaścit samudghātitaśukladharmā / amokṣabhāgīyaśubho 'sti kaścin nihīnaśuklo 'sty api hetuhīnaḥ

### 6.1 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-6-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The characteristic of the ultimate is that it is neither existent nor non-existent, neither thus nor otherwise; it is not born, does not perish, does not decrease or increase, and is not purified — yet it is purified.

> na san na cāsan na tathā na cānyathā na jāyate vyeti na cāvahīyate / na vardhate nāpi viśudhyate punar viśudhyate tatparamārthalakṣaṇaṃ

### 6.6-8 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-6-6-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Having amassed the accumulations of merit and knowledge, the bodhisattva, well settled in reflection on the teachings, understands that objects arise from mental speech; knowing objects as mere speech he rests in mind-only that appears as them; then the dharmadhātu becomes directly perceived, free of the characteristic of duality. Understanding by insight that nothing exists apart from mind, he then understands the non-existence of mind itself; understanding the non-existence of both, the wise one rests in the dharmadhātu, which is not that (duality).

> arthān sa vijñāya ca jalpamātrān saṃtiṣṭhate tannibhacittamātre / pratyakṣatām eti ca dharmadhātus tasmād viyukto dvayalakṣaṇena

### 9.37 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-9-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Though suchness is undifferentiated in all, when it has become pure it is tathāgatahood; therefore all embodied beings have it as their embryo (tadgarbha).

### 9.59-61 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-9-59-61"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The pure dharmadhātu of the buddhas operates in distinct ways as the natural body, the enjoyment body and the emanation body; the natural body is the basis of the other two; the enjoyment body differs in all realms by assemblies, fields, names and bodies, enjoying the Dharma.

> svābhāviko 'tha sāṃbhogyaḥ kāyo nairmāṇiko 'paraḥ / kāyabhedā hi buddhānāṃ prathamas tu dvayāśrayaḥ

### 9.67-76 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-9-67-76"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mirror-like wisdom is unmoving, and the three other wisdoms depend on it: equality, discernment and accomplishment of what is to be done. Mirror-like wisdom is without 'mine', unbounded, always present, unconfused about all knowables yet never facing them; equality-wisdom sees beings as equal and abides in non-abiding peace; discerning wisdom is unobstructed toward all knowables, like a treasury of dhāraṇīs and concentrations, and rains the Dharma; the wisdom that accomplishes what is to be done acts for all beings through countless emanations.

> ādarśajñānamacalaṃ trayajñānaṃ tadāśritam / samatāpratyavekṣāyāṃ kṛtyānuṣṭhāna eva ca

### 13.19 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-13-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mind is held to be always luminous by nature, and to be sullied by adventitious faults; no mind other than the mind that is the true nature (dharmatā) is said to be luminous by nature.

### 14.8-14 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-14-8-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The path of calm is the gathering of the teachings' names, the path of insight the investigation of their meanings, and their union the joined path. One should lift the sunken mind and calm the agitated one; when calm is reached, let it be; then: fix the mind on the object and not let it stray; notice distraction quickly and bring it back; withdraw the mind inward ever more; delight it in concentration by seeing its qualities; quell displeasure by seeing the fault of distraction; quell covetousness, grief and the like as they arise; then the mind flows by itself with effort; and by practice it flows without effort.

> nibadhyālambane cittaṃ tatpravedhaṃ na vikṣipet / avagamyāśu vikṣepaṃ tasmin pratiharet punaḥ

### 14.20-22 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-14-20-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In the kasiṇa and other (concentrations), his badness slight, his body becomes pliant ('melts') moment by moment, and body and mind are filled all over with pliancy; he knows the unbounded appearance of dharmas everywhere and, in purity, sees signs that are not imagined; so the wise one constantly lays hold of the cause for the fulfilment and purification of the dharma-body.

### 14.23-28 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-14-23-28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Then the bodhisattva in concentration no longer sees objects apart from mental speech (heat); he strives firmly to increase the light of the teaching (peak); by its increase he abides in mind-only and sees all objects as appearances in mind, and the distraction of the grasped is abandoned (patience); only the distraction of the grasper remains, and he quickly touches the immediate concentration (supreme mundane quality); after it the distraction of the grasper also ends. These are heat and the others in order. Then he gains supramundane, unsurpassed, non-conceptual, stainless knowledge free from the grasping of duality.

> yato grāhakavikṣepo hīyate tadanantaraṃ / jñeyāny uṣmagatādīni etāni hi yathākramaṃ

### 14.42-46 <a id="tea-mahayanasutralamkara-14-42-46"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Then on the path of cultivation, in the remaining grounds, he trains in the two kinds of knowledge — non-conceptual knowledge that purifies the buddha-qualities, and the other that matures beings as things are established; at the end of two incalculable aeons, consecrated in the last cultivation, he attains the diamond-like concentration unbreakable by conceptions, the final transformation of the basis stainless of all obscurations, and omniscience, from which he works for all beings.
