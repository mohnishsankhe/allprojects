A short account of Baizhang's founding of separate Chan monasteries: an abbot called 'elder' living in the 'ten-foot square', no Buddha hall but a Dharma hall, a common Sangha hall where monks sit and sleep by seniority, morning and evening assemblies, the 'universal invitation' to communal labour (puqing) with high and low sharing the work, and ten offices.

## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### 251a04 <a id="tea-chanmen-guishi-251a04"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Baizhang's founding of Chan monasteries: what he follows is neither confined to nor different from the Lesser and Great Vehicles, so he selects what is fitting; the abbot, called 'elder', lives in the ten-foot square; there is no Buddha hall but a Dharma hall; all monks live in the Sangha hall by seniority; morning and evening assemblies; the universal invitation to labour means high and low share the work equally.

> 吾所宗非局大小乘。非異大小乘。當博約折中設於制範務其宜也
