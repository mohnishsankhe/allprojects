The science of statecraft: the training and conduct of the king, administration, law and punishment, foreign policy and war. It names the four sciences — investigation (Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Lokāyata), the triple Veda, economics and governance — calling investigation 'the lamp of all sciences' (1.2.1–12); gives the duties common to all (non-injury, truth, purity, freedom from envy, compassion, patience) and says one's own dharma leads to heaven and infinity (1.3.13–14); and holds that artha is foremost among the three aims because dharma and kāma are rooted in it (1.7.6–7).

### 1.2.10-12 <a id="tea-arthasastra-1-2-10-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sāṃkhya, Yoga and Lokāyata — this is investigation (ānvīkṣikī); it benefits the world, keeps the mind steady in adversity and prosperity, and gives skill in thought, speech and action: it is ever regarded as the lamp of all sciences, the means of all actions, the support of all dharmas.
