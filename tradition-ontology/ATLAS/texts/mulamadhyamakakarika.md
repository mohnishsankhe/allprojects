Nāgārjuna's root verses on the middle way: in 27 examinations (parīkṣā) it shows that conditions, motion, the faculties, the aggregates, the elements, desire, the conditioned, agent and action, the self, time, the Tathāgata, the four truths and nirvāṇa lack own-nature because they arise dependently, and that this emptiness — itself not a view — is the middle way, taught through the two truths.

### 0.1-2 <a id="tea-mulamadhyamakakarika-0-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

I bow to the perfectly awakened one, best of speakers, who taught dependent origination — without cessation, without arising, without annihilation, without permanence, without single meaning, without manifold meaning, without coming, without going — the pacification of elaboration, the auspicious.

> anirodhamanutpādamanucchedamaśāśvatam | anekārthamanānārthamanāgamamanirgamam || yaḥ pratītyasamutpādaṃ prapañcopaśamaṃ śivam | deśayāmāsa saṃbuddhastaṃ vande vadatāṃ varam ||

### 1.1 <a id="tea-mulamadhyamakakarika-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

No existents whatsoever are ever found anywhere that have arisen from themselves, from something other, from both, or without a cause.

### 2.18-21 <a id="tea-mulamadhyamakakarika-2-18-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high
