# Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to Enlightenment (lam rim chen mo)

`src:lamrim-chenmo` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Byang chub lam rim chen mo, The Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to Enlightenment, LRC
**Language:** Tibetan
**Family:** ascetic
**Lineages:** [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md), [Kadam](../lineages/kadam.md)
**Genre:** stages of the path (lamrim)
**Authors:** 
  - [Tsongkhapa Lobsang Drakpa](../teachers/tsongkhapa.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: composed at Reting in 1402 after visions of the lamrim lineage masters, with Atiśa's Lamp as the root; scholarly: accepted; confidence: high
**Dates:** Tradition's account: written at Reting; Scholarly account: 1402 CE; (confidence high)
**Structure:** preliminaries; the persons of small, middle and great scope; calm abiding and insight as the last two perfections
**Availability:** digitized-translation

Tsongkhapa's comprehensive exposition of Atiśa's graded path: reliance on the teacher and the precious human life; the small scope (death, lower realms, refuge, karma); the middle scope (the truths of suffering and origin, the twelve links, the three trainings); the great scope (bodhicitta by the seven-point cause and effect and by exchanging self and other, the bodhisattva deeds), culminating in long sections on calm abiding and on insight (identifying the object of negation, Prāsaṅgika reasoning, the union of calm and insight).
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: original; name: Tibetan: Tsongkhapa's collected works; many modern prints
  - kind: translation; name: English: The Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path to Enlightenment (Lamrim Chenmo Translation Committee, 3 vols., Snow Lion 2000–2004)

### calm.faults-antidotes <a id="tea-lamrim-chenmo-calm-faults-antidotes"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Calm abiding is achieved by abandoning the five faults — laziness, forgetting the instruction, laxity and excitement, non-application (of the antidote), and over-application — through the eight antidotes: faith, aspiration, effort and pliancy (against laziness), mindfulness, introspective alertness, application, and equanimity.

### calm.measure <a id="tea-lamrim-chenmo-calm-measure"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Calm abiding is achieved only when, after the ninth state, mental and then physical pliancy arise with their joy and, that joy settling, the mind rests stably on its object at will; before that one has only a likeness of calm abiding.

### calm.need <a id="tea-lamrim-chenmo-calm-need"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Both calm abiding and insight are needed: calm abiding is a mind stably placed on its object without distraction, insight is the wisdom analysing the object; like a lamp that is both bright and unflickering, the view of reality needs both.

The nine mental states are achieved in order: placing the mind, continuous placement, repeated placement, close placement, taming, pacifying, thorough pacifying, one-pointedness and equipoise; by six powers (hearing, reflection, mindfulness, alertness, effort and complete familiarity) and four attentions (tight focus, interrupted, uninterrupted and spontaneous).

### great.seven-point <a id="tea-lamrim-chenmo-great-seven-point"></a>

### insight.conventional-existence <a id="tea-lamrim-chenmo-insight-conventional-existence"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Things lack inherent existence yet exist conventionally as mere designations; because they are dependently arisen they are empty, and because they are empty they can function — the emptiness of inherent existence and the working of cause and effect are one meaning.

`skeleton` · confidence high

Calm abiding alone does not cut the root of saṃsāra — even non-Buddhist sages attain it and still are reborn; the root, grasping at self, is cut only by the wisdom that realizes selflessness.

### insight.object-of-negation <a id="tea-lamrim-chenmo-insight-object-of-negation"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Just as, to know that a person is absent, one must know the person who is absent, so to realise selflessness one must first correctly identify the self that is to be negated; the object of negation is inherent existence — existence by way of the object's own nature, not posited by the force of conceptual designation. If this is not identified, one's negation will either go too far or not far enough.

### small.death <a id="tea-lamrim-chenmo-small-death"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### small.refuge <a id="tea-lamrim-chenmo-small-refuge"></a>

Going for refuge arises from two causes — fear of the lower realms and faith that the Three Jewels can protect; one knows the qualities of Buddha, Dharma and Saṅgha, goes for refuge by knowing their differences, by commitment, and without speaking of other refuges, and keeps the precepts of refuge.
