The hymn recited daily in Shinshū: homage to Amida; the vow and the name as the rightly determined act and shinjin as the cause; the evil and good ordinary beings who hear and entrust being called white lotuses; then the teachings of the seven masters from Nāgārjuna to Hōnen.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### opening <a id="tea-shoshinge-opening"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

I take refuge in the Tathāgata of Immeasurable Life, I entrust myself to the Buddha of Inconceivable Light. ... The name of the primal vow is the act of right assurance; the vow of sincere mind and entrusting is the cause; to attain equal enlightenment and realise great nirvāṇa is fulfilled by the vow of necessary attainment of nirvāṇa.

### pundarika <a id="tea-shoshinge-pundarika"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When ordinary beings, good or evil, hear and entrust themselves to the Tathāgata's universal vow, the Buddha praises them as persons of vast and excellent understanding and calls them white lotuses; yet for evil beings of wrong views and arrogance, to accept and hold the nenbutsu of Amida's primal vow with joyful trust is the most difficult of difficulties.

### seven-masters <a id="tea-shoshinge-seven-masters"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The masters of India, China and Japan revealed the Buddha's intent: Nāgārjuna taught the easy practice and that one who recollects Amida's vow at once enters the stage of assurance; Vasubandhu took refuge with single mind; Tanluan made other-power plain; Daochuo showed that the holy path is hard to realise and only the Pure Land passable; Shandao revealed the Buddha's true intent and the deep entrusting; Genshin said that though passions blind our eyes, great compassion constantly illumines us; Hōnen showed that doubt keeps us in birth-and-death and that shinjin is the entrance to nirvāṇa.
