### 2.1-5 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-1-5"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

After the forty saṃskāras, one who has dispassion may renounce; three are called fallen — who makes a renouncer fall, who makes a fallen one renounce, who obstructs renunciation; the ineligible (including the leprous and the unbelieving) may renounce only when sick unto death.

### 2.6-8 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-6-8"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

When the sun sees a renouncer it trembles, thinking 'he will pierce my orb'; one who renounces saves sixty generations before and after; the fire of the renunciation declaration burns faults.

### 2.12 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-12"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

'Abandon dharma and adharma, abandon truth and untruth; having abandoned both, abandon that by which you abandon.'

### 2.59-64 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-59-64"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Food: fill half the stomach with food, a quarter with water, leave a quarter for air; fasting is better than eating at night, unsolicited food better than fasting, alms better than unsolicited food.

### 2.65-71 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-65-71"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Five kinds of alms: mādhūkara (a little from several houses like a bee), prākpraṇīta (arranged in advance), ayācita (unasked), tātkālika (what comes at the moment) and upapanna (brought to him); mādhūkara may be taken even from a mleccha, never food from a single house.

### 2.74-76 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-74-76"></a>
`sourced` · confidence low

He avoids food from the accused, the fallen, heretics and temple priests; ghee is like dog's urine to him.

### 2.79-85 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-79-85"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Six things bind a renouncer: a fixed seat (staying beyond the rains), losing his bowl, hoarding, gathering disciples for service, gain, worship or fame (not out of compassion), sleep (negligence in study) and idle talk.

### 2.91 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-91"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Six things he must never accept: immovable property, movables, seed, metal vessels, poison and weapons.

### 2.97 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-97"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Tridaṇḍa means three restraints: silence the staff of speech, not eating (desire-less eating) the staff of body, breath-control the staff of mind.

### 2.98-100 <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-2-98-100"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Beings are bound by action and freed by knowledge; streets full of rags, alms everywhere, the earth a wide bed — why should ascetics be unhappy? One who offers the world into the fire of knowledge is the true agnihotrin.

### 1 (rite) <a id="tea-sannyasa-upanisad-1-rite"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

One who renounces in due order performs the Vaiśvānara sacrifice, places the fires in himself, tells his son 'you are the sacrifice, you are all' (or without a son departs east or north), begs among the three higher varṇas, uses the hand as bowl, stays one night in a village, five in a town, four months in the rains; his thread is meditation on the Self, his topknot knowledge; for him there is no night or day.
