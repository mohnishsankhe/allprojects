# Spandakārikā

`src:spanda-karika` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Spandasūtra (Bhāskara), Spandaśāstra
**Original title:** स्पन्दकारिका
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Spanda (the doctrine of vibration)](../lineages/spanda.md), [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
**Genre:** kārikā
**Authors:** 
  - [Vasugupta](../teachers/vasugupta.md) — role: author; attribution: disputed
  - [Bhaṭṭa Kallaṭa](../teachers/kallata.md) — role: author; attribution: disputed
**Attribution:** tradition: Disputed within the tradition: Kṣemarāja (Spandanirṇaya) holds that Vasugupta composed the verses; Bhāskara (Śivasūtravārttika 1.5: Kallaṭa explained three sections of the Śiva Sūtra 'with his own Spanda-sūtras'), Rāmakaṇṭha and Bhagavadutpala ascribe them to Kallaṭa, conveying Vasugupta's teaching.; scholarly: Author debated between Vasugupta and Kallaṭa; c. 850-900 CE.; confidence: disputed
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 850-900 CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** Kṣemarāja's recension: 3 niḥṣyandas of 25 + 7 + 19 verses plus 2 concluding verses (53); the recensions of Kallaṭa and Rāmakaṇṭha differ slightly in division and count.
**Availability:** digitized-original

Fifty-odd verses on spanda, the vibrant creative awareness of Śaṅkara: its unobstructed nature and persistence through the states (ch. 1), the identity of mantras and the soul with Śiva (ch. 2), the powers (vibhūti) that come from contact with spanda and the bondage caused by the powers of the mass of sounds when unrecognized (ch. 3).
