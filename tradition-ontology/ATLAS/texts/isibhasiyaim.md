# Isibhāsiyāiṃ (Ṛṣibhāṣitāni)

'Sayings of the seers': 45 short chapters of teachings ascribed to sages, many of them non-Jain (Nārada, Yājñavalkya, Sāriputta, Mahākāsava, Maṅkhaliputta and others), each called 'an arhat seer'. Ch. 11 gives a teaching of Maṅkhaliputta on the steadfast protector (tāyin) who is not moved; ch. 20 (Ukkala) classifies five kinds of 'ukkala' who cut off the doctrine of the soul and states the materialist view of the self bounded by the skin.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 11 <a id="tea-isibhasiyaim-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Thus spoke the arhat seer Maṅkhaliputta: one who stirs, trembles, is agitated, disturbed, moved, shaken and roused, and turns into this or that state, is not a protector (tāyin); one who does not stir or turn into this or that state is a protector — the protector saves himself and others from the four-bordered wilderness of saṃsāra. As the guide who knows the road leads others, the skilled physician frees the sick, and the one who knows the mixing of substances completes the work, so one who rightly knows the way of release and casts off attachment and aversion will reach perfection.

### 20 <a id="tea-isibhasiyaim-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There are five kinds of 'ukkala': the staff-ukkala, who by the example of a staff (whose beginning, middle and end are only an aggregate) says 'there is no soul beyond the body' and cuts off rebirth; the rope-ukkala, who by the example of a rope teaches that there is only an aggregate of the five great elements and cuts off the continuity of saṃsāra; the thief-ukkala, who takes others' examples and arguments to promote his own side and cuts off compassion for others; the partial ukkala, who admits the soul but cuts it off in part by teaching its non-agency; and the total ukkala, who says nothing arises from anything and cuts off everything.

### 20/2 <a id="tea-isibhasiyaim-20-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The materialist's statement: 'Upward from the soles of the feet, downward from the tips of the hair, bounded by the skin — this is the self; this soul lives, this is its life. As burnt seeds do not sprout again, so when the body is burnt no body arises again. This alone is life; there is no other world, no fruit of good and bad deeds; souls are not reborn, merit and demerit do not touch them; good and evil are fruitless.'

### whole <a id="tea-isibhasiyaim-whole"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Forty-five seers — including Nārada, Asita Devala, Aṅgiras, Yājñavalkya, the Buddhist Vajjiyaputta, Mahākāśyapa and Śāriputra, Maṅkhali Gośāla, Pārśva and Vardhamāna — are each called 'arhat-ṛṣi' and give a short teaching on karma, passion, detachment and the path.
