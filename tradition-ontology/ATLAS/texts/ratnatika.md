Prose commentary on the Gaṇakārikā. It explains each of the nine groups; maps the five stages to places (teacher, people, cave, cremation ground, Rudra) and livelihoods; describes the daily observance (ash-baths at the three junctures, the six-limbed offering in the temple, circumambulation, salutation of the tīrthakaras from Lakulīśa to Rāśīkara); defines the six 'doors'; and distinguishes a true teacher from a pseudo-teacher (ācāryābhāsa).

## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 1 <a id="tea-ratnatika-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The end of suffering is reached not only by fulfilling every injunction of the Pañcārtha but also by rightly favouring qualified brahmin disciples, since preserving the tradition brings the end of suffering to many. The teacher must know the nine groups; one content with mere knowledge of scripture, lacking faith or lacking knowledge of the gains and the rest, is only a semblance of a teacher (ācāryābhāsa), not one who goes to release.

### 2 <a id="tea-ratnatika-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Livelihoods are the means of getting food not contrary to scripture: alms, discarded food and what is obtained by chance. In the first stage the livelihood is alms, gathered with purity and restraint; in the second, discarded food; in the third, alms again (without the expiations and re-bathing, being a supreme yogin); in the fourth, whatever comes while staying in the cremation ground; in the fifth there is no livelihood, since there is no body.

### 5 <a id="tea-ratnatika-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The first stage is 'manifest' because the Pāśupata sign is made manifest by ash-bathing, lying in ash and the rest; the second 'unmanifest' because birth and the rest are not shown - the sign is hidden and knowledge concealed (the states of feigned madness and folly belong here); the third is 'conquest', aiming at conquest of the senses; the fourth ends in 'cutting'; the fifth, 'completion', is the total cessation of all the practitioner's activity. Some posit a 'stage of the perfected' as fifth, but in this system completion is the fifth, since only the practitioner's stages are described.

### 6 <a id="tea-ratnatika-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The place of the first stage is the teacher (the temple-dwelling is under his command); of the second, people, the members of the varṇas and āśramas; of the third, a cave or empty house free of distraction; of the fourth, the cremation ground, which he does not leave until the body falls; of the fifth, Rudra alone, since he is then without body.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: general · stage: all · types: practice, death-dying_

concepts: [The five places (deśa)](../concepts/pasupata-five-places.md), [Death and the final stage in Pāśupata practice](../concepts/pasupata-death-final-stage.md)

### 7 <a id="tea-ratnatika-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'Residence' names the disciple's faculties of grasping, retaining, inferring, rejecting, understanding, speaking, service and application. Observance (caryā) - from ash-bathing to feigned folly - has three limbs: gift (self-offering to the Lord with prostrations while repeating the five mantras), worship (doing the prescribed acts as the Lord's command) and austerity (bearing the three sufferings without remedy); it is twofold, vows and 'doors'. Daily, at the three junctures, he bathes in consecrated ash while muttering, enters the temple, sits south of the image, mutters, laughs loudly again and again, sings and dances, makes the huḍukkāra, prostrates and mutters; muttering and prostration are mental, dance bodily, laughter, song and huḍukkāra vocal. He then reports the offering, circumambulates, takes the closing bath and salutes the tīrthakaras from Lakulīśa to Rāśīkara.
