## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### anuccheda 71 <a id="tea-paramatma-sandarbha-71"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Jīva Gosvāmī applies Gītā 16.8 ('they say the world is unreal, without foundation, without a Lord') to those who deny the Lord: they call the world false, indeterminable as real or unreal, and hold that Brahman's lordship is imagined by ignorance, so that the world arises from beginningless ignorance like a dream. He cites Śaṅkara on BS 2.1.14 (name and form, imagined by avidyā and indeterminable, called the Lord's māyā-śakti) as the source of this view, says it conflicts with the Lord's own word ('knowledge and ignorance are my bodies', BhP 11.11.3), and so names it māyāvāda. He then quotes the Padma Purāṇa's Uttarakhaṇḍa, where Mahādeva lists it among the heretical teachings as 'Buddhism in disguise', adding that this was done at the Lord's own command to delude the asuras.

> अतो मायावादतया चायं वादः ख्यायते ।

### jiva-and-world-section <a id="tea-paramatma-sandarbha-jiva-and-world-section"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The souls are the Lord's marginal power, eternally distinct from yet dependent on him; the world is a real transformation (pariṇāma) of his external power while he remains unchanged; the Advaita doctrine of unreal appearance (vivarta) is rejected.
