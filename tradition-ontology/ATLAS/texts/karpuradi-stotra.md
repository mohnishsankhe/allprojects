A hymn of 22 verses to Dakṣiṇakālī, ascribed to Mahākāla, whose opening verses conceal her mantra ('karpūra…') and which praises her vīra worship in the cremation ground; read with the commentary of Vimalānanda Svāmī.

### 1-5 <a id="tea-karpuradi-stotra-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The opening verses conceal the seed-syllables of Dakṣiṇakālī's mantra in riddles and promise eloquence, mastery and the eight powers to those who repeat them; verse 4 gives her form — sword and severed head in the left hands, fearlessness and boon in the right. Mantra syllables not reproduced.

### 9 <a id="tea-karpuradi-stotra-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

What shall we say of you, Mother, dull-witted as we are? Neither Brahmā nor Śiva nor even Hari knows your supreme nature; yet devotion to you makes us speak — forgive it; anger at a mere beast (paśu) is not fitting.

### 12-14 <a id="tea-karpuradi-stotra-12-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

You give birth to the world, protect it and at dissolution withdraw it — so you are Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheśa; many serve other gods, deluded, not knowing the supreme; I take refuge in the primal one worshipped by Hari, Hara and Brahmā; earth, water, fire, wind and sky — you alone, auspicious Kālī, beloved of Śiva, are all; be gracious, that I be born no more.
