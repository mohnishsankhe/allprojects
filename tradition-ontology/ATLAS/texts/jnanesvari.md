# Jñāneśvarī (Bhāvārthadīpikā)

**Availability:** digitized-original

Jñāneśvar's Old Marathi verse (ovī) commentary on the Bhagavad Gītā, expanding each verse with images from village life. It teaches non-dual knowledge crowned by devotion, gives a celebrated account of kuṇḍalinī yoga in chapter 6 (the 'royal road', pantharāja), names its Nāth lineage from Ādinātha to Nivṛttināth, and closes with the prayer for grace (Pasāydān). It is the first of the three foundational books of the Vārkarīs.

  - kind: original; name: Eknāth's recension (Śaka 1506 / 1584 CE), basis of the vulgate
  - kind: original; name: Sākhare Mahārāj edition (Vārkarī standard numbering)

### 18.1794–1802 (Pasāydān) <a id="tea-jnanesvari-18-1794-1802"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The closing prayer (Pasāydān): may the Lord who is the Self of the universe be pleased with this sacrifice of words and grant this gift — that the wicked give up their crookedness and delight in good deeds; that friendship grow among all beings; that the darkness of sin depart and the world see by the sun of its own dharma; that every being obtain what it desires; that the assemblies of devotees shower blessings on the earth; and that the three worlds be happy.

### ch.14 (on BhG 14.5–26) <a id="tea-jnanesvari-14-gunas"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On the three guṇas (BhG 14), Jñāneśvar describes how sattva binds through attachment to happiness and knowledge, rajas through attachment to action, tamas through delusion and sloth, and teaches that one who serves the Lord with undeviating devotion passes beyond them (BhG 14.26).

### ch.6 (kuṇḍalinī passage, approx. ovīs 190–320) <a id="tea-jnanesvari-6-kundalini"></a>

Commenting on the Gītā's teaching of seated meditation (BhG 6.10–15), Jñāneśvar describes the 'royal road' (pantharāja) of the Nāth yogīs: through posture and the bodily locks the coiled power (kuṇḍalinī), asleep at the base of the body, wakes, rises through the centres consuming the body's elements, and merges into the supreme at the crown; the yogī's body becomes light and radiant and the sense of a separate self dissolves. On the Gītā's promise that women, vaiśyas, śūdras and those of low birth reach the highest by taking refuge in the Lord (BhG 9.32–33), Jñāneśvar says birth and caste count for nothing once one turns to God: as rivers lose their names in the ocean and every kind of wood becomes the same fire, all who come to him become one with him.
