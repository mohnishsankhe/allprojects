Hōnen's argument for the exclusive nenbutsu: following Daochuo, set aside the holy path; following Shandao, set aside miscellaneous practices; the eighteenth vow selected the nenbutsu as the one practice for birth because the name holds all virtues and is easy for all; the three grades, the three minds, the four modes, the Buddha's light embracing reciters, the entrustment of the name to Ānanda and the testimony of all buddhas show the same; 'I rely solely on Shandao'.

## Teachings (7: skeleton 7)

### 1 <a id="tea-senchakushu-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Following Daochuo, all the Buddha's teachings fall into two gates, the holy path and the Pure Land; one should set aside the holy path and take refuge in the Pure Land. The Pure Land school has its canon — the three sūtras (the Larger, the Contemplation and the Amitābha) and one treatise (Vasubandhu's) — and its lineages of transmission (Hōnen names those of Huiyuan, of Cimin and of Daochuo–Shandao).

### 2 <a id="tea-senchakushu-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Following Shandao, set aside the miscellaneous practices and take the right: the five right practices, of which calling the name is the rightly determined act and the others auxiliary. Right and miscellaneous differ in five pairs: intimate or distant, near or far, uninterrupted or interrupted, not requiring transfer or requiring transfer of merit, pure or mixed.

### 3 <a id="tea-senchakushu-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dharmākara, surveying 21 billion buddha-lands, selected (senchaku) the good and rejected the bad; in the eighteenth vow he rejected all other practices and selected solely the nenbutsu as the practice for birth. Why? First, superior and inferior: the name contains all the virtues of the Buddha's inner realization and outer activity, as the word 'house' contains beams, rafters and pillars, whereas other practices each hold only a part. Second, difficult and easy: the nenbutsu is easy for all; if building images and stūpas were the vow's practice the poor would lose hope, if wisdom the dull, if much learning the little-learned, if keeping precepts the precept-breakers — who are the many; so, out of compassion for all equally, he chose the nenbutsu alone.

### 4 <a id="tea-senchakushu-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Larger Sūtra mentions other practices for the three grades besides the nenbutsu; this may be read in three ways — they are taught in order to be abandoned in favour of the nenbutsu (establishing by abandoning), as aids to the nenbutsu, or as secondary parallel paths; Hōnen takes the first as the main intent, the Buddha's aim being exclusive nenbutsu.

### 8 <a id="tea-senchakushu-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The practitioner of the nenbutsu must possess the three minds of the Contemplation Sūtra, sincere, deep and aspiring; quoting Shandao at length, Hōnen explains that the deep mind includes deep entrusting that one is an ordinary being of sin and that one will surely be born by the vow; those lacking the three minds are not born.

### 16 <a id="tea-senchakushu-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

If you wish quickly to leave birth-and-death, of the two excellent teachings set aside the holy path and choose to enter the Pure Land; wishing to enter the Pure Land, of the right and miscellaneous practices cast aside the miscellaneous and choose to take refuge in the right; wishing to practise the right, of the rightly determined and auxiliary acts set aside the auxiliary and choose the rightly determined. The rightly determined act is to call the Buddha's name; calling the name, one will surely be born, because it is based on the Buddha's primal vow.

### postscript <a id="tea-senchakushu-postscript"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Hōnen explains that he relies solely on Shandao, whose commentary is like a sūtra, as the 'master' of the Pure Land; he wrote the work at the request of the regent (Kanezane) and asks that, once read, it be buried in the wall and not left by the window, lest those who slander the Dharma see it and fall into evil paths.
