# Apaṇṇaka Sutta

**Location in parent:** MN 60

The Buddha gives the brahmin householders of Sālā an 'incontrovertible teaching': against the doctrines that there is no gift or other world (natthika), no fruit of action (akiriya), no cause (ahetuka), no formless attainments and no cessation of existence, he shows that the opposite view is the safer wager in either case.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 5-12 <a id="tea-apannaka-sutta-5-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Against those who say there is no gift, no fruit of deeds, no other world (natthika), the Buddha gives the 'incontrovertible' reckoning: they will abandon good conduct and take up bad, seeing no danger; if there is another world they have wagered badly on both counts — blamed here by the wise and reborn in a bad destiny; one who holds that there is another world, if wrong, is still praised here as virtuous, and if right wins both. The positive view is the safe bet (apaṇṇaka).

### 13-20 <a id="tea-apannaka-sutta-13-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The same reckoning against the doctrine that the doer does no evil and the giver gains no merit (akiriya): the doctrine of the efficacy of action (kiriya) is the safe course.

### 21-28 <a id="tea-apannaka-sutta-21-28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The same reckoning against the doctrine that beings are defiled and purified without cause, having no power or energy and experiencing pleasure and pain in six classes by destiny (ahetuka): the doctrine of cause (hetu) is the safe course.

### 29-34 <a id="tea-apannaka-sutta-29-34"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

On the views that there are no formless attainments and that there is no cessation of existence, the Buddha shows that the view closer to non-greed and non-grasping is to be preferred, and the practitioner turns to dispassion toward the formless and toward existence itself.
