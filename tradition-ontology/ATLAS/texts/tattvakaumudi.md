Vācaspati Miśra's commentary, the standard Sāṃkhya text of later tradition. It defines the three kinds of suffering; defends the impurity of sacrificial killing with a saying of Pañcaśikha; describes the path from hearing scripture through reasoning to long, uninterrupted, reverent practice ending in the knowledge 'I am not'; classifies inference; reduces other proposed means of knowledge to three; and explains how buddhi, taking on the reflection of consciousness, appears conscious.

## Teachings (7: skeleton 7)

### 1 <a id="tea-tattvakaumudi-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Suffering is threefold: internal — bodily, from imbalance of wind, bile and phlegm, and mental, from desire, anger, greed, delusion, fear, envy and not seeing particular objects — curable by internal means; from other beings, caused by humans, beasts, birds, reptiles and plants; and from the divine, caused by possession by yakṣas, rākṣasas, vināyakas, planets and the like — both curable by external means. Suffering is a modification of rajas felt by each; its contact with the conscious power as something disagreeable is the 'striking'. Seen remedies — medicine, pleasures, statecraft, gems and spells — fail to remove it certainly and finally.

### 2 <a id="tea-tattvakaumudi-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The better means is the direct realization of the distinctness of sattva (buddhi) and puruṣa: having heard the manifest, the unmanifest and the knower discriminatingly from śruti, smṛti, itihāsa and purāṇa, and established them by the reasoning of the treatise, one cultivates the principles through long, reverent, uninterrupted meditative practice, from which arises the knowledge 'I am not, not mine, not I', complete, pure and single.

### 2/2 <a id="tea-tattvakaumudi-2-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The impurity of the Vedic means is that soma and other sacrifices involve killing animals and seeds; as the teacher Pañcaśikha says, 'a slight admixture, removable, bearable': the demerit from the killing is small, can be removed by expiation, and if not, is borne amid the ocean of merit's delights. The general prohibition 'do not harm any being' is not cancelled by the specific injunction to offer the animal to Agni-Soma, because they have different objects: the prohibition makes known that harming causes evil to the agent, the injunction that it serves the sacrifice.

### 5 <a id="tea-tattvakaumudi-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Perception is buddhi's determination — a predominance of sattva over tamas — when a sense is in contact with its object. Buddhi, being of prakṛti, is unconscious; puruṣa, reflected in it and taking on its shade, appears to have knowledge and pleasure, while buddhi, taking on the shade of consciousness, appears conscious. Inference is vīta (affirmative) or avīta (by exclusion; = śeṣavat), and vīta is pūrvavat or sāmānyatodṛṣṭa. Testimony from the authorless Veda is self-valid, and Kapila at the start of the eon could remember the Veda learned in a prior eon.

### 5/2 <a id="tea-tattvakaumudi-5-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Postulation (arthāpatti) is a form of inference; absence (abhāva) is perception of a particular transformation of the ground; inclusion (sambhava) is inference; tradition without a named speaker (aitihya) is not a means of knowledge unless the speaker is reliable, when it is testimony. Hence three means of knowledge.

### 51 <a id="tea-tattvakaumudi-51"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Of the eight accomplishments the three destructions of suffering are primary and the other five are means to them in order: study of the texts from a teacher, understanding their meaning (śabda), examination by reasoning (ūha), confirmation with teachers and fellow students (suhṛtprāpti), and 'dāna' as the purification of discriminative knowledge (from the root 'dai', to purify).

### 72 <a id="tea-tattvakaumudi-72"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Vācaspati quotes the Rājavārttika listing the sixty topics: pradhāna's existence, oneness, purposiveness, distinctness, being for another, plurality (of puruṣas), separation, conjunction, the remaining function and non-agency — ten fundamental topics — with five errors, nine contentments, twenty-eight incapacities and eight accomplishments.
