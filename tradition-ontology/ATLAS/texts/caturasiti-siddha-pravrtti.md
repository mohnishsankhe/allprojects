Abhayadatta's lives of the eighty-four siddhas, each a short narrative of a person of any caste or trade who met a guru, received an instruction suited to his or her life, practised (often twelve years) and attained mahāmudrā, with a verse of realization; preserved in Tibetan (translated by sMon grub shes rab).

### 3 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Virūpa, a monk, recited the mantra of Vajravārāhī for twelve years without a sign, threw his rosary into the latrine in despair, and that night the goddess Nairātmyā appeared and gave him consecration; later, expelled for eating pigeons, he walked across the Ganges and, drinking at a tavern, held the sun still until the king paid.

### 6 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Saraha, a brahmin who secretly practised the Buddha's teaching and drank liquor, was expelled; he went to live with a low-caste arrowsmith woman, who showed him the meaning through the making of an arrow; to the king who came to punish him, and to the queen and the people, he sang the three cycles of dohās and they were converted.

### 8 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Mīnapa, swallowed by a fish, overheard in its belly Śiva teaching Umā at the bottom of the ocean, practised for twelve years inside the fish and emerged a siddha.

### 12 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Śāntipa, a great scholar, returning from teaching in Siṃhala, found that Koṭali, the peasant to whom he had once given a short instruction, had realized it, while he himself, who knew so much, had not; he then asked Koṭali for the instruction and practised twelve years.

### 20 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Nāropa, a great scholar, was asked by an old woman (a ḍākinī) whether he understood the words or the meaning; when he said he understood the meaning she wept, told him that only her brother Tilopa did, and he left his post to search for Tilopa, undergoing many trials before receiving the instructions.

_level: conventional · standpoint: experiential · path: devotion, knowledge · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission_

### 22 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Tilopa, having practised as a monk, was told by a ḍākinī to practise among ordinary people; he pounded sesame by day and served a courtesan by night, and realized that as oil is in the sesame, the innate is in mind; he rose into the sky and sang his realization.

### 41 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-41"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Bhusuku, the monk who only ate, slept and walked, was made to recite before the assembly to shame him; he asked whether they wanted something old or something never heard, recited the Bodhicaryāvatāra and rose into the air.

### 65 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-65"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Maṇibhadrā, a young housewife, took instruction from Kukkuripa and practised a year while keeping house; when she fell and broke her water pot, she realized that as the pot was broken so were all things, and later rose into the sky.

### 66 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-66"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Mekhalā and Kanakhalā, two sisters of Devīkoṭa, received instruction from Kāṇha; when he later asked for their heads as his fee, they cut them off and offered them, singing of realization; he restored them and they lived as siddhas.

_level: conventional · standpoint: experiential · path: devotion · stage: realized · types: powers-experiences, teacher-transmission_

### 82 <a id="tea-caturasiti-siddha-pravrtti-82"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Lakṣmīṅkarā, princess of Oḍḍiyāna betrothed to a king of Laṅkāpurī, rejected the marriage and the hunting and killing she saw there, feigned madness, took to the cremation grounds and attained realization; she later taught the king's sweeper and the king.
