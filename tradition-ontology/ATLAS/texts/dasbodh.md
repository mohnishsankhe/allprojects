Samartha Rāmdās's teaching in dialogue between master and disciple: the marks of the fool and the wise, the nine forms of devotion and four kinds of liberation, the true guru and the true disciple, the bound, the seeker, the practitioner and the accomplished, the elements and the discrimination of the self from the non-self, and the conduct of the active leader (mahant) in the world.

The four liberations — dwelling in the Lord's world, nearness, likeness of form and union: the first three perish with the worlds; union (sāyujya) alone lasts.

### daśaka 4, samāsas 1–9 <a id="tea-dasbodh-4-1-9"></a>

The nine forms of devotion expounded one by one — hearing, singing, remembering, serving the feet, worship, bowing, servanthood, friendship and self-offering; self-offering, in which the separate self is surrendered, is the culmination.

### daśaka 5, samāsas 7–10 <a id="tea-dasbodh-5-7-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The four conditions: the bound (baddha) are sunk in the world; the seeker (mumukṣu) turns from it and longs for freedom; the practitioner (sādhaka) takes up hearing, reflection and devotion under the guru; the accomplished (siddha) abides in the Self, free of doubt, while still acting rightly.
