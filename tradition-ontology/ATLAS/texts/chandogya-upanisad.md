# Chāndogya Upaniṣad

`src:chandogya-upanisad` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Chandogyopanisad
**Original title:** छान्दोग्योपनिषद्
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md), [Kauthuma śākhā (Sāmaveda)](../lineages/sakha-kauthuma.md), [Rāṇāyanīya śākhā (Sāmaveda)](../lineages/sakha-ranayaniya.md)

**Attribution:** tradition: śruti, 'seen' by the sages whose teaching it records; not a human composition; scholarly: anonymous compilation within the Vedic schools (śākhās), incorporating older materials; confidence: high
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Revealed scripture (śruti): in the Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta view without human author and beginningless; the texts themselves trace their teaching through lineages of teachers back to Brahma/Prajāpati and brahman, the self-existent; Scholarly account: c. 7th-6th c. BCE (rough estimate; with BAU the oldest); (confidence low)
**Structure:** 8 prapathakas (chapters), about 154 khandas, mainly prose (Śaṅkara's text numbers ch.kh.verse)
**Availability:** digitized-original

One of the two oldest and largest Upanisads: meditations on the udgītha and Om; the honey of the sun; Śāṇḍilya's teaching that all this is brahman and the self in the heart is brahman; Raikva, Satyakāma Jābāla and Upakosala; the contest of the breaths; the five fires and the two paths; Aśvapati's vaiśvānara self; Uddalaka's teaching to Śvetaketu of being (sat) with its refrain tat tvam asi; Sanatkumāra's ladder to plenitude (bhūman); the small space in the heart; Prajāpati's teaching to Indra and Virocana.

### 1.1.1-3 <a id="tea-chandogya-upanisad-1-1-1-3"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

One should venerate the syllable Om as the udgītha, for one begins the chant with Om. The essence of beings is the earth, of earth the waters, of waters plants, of plants the person, of the person speech, of speech the ṛc, of the ṛc the sāman, of the sāman the udgītha: it is the highest essence, the eighth.

> om ity etad akṣaram udgītham upāsīta

### 1.2.1-9 <a id="tea-chandogya-upanisad-1-2-1-9"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

When the gods and asuras contended, the gods venerated the udgītha as the breath in the nose, as speech, sight, hearing and mind; the asuras pierced each with evil. Then they venerated it as the breath in the mouth, and the asuras, striking it, were destroyed like a clod striking a rock.
