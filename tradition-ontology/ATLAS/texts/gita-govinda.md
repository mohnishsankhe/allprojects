**Availability:** digitized-original

Jayadeva's Sanskrit song-poem in twelve cantos and twenty-four songs (aṣṭapadīs, each set to a named rāga and tāla) with linking verses: Kṛṣṇa's spring dance with the cowherd women, Rādhā's jealousy and withdrawal, the longing of both in separation carried by Rādhā's friend, Kṛṣṇa's plea for forgiveness, and their union. It opens with a hymn to the ten avatāras borne by Kṛṣṇa and offers itself to those whose minds delight in remembering Hari and in the arts of love.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Rasikapriyā](rasikapriya.md)

### 1.astapadi-1 <a id="tea-gita-govinda-1-astapadi-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The hymn of the ten avatāras: O Keśava, taking the body of the fish you held the Veda in the waters of dissolution; as tortoise you bore the earth; as boar, man-lion, dwarf, Paraśurāma, Rāma, Balarāma; as the Buddha, with a compassionate heart you condemned the Vedic ordinance of sacrifice that showed the killing of animals; as Kalki you will destroy the barbarians — victory to you, Hari, lord of the world. The closing verse offers homage to Kṛṣṇa who takes these ten forms.

### 12.closing <a id="tea-gita-govinda-12-closing"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Whatever skill there is in the arts of music, whatever meditation on Viṣṇu, whatever discernment of the truth of love and whatever play in poetry — let the joyful wise learn it all from the Gīta Govinda of the learned poet Jayadeva, whose self is wholly intent on Kṛṣṇa. May the poetry of the Gīta Govinda of Jayadeva, son of Bhojadeva and Rāmādevī, be on the lips of Parāśara and his other dear friends.
