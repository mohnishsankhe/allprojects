# Yogatattva Upaniṣad

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md), `lin:hatha-yoga`
**Genre:** upaniṣad
**Attribution:** tradition: Śruti: revealed, without human author (apauruṣeya); the Muktikā presents it as one of the 108 Upaniṣads that Rāma teaches Hanumān, each belonging to a Vedic śākhā; scholarly: anonymous; assigned to a Veda by the Muktikā list, not by any surviving śākhā transmission; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Śruti: eternal and authorless; no human date; Scholarly account: Scholarly accounts differ by text. Several Yoga Upaniṣads (e.g. Amṛtabindu, Amṛtanāda, Kṣurikā, Tejobindu, Nādabindu, Dhyānabindu, Brahmavidyā, Yogatattva, Yogaśikhā, Haṃsa) survive in shorter northern versions that are older; the expanded southern recensions (as in this collection) borrow from medieval haṭha works and were probably compiled in South India, perhaps as late as the 18th c. (Bouy 1994).; (confidence low)
**Availability:** digitized-original

Viṣṇu teaches Brahmā: yoga and knowledge are both needed; four yogas (mantra, laya, haṭha, rāja) and four stages (ārambha, ghaṭa, paricaya, niṣpatti); eight-limbed haṭha with mudrās; diet, place, obstacles; signs and powers (to be hidden); the five element concentrations; praise of Oṃ.

### 12-13 <a id="tea-yogatattva-upanisad-12-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The jīva is bound by twenty faults — desire, anger, fear, delusion, greed, pride, passion, birth, death, miserliness, grief, drowsiness, hunger, thirst, craving, shame, fright, sorrow, dejection and elation; free of them, it is called Śiva.

### 14-15 <a id="tea-yogatattva-upanisad-14-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

How can knowledge without yoga give liberation? Yoga without knowledge is also incapable of it; the seeker should firmly practise both knowledge and yoga.

> yogahīnaṃ kathaṃ jñānaṃ mokṣadaṃ bhavati dhruvam | yogo hi jñānahīnas tu na kṣamo mokṣakarmaṇi | tasmāj jñānaṃ ca yogaṃ ca mumukṣur dṛḍham abhyaset

_level: bridging · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: intermediate · types: practice, karma-liberation, dispute_

concepts: [Yoga and knowledge together](../concepts/yoga-jnana-samuccaya.md) · disputes: [Is knowledge alone sufficient for liberation, or must bodily yoga accompany it?](../debates/yoga-or-knowledge-for-liberation.md)

### 16 <a id="tea-yogatattva-upanisad-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

_level: ultimate · standpoint: absolute · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: karma-liberation_

### 19-20 <a id="tea-yogatattva-upanisad-19-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Yoga, though one, is divided in practice into mantra-yoga, laya-yoga, haṭha-yoga and rāja-yoga; its stages everywhere are four: ārambha, ghaṭa, paricaya and niṣpatti.

> yogo hi bahudhā brahman bhidyate vyavahārataḥ | mantrayogo layaś caiva haṭho 'sau rājayogataḥ

### 83-102 (summary) <a id="tea-yogatattva-upanisad-83-102-summary"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Five element-concentrations: earth (feet to knees, square, yellow, la, Brahmā), water (knees to anus, half-moon, white, va, Nārāyaṇa), fire (anus to heart, triangle, red, ra, Rudra), air (heart to brows, hexagon, black, ya, Īśvara), space (brows to crown, circle, smoky, ha, Sadāśiva); each conquers its element. Durations omitted.
