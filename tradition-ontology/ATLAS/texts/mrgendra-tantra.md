An upāgama of the Kāmika in the tradition's classification, and one of the most systematic Siddhānta scriptures, with four pādas. Its yogapāda lists seven auxiliaries of yoga (prāṇāyāma, pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna, vīkṣaṇa, japa, samādhi) with yoga itself as the eighth; its caryāpāda includes the annual pavitra rite.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### caryapada.128-130 <a id="tea-mrgendra-tantra-caryapada-128-130"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Another rite, which removes all defects in their (observances), is taught — one the gods obtained from Hara by austerity to strengthen their own rites: in the month of Śrāvaṇa, at its end, or in Nabhasya, having offered the pavitra (thread-garland) to Śambhu, one should complete the year's ritual.

### yogapada.1-3 <a id="tea-mrgendra-tantra-yogapada-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Knowing their own office to be very hard to fulfil, teachers and others who seek mastery should strive for self-possession; that self-possession, which is the yogin's state, belongs to one who has conquered the senses, and conquest of the senses comes gradually through practising breath-control and the rest. Breath-control (prāṇāyāma), withdrawal (pratyāhāra), fixation (dhāraṇā), meditation (dhyāna), examination (vīkṣaṇa), recitation (japa) and absorption (samādhi) are the limbs; the possessor of the limbs, yoga itself, is the eighth.

> प्राणायामः प्रत्याहारो धारणा ध्यानवीक्षणे । जपः समाधिरित्यङ्गान्य् अङ्गी योगो ऽष्टमः स्वयम् ॥

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation, body-breath · stage: unmarked · types: practice_

practices: [Yoga of the Mṛgendra (seven auxiliaries)](../practices/mrgendra-yoga.md)

### yogapada.4-9 <a id="tea-mrgendra-tantra-yogapada-4-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Prāṇa is the air described before; its restraint is its exertion, marked by propelling, drawing in and holding, and it removes the faults of ritual. When the mind then tastes a little happiness, withdrawal is to be practised — turning back from everything. Thereby turned away from contact of senses and objects, the mind becomes fit for fixation on a place chosen at will. Thought upon that object, directed again and again, is meditation; when it becomes one continuous flow, that is called absorption. Recitation is the uttering of it, bringing the object of meditation face to face; examination (ūha) is the scrutiny arising after the object has been conceived — knowing the place, what is to be rejected and accepted there, what sustains it and what opposes it.

### yogapada.10-11 <a id="tea-mrgendra-tantra-yogapada-10-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

For the yogin who has striven in these limbs, singly or together, powers (śakti) shine forth pervading the universe like the rays of the sun; no being — rākṣasa, dānava or man — can master him, as he blazes beyond them with the radiance of vision, action and breath.
