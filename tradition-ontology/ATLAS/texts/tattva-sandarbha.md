On the means of knowledge: revelation (śabda) is supreme; among scriptures the Purāṇas, and among them the Bhāgavata, are decisive; the Bhāgavata is the natural commentary on the Brahma Sūtras.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### intro <a id="tea-tattva-sandarbha-intro"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Jīva states that a southern Bhaṭṭa, a friend of Rūpa and Sanātana, wrote a first draft of this work, partly in order, partly out of order and partly incomplete, and that he, Jīva, has examined and arranged it.

### pramana-section <a id="tea-tattva-sandarbha-pramana-section"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Perception and inference are fallible in the matter of the transcendent; revelation (śabda) alone is decisive; the Purāṇas complete the Veda for this age, and the Bhāgavata, the natural commentary on the Brahma Sūtras by their own author, is the supreme authority among them.
