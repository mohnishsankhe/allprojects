Aśvaghoṣa's epic life of the Buddha. Canto 12 gives the fullest early account of the bodhisattva's teachers: Arāḍa Kālāma expounds a Sāṃkhya-like path — prakṛti and its modifications, the field-knower (kṣetrajña), a five-jointed ignorance (tamas, moha, mahāmoha, tāmisra, andhatāmisra), the four meditations and the sphere of nothingness — which the bodhisattva rejects because a self remains; Udraka teaches neither-perception-nor-non-perception.

## Teachings (9: skeleton 9)

### 12.17-22 <a id="tea-buddhacarita-12-17-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Arāḍa: nature (prakṛti) and modification (vikāra), birth, death and old age — that is called 'the being'. Know as prakṛti the five elements, the I-maker (ahaṃkāra), intellect (buddhi) and the unmanifest; as modifications, the objects, the senses, hands, feet, speech, anus, genitals and mind. Because it knows this field it is called the field-knower (kṣetrajña), and those who reflect on the self call the self the field-knower. Kapila with his disciple is remembered here as the awakened (pratibuddha); Prajāpati with his sons as the unawakened. What is born, ages, is afflicted and dies is the manifest; the unmanifest is its opposite.

### 12.23 <a id="tea-buddhacarita-12-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ignorance, action and craving are to be known as the causes of saṃsāra; the being standing in these three does not pass beyond that state.

### 12.33-37 <a id="tea-buddhacarita-12-33-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Arāḍa: the ignorant one exerts himself in a five-jointed ignorance — darkness (tamas), delusion (moha), great delusion (mahāmoha) and the two blindnesses (tāmisra, andhatāmisra). Darkness is sloth; delusion is birth and death; great delusion is desire; tāmisra is anger; andhatāmisra is despondency. Joined with this five-jointed ignorance the fool is poured into births in a saṃsāra full of suffering.

> tamo mohaṃ mahāmohaṃ tāmisradvayam eva ca

### 12.40-42 <a id="tea-buddhacarita-12-40-42"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Arāḍa: the one who seeks liberation should know the four — the awakened and the unawakened, the manifest and the unmanifest; knowing these correctly the field-knower leaves the round of births and attains the imperishable state. For this purpose brahmins who teach the supreme Brahman practise the holy life (brahmacarya) and make others do so.

### 12.45-58 <a id="tea-buddhacarita-12-45-58"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Arāḍa's path: leaving home, taking the mendicant's sign and moral conduct, contentment and solitude, seeing danger in passion, he restrains the senses; secluded from desires and ill will he attains the first meditation with reasoning (vitarka); then the second with joy and pleasure, the third with pleasure without joy, the fourth free of pleasure and pain. Some, satisfied, take each attainment for liberation and reach the corresponding heavens (Brahmā's world, the radiant gods, the Śubhakṛtsna gods, the Bṛhatphala gods).

### 12.60-65 <a id="tea-buddhacarita-12-60-65"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Arāḍa: detached even from form, the wise one first imagines the spaces of the body as space, then attains the sphere of space; another, turning the self back by the self and seeing 'there is nothing', is called the one of nothingness (ākiñcanya). Then, like a reed from its sheath or a bird from its cage, the field-knower goes out from the body and is called liberated. This is the supreme Brahman, without mark, constant, imperishable, which the knowers of reality call liberation.

> tato muñjād iṣīkeva śakuniḥ pañjarād iva / kṣetrajño niḥsṛto dehān mukta ity abhidhīyate // etat tat paramaṃ brahma nirliṅgaṃ dhruvam akṣaram / yan mokṣa iti tattvajñāḥ kathayanti manīṣiṇaḥ

teachers: [Āḷāra Kālāma](../teachers/alara-kalama.md), [Aśvaghoṣa](../teachers/asvaghosa.md)

### 12.69-82 <a id="tea-buddhacarita-12-69-82"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The bodhisattva's reply: this subtle knowledge, auspicious beyond the higher, is not final, because the field-knower is not given up. The field-knower, though freed from nature and modifications, still has the nature of producing and of a seed; as long as a self remains, the relinquishing of ignorance, action and craving is not complete, and the freedom from I-making is only imagined; if the self is a knower it has something to know and is not freed, if not a knower a posited self is useless. Because relinquishing is called complete only when everything is relinquished, I hold that total relinquishment is the full accomplishment.

> parataḥ paratas tyāgo yasmāt tu guṇavān smṛtaḥ / tasmāt sarvaparityāgān manye kṛtsnāṃ kṛtārthatām

### 12.84-88 <a id="tea-buddhacarita-12-84-88"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Wishing to hear something higher, the bodhisattva went to Udraka's hermitage but did not accept his view either, because of its grasping at a self: Udraka, seeing the fault of both perception and non-perception, had gone beyond nothingness to the state that is neither perception nor non-perception; but since one returns (to the world) even after reaching it, the bodhisattva left him.
