**Attribution:** tradition: Śiva's teaching to Pārvatī on Mount Kailāsa on the guru, told by Sūta to the sages; scholarly: a late devotional text in several recensions (c. 180 to over 350 verses); its place in the Skanda Purāṇa is not borne out by the standard printed editions (low); confidence: low
**Dates:** Tradition's account: spoken by Śiva on Kailāsa; Scholarly account: uncertain; late medieval; (confidence low)
**Structure:** recensions differ: the Ganeshpuri (Siddha Yoga) recension numbers 182 verses plus preliminary and closing verses; a Kashi 1920 edition prints about 230; three-chapter versions of about 350 verses exist
**Availability:** digitized-original

Śiva teaches that the guru is Brahman itself: the syllable 'gu' is darkness and 'ru' its remover; the guru is Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheśvara and the supreme Brahman; the root of meditation is the guru's form, of worship his feet, of mantra his word, of liberation his grace. Without knowing the guru-principle, sacrifices, vows and pilgrimages are fruitless; the teaching is secret and to be given only to the devoted.
