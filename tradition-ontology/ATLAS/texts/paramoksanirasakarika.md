Sadyojyoti's 'Verses refuting the liberations of others': states and refutes some twenty conceptions of liberation held by other schools (among them Sāṃkhya, Vaiśeṣika, Buddhist, Pāñcarātra, Vedāntic, Pāśupata and other Śaiva views) and upholds the Siddhānta view that liberation is the manifestation of the soul's own Śiva-nature once mala is removed.

## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### 1-60 <a id="tea-paramoksanirasakarika-1-60"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The liberations taught by others — the isolation of puruṣa (Sāṃkhya), the extinction of the soul's special qualities (Vaiśeṣika), the cessation of the stream of cognitions (Buddhists), merging into the one Brahman (Vedānta), the Pāñcarātra and Pāśupata views of the soul's attaining or receiving the Lord's qualities, and others — are refuted; none is true release.
