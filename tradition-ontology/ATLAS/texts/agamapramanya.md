Yāmuna's defence of the Vedic authority of the Pāñcarātra. Contrasting it with the 'Māheśvara' tantras, he names four Śaiva groups - Kāpālikas, Kālāmukhas, Pāśupatas and Śaivas - and reports the Kāpālika six insignia and yoni-meditation, the Kālāmukha skull and corpse-ash practices, the Pāśupata five categories, and the claim that initiation makes one a brahmin at once; he judges all of them outside the Veda.

## Teachings (6: skeleton 6)

### mahesvara-tantra <a id="tea-agamapramanya-mahesvara-tantra"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Māheśvara tantra teaches much that is contradictory; its followers are fourfold - Kāpālikas, Kālāmukhas, Pāśupatas and Śaivas. The Kāpālas say release comes from knowing and wearing the six insignia, not from knowing Brahman: 'the one who knows the truth of the six insignia, skilled in the supreme seal, meditating on the self seated in the yoni, attains nirvāṇa'; the six are an ear-ornament, the rucaka, earrings, a crest-jewel, ash and the sacred thread, and the skull and the khaṭvāṅga are sub-insignia; 'one whose body is sealed with these is not born here again'.

> karṇikā rucakaṃ caiva kuṇḍalaṃ ca śikhāmaṇim | bhasma yajñopavītaṃ ca mudrāṣaṭkaṃ pracakṣate || kapālam atha khaṭvāṅgam upamudre prakīrtite | ābhir mudritadehas tu na bhūya iha jāyate

### mahesvara-tantra <a id="tea-agamapramanya-mahesvara-tantra-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Kālāmukhas likewise declare that eating from a skull, bathing in the ash of corpses and eating it, carrying a club, and setting up a pot of liquor and worshipping the deity in it - all forbidden by every scripture - accomplish seen and unseen desired ends; they are excluded by the Veda.

### mahesvara-tantra <a id="tea-agamapramanya-mahesvara-tantra-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Pāśupata doctrine: souls are called paśus and Śiva is their lord; for their benefit he composed the five-chapter text teaching five categories - cause, effect, rule, yoga and end of suffering; the cause is twofold, Rudra the efficient cause and his Kalā the other; the effect runs from mahat to earth; the rule runs from secret conduct to bathing in cremation-ground ash; yoga is holding the mind in the heart preceded by Oṃ; the end of suffering, release, is the absolute cessation of pain, understood as the destruction of all the soul's special qualities.

### purvapaksa <a id="tea-agamapramanya-purvapaksa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The objector holds that the Pāñcarātra is not a valid means of knowledge: its validity cannot rest on perception, inference or the Veda; its followers, the Sātvatas or Bhāgavatas, are not brahmins but a class who serve in temples for a living, as smṛti texts describe (e.g. the Sātvata listed among the offspring of degraded twice-born); smṛti verses class the Pāñcarātra with Śaiva, Pāśupata, Buddhist, Jain and Kāpāla doctrines as outside the Veda; and Bādarāyaṇa refutes it in the Brahma Sūtra.

### siddhanta <a id="tea-agamapramanya-siddhanta"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Yāmuna answers that the Pāñcarātra is valid because it was spoken by the omniscient Lord Vāsudeva; its followers are brahmins, called Sātvata or Bhāgavata because of a quality, as brahmins are called 'renouncers'; the verses condemning outside doctrines concern the Kāpālika, Kālāmukha, Pāśupata and Śaiva schools and the like, not the Bhagavat's scripture; and Vyāsa (Bādarāyaṇa), himself a supreme Bhāgavata, cannot be imagined to refute the Bhāgavata teaching — the sūtras on the origination of the soul are rightly understood as not refuting it.
