# Āgamaprāmāṇya

**Authors:**

**Dates:** Tradition's account: by Yāmunācārya, grand-teacher of Rāmānuja; Scholarly account: c. 10th–11th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Availability:** digitized-original

Yāmuna's treatise establishing the validity (prāmāṇya) of the Pāñcarātra: it sets out the objections — that the Pāñcarātra is outside the Veda, that its followers (Sātvatas/Bhāgavatas) are a low temple-serving caste, that Bādarāyaṇa refuted it — and answers that it was spoken by the omniscient Lord, is Vedic in root, and that its followers are brahmins; it distinguishes it from the Śaiva schools (Kāpālika, Kālāmukha, Pāśupata, Śaiva).
