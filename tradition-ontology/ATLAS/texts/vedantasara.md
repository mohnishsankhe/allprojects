# Vedāntasāra

Sadānanda's standard primer: the four preliminaries (qualified student, subject, relation, purpose); superimposition and retraction; ignorance with its two powers, collective and individual, and the three bodies, states and sheaths; quintuplication; a graded refutation of mistaken views of the self; the analysis of 'tat tvam asi' by exclusive-inclusive implication; hearing, reflection, contemplation and samādhi with eight auxiliaries and four obstacles; the jīvanmukta.

### adhikarin <a id="tea-vedantasara-adhikarin"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The qualified student has studied the Vedas and their auxiliaries, has understood their meaning, has avoided desire-prompted and forbidden acts, has performed the daily, occasional and expiatory rites and the meditations, and so, purified, possesses the four means.

### adhyaropa <a id="tea-vedantasara-adhyaropa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Superimposition (adhyāropa) is the imputing of the unreal on the real, like the snake on the rope; retraction (apavāda) is showing that the modification is only its substratum, as the snake is only the rope, so the world superimposed on Brahman is only Brahman.

> vastuny avastvāropo 'dhyāropaḥ

### ajnana <a id="tea-vedantasara-ajnana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ignorance is said to be something positive, indescribable as either being or non-being, made of the three guṇas, opposed to knowledge; taken collectively it is the adjunct of Īśvara, taken individually of prājña; it has two powers, veiling and projecting, as a small cloud hides the vast sun from the viewer's eye.

> sadasadbhyām anirvacanīyaṃ triguṇātmakaṃ jñānavirodhi bhāvarūpaṃ yat kiṃcid iti vadanti

### akhandakara <a id="tea-vedantasara-akhandakara"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On hearing the mahāvākya, a mental mode in the form of the partless Brahman (akhaṇḍākāra-vṛtti) arises; it pervades Brahman and destroys ignorance, but the reflected consciousness in it does not illumine Brahman, which is self-luminous — as a lamp is not needed to see the sun.

### apavāda section (verse quoted there) <a id="tea-vedantasara-apavada"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Retraction (apavāda) is showing that the unreal world-appearance beginning with ignorance, an apparent transformation (vivarta) of the real, is nothing but the real — as the snake, an apparent transformation of the rope, is only the rope. A verse is quoted: when a thing appears otherwise in reality it is called modification (vikāra); when it appears otherwise not in reality it is called apparent transformation (vivarta). By superimposition and retraction the meanings of the words 'that' and 'you' are also purified.

> सतत्त्वतोऽन्यथा प्रथा विकार इत्युदीरितः । अतत्त्वतोऽन्यथा प्रथा विवर्त इत्युदाहृतः ॥

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: ultimate, world-fate_

teachers: [Sadānanda (Yogīndra)](../teachers/sadananda.md) · disputes: [Does the effect pre-exist in its cause (satkārya), is it newly produced (asatkārya/ārambha), is it an apparent transformation of an unchanging cause (vivarta), a real manifestation of consciousness (ābhāsa), a transformation of God that leaves him unchanged (avikṛta-pariṇāma), or dependently arisen and ultimately unarisen?](../debates/causation.md), [Is the world real, or an appearance (mithyā) that is neither real nor unreal, superimposed on the one reality — and is māyā an indeterminable ignorance or the Lord's real power?](../debates/world-real-or-appearance.md)

### jivanmukta <a id="tea-vedantasara-jivanmukta"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The one liberated while living, knowing Brahman, lives with the body sustained by prārabdha; his conduct continues in good tendencies only, without sense of agency; the virtues of humility and the like remain as ornaments (quoting Naiṣkarmyasiddhi 4.62 and 4.69); at death his prāṇas merge and he abides as Brahman.

### obstacles <a id="tea-vedantasara-obstacles"></a>

Nirvikalpa samādhi has four obstacles: dullness (laya), the mind's lapse into sleep; distraction (vikṣepa), its turning to other things; latent attachment (kaṣāya), its stiffening by impressions of attachment; and relishing (rasāsvāda), its enjoyment of the bliss of savikalpa samādhi.

### refutation <a id="tea-vedantasara-refutation"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

A graded series of mistaken views of the self: the most ignorant take the son as the self, Cārvākas the gross body, the senses, the vital breath or the mind; a Buddhist the intellect; Prābhākaras and Naiyāyikas the self as associated with ignorance; Bhāṭṭas consciousness mixed with ignorance; another Buddhist the void — each refuted by the next and all by scripture and experience.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: dispute, consciousness-mind_

### samadhi <a id="tea-vedantasara-samadhi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Samādhi is twofold: with distinctions (savikalpa), in which the mental mode takes the form of Brahman while the distinction of knower and known remains, like a clay elephant seen as clay; and without distinctions (nirvikalpa), in which that distinction dissolves, like salt dissolved in water. Its eight auxiliaries are restraint, observance, posture, breath control, withdrawal, holding, meditation and (savikalpa) samādhi.

_level: conventional · standpoint: experiential · path: meditation, knowledge · stage: advanced · types: practice, consciousness-mind_

### sravana <a id="tea-vedantasara-sravana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Hearing is the determination, by the six marks, that the Upaniṣads' purport is the non-dual Brahman: opening and close, repetition, novelty, fruit, praise and demonstration; reflection is the continual consideration of this by reasoning in accord with it; contemplation is the flow of like ideas excluding unlike ones.

### tat-tvam-asi <a id="tea-vedantasara-tat-tvam-asi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In 'tat tvam asi' the relation of the words is co-reference, of their meanings qualifier–qualified, and of the self to them indicated–indicator; since the direct meanings conflict, exclusive implication and non-exclusive implication both fail, and exclusive-non-exclusive implication (bhāga-lakṣaṇā) applies, as in 'this is that Devadatta': dropping the contradictory parts, pure consciousness is indicated.
