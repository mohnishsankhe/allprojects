A mind-training poem attributed to Dharmarakṣita, Atiśa's teacher: suffering is the wheel of one's own past deeds returning; the bodhisattva, like a peacock turning poison into brilliance, uses afflictions and adversity, and turns the weapon against the true enemy, self-cherishing and self-grasping.

### peacock <a id="tea-wheel-of-sharp-weapons-peacock"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

As peacocks roam the forest of poisonous plants and thrive on poison where other birds would die, so bodhisattvas remain in the jungle of saṃsāra and turn the poisonous afflictions into the path.

### wheel <a id="tea-wheel-of-sharp-weapons-wheel"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Whatever misfortune befalls one is the wheel of sharp weapons of one's own wrong deeds turning back upon oneself; so turn the weapon on the true enemy, the demon of self-grasping and self-cherishing, and trample it.
