Udayana's defence of an enduring self against Buddhist positions, organized as refutations of momentariness (kṣaṇabhaṅga), of the denial of external objects (bāhyārthabhaṅga), of the denial of the difference between quality and qualified (guṇaguṇibhedabhaṅga), and of the argument from the non-apprehension of a self (anupalambha); it closes with the knowledge of the self and liberation.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Ātmatattvavivekadīdhiti](atmatattvaviveka-didhiti.md)

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### kṣaṇabhaṅga section <a id="tea-atmatattvaviveka-ksanabhanga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Against the argument that whatever exists is momentary because existence is causal efficacy and an enduring thing could not produce effects successively, Udayana holds that an enduring thing produces effects in succession as its auxiliary causes come together.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: world-fate, dispute_

concepts: [Causal efficacy without a separate power (śakti)](../concepts/samagri-without-sakti.md) · disputes: [Is everything that exists momentary?](../debates/momentariness.md)

### structure of the work <a id="tea-atmatattvaviveka-structure"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The self is established by refuting four positions that would exclude it: that everything is momentary; that there are no external objects; that qualities are not different from what has them; and that no self is apprehended.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: consciousness-mind, dispute_
