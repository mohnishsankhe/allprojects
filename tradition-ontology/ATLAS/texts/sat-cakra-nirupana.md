A detailed description of the six centres along the central channel (citriṇī within vajrā within suṣumnā) and the thousand-petalled lotus above: for each, its petals and letters, element and seed-syllable, deity and śakti; then the rousing of kuṇḍalinī, her ascent through the centres dissolving each element into the next, her union with Paramaśiva and her return. Made known in the West by Arthur Avalon's The Serpent Power (1919).

### 1-2 <a id="tea-sat-cakra-nirupana-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Outside Meru (the spine), to its left and right, are the channels of moon and sun, iḍā and piṅgalā; between them is suṣumnā, within it vajrā, within that citriṇī, fine as a spider's thread, and within that the channel of Brahman (brahmanāḍī), which the centres are strung upon.

### 4-13 <a id="tea-sat-cakra-nirupana-4-13"></a>

### 50 <a id="tea-sat-cakra-nirupana-50"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Within the Nirvāṇa-śakti is the stainless abode of Śiva, eternal, reached by yoga, called eternal bliss, full of all happiness, the light of pure knowledge; some of the wise call it Brahman, others call it Viṣṇu's state, some call it Haṃsa, and some the indescribable place that awakens the way to liberation.
