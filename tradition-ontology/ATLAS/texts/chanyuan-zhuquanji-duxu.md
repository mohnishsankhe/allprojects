Zongmi's preface (c. 833) to his lost anthology of Chan writings: correlates three Chan tenets (stopping the false and cultivating mind; eliminating traces; directly revealing the mind-nature) with three teachings, defends sudden awakening followed by gradual cultivation, and identifies the mind's essence as 'empty and calm knowing' — 'the one word knowing is the gate of all wonders'.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 402c28 <a id="tea-chanyuan-zhuquanji-duxu-402c28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Zongmi presenting the Heze teaching:) all dharmas are like a dream; deluded thoughts are originally quiescent, objects originally empty; the numinous knowing of the empty, quiescent mind is never obscured — this empty, quiescent knowing is your true nature; 'the one word knowing is the gate of all wonders'; when a thought arises, be aware of it; aware of it, it vanishes.

> 空寂之心靈知不昧。即此空寂之知。是汝真性

### 405b12 <a id="tea-chanyuan-zhuquanji-duxu-405b12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Zongmi's account of transmission: the six patriarchs transmitted the mind-seal silently, the silence concerning only the word 'knowing'; when other schools spread in Heze's time and no suitable recipient appeared, Shenhui, remembering Bodhidharma's prophecy that the teaching would hang by a thread, openly declared 'the one word knowing is the gate of all wonders'; the robe served as token for silent transmission.

> 至荷澤時他宗競播。欲求默契不遇機緣。又思惟達摩懸絲之記恐宗旨滅絕。遂明言知之一字眾妙之門
