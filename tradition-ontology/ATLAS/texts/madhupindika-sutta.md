## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 8 <a id="tea-madhupindika-sutta-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Buddha's brief statement: from whatever source perceptions and notions tinged with proliferation beset a person, if nothing there is found to delight in, welcome or hold to, that is the end of the underlying tendencies to lust, aversion, views, doubt, conceit, lust for becoming and ignorance, and of taking up weapons, quarrels and lies.

### 16.1-16.2 <a id="tea-madhupindika-sutta-16-1-16-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dependent on the eye and forms eye-consciousness arises; the meeting of the three is contact; with contact as condition there is feeling; what one feels one perceives; what one perceives one thinks about; what one thinks about one proliferates; and from that source, perceptions and notions tinged with proliferation beset a person with regard to past, future and present forms.

> Cakkhuñcāvuso, paṭicca rūpe ca uppajjati cakkhuviññāṇaṁ, tiṇṇaṁ saṅgati phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā, yaṁ vedeti taṁ sañjānāti, yaṁ sañjānāti taṁ vitakketi, yaṁ vitakketi taṁ papañceti, yaṁ papañceti tatonidānaṁ purisaṁ papañcasaññāsaṅkhā samudācaranti atītānāgatapaccuppannesu cakkhuviññeyyesu rūpesu. ### 16-22 <a id="tea-madhupindika-sutta-16-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mahākaccāna's full analysis for each of the six senses: where there is eye, form and eye-consciousness one can point to contact, feeling, perception, thought and being beset by proliferation; where these are absent none can be pointed to. The Buddha praises the exposition as a honey-ball, sweet wherever one tastes it.
