## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 2-7 <a id="tea-culamalunkya-sutta-2-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Māluṅkyaputta demands answers to ten questions (the world eternal or not, finite or infinite, the soul the same as the body or different, the Tathāgata existing after death or not, both or neither) or he will leave the training. The Buddha: it is like a man struck by a poisoned arrow who refuses its removal until he knows who shot it, his caste, name, height, complexion and the bow — he would die first; the holy life does not depend on these views, for there is still birth, ageing and death, whose destruction here and now the Buddha teaches.

### 8-10 <a id="tea-culamalunkya-sutta-8-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Why undeclared? Because it is unbeneficial, not the basis of the holy life, and does not lead to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, peace, direct knowledge, awakening and nibbāna. What is declared is suffering, its origin, its cessation and the way, because that is beneficial. Remember what is undeclared as undeclared, and what is declared as declared.
