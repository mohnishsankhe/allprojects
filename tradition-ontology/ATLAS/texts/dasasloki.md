Nimbārka's ten verses summarising the school: the soul, bondage and release, the three kinds of non-conscious reality, Kṛṣṇa, Rādhā, worship, the reality of all as Brahman's nature, refuge in Kṛṣṇa alone, grace and devotion, and the five things to be known.

## Teachings (10: skeleton 10)

### 1 <a id="tea-dasasloki-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The soul is of the nature of knowledge, dependent on Hari, fit to be joined with and separated from bodies, atomic, different in each body, a knower, and infinite in number.

### 2 <a id="tea-dasasloki-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Souls are bound, their form covered by beginningless māyā, or released by the Lord's grace; the released are of different kinds.

### 3 <a id="tea-dasasloki-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The non-conscious (acit) is of three kinds: the non-material (aprākṛta), the material (prākṛta) and time (kāla).

### 4 <a id="tea-dasasloki-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Let us meditate on Kṛṣṇa, the lotus-eyed Hari, the supreme Brahman, who by nature is free of every fault, is the single mass of all auspicious qualities and has the vyūhas as his limbs.

### 5 <a id="tea-dasasloki-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Let us remember the goddess, daughter of Vṛṣabhānu, shining joyfully on his left side, of matching beauty, always served by thousands of sakhīs, granting all desired ends.

### 6 <a id="tea-dasasloki-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

### 7 <a id="tea-dasasloki-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

All knowledge is true, since according to scripture everything has Brahman as its self; the threefold nature (cit, acit, Īśvara) is established by scripture and the sūtras.

### 8 <a id="tea-dasasloki-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

No other refuge is seen than Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, worshipped by Brahmā, Śiva and the rest, who takes a form at his devotees' wish, whose power is inconceivable and whose intent is beyond thought.

His grace arises for one endowed with humility and the like; by it arises devotion marked by special love for the Lord who has no other master; that devotion is the highest (parā), and there is also the devotion that is a means (sādhana).
