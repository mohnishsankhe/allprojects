### 1.3 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A means of valid cognition is a non-deceptive cognition; non-deception consists in the continuance of (the object's capacity for) causal efficacy; verbal cognition too is non-deceptive in that it conveys the speaker's intention.

> pramāṇamavisaṃvādi jñānamarthakriyāsthitiḥ / avisaṃvādanaṃ śābde 'pyabhiprāyanivedanāt

### 1.7 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-7"></a>

### 1.9 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-9"></a>

### 1.10 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There is no permanent means of knowledge, since validity is the apprehension of an existent thing; because knowables are impermanent, the cognition of them is not constant, for they arise in succession.

> nityaṃ pramāṇaṃ naivāsti prāmāṇyādvastusadgateḥ

### 1.34-35 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-34-35"></a>

The one who knows the reality of what is to be abandoned and what is to be adopted, together with the means, is accepted as a means of valid cognition — not one who knows everything. ### 1.36-37 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-36-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The proof (of his authority) is the cultivation of compassion. ### 1.126-133 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-126-133"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

> abhyāsajāḥ pravarttante svarasena kṛpādayaḥ

### 1.137-140 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-137-140"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Attachment is produced by grasping at self and what belongs to self and has conditioned things as its object; its opposing cause is the seeing of selflessness, which destroys it; by long cultivation of the method in many ways, the virtues and faults become clear, and through the sharpness of understanding the imprints are abandoned; the great sage differs from the rhinoceros-like (solitary buddha) in acting for others.

### 1.147-149 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-147-149"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

### 1.219-221 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-1-219-221"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One who sees a self has lasting love for it as 'I'; from love he thirsts for happiness; thirst hides the faults; seeing virtues and thirsting, he appropriates the means as 'mine'; so as long as there is attachment to self, he is in saṃsāra. ### 2.281-285 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-2-281-285"></a>

### 3.45-47 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-3-45-47"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Since a thing is grasped as a whole in perception, all its properties are grasped; but because of error they are not ascertained, and so proof operates. If inference grasped a real thing, ascertaining one property would ascertain all; with exclusion this fault does not follow; therefore the object of the reason is declared to be exclusion.

> tasmādapohaviṣayamiti liṅgaṃ prakīrtitam

### 3.225-227 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-3-225-227"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 3.273-276 <a id="tea-pramanavarttika-3-273-276"></a>
