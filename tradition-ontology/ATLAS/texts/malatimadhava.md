A ten-act drama. The Kāpālika Aghoraghaṇṭa, come from Śrīparvata, and his female disciple Kapālakuṇḍalā plan to offer the heroine Mālatī to the goddess Karālā (Cāmuṇḍā) at the great cremation ground; the hero Mādhava kills Aghoraghaṇṭa (act 5) and Kapālakuṇḍalā seeks revenge. Kapālakuṇḍalā enters flying and speaks of the channels, the heart-lotus and the Lord of the Śaktis.

### 1 <a id="tea-malatimadhava-1"></a>

Near the city's great cremation ground stands the Cāmuṇḍā called Karālā, said to delight in offerings of living beings; the powerful Kapālakuṇḍalā, disciple of Aghoraghaṇṭa - a night-roaming adept come from Śrīparvata who bears skulls and lives in the nearby forest - comes there every evening.

### 5 <a id="tea-malatimadhava-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Aghoraghaṇṭa, his mantra-rite complete, has ordered that a jewel of a woman long promised to the goddess Karālā be offered that night; the two Kāpālikas worship Cāmuṇḍā and are about to kill Mālatī when Mādhava, who had come to the cremation ground to sell 'great flesh' to its spirits, intervenes and kills Aghoraghaṇṭa.

### 5.1-2 <a id="tea-malatimadhava-5-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Kapālakuṇḍalā, arriving through the air, praises the Lord of the Śaktis, encircled by Śaktis, whose self dwells in the middle of the circle of sixteen channels, whose form is placed in the heart, who grants powers to those who know him, sought by adepts of unwavering mind; she says that, seeing the self in Śiva's form in the heart-lotus of the six-limbed wheel, she has come, drawing up the 'five nectars' of the world through the rising order of the channels, without the fatigue of flight, parting the clouds before her.
