Citra Gāṅgyāyani teaches Aruni and Śvetaketu the path to the world of brahman (the moon's question, rebirth by karma and knowledge, the journey through the brahma-world and the dialogue with Brahma); prāṇa as brahman and domestic rites; Indra teaches Pratardana that he is breath, the intelligent self; Balaki and Ajātaśatru.

### 1.1 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Citra Gāṅgyāyani, about to sacrifice, chose Āruṇi as priest; Āruṇi sent his son Śvetaketu, whom Citra asked: 'Is there a hidden place in the world where you will set me, or another way?' Śvetaketu did not know; he and his father came to Citra with fuel in hand; Citra said: 'You are worthy of brahman, Gautama, for you were not led by pride; come, I will make you understand.'

### 1.2 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

All who depart this world go to the moon; the moon is the door of the heavenly world. It asks each one who he is; whoever answers it rightly it lets pass; whoever does not, it rains down as rain, and he is born here as a worm, a moth, a bird, a tiger, a lion, a fish, a boar(?), a man or some other creature in this or that place, according to his deeds and his knowledge. The right answer includes 'I am the season, born of the seasons... who are you? I am you (tvam asmi).'

### 1.3 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Reaching the path of the gods, he comes to the world of fire, of wind, of Varuṇa, of the sun, of Indra, of Prajāpati and of brahman. In the brahma-world are the lake Āra, the moments (muhūrta) that destroy sacrifice, the ageless (Vijarā; or stainless, Virajā) river, the tree Ilya (Tilya), the city Sālajya (Sāyujya), the palace Aparājita, the doorkeepers Indra and Prajāpati, the hall Vibhu, the throne Vicakṣaṇā, the couch Amitaujas, and the beloved Mānasī and her counterpart Cākṣuṣī, with the Ambās, Ambāyavīs and apsarases.

### 1.4 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Five hundred apsarases run to meet him with garlands, ointments, powders, clothes and fruits and adorn him with the adornment of brahman. He crosses the lake by the mind; the moments flee; he crosses the river by the mind and there shakes off his good and bad deeds: his dear relatives receive the good deeds, those not dear the bad. As one driving a chariot looks down on its wheels, so he looks down on day and night, on good and bad deeds and all pairs of opposites; free of good and bad deeds, knowing brahman, he goes to brahman.

### 1.5-7 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-1-5-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

He reaches the tree, the city, the palace and the hall, and brahman's fragrance, flavour, radiance and glory enter him; he comes to the throne Vicakṣaṇā, which is wisdom (prajñā), and the couch Amitaujas, which is breath, on which Brahmā sits. Brahmā asks 'who are you?'; he answers: 'I am the season, born of the seasons...; you are the self of every being; what you are, that am I.' 'Who am I?' 'The real (satyam)' — what is other than the gods and the breaths is sat, the gods and breaths are tyam. Asked by what he obtains Brahmā's male names, female names, neuter names, smells, forms, sounds, tastes, actions, pleasure and pain, bliss, movements and thoughts, he answers: by breath, speech, mind, smell, sight, hearing, tongue, hands, body, the sexual organ, feet and wisdom.

### 2.1-2 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-2-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'Breath is brahman', said Kauṣītaki: mind is its messenger, speech its housekeeper, the eye its guard, the ear its herald; to one who knows this all the deities bring tribute without his asking; its secret rule (upaniṣad) is: one should not beg. 'Breath is brahman', said Paiṅgya, ordering the senses as enclosing one another.

### 2.5 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-2-5"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Now the self-restraint of Pratardana, which they call the inner fire-offering (āntaram agnihotram): as long as a person speaks he cannot breathe; then he offers breath into speech; as long as he breathes he cannot speak; then he offers speech into breath. These two endless, immortal offerings he makes continually, waking or asleep; all other offerings have an end, for they consist of works. Knowing this, the ancients did not offer the agnihotra.

### 2.12-13 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-2-12-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The dying around the deities (daivaḥ parimaraḥ): brahman shines when fire blazes, dies when it does not blaze; its radiance goes to the sun, its breath to the wind; so the sun, moon and lightning, when they die, enter the wind and rise again from it. With respect to the self: speech, sight, hearing and mind enter breath in sleep and rise from it on waking. Even if mountains roll against one who knows this, they cannot overcome him.

### 2.14 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-2-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The deities contended, each claiming superiority, and left the body; it lay breathless like a log; speech, sight, hearing and mind entering it could not raise it; when breath entered it rose. They recognized breath as the best and, merging into breath, departed from this world with it and became immortal.

### 2.15 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-2-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The father-son transfer (saṃpradāna): a father about to die lies down on his son, touching his senses with his, and transfers them: 'I place my speech in you' — 'I take your speech into me'; likewise breath, sight, hearing, taste, actions, pleasure and pain, bliss and procreation, movement, mind and intelligence. If he recovers he should live under his son's lordship or wander as a mendicant.

### 3.1 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-3-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Pratardana Daivodāsi came to Indra's dear abode by fighting and valour; Indra offered a boon; Pratardana asked him to choose what he thought most beneficial to humans. Indra: 'Know me alone; that is most beneficial. I killed the three-headed son of Tvaṣṭṛ, gave the Arunmukha ascetics to the hyenas, broke many treaties, yet not a hair of mine was harmed. Whoever knows me, his world is not harmed by any deed — not by theft, killing an embryo, killing his mother or father; if he has done evil, the dark colour does not leave his face.'

### 3.2 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-3-2"></a>

'I am breath, the intelligent self (prajñātman); venerate me as life, as immortality. Life is breath, breath is life; breath is immortality; as long as breath remains in the body, so long there is life; by breath one obtains immortality in the other world, by intelligence true intention.'

### 3.3-8 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-3-3-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Breath is intelligence and intelligence is breath; they live together in the body and depart together. In deep sleep speech with all names, sight with all forms, hearing with all sounds, mind with all thoughts go into breath; on waking they spread out from it like sparks from a fire. One should not seek to know speech but the speaker, not smell but the smeller, and so on to the thinker. The ten elements of being (bhūtamātrā) and the ten of intelligence (prajñāmātrā) rest on one another like the felly on the spokes and the spokes on the hub.

### 3.9 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-3-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This breath, the intelligent self, is bliss, unaging, immortal; it does not become greater by good action nor less by bad. It makes him whom it wishes to lead up from these worlds do good action, and him whom it wishes to lead down do bad action. It is the guardian of the world, the lord of the world, the lord of all — 'he is my self', so one should know.

### 4.19-20 <a id="tea-kausitaki-upanisad-4-19-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ajātaśatru leads him to a sleeping man; when he is asleep and sees no dream, he becomes one in breath, and speech, sight, hearing and mind go into it; on waking they spread out as sparks from a fire. This intelligent self has entered the body up to the hair and nails, like a razor in its case or fire in its holder; as a chief with his people, so the self enjoys with these selves. As long as Indra did not understand this self the asuras overcame him; when he understood it he conquered them; so one who knows this gains supremacy.
