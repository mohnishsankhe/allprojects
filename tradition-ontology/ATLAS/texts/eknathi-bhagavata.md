Eknāth's great Marathi ovī commentary on the eleventh book of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa (Kṛṣṇa's teaching to Uddhava and the nine sages' teaching to Nimi): devotion to the name, the guru and the saints, the avadhūta's twenty-four teachers, and non-dual knowledge, with a defence of teaching in Marathi. It is the second of the Vārkarīs' three foundational books.

The best devotee sees the Lord in all beings and all beings in the Lord; Eknāth makes this vision the mark of the true bhāgavata.

### chs. 7–9 (on BhP 11.7–9) <a id="tea-eknathi-bhagavata-7-9-avadhuta"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The avadhūta tells King Yadu of his twenty-four teachers — earth, air, sky, water, fire, moon, sun, pigeon, python, ocean, moth, bee, elephant, honey-gatherer, deer, fish, the courtesan Piṅgalā, osprey, child, maiden, arrow-maker, serpent, spider and wasp — each teaching detachment; Eknāth draws from each a lesson for the devotee.

If God made Sanskrit speech, was the vernacular born of thieves? The Lord is pleased by devotion, not by the language; the Bhāgavata's meaning told in Marathi is as holy as in Sanskrit.
