A Chedasūtra in ten chapters: the twenty faults disturbing concentration (asamādhi-sthāna), the twenty-one grave faults (śabala), the thirty-three disrespects to the teacher (āśātanā), the eight accomplishments of a gaṇin, the ten states of mental concentration, the eleven stages of the layman (upāsaka-pratimā), the twelve special vows of the monk (bhikṣu-pratimā), the Paryuṣaṇākalpa (the Kalpa Sūtra), the thirty sources of deluding karma, and the nine kinds of 'wishing for a reward' (nidāna).

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 6 <a id="tea-dasasrutaskandha-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The eleven stages (pratimā) of the lay follower: right faith, vows, equanimity, fasting on holy days (pauṣadha), standing meditation at night, chastity, renouncing living (uncooked) food, not initiating harmful undertakings, not having them done by servants, refusing food prepared for oneself, and living like a monk (śramaṇa-bhūta).

### 10 <a id="tea-dasasrutaskandha-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The nine kinds of nidāna: a monk or nun who wishes that his austerities should bring him a particular reward — to be reborn as a king, a man, a woman, a god, or even a pious layman — binds himself by that wish and does not reach liberation in that life.
