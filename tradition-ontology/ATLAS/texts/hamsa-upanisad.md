Gautama asks Sanatsujāta (in this recension) for the knowledge Śiva gave Pārvatī: the haṃsa (the self as the natural breath-mantra haṃsa/so'ham) pervading all bodies, its ascent through the centres, the mantra's seer, metre and deity, the eight-petalled heart lotus whose directions govern moods, and the ten inner sounds with their effects, ending in becoming Brahman.

### 1-2 <a id="tea-hamsa-upanisad-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Gautama asks how knowledge of Brahman arises; Sanatsujāta answers with the teaching Śiva gave Pārvatī after examining all the Vedas, a secret for yogins that gives enjoyment and liberation.

### 3 (ascent through the centres) <a id="tea-hamsa-upanisad-3-ascent-through-the-centres"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Pressing the anus and raising the breath from the ādhāra, the yogin circles the svādhiṣṭhāna, passes the maṇipūra, anāhata, viśuddhi and ājñā and meditates at the brahmarandhra: 'I am the three-measured one'; thus he sees and becomes formless.

### 3 (haṃsa as bird) <a id="tea-hamsa-upanisad-3-hamsa-as-bird"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The haṃsa is visualized as a bird: fire and moon its wings, oṃkāra its head, bindu its eye.

### 3 (haṃsa mantra) <a id="tea-hamsa-upanisad-3-hamsa-mantra"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The haṃsa mantra has haṃsa as seer, avyakta-gāyatrī as metre and paramahaṃsa as deity; ham is its seed, sa its power, so'ham its bolt; it is repeated 21,600 times a day with every breath.

### 3 (haṃsa pervades bodies) <a id="tea-hamsa-upanisad-3-hamsa-pervades-bodies"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The haṃsa, repeating 'haṃsa haṃsa', pervades all bodies as fire in wood and oil in sesame; knowing it one passes beyond death. The teaching is for the celibate, calm, restrained student devoted to the guru.

_level: bridging · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: intermediate · types: ultimate, sound-language_

### 3 (heart lotus) <a id="tea-hamsa-upanisad-3-heart-lotus"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the eight-petalled heart lotus the mind's disposition follows the petal it rests on — east virtue, south-east sleep and sloth, south cruelty, south-west sin, west play, north-west going and moving, north love, north-east giving; at the centre dispassion; filaments waking, pericarp dream, liṅga deep sleep; leaving the lotus, the fourth; when the haṃsa is absorbed in nāda, the state beyond the fourth (unmanī).

_level: conventional · standpoint: experiential · path: body-breath, meditation · stage: advanced · types: consciousness-mind, body-layers_

### 4 (effects of the sounds) <a id="tea-hamsa-upanisad-4-effects-of-the-sounds"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Effects follow the sounds in order: tingling, the limbs 'breaking', fatigue, head shaking, the palate dripping, drinking nectar, secret knowledge, the supreme speech, the body becoming invisible with the divine eye, and with the tenth he becomes Brahman — where mind dissolves, merit and sin are burnt, and the Self shines as Sadāśiva.

_level: conventional · standpoint: experiential · path: body-breath, meditation · stage: advanced · types: powers-experiences, karma-liberation_

### 4 (ten sounds) <a id="tea-hamsa-upanisad-4-ten-sounds"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

After much japa the nāda is heard in ten forms: ciṇī, ciñciṇī, bell, conch, lute, cymbal, flute, mṛdaṅga drum, bherī drum and thunder-cloud; the yogin abandons the first nine and practises the tenth.
