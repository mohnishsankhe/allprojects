# Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā (Haṭhapradīpikā)

`src:hatha-yoga-pradipika` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Haṭhapradīpikā, Haṭhayogapradīpikā, HYP
**Original title:** हठयोगप्रदीपिका
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md), [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md)
**Genre:** yoga manual
**Authors:** 
  - [Svātmārāma](../teachers/svatmarama.md) — role: compiler; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: by Svātmārāma, who received haṭhavidyā through the grace of Matsyendra, Gorakṣa and the Siddhas (1.4); scholarly: compiled by Svātmārāma c. mid-15th c. from some twenty earlier texts (Amṛtasiddhi, DYŚ, Vivekamārtaṇḍa, Khecarīvidyā, Amaraughaprabodha, Amanaska and others); confidence: high
**Dates:** Tradition's account: in the succession of Ādinātha, Matsyendra and Gorakṣa; Scholarly account: c. 1450 CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** vulgate: 4 upadeśas, c. 389 verses (āsana; prāṇāyāma and ṣaṭkarma; mudrā; samādhi/nādānusandhāna). A longer recension in 10 chapters is also transmitted.
**Availability:** digitized-original

The classic compendium of haṭha, taught 'solely for rājayoga'. Upadeśa 1: lineage, secrecy, the hut, what destroys and promotes yoga, postures, diet. 2: breath-control, the purification of the channels, the six cleansing acts and the eight retentions, with warnings about improper practice. 3: kuṇḍalinī and the ten mudrās (including khecarī and vajrolī). 4: samādhi and its synonyms, the dissolution of mind and breath, śāmbhavī, the four stages and the inner sounds (nādānusandhāna), and the liberated yogin.

### 1.12-13 <a id="tea-hatha-yoga-pradipika-1-12-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The haṭha yogin should live alone in a small hut (maṭhikā) in a well-ruled, righteous country where alms are plentiful and there is no disturbance, with no rocks, fire or water within a bow's length. The hut has a small door and no windows, holes or pits; it is neither too high nor too low, well smeared with cow-dung, clean and free of creatures, with a pavilion, raised platform and well outside and a surrounding wall — this is the mark of the yoga-hut as the siddhas who practise haṭha describe it.

The yogin joined in samādhi is not devoured by time, not bound by karma, not overcome by anyone; he knows neither smell, taste, form, touch nor sound, neither himself nor another; his mind is neither asleep nor awake, free of memory and forgetting, neither setting nor rising; he knows neither cold nor heat, pain nor pleasure, honour nor dishonour; in the waking state he abides as if asleep, without in- or out-breath; he cannot be killed by any weapon, is beyond the power of all beings and beyond the reach of mantras and yantras.

### 4.114 <a id="tea-hatha-yoga-pradipika-4-114"></a>

As long as the moving breath does not enter the middle path, as long as the bindu is not firm through binding the breath, as long as the natural (sahaja) principle does not arise in meditation, so long talk of knowledge is hypocritical, false prattle.
