Kundakunda's treatise on 'what must be done' (niyama) — right knowledge, faith and conduct — describing the reliable teacher (āpta) free from the eighteen faults, the substances, and conduct from the ultimate standpoint: real repentance, renunciation, confession, equanimity and devotion as the self's abiding in itself; he says he wrote it for his own meditation.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 3 <a id="tea-niyamasara-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

What must be done without fail (niyama) is knowledge, perception and conduct; 'essence' (sāra) is added to exclude their opposites.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: practice, karma-liberation_

### 6 <a id="tea-niyamasara-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The reliable one (āpta) is free of the eighteen faults: hunger, thirst, fear, anger, attachment, delusion, anxiety, old age, disease, death, sweat, fatigue, pride, pleasure, wonder, sleep, birth and distress.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: divine · path: knowledge · stage: realized · types: ultimate, powers-experiences_

`skeleton` · confidence moderate

I have composed this scripture named Niyamasāra for the sake of my own contemplation, having heard the teaching of the Jina.
