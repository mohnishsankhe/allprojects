The Sūtra of Golden Light: the Buddha's lifespan is immeasurable; the confession chapter (born from a golden drum seen in a dream); emptiness; the four great kings, Sarasvatī, Śrī and the earth goddess vow to protect lands where it is honored; a treatise on righteous kingship; the prince who gave his body to a starving tigress. Central to state-protection rites in East Asia and to Tibetan and Newar practice.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 2 <a id="tea-suvarnaprabhasa-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Buddha's lifespan is immeasurable; he entered parinirvāṇa only to ripen beings — the Buddha does not truly pass away, the dharma does not disappear.

_level: bridging · standpoint: divine · path: devotion · stage: all · types: ultimate, death-dying_

### 4 <a id="tea-suvarnaprabhasa-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The confession (deśanā) heard from a golden drum seen in a dream: confessing before the buddhas all evil done through ignorance, passion and pride, in this and past lives, and rejoicing in all merit, removes evil karma.

_level: conventional · standpoint: ritual · path: ritual, devotion · stage: all · types: practice, karma-liberation_

Prince Mahāsattva, seeing a starving tigress about to eat her cubs, gives his own body to her; he was a past life of the Buddha.
