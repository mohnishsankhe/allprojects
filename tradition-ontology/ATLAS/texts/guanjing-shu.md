Shandao's commentary, written to 'settle the old and the new' interpretations: the Contemplation Sūtra was taught for ordinary beings, all nine grades are ordinary people; Sukhāvatī is a reward land into which ordinary beings are born by the Buddha's vow; the first thirteen contemplations are meditative good, the last three non-meditative good; the three minds (sincere, deep, transferring) with the two kinds of deep entrusting; the five right practices, of which the calling of the name is the rightly determined act 'because it accords with the Buddha's vow'; the parable of the two rivers and the white path; the sūtra's final entrustment of the name shows that the Buddha's intent is exclusive calling of Amitābha's name. Hōnen converted on reading it (1175).

### 1.essential-and-universal <a id="tea-guanjing-shu-1-essential-and-universal"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The sūtra has two gates: the essential gate (yaomen) — Śākyamuni's teaching of the meditative and non-meditative good dedicated to birth — and the universal vow (hongyuan), Amitābha's vow by whose power all good and evil ordinary beings are born.

### 1.reward-land <a id="tea-guanjing-shu-1-reward-land"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Shandao 'settles the old and the new': the Buddha and land are reward, not transformation, since they arise from Dharmākara's vows and practice; though a reward land is lofty, ordinary beings full of defilements can enter it because they ride on the power of the Buddha's vow as the augmenting condition; and all nine grades of the sūtra are ordinary beings who have met evil or good conditions, not sages of the stages, against masters who ranked the upper grades as bodhisattvas.

### 4.buddha-intent <a id="tea-guanjing-shu-4-buddha-intent"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Although the sūtra has taught the benefits of meditative and non-meditative good, when one looks to the Buddha's original vow its intent is that beings single-mindedly and exclusively call Amitābha's name.

### 4.postscript-dream <a id="tea-guanjing-shu-4-postscript-dream"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Shandao writes that before composing the commentary he vowed to seek a sign; each night in dreams a monk instructed him on the arrangement of the essential meaning, and after completing it he had further visions; he therefore asks that the work be treated as if it were a sūtra, not to be added to or subtracted from.

### 4.restraint-gate <a id="tea-guanjing-shu-4-restraint-gate"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Why does the eighteenth vow exclude the five grave offences and slander while the Contemplation Sūtra admits five-offence evil-doers? The vow's exclusion is the gate of restraint (yizhi men): the Buddha, fearing that beings would commit these two gravest faults and fall into hell for long ages, speaks of exclusion to restrain them; it does not mean they are not embraced. Once committed, they too are taken in and born — though those so born remain in closed lotuses for many kalpas.

### 4.rightly-determined-act <a id="tea-guanjing-shu-4-rightly-determined-act"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Practices are of two kinds, right and miscellaneous. The right practices are five, all directed to Amitābha: reading and reciting the Pure Land sūtras, contemplating that land and Buddha, worshipping him, calling his name, and praising and making offerings. Of these, to single-mindedly recollect Amitābha's name, whether walking, standing, sitting or lying, without regard to the length of time, never abandoning it moment to moment — this is called the rightly determined act, because it accords with that Buddha's vow; the others are auxiliary acts. All other good practices are called miscellaneous.

### 4.sincere-mind <a id="tea-guanjing-shu-4-sincere-mind"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The sincere mind means that practice of body, speech and mind must be done with a true mind: one should not outwardly show signs of wisdom, goodness and diligence while inwardly harbouring falsehood; practice mixed with greed, anger and deceit, even done with urgency as if brushing fire from one's head, is 'poisoned good', unfit for birth in the land built by the Buddha's true mind.

### 4.two-deep-minds <a id="tea-guanjing-shu-4-two-deep-minds"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The deep mind is deeply entrusting mind, of two kinds: first, to believe deeply and decisively that one is now a foolish ordinary being of sin and evil in birth-and-death, ever sinking and ever flowing since countless kalpas, without any condition for escape; second, to believe deeply and decisively that Amitābha's forty-eight vows embrace beings and that, without doubt or anxiety, riding the power of his vow one will surely be born.

### 4.two-rivers <a id="tea-guanjing-shu-4-two-rivers"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A traveller going west across a wilderness, pursued by bandits and wild beasts, meets two rivers — fire to the south, water to the north — each a hundred paces wide and bottomless, with a white path only four or five inches wide between them, lashed by waves and flames. From the eastern bank a voice urges him to go resolutely; from the western bank a voice calls him to come straight on without fear. Bandits call him back, saying the path is dangerous; he goes on single-mindedly and reaches the western bank, free of all dangers, meeting good friends. The east bank is this burning house, the west the Pure Land; the bandits and beasts are the six senses, consciousnesses and elements, and those of differing views; the water is greed, the fire anger; the white path is the pure aspiration for birth arising amid passions; the voice from the east is Śākyamuni's exhortation, from the west Amitābha's call.
