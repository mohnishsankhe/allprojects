Rāmānuja's independent treatise on the meaning of the Upaniṣads: it criticizes the views of Śaṅkara, Bhāskara and Yādavaprakāśa, classifies scriptural texts into those of difference, non-difference and mediation (ghaṭaka), reads 'tat tvam asi' by co-reference, and establishes Nārāyaṇa as the supreme Brahman reached through bhakti.

### 1 <a id="tea-vedarthasangraha-1"></a>

Homage to Viṣṇu, the master (śeṣin) of all sentient and insentient things, who reclines on Śeṣa, the treasury of spotless, infinite auspicious qualities.

### critique of the three schools <a id="tea-vedarthasangraha-critique-of-three-schools"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Rāmānuja rejects three rival readings of the Upaniṣads: that of Śaṅkara (Brahman, pure consciousness, is covered by avidyā), of Bhāskara (Brahman itself is bound by real limiting adjuncts) and of Yādavaprakāśa (Brahman itself transforms into souls and matter); each makes Brahman the seat of ignorance, bondage or change.

### opening prose (the purport of the Upaniṣads) <a id="tea-vedarthasangraha-purport"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The purport of the Upaniṣads is this: meditation on, worship of and prostration to the feet of the supreme Person, which is exceedingly dear, preceded by true knowledge of the individual and the supreme self and accompanied by the duties of one's class and stage of life, and whose fruit is attaining him.

### section on 'tat tvam asi' <a id="tea-vedarthasangraha-tat-tvam-asi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In 'tat tvam asi' both words denote Brahman: 'that' denotes Brahman as the omniscient cause of the world, 'you' denotes Brahman having the individual soul as its body; co-reference (sāmānādhikaraṇya) of two words with different grounds (pravṛtti-nimitta) in one object — without giving up their primary meanings — teaches Brahman as qualified, not an attributeless identity.

### section on Nārāyaṇa's supremacy <a id="tea-vedarthasangraha-narayana-supreme"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Nārāyaṇa alone is the supreme Brahman: texts such as 'Nārāyaṇa alone existed, not Brahmā nor Īśāna' show that Brahmā and Śiva are individual souls, parts of his body, who hold office by his grant.

### section on bhakti as the means <a id="tea-vedarthasangraha-bhakti"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The means to reach the Lord is bhakti — knowledge that has taken the form of love (prīti) — which is born of karma-yoga and jñāna-yoga performed as his worship, and which is won through his grace.

### section on kapyāsa (Chāndogya 1.6.7) <a id="tea-vedarthasangraha-kapyasa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Chāndogya's description of the golden Person's eyes as 'kapyāsa' lotuses means lotuses opened by the sun (the one who drinks water), not something ignoble; the Lord's form is supremely beautiful, and descriptions of his form are to be read so.

### section on the three kinds of scriptural text <a id="tea-vedarthasangraha-ghataka-sruti"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Scripture has texts of difference (bheda-śruti), texts of non-difference (abheda-śruti) and mediating texts (ghaṭaka-śruti) such as the Antaryāmi Brāhmaṇa and the Subāla Upaniṣad, which declare souls and matter to be the Lord's body; through the body–self relation all three are true together, without rejecting any.
