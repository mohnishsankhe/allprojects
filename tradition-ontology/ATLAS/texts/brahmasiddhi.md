Maṇḍana Miśra's independent Advaita treatise in four chapters (Brahma-, Tarka-, Niyoga-, Siddhi-kāṇḍa): Brahman as bliss and consciousness; avidyā residing in the individual selves as non-apprehension and misapprehension; perception grasps being, not difference; critique of the view that the Upaniṣads enjoin (niyoga); liberation through knowledge made direct by repeated contemplation (prasaṅkhyāna), with works as helpful.

## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 1 <a id="tea-brahmasiddhi-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Brahman is bliss and consciousness; ignorance (avidyā) belongs to the individual selves, not to Brahman, and is of two kinds — non-apprehension (agrahaṇa) and misapprehension (anyathā-grahaṇa); it is neither Brahman's nature nor another real entity.

### 2.1 <a id="tea-brahmasiddhi-2-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The wise say perception is affirmative (it presents what is), not negating (it does not grasp difference); therefore scripture's teaching of oneness is not contradicted by perception.

> āhur vidhātṛ pratyakṣaṃ na niṣeddhṛ vipaścitaḥ / naikatva āgamas tena pratyakṣeṇa virudhyate

### 3 <a id="tea-brahmasiddhi-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Upaniṣads do not have injunction (niyoga) as their purport, against the Prābhākara view; they make known the existent Brahman.

### 4 <a id="tea-brahmasiddhi-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Knowledge from the Upaniṣadic sentence is not yet direct and does not by itself remove the deep-rooted impressions of difference; repeated contemplation (prasaṅkhyāna, upāsanā) of that knowledge makes it direct and removes them; works performed without desire help by purifying and assisting the rise of knowledge.

### 4 <a id="tea-brahmasiddhi-4-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The one called 'liberated while living' or 'of steady wisdom' is in truth an advanced seeker (sādhaka), not one fully freed, since full liberation comes only with the fall of the body.
