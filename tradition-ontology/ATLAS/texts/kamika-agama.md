# Kāmikāgama

`src:kamika-agama` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Alternate titles:** Kāmika, Kāmaja (Śrīkaṇṭhīya)
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md), [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md)
**Genre:** āgama (Śaiva Siddhānta mūlāgama)
**Attribution:** tradition: revealed by Sadāśiva from the upper (Īśāna) face; first received by Praṇava, then Trikala, then Hara (Kāmika pūrva 1.30–92; Kiraṇa vidyāpāda 10); scholarly: texts now transmitted under this title are mostly South Indian redactions centred on temple ritual; their relation to the early scripture of the same name is not established here; confidence: low
**Structure:** Śivabheda no. 1 in the Kāmika's order; 3 upabhedas in the Kāmika's list; Pūrvabhāga of 75 paṭalas in the Madras 1909 edition
**Availability:** digitized-original

No. 1 of the 28 Śaiva Siddhānta mūlāgamas (Śivabheda). According to the Kāmika it was first received by Praṇava, then Trikala, then Hara. The Kāmika assigns it 3 upabheda(s): Vaktra, Bhairavottara, Nārasiṃha. The surviving South Indian Kāmika is extant in a Pūrvabhāga (75 paṭalas: tantrāvatāra, mantras, bathing, worship, temple architecture, liṅga and image installation) and an Uttarabhāga (festivals, expiations, funeral and other rites).
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: original; name: Kāmikāgama, Pūrva- and Uttarabhāga, Grantha script, Madras 1909 (Muktabodha e-texts M00124, M00125)

### purva.1.12-13 <a id="tea-kamika-agama-purva-1-12-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Śiva tells the sages that this excellent śāstra, drawn out of the great tantra called Kāmika, was brought down on Meru by the transmitters beginning with Praṇava, and that it is supreme, endowed with ritual (kriyā) and conduct (caryā) and furnished with yoga and knowledge (jñāna).

### purva.1.14-17 <a id="tea-kamika-agama-purva-1-14-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

At the beginning knowledge became twofold according to the difference of those qualified (adhikārin), as higher and lower. The knowledge that reveals Śiva, called Śiva-knowledge (śivajñāna), is remembered as the higher; the lower knowledge, beginning with the Veda, makes known the bound soul and its bonds (paśu, pāśa). As the eyes of a man and of a cat differ at night, so this knowledge differs as higher and lower.

> आदावभूद्द्विधा ज्ञानमधिकारिविभेदतः परापरेण भेदेन पतिपश्वर्थदर्शकम् शिवप्रकाशकं ज्ञानं शिवज्ञानं परं स्मृतम् वेदाद्यमपरं ज्ञानं पशुपाशार्थदर्शकम्

### purva.1.17-19 <a id="tea-kamika-agama-purva-1-17-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This teaching is manifold: worldly (laukika), Vedic (vaidika), spiritual (ādhyātmika), the Atimārga, and that called Mantra. Sadyojāta, Vāma(deva), Aghora, (Tat)puruṣa and Īśāna — each of them five-faced — spoke the worldly and the rest; thus the division of streams is declared to be twenty-five.

### purva.1.20-27 <a id="tea-kamika-agama-purva-1-20-27"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Within these is contained all verbal lore; among them the Mantra-tantra issued from Sadāśiva's faces as Siddhānta, Gāruḍa, Vāma, Bhūta tantra and Bhairava (v. 21 names the upper, eastern, northern and southern faces in order; v. 26 assigns the Bhūta tantras to Sadyojāta). The knowledge from the upper Īśāna face, beginning with the Kāmika, was told in ten and eighteen divisions by Śiva- and Rudra-transmitters; the Gāruḍa came from the eastern face; the Vāma from the Vāma face, twenty-four in number beginning with the Nayasūtra; the Bhūta tantra, twenty in number beginning with 'Kaula', from Sadyojāta; and the Bhairava tantra, twofold, from the southern face, told on earth in many forms by Asitāṅga and the others. (Verses 22–23 on the number of transmitters and on revelation in the ages are not paraphrased here.)

### purva.1.29 <a id="tea-kamika-agama-purva-1-29"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### purva.1.30-92 <a id="tea-kamika-agama-purva-1-30-92"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The descent of the Siddhānta tantras is told: ten Śiva-divisions (Kāmika, Yogaja, Cintya, Kāraṇa, Ajita, Dīpta, Sūkṣma, Sahasra, Aṃśumān, Suprabheda) and eighteen Rudra-divisions (Vijaya, Niśvāsa, Svāyambhuva, Āgneya, Vīra, Raurava, Makuṭa, Vimala, Candrajñāna, Mukhabimba, Prodgīta, Lalita, Siddha, Santāna, Śarvokta, Pārameśvara, Kiraṇa, Vātula). For each it names the being to whom it was first taught (e.g. the Kāmika to Praṇava), its extent in verses (e.g. a parārdha for the Kāmika), the chain of later recipients (Praṇava → Trikala → Hara) and its sub-divisions (upabheda); the Suprabheda has none. It concludes: these are the root divisions of the twenty-eight tantras.

### purva.1.93-103 <a id="tea-kamika-agama-purva-1-93-103"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The twenty-eight tantras are the limbs of Sadāśiva's body made of tantras and mantra-forms: the Kāmika is his two feet, Yogaja the ankles, Cintya the toes, Kāraṇa the shanks, Ajita the knees, Dīpta the thighs, Sūkṣma the private part, Sahasra the hips, Aṃśumān the back, Suprabheda the navel, Vijaya the belly, Niśvāsa the heart, Svāyambhuva the two breasts, Anala the eyes, Vīra the throat, Raurava the ears, Makuṭa the crown, Vimala the arms, Candrajñāna the chest, Bimba the face, Prodgīta the tongue, Lalita the cheeks, Siddha the forehead, Santāna the ear-ornaments, Śarvokta the sacred thread, Pārameśa the necklace, Kiraṇa the jewel ornaments and Vātula the garment; the Śivadharma is named for the tripuṇḍra mark and unguent (vv. 100–101 are partly unclear in the printed text). With their sub-divisions the Sādākhya stands embodied. This Śiva- and Rudra-tantra shines like a wish-granting gem, one and many; though the speaker is one, it is manifold through the difference of hearers.

### purva.1.104-107 <a id="tea-kamika-agama-purva-1-104-107"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

From the ploughing of the site (karṣaṇa) to the installation (pratiṣṭhā) one should perform everything with the root tantra; if done with a sub-division, the performer and the patron perish. Worship alone is taught especially by the sub-divisions; installation and the rest are best done with the twenty-eight roots. Whatever tantra one began with, from ploughing to worship, everything is to be done by it, not from another tantra — unless something is not specifically stated there, in which case one may have it done by another tantra.

### purva.4.1-5 <a id="tea-kamika-agama-purva-4-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Worship (arcana), giving enjoyment and liberation, is twofold: for oneself (svārtha) and for others (parārtha). Worship for oneself is performed in the liṅga given by the guru at the end of initiation, or on a ground-altar (sthaṇḍila) and the like, and gives one's own desired fruit. Worship for others is performed in Śiva temples — in villages, towns, by rivers or on mountains, in the sixty-eight great holy places or elsewhere — with self-manifest, divine, bāṇa and sages' liṅgas or with liṅgas installed by men, for the increase of the king's life, health, victory and prosperity.

`skeleton` · confidence high

The five purifications are declared: first the purification of oneself, second of the place, third of the substances, fourth the purifying of the mantras, fifth of the liṅga.
