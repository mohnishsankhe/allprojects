## Teachings (138: skeleton 138)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Having bowed to him who destroyed darkness entirely and in every way and drew the world out of the mire of saṃsāra, the teacher of things as they are, I will expound the treatise, the Treasury of Abhidharma.

> yaḥ sarvathāsarvahatāndhakāraḥ saṃsārapaṅkājjagadujjahāra | tasmai namaskṛtya yathārthaśāstre śāstraṃ pravakṣyāmyabhidharmakośam

### 1.2 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Abhidharma in the true sense is stainless wisdom together with its retinue; in the conventional sense it is also the wisdom and the treatise that lead to obtaining it. Because the meaning of that Abhidharma is contained in this work, or because it is this work's support, the work is called the 'Treasury of Abhidharma'.

> prajñāmalā sānucarābhidharmaḥ tatprāptaye yāpi ca yacca śāstram | tasyārthato 'smin samanupraveśāt sa cā śrayo 'syetyabhidharmakośam

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission_

### 1.3 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Without the discernment of dharmas there is no means of calming the defilements, and because of the defilements the world wanders in the ocean of existence; therefore, it is said, the Teacher spoke the Abhidharma.

> dharmāṇāṃ pravicayamantareṇa nāsti kleśānāṃ yata upaśāntaye 'bhyupāyaḥ | kleśaiśca bhramati bhavārṇave 'tra lokastaddhetorata uditaḥ kilaiṣa śāstrā

### 1.4 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dharmas are either with outflows (sāsrava) or without outflows (anāsrava). Conditioned dharmas other than the path are with outflows, because the outflows adhere to and grow in them.

> sāsravānāsravā dharmāḥ saṃskṛtā mārgavarjitāḥ | sāsravāḥ āsravāsteṣu yasmātsamanuśerate

_level: ultimate · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 1.5-6 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-5-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Without outflows are the truth of the path and the three unconditioned dharmas: space, which does not obstruct; cessation through discernment (pratisaṃkhyā-nirodha), which is disjunction, obtained separately for each thing bound; and cessation not through discernment, the absolute obstruction of arising.

> anāsravā mārgasatyaṃ trividhaṃ cāpyasaṃskṛtam | ākāśaṃ dvau nirodhau ca tatrākāśamanāvṛtiḥ || pratisaṅkhyānirodho yo visaṃyogaḥ pṛthak pṛthak | utpādātyantavighno 'nyo nirodho 'pratisaṅkhyayā

### 1.7 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Conditioned dharmas are the five aggregates beginning with matter; they are also called the times (adhvan), the topics of discourse, 'having an exit' and 'having a basis'.

> te punaḥ saṃskṛtā dharmā rūpādiskandhapañcakam | sa evādhvā kathāvastu saniḥsārāḥ savastukāḥ

### 1.9 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Matter (rūpa) is the five sense faculties, the five sense objects and unmanifest matter (avijñapti); the eye and the other faculties are subtle, translucent matter that supports the corresponding consciousnesses.

> rūpaṃ pañcendriyāṇyarthāḥ pañcāvijñaptireva ca | tadvijñānāśrayā rūpaprasādāścakṣurādayaḥ

`skeleton` · confidence high

Unmanifest matter (avijñapti) is the good or bad continuum, derived from the great elements, that continues even in one whose mind is distracted or who is without mind.

### 1.12 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The great elements are the earth, water, fire and wind elements; they accomplish the functions of supporting, cohering, maturing and expanding, and their natures are solidity, moisture, heat and motion.

### 1.14 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The ten sense-bases and elements are the faculties and objects already named. Feeling (vedanā) is experience; perception (saṃjñā) is the grasping of characteristics.

> indriyārthāsta eveṣṭā daśāyatanadhātavaḥ | vedanānubhavaḥ sañjñā nimittodgrahaṇātmikā

### 1.16 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Consciousness (vijñāna) is the representation of each object; it is also the mind-base and seven elements (the six consciousnesses and mind).

> vijñānaṃ prativijñaptiḥ mana āyatanaṃ ca tat | dhātavaḥ sapta ca matāḥ ṣaḍ vijñānānyatho mahaḥ

### 1.17 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mind (manas) is the consciousness that has just passed away among the six; eighteen elements are taught in order to establish a support for the sixth (mental) consciousness.

> ṣaṇṇāmanantarātītaṃ vijñānaṃ yaddhi tanmanaḥ | ṣaṣṭhāśrayaprasiddhayarthaṃ dhatavo 'ṣṭādaśa smṛtāḥ

### 1.20 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-1-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Aggregate, sense-base and element mean heap, gate of arising, and lineage (gotra). The three teachings are given because beings differ in the kind of their delusion, in their faculties and in their inclination.

> rāśyāyadvāragotrārthāḥ skandhāyatanadhātavaḥ | mohendriyarūcitraidhāttistraḥ skandhādideśanāḥ

### 2.1-4 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-2-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Twenty-two faculties (indriya) are taught by their dominance: the six sense faculties over their objects; the female and male faculties over sex; the life faculty over the continuance of a class of beings; the five feelings over defilement; faith and the others over purification; and the three pure faculties ('I shall know the unknown', 'knowing', 'having known') over successive attainment up to nirvāṇa.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind, karma-liberation_

### 2.23 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-2-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mind and mental factors necessarily arise together, and with the characteristics of the conditioned and with possession; the mental factors are fivefold, divided into the universal (mahābhūmika) and the other classes.

> cittaṃ caittāḥ sahāvaśyaṃ sarva saṃskṛtalakṣaṇaiḥ | prāptyā vā pañcadhā caittā mahābhūmyādibhedataḥ

### 2.24 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-2-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Feeling, volition, perception, desire-to-act, contact, understanding, mindfulness, attention, resolve and concentration are present in every mind — the ten universal mental factors.

> vedanā cetanā sañjñā cchandaḥ sparśo matiḥ smṛtiḥ | manaskāro 'dhimokṣaśca samādhiḥ sarvacetasi

### 2.25 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-2-25"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Faith, heedfulness, tranquillity, equanimity, shame, dread of blame, the two roots (non-greed and non-hatred), non-harming and vigour are always present in a good mind — the ten universal good factors.

> śraddhāpramādaḥ praśrabdhirupekṣā hrīrapatrapā | mūladvayamahiṃsā ca vīryaṃ ca kuśale sadā

`skeleton` · confidence high

Delusion, heedlessness, laziness, lack of faith, torpor and restlessness are always present in a defiled mind; shamelessness and lack of dread of blame are always present in a bad mind.

> mohaḥ pramādaḥ kauśīdyamāśraddhayaṃ styānamuddhavaḥ | kliṣṭe sadaiva akuśale tvāhrīkyamanapatrapā

### 2.34 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-2-34"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Citta, manas and vijñāna have one meaning. Mind and mental factors have a support and an object, bear an aspect, and are associated in five ways.

### 2.35-36 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-2-35-36"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The formations dissociated from mind are possession and non-possession, homogeneity, the non-perceptual state, the two attainments (non-perception and cessation), the life faculty, the characteristics, and the collections of names and the rest. ### 2.41 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-2-41"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Homogeneity (sabhāgatā) is the sameness of beings. The non-perceptual state (āsaṃjñika) among the non-perceiving gods is a cessation of mind and mental factors, a result of ripening; those gods dwell in the Bṛhatphala heaven.

> sabhāgatā sattvasāmyaṃ āsañjñikamasañjñiṣu | nirodhaścittacaittānāṃ vipākaḥ te bṛhatphalāḥ

### 2.42-43 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-2-42-43"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The attainment of non-perception (asaṃjñi-samāpatti) is entered in the last dhyāna by one who wishes for escape; ordinary beings obtain it, not noble ones. The attainment called cessation (nirodha) is likewise a stopping of mind, entered at the peak of existence for the sake of dwelling in peace; it is obtained by noble ones through effort.

> tathāsañjñisamāpattiḥ dhyāne 'ntye niḥsṛtīcchayā | śubhā upapadyavedyaiva nāryasya ekādhvikāpyate || nirodhākhyā tathaiveyaṃ vihārārthaṃ bhavāgrajā | śubhā dvivedyāniyatā ca āryasya āpyā prayogataḥ

### 2.46 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosa-2-46"></a>
