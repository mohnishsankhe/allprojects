# Rāmāyaṇa (of Vālmīki)

**Alternate titles:** Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Ādikāvya
**Original title:** रामायण
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
**Genre:** kāvya / itihāsa
**Authors:** 
  - [Vālmīki](../teachers/valmiki.md) — role: author; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: Composed by the sage Vālmīki after Nārada told him Rāma's story; the first śloka arose from his grief at a hunter's killing of one of a pair of krauñca birds (1.2.15–18); sung by Rāma's sons Kuśa and Lava.; scholarly: An oral-bardic work fixed in writing over centuries; the first and seventh books are widely held to be later additions; transmitted in northern and southern recensions.; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: composed by Vālmīki in the Tretā yuga, in Rāma's own time; Scholarly account: c. 5th c. BCE – 3rd c. CE, in stages; books 2–6 generally held older than books 1 and 7; (confidence low)
**Structure:** 7 books (kāṇḍas). The poem itself speaks of 24,000 ślokas in 500 sargas, six books and the Uttara (1.4.2, vulgate). The local southern vulgate text has Bāla 77, Ayodhyā 119, Araṇya 75, Kiṣkindhā 67, Sundara 68, Yuddha 128, Uttara 111 sargas. The Baroda critical edition (1960–75) constitutes a shorter text.
**Availability:** digitized-original

The first poem (ādikāvya): the life of Rāma, prince of Ayodhyā — his exile in obedience to his father's word, the abduction of Sītā by Rāvaṇa, the alliance with the monkeys and Hanumān, the war in Laṅkā, his righteous reign, and (in the last book) the banishment of Sītā. It presents Rāma as dharma embodied: truth to one's word, obedience to parents, fraternal loyalty, protection of those who seek refuge, and the king's duty to his subjects.
