King Bhoja's concise vṛtti on the sūtras (not on the bhāṣya). It sets out the four samprajñāta samādhis as savitarka, savicāra, sānanda and sāsmita, calls those who rest content in bliss 'videhas' and those who rest in I-am-ness 'prakṛtilayas', and treats discernment of the supreme puruṣa as the grasper-coalescence (1.17); asamprajñāta is reached by rejecting every arising activity 'not this, not this' (1.18); devotion to Īśvara is offering all actions to the supreme teacher without desire for fruit (1.23, 2.1).

## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 1.17 <a id="tea-rajamartanda-1-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Samprajñāta samādhi is a special cultivation (bhāvanā), fourfold: savitarka, savicāra, sānanda, sāsmita. What is cultivated is Īśvara or the principles (tattvas). Cultivation on the gross elements and senses with word and meaning mixed is savitarka, without them nirvitarka; on the subtle (tanmātras, inner organ) with place, time and property savicāra, without them nirvicāra — these are 'coalescence with the grasped'. When the inner organ's sattva tinged with rajas and tamas is cultivated, bliss predominates: sānanda — those who rest content here, not seeing pradhāna and puruṣa, are the 'bodiless' (videha); this is 'coalescence with the grasping'. Cultivation on pure sattva, where mere being remains, is sāsmita — asmitā is not ahaṃkāra — and those content here, not seeing the supreme puruṣa, are 'merged in prakṛti'. Those who know the supreme puruṣa and cultivate it have discriminative discernment, 'coalescence with the grasper'. Each stage is left for the next.

### 1.18 <a id="tea-rajamartanda-1-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The cognition of cessation is the giving up of all thought of reasoning and the rest; its practice is rejecting whatever activity springs up, continuously, as 'not this, not this'; preceded by that, the other samādhi, with only impressions remaining, is asamprajñāta.

### 1.23 <a id="tea-rajamartanda-1-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Devotion (praṇidhāna) to Īśvara is a special bhakti, a distinguished worship: offering all actions to him, the supreme teacher, without desiring fruits such as sense-pleasure; it is the foremost means to samādhi and its fruit. Īśvara is the special puruṣa who, by his mere wish, is able to lift up the whole world.

### 2.1 <a id="tea-rajamartanda-2-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Having taught yoga with its means for the concentrated mind, Patañjali now teaches kriyā-yoga for the emergent mind: austerity is kṛcchra, cāndrāyaṇa and the like taught in other śāstras; self-study is repetition of mantras preceded by the praṇava; devotion to Īśvara is offering all actions to the supreme teacher without regard to fruit. The yogin should first be intent on kriyā-yoga.
