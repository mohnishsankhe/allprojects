# Vijñānabhairava

`src:vijnana-bhairava-tantra` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Vijñānabhairavabhaṭṭāraka, Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra
**Original title:** विज्ञानभैरव
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Trika ('the Triad')](../lineages/trika.md), [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
**Genre:** tantra (dialogue)
**Attribution:** tradition: Spoken by Bhairava to Bhairavī; claims to be the essence of the Rudrayāmala and of the Trika (vv. 1, 161-162).; scholarly: Anonymous. The claimed Rudrayāmala origin cannot be verified: the early Rudrayāmala is lost, and the extant Rudrayāmala (Uttaratantra) is a different and later text. Cited by Kṣemarāja, who began a commentary on it.; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Part of the revealed Rudrayāmala.; Scholarly account: c. 7th-9th c. CE (before Kṣemarāja, c. 1000; uncertain); (confidence low)
**Structure:** 163 verses in the numbering of KSTS 8 (with a verse 155b on the haṃsa-japa); 162 in the GRETIL e-text; the Kaumudī recension (KSTS 9) lacks vv. 45 (merged with 44), 84 and 114 and so has exactly 112 verses in its dhāraṇā section. Dhāraṇās: vv. 24-138.
**Availability:** digitized-original

A dialogue in which the Goddess (Bhairavī) asks Bhairava about his true nature; he rejects every form 'with parts' (the ninefold, the mass of sounds, the three-headed, nāda-bindu...) as a device for the confused (vv. 7-13), describes the supreme state as indescribable fullness identical with the Goddess (14-21), and in answer to her request for means (22-23) teaches 112 contemplations (dhāraṇā) of the 'waveless state' (24-138; counted at 139). It then redefines japa, meditation, worship, oblation, sacrifice and bathing as inner acts (144-153), teaches the natural 'haṃsa' repetition of the breath (154-156), enjoins secrecy (157-160), and closes with the Goddess embracing Śiva, having grasped 'the essence of the Rudrayāmala' (161-163).

## Teachings (186: text-verified 167, skeleton 19)

### 1 <a id="tea-vijnana-bhairava-tantra-1"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

The Goddess says: 'O god (deva), I have heard everything that came forth from the Rudrayāmala: the Trika in all its divisions, entire, essence by essence, with the essences distinguished (sārāt sāravibhāgaśaḥ).'

> śrī devy uvāca / śrutaṃ deva mayā sarvaṃ rudrayāmalasambhavam / trikabhedam aśeṣeṇa sārāt sāravibhāgaśaḥ

_level: unmarked · standpoint: divine · path: general · stage: all · types: narrative, teacher-transmission_

### 2 <a id="tea-vijnana-bhairava-tantra-2"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

She says that even now her doubt has not gone, and asks the Supreme Lord what his form is in truth (tattvataḥ): is it made of the parts (kalā) of the mass of sounds (śabdarāśi)?

> adyāpi na nivṛtto me saṃśayaḥ parameśvara / kiṃ rūpaṃ tattvato deva śabdarāśikalāmayam

_level: unmarked · standpoint: divine · path: general · stage: all · types: ultimate_

terms: [śabdarāśi](../terms/sabdarasi-trika.md)

### 2-6 <a id="tea-vijnana-bhairava-tantra-2-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Her doubt remains: what is Bhairava's nature in truth - made of the kalās of the mass of sounds, or the ninefold, or the three-headed, or of three powers, or of nāda and bindu, or the half-moon and nirodhikā, or the vowelless mounted on the wheel, or power itself? And if the supreme had parts it would contradict its supremacy, which cannot come from distinctions of letters or bodies but only from being partless.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: ultimate_

### 24 <a id="tea-vijnana-bhairava-tantra-24"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

### sec-1-23 <a id="tea-vijnana-bhairava-tantra-sec-1-23"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Frame dialogue. The Goddess says she has heard the whole Trika from the Rudrayāmala and still doubts what Bhairava's form is in truth: the mass of sounds, the ninefold, the three-headed, the three powers, nāda-bindu, and so on; she argues that supremacy cannot belong to what has parts (1-6). Bhairava answers that the forms with parts are unsubstantial, given for meditation to the confused and as an inducement to engage (7-13); the supreme state is unspeakable, free of direction, time and place, blissful, thought-free and full, and is the Goddess Parā, not different from power as fire is not from its burning (14-21). The Goddess asks by what means it is entered (22-23).

### sec-139-162 <a id="tea-vijnana-bhairava-tantra-sec-139-162"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Closing. The means are summed as a hundred and twelve teachings of the waveless state, knowing which one becomes a knower of knowledge; established in any one, one becomes Bhairava, released while living (139-142). Asked who is repeated, meditated on, worshipped and satisfied, Bhairava redefines japa, meditation, worship, oblation, sacrifice and bathing as inner acts (143-153), describes the Goddess's natural movement as breath (154-156), forbids revealing the teaching except to worthy recipients (157-160), and the Goddess, satisfied that she has ascertained the essence of the Rudrayāmala, embraces Śiva (161-162).

### thesis <a id="tea-vijnana-bhairava-tantra-thesis"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Thesis, decided by the six marks. The text presents Bhairava's supreme state, which is also the Goddess Parā, as unspeakable fullness, free of direction, time and place and not any of the forms with parts (vv.7-21), and offers, as the means of entering it, a run of short contemplations of the waveless state (24-138), each turning the mind to something in ordinary experience (a breath, a void, a sound's end, the middle between two states, joy, the thought that all is consciousness) or away from the mind's supports; established in any one of them one becomes Bhairava (140), and the outer acts of worship are to be understood as these inner acts (144-153). Marks. Opening and closing (upakrama/upasaṃhāra): opens with the Goddess's question about Bhairava's form (1-6) and closes with her statement that the essence of the Rudrayāmala has been ascertained (161-162). Repetition (abhyāsa): the refrains of the thought-free (nirvikalpa), the supportless (nirādhāra), the void (śūnya), 'made of it' (tanmaya), endings on 'Bhairava's form' (84, 112, 138) and 'wherever the mind goes' (116, 129). Novelty (apūrvatā): 'the essence of tantra, most secret' (7-8, 157). Stated result (phala): 140-142 and 148, and a result at the end of most verses. Praise (arthavāda): 141, 156 ('easy'), 157-160. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti): 5-6 (supremacy is contradicted by having parts), 18-19 (power and fire), 110 (waves and water), 134-137, 152-153.
