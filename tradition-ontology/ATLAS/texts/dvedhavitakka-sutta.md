## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 2-11 <a id="tea-dvedhavitakka-sutta-2-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Before his awakening the bodhisatta divided his thoughts into two classes: thoughts of sensuality, ill will and cruelty lead to affliction of self and others and away from nibbāna, and he abandoned them; thoughts of renunciation, non-ill-will and non-cruelty lead to no affliction. Whatever a monk frequently thinks and ponders becomes the inclination of his mind — like a cowherd who must guard his cattle closely when the crops are ripe.

### 6.1 <a id="tea-dvedhavitakka-sutta-6-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Whatever a mendicant frequently thinks about and ponders, that becomes the inclination of the mind.

> Yaññadeva, bhikkhave, bhikkhu bahulamanuvitakketi anuvicāreti, tathā tathā nati hoti cetaso.
