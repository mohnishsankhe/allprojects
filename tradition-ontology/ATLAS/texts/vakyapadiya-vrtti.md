The prose commentary on books 1 and 2 of the Vākyapadīya, which explains the three levels of speech with verses defining vaikharī, madhyamā and paśyantī, the yoga of the word (śabdapūrva-yoga) and grammar's place among the sciences.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1.14 <a id="tea-vakyapadiya-vrtti-1-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Vṛtti teaches a 'yoga preceded by the word' (śabdapūrva-yoga): by the correct use of purified words one gains merit, by knowledge of their nature one reaches the Word-principle, and union with it is union with Brahman.

### 1.142 <a id="tea-vakyapadiya-vrtti-1-142"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Vṛtti defines the three levels: vaikharī is speech whose letters are grasped by the hearer, articulated when breath strikes the places of articulation; madhyamā has the intellect alone as its material, follows sequence and goes beyond the working of breath; paśyantī is undivided, with all sequence withdrawn, the inner light of its own form, subtle and imperishable.
