Madhusūdana Sarasvatī's great dialectical defence of Advaita (four chapters) against Vyāsatīrtha's Nyāyāmṛta, answering it point by point in Navya-Nyāya language: the falsity (mithyātva) of the world under five definitions, the inference 'the world is false because it is seen, insentient and limited', indescribable ignorance, the partless meaning of the mahāvākya, the means and liberation.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Laghucandrikā (Gauḍabrahmānandī)](laghucandrika.md)

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1 <a id="tea-advaitasiddhi-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The world is false (mithyā) because it is seen, insentient and limited, like the silver seen in shell; falsity is defended under five definitions: not being the locus of either existence or non-existence (Pañcapādikā); being the counterpositive of the absence in all three times in the very locus of appearance (Vivaraṇa); being removable by knowledge (Vivaraṇa); being the counterpositive of an absolute absence in its own locus (Citsukha); being other than the real (Ānandabodha).

### 1 <a id="tea-advaitasiddhi-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ignorance is positive (bhāva-rūpa) and indescribable; it is established by the perception 'I am ignorant', by inference and by presumption, against the Dvaita objection that ignorance is only the absence of knowledge.

### 3 <a id="tea-advaitasiddhi-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The mahāvākya conveys a partless meaning (akhaṇḍārtha): it does not predicate a relation between two things but indicates the one self through implication.
