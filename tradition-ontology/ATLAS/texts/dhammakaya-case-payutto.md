P. A. Payutto's critique of the Dhammakāya movement's teaching that nibbāna is a self (attā), arguing from the canon and commentaries that nibbāna is not-self.

## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### thesis <a id="tea-dhammakaya-case-payutto-thesis"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _recent (post-1800)_

The canon and commentaries teach that all dhammas, conditioned and unconditioned, are not-self; nibbāna is therefore not a self (attā), and the teaching that it is a true self departs from the Theravāda.
