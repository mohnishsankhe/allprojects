Om is all this; this self is brahman and has four quarters: waking (vaiśvānara), dream (taijasa), deep sleep (prajna) and the fourth, which is neither inward- nor outward-knowing, unseen, ungraspable, the cessation of the world, peaceful, auspicious, non-dual; the letters A, U, M correspond to the three states and the measureless (amatra) to the fourth.

### 1 <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-1"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Om (oṃ) — this syllable (akṣara) is all this. Its explanation (upavyākhyāna) follows: what was, what is and what will be — all is just Oṃ; and whatever else lies beyond the three times is also just Oṃ.

> oṃ ity etad akṣaram idaṃ sarvaṃ tasyopavyākhyānaṃ bhūtaṃ bhavad bhaviṣyad iti sarvam oṅkāra eva | yac cānyat trikālātītaṃ tad apy oṅkāra eva

### 3 <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-3"></a>

### 5 <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-5"></a>

### 7 <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-7"></a>

### 8-11 <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-8-11"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

This self, with respect to the syllable, is Om, with respect to its measures: the quarters are the measures and the measures the quarters — A, U and M. Waking, the one common to all men, is A, from 'obtaining' (āpti) or 'being first'; dream, the brilliant one, is U, from 'elevation' or 'being between both'; deep sleep, the intelligent one, is M, from 'measuring' or 'merging'. One who knows this obtains his desires, raises the continuity of knowledge, measures all this and merges into it.

### thesis <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-thesis"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Thesis of the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, decided from the six marks of purport. The text identifies Oṃ, all time, brahman and the self (MU 1, 2, 8, 12) and teaches that this self has four quarters: waking (vaiśvānara), dream (taijasa) and deep sleep (prājña), which are matched to the measures a, u, m, and a fourth (caturtha) that is without measure and known only by negation and by the words "stilling of the world, auspicious, non-dual" (MU 7, 12). Opening and closing (upakrama-upasaṃhāra): the text begins with "Oṃ is all this" (MU 1) and ends with "Oṃ is just the self; one who knows thus enters the self by the self" (MU 12). Repetition (abhyāsa): the quarters/states and the phrase "ya evaṃ veda" are repeated for each measure (MU 9–11), and prapañcopaśama-śiva-advaita recurs at MU 7 and 12. Novelty (apūrvatā): the four-quarter scheme of the self, the fourth as the unspoken beyond the three states, and the quarter-measure correspondence are taught here without a stated earlier source in the text itself. Stated result (phala): obtaining all desires and becoming first (MU 9), raising the continuity of knowledge (MU 10), measuring all and becoming its merging (MU 11), and entering the self by the self (MU 12). Praise (arthavāda): the fruit-promises of MU 9–11 are in the form of praise of the knower; their weight is left to the interpretation layer. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti): the passages give brief grounds only for the a-u-m assignments (obtaining/being first; exaltation/both-ness; measuring/merging, MU 9–11); the four quarters are otherwise stated, not argued. Main teaching: the self is one and non-dual; the three states are its quarters and the fourth is its own nature; Oṃ, with its three measures and the measureless, is identified with this self and its quarters (MU 1, 8, 12).
