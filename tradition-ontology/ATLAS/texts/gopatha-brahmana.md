The only surviving Brāhmaṇa of the Atharvaveda. It opens with Brahman, the self-existent, alone in the beginning, who creates a second god from himself (1.1.1), tells of the origin of Atharvan and the Aṅgirases and of the Atharvaveda as the Veda of the brahman priest, discusses Oṃ at length, gives rules for the Vedic student, and in its later part explains Śrauta rites, often in words shared with other Brāhmaṇas.

## Teachings (2: sourced 2)

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-gopatha-brahmana-1-1-1"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

In the beginning Brahman, the self-existent, was alone. It considered: 'I am a great wonder (yakṣa), being only one; let me fashion from myself a second god of my own measure.' It toiled and heated itself with austerity, and from it the waters and the rest of creation came forth.

### 1.1.16-30 <a id="tea-gopatha-brahmana-1-1-16-30"></a>
`sourced` · confidence low

An extended exposition of Oṃ (praṇava): its origin, its parts, its seer, metre and deity, and its use at the beginning of every Vedic recitation.
