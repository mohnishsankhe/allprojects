A prose primer of Advaita definitions: the four qualifications and six virtues, the three bodies, five sheaths, three states, the twenty-four principles, the creation of the elements, jīva and Īśvara, 'tat tvam asi', the jīvanmukta, and the three kinds of karma and their fate after knowledge.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### karma <a id="tea-tattvabodha-karma"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Karma is threefold: accumulated (sañcita), to come (āgāmi) and begun (prārabdha). Knowledge burns the accumulated; the to-come does not cling to the knower, as water does not cling to a lotus leaf; the begun is exhausted by experience. The knower's future merit goes to those who serve and praise him, his demerit to those who revile him.

### sadhana-catustaya <a id="tea-tattvabodha-sadhana-catustaya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The six virtues defined: śama is control of the mind; dama control of the outer senses; uparati is the observance of one's own dharma; titikṣā is endurance of heat and cold, pleasure and pain; śraddhā is trust in the words of guru and Vedānta; samādhāna is one-pointedness of mind.

### sarira-traya <a id="tea-tattvabodha-sarira-traya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The gross body is made of the quintuplicated elements and is the seat of experience; the subtle body of seventeen members (five organs of knowledge, five of action, five prāṇas, mind and intellect) made of the unquintuplicated elements, is the instrument of experience; the causal body is indescribable, beginningless ignorance.
