# Tipiṭaka (the Pali Canon)

`src:pali-tipitaka` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Pāli Canon, Tepiṭaka, Pāḷi
**Language:** Pali
**Family:** ascetic
**Lineages:** [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md), [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md)
**Genre:** canon
**Dates:** Tradition's account: rehearsed at the First Council at Rājagaha in the first rains after the parinibbāna (543 BCE by the Theravāda reckoning); written down on palm leaves at the Āloka-vihāra (Aluvihāra), Sri Lanka, under King Vaṭṭagāmaṇī Abhaya; Scholarly account: oral composition and redaction c. 5th–1st c. BCE; first written c. 29–17 BCE (Vaṭṭagāmaṇī); later KN books and the Abhidhamma the latest strata; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** Vinaya Piṭaka (Suttavibhaṅga, Khandhaka, Parivāra); Sutta Piṭaka (Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, Aṅguttara and Khuddaka Nikāyas); Abhidhamma Piṭaka (seven books: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, Vibhaṅga, Dhātukathā, Puggalapaññatti, Kathāvatthu, Yamaka, Paṭṭhāna)
**Availability:** digitized-original

The three 'baskets' of the Theravāda canon — Vinaya (monastic discipline), Sutta (discourses) and Abhidhamma (analysis of phenomena) — held to be the word of the Buddha as rehearsed at the First Council and transmitted orally until written down in Sri Lanka.
