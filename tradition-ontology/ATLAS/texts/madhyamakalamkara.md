## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 1 <a id="tea-madhyamakalamkara-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

These entities that we and others hold to be real lack own-nature in reality, because they have neither a single nor a manifold nature — like a reflection.

### 16-17 <a id="tea-madhyamakalamkara-16-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Consciousness arises as the opposite of what is inert; its not being inert is its self-awareness; this self-knowing is not the action of an agent on an object, for a single partless consciousness cannot be threefold.

### 64 <a id="tea-madhyamakalamkara-64"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

### 92-93 <a id="tea-madhyamakalamkara-92-93"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Relying on mind-only one should know that external things do not exist; relying on this (Madhyamaka) way one should know that even that (mind) is utterly without self. Those who ride the chariot of the two systems, holding the reins of reasoning, attain the rank of true Mahāyānists.
