# Sāṃkhya Kārikā

`src:samkhya-karika` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Sāṃkhyasaptati, Sāṅkhyakārikā, Āryāsaptati
**Original title:** सांख्यकारिका
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Sāṃkhya](../lineages/samkhya.md)
**Genre:** kārikā (āryā verses)
**Authors:** 
  - [Īśvarakṛṣṇa](../teachers/isvarakrsna.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: Composed by Īśvarakṛṣṇa, who summarized in āryā verses the doctrine handed down from the supreme sage (Kapila) through Āsuri and Pañcaśikha by a succession of pupils (SK 70–71); its topics are those of the whole Ṣaṣṭitantra (SK 72).; scholarly: Authorship accepted. Verses 70–72 are not commented on by Gauḍapāda; Paramārtha's Chinese version lacks v. 63 and its commentary remarks that v. 72 is not original; a 73rd verse is found only in the Māṭhara Vṛtti and in one anonymous vṛtti (V1).; confidence: high
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 350–450 CE (before Paramārtha's Chinese translation, c. 557–569 CE); (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 72 āryā verses in the vulgate (69 doctrinal verses and 3 on the transmission); some versions add a 73rd; 'saptati' (seventy) is a round name
**Availability:** digitized-original

The classical root text of Sāṃkhya. Starting from the threefold suffering and the inadequacy of seen and Vedic remedies, it sets out the three means of knowledge, the pre-existence of the effect, the manifest and the unmanifest, the three guṇas, the proofs of prakṛti and of many puruṣas, the emergence of the twenty-five principles, the thirteen instruments and the subtle body, the fifty-fold creation of cognitions, and liberation (kaivalya) through the knowledge 'I am not, not mine, not I'.

## Teachings (73: skeleton 73)

### 1 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Because one is struck by the three kinds of suffering, there arises the desire to know the means of striking them down. If it is objected that the inquiry is pointless because visible means exist, (the answer is) no, because those means do not remove suffering with certainty or finally.

> duḥkhatrayābhighātāj jijñāsā tadabhighātake hetau / dṛṣṭe sāpārthā cen naikāntātyantato 'bhāvāt

### 2 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The means known from Vedic tradition (ānuśravika) are like the visible means, for they are bound up with impurity, decay and gradation. The opposite means is better: the discriminating knowledge of the manifest, the unmanifest and the knower.

> dṛṣṭavad ānuśravikaḥ sa hy aviśuddhikṣayātiśayayuktaḥ / tadviparītaḥ śreyān vyaktāvyaktajñavijñānāt

### 3 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Root-nature (mūlaprakṛti) is not a modification; the seven beginning with the great one (mahat) are both producers and modifications; the group of sixteen is modification only; puruṣa is neither producer nor modification.

> mūlaprakṛtir avikṛtir mahadādyāḥ prakṛtivikṛtayaḥ sapta / ṣoḍaśakas tu vikāro na prakṛtir na vikṛtiḥ puruṣaḥ

### 4 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Three means of valid knowledge are accepted — perception, inference and reliable testimony — since every other means is established within these; for what is to be known is established through a means of knowledge.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 5 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Perception is the determination (adhyavasāya) concerning each (sense-)object; inference is declared to be threefold and preceded by (knowledge of) the mark and what bears the mark; reliable testimony is reliable revelation (āptaśruti).

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 6 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Things beyond the senses are known through inference from general correlation (sāmānyato dṛṣṭa); what is imperceptible and not established even by that is established by reliable scripture.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 7 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Existing things are not perceived) because of excessive distance, excessive nearness, impairment of the senses, inattention of the mind, subtlety, obstruction, being overpowered, and mixture with what is similar.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 8 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Its (prakṛti's) non-perception is due to subtlety, not to non-existence; it is apprehended through its effects. That effect is the great one (mahat) and the rest, unlike prakṛti and also like it.

### 9 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The effect exists (in its cause before production): because the non-existent cannot be produced; because a definite material cause is taken up; because not everything arises from everything; because a capable cause produces only what it is capable of producing; and because the effect has the nature of its cause.

> asadakaraṇād upādānagrahaṇāt sarvasaṃbhavābhāvāt / śaktasya śakyakaraṇāt kāraṇabhāvāc ca satkāryam

### 10 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The manifest has a cause, is impermanent, non-pervasive, active, many, dependent (on its cause), a mark (that merges back), composed of parts and subordinate; the unmanifest is the opposite.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: ultimate, world-fate_

### 11 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The manifest consists of the three guṇas, is non-discriminating, an object, common, unconscious and productive; so too is pradhāna. Puruṣa is the opposite of these, and also (in other respects) similar.

> triguṇam aviveki viṣayaḥ sāmānyam acetanaṃ prasavadharmi / vyaktaṃ tathā pradhānaṃ tadviparītas tathā ca pumān

_level: ultimate · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: ultimate, consciousness-mind_

### 12 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The guṇas have the nature of pleasure, pain and delusion; they serve to illuminate, to activate and to restrain; and they function by dominating, supporting and producing one another and by being paired together.

> prītyaprītiviṣādātmakāḥ prakāśapravṛttiniyamārthāḥ / anyonyābhibhavāśrayajananamithunavṛttayaś ca guṇāḥ

### 13 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sattva is held to be light and illuminating; rajas stimulating and mobile; tamas heavy and enveloping. Their functioning (together) serves a purpose, like a lamp.

> sattvaṃ laghu prakāśakam iṣṭam upaṣṭambhakaṃ calaṃ ca rajaḥ / guru varaṇakam eva tamaḥ pradīpavac cārthato vṛttiḥ

### 14 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Non-discrimination and the other properties (of the manifest) are established because it consists of the three guṇas and their opposite is absent (in it); the unmanifest too is established, because an effect has the nature of the guṇas of its cause.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: causal · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: world-fate, ultimate_

### 15 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(The unmanifest exists as cause) because particular things are limited in measure; because of their homogeneity; because activity proceeds from a potency; because cause and effect are distinguished; and because the whole manifold world merges back (into non-distinction).

_level: unmarked · standpoint: causal · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: world-fate, ultimate_

### 16 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The unmanifest exists as cause. It operates through the three guṇas, by their combination and by transformation, like water, (becoming different) according to the differences of the abodes of the respective guṇas.

### 17 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Puruṣa exists: because aggregates are for the sake of another; because there must be something opposite to what has the three guṇas and the rest; because there must be a controller; because there is an enjoyer; and because activity is for the sake of isolation.

> saṃghātaparārthatvāt triguṇādiviparyayād adhiṣṭhānāt / puruṣo 'sti bhoktṛbhāvāt kaivalyārthaṃ pravṛtteś ca
