The Sūtra of the Ten Grounds, spoken by the bodhisattva Vajragarbha: the ten stages (bhūmi) of the bodhisattva from Joyful (pramuditā) to Cloud of Dharma (dharmameghā), each with its perfection, purifications and attainments; in the sixth ground the three realms are 'mind only' and the twelve links depend on mind; at the eighth, Immovable, the bodhisattva attains the acceptance of non-arising and is roused by the buddhas not to enter peace.

## Teachings (10: skeleton 10)

### 1 <a id="tea-dasabhumika-sutra-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pramuditā (Joyful): The bodhisattva who has produced the thought of awakening enters the first ground, is joyful at being born into the Tathāgata's family, is freed from five fears (of livelihood, disrepute, death, bad destinies, assemblies) and makes ten great vows; the perfection of giving predominates.

### 6 <a id="tea-dasabhumika-sutra-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Abhimukhī (Facing): examining dependent origination in all its forms, the bodhisattva realizes: 'this triple world is mind only; even the twelve links of existence taught by the Tathāgata all rest on mind'; the three doors of liberation appear before him; the perfection of wisdom predominates; he has keen conforming patience but not yet the acceptance of non-arising.

> cittamātramidaṃ yadidaṃ traidhātukam

Acalā (Immovable): the bodhisattva attains the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas and effortless, non-conceptual knowledge, as one waking from a dream of crossing a river drops all effort; about to rest in that peace, he is roused by the buddhas to remember his vows and beings, and so continues; the perfection of vow predominates; he is never apart from seeing buddhas.
