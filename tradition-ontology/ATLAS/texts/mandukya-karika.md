# Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (Gauḍapāda Kārikā, Āgamaśāstra)

`src:mandukya-karika` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Gauḍapādīya Kārikā, Āgamaśāstra
**Original title:** माण्डूक्यकारिका
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
**Genre:** kārikā
**Commentary on:** [Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad](mandukya-upanisad.md)
**Authors:** 
  - [Gauḍapāda](../teachers/gaudapada.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: Gauḍapāda, teacher of Śaṅkara's teacher Govinda; Madhva's school regards the verses of the first prakaraṇa as part of the Upaniṣad (śruti); scholarly: Gauḍapāda (c. 6th c.); unity of the four prakaraṇas debated, the fourth sometimes thought an independent work; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 6th c. CE; (confidence low)
**Structure:** 4 prakaraṇas: 29 + 38 + 48 + 100 = 215 kārikās
**Availability:** digitized-original

Gauḍapāda's 215 verses in four prakaraṇas on the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad: Āgama (the three states, their three experiencers and the fourth; Oṃ), Vaitathya (the unreality of the waking world, like dream), Advaita (non-duality; the pot-space illustration; asparśa-yoga and the discipline of the mind), and Alātaśānti (the quenching of the firebrand: non-origination, ajātivāda, argued dialectically).
**Commentaries on this text:** [Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-Gauḍapādīyakārikā-bhāṣya (ascribed to Śaṅkara)](mandukya-karika-bhasya-sankara.md)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-1"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

The all-pervading (vibhu) viśva is outward-knowing (bahiḥprajña); taijasa is inward-knowing (antaḥprajña); prājña is a mass of cognition (ghanaprajña); thus the one alone is held (smṛta) to be threefold.

> bahiḥprajño vibhuḥ viśvo hy antaḥprajñas tu taijasaḥ / ghanaprajñaḥ tathā prājña eka eva tridhā smṛtaḥ

_level: ultimate · standpoint: experiential · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 1.2 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-2"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Viśva is in the right eye (dakṣiṇākṣimukha); taijasa is within, in the mind (manas); prājña is in the space (ākāśa) in the heart (hṛd); thus the threefold is established in the body (dehe vyavasthita).

> dakṣiṇākṣimukhe viśvo manasy antas tu taijasaḥ / ākāśe ca hṛdi prājñas tridhā dehe vyavasthitaḥ

### 1.3 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-3"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Viśva is always the enjoyer of the gross (sthūlabhuj); taijasa the enjoyer of the separated (praviviktabhuj); likewise prājña the enjoyer of bliss (ānandabhuj); know the threefold enjoyment (bhoga).

> viśvaḥ hi sthūlabhuṅ nityaṃ taijasaḥ praviviktabhuk / ānandabhuk tathā prājñastridhā bhogaṃ nibodhata

The gross satisfies (tarpayate) viśva; the separated (pravivikta) satisfies taijasa; and bliss, likewise, satisfies prājña; know the threefold satisfaction (tṛpti).

> sthūlaṃ tarpayate viśvaṃ praviviktaṃ tu taijasam / ānandaś ca tathā prājñaṃ tridhā tṛptiṃ nibodhata

### 1.5 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-5"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

In the three stations (dhāma) what is the object of enjoyment (bhojya) and who is the enjoyer (bhoktṛ) are declared; one who knows both of these, though he enjoys, is not tainted (na lipyate).

> triṣu dhāmasu yadbhojyaṃ bhoktā yaś ca prakīrtitaḥ / vadaitadubhayaṃ yas tu sa bhuñjāno na lipyate

### 1.6 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-6"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence low

The settled view (viniścaya) about the origin (prabhava) of all existing entities (sarvabhāvānāṃ satām) is: prāṇa produces everything; the puruṣa produces separately the rays (aṃśu) of consciousness (cetas).

> prabhavaḥ sarvabhāvānāṃ satām iti viniścayaḥ / sarvaṃ janayati prāṇaś ceto 'ṃśūn puruṣaḥ pṛthak

_level: conventional · standpoint: cosmic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: world-fate_

### 1.7 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-7"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Other creation-thinkers (sṛṣṭi-cintaka) hold creation to be an expansion (vibhūti) or a production (prasava); others imagine (vikalpita) creation to be of the nature of dream and illusion (svapna-māyā).

> vibhūtiṃ prasavaṃ tv anye manyante sṛṣṭicintakāḥ / svapnamāyāsarūpeti sṛṣṭir anyaiḥ vikalpitā

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: world-fate_

terms: [vikalpa](../terms/vikalpa.md), [māyā](../terms/maya.md)

### 1.8 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-8"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Others are convinced that creation is the mere will (icchā-mātra) of the Lord (prabhu); the time-thinkers (kāla-cintaka) hold that beings are born from time (kāla).

> icchāmātraṃ prabhoḥ sṛṣṭir iti sṛṣṭau viniścitāḥ / kālāt prasūtiṃ bhūtānāṃ manyante kālacintakāḥ

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: world-fate_

### 1.9 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-9"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Others say creation is for enjoyment (bhoga), yet others say it is for sport (krīḍā). This is the very nature (svabhāva) of the shining one (deva) whose desires are fulfilled (āptakāma): what longing (spṛhā) could there be?

> bhogārthaṃ sṛṣṭir iti anye krīḍārtham iti cāpare / devasyaiṣa svabhāvo 'yam āptakāmasya kā spṛhā

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: world-fate_

### 1.10 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-10"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

The fourth (turya), the shining one (deva), is held to be the ruler (īśāna) of the ending of all sorrows, the powerful (prabhu), imperishable (avyaya), non-dual (advaita) among all entities, and all-pervading (vibhu).

> nivṛtteḥ sarvaduḥkhānām īśānaḥ prabhur avyayaḥ / advaitaḥ sarvabhāvānāṃ devās turyo vibhuḥ smṛtaḥ

### 1.11 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-11"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Viśva and taijasa are held to be bound by effect and cause (kārya-kāraṇa-baddha); prājña is bound by cause alone; these two (bonds) are not established (na sidhyataḥ) in the fourth.

> kāryakāraṇabaddhau tāv iṣyete viśvataijasau / prājñaḥ kāraṇabaddhas tu dvau tau turye na sidhyataḥ

_level: ultimate · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind, karma-liberation_

### 1.12 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-12"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Prājña knows nothing — neither self nor others, neither truth nor untruth; that fourth (turya) is always all-seeing (sarvadṛk).

> nātmānaṃ na parāś caiva na satyaṃ na api cānṛtam / prājñaḥ kiñcana saṃvetti turyaṃ tat sarvadṛk sadā

### 1.13 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-13"></a>

The non-apprehension of duality (dvaitasya agrahaṇa) is the same in both prājña and the fourth; but prājña is joined with sleep that is a seed (bīja-nidrā), and that does not exist in the fourth.

> dvaitasyāgrahaṇaṃ tulyam ubhayoḥ prājñaturyayoḥ / bījanidrāyutaḥ prājñaḥ sā ca turye na vidyate

### 1.14 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-14"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

The first two (viśva, taijasa) are joined with dream-sleep (svapna-nidrā); prājña is joined with sleep that has no dream (asvapna-nidrā); in the fourth, those who are certain (niścita) see neither sleep nor dream.

> svapnanidrāyutāv ādyau prājñas tv asvapnanidrayā / na nidrāṃ naiva ca svapnaṃ turye paśyanti niścitaḥ

### 1.15 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-15"></a>

Dream belongs to one who grasps otherwise (anyathā gṛhṇataḥ); sleep to one who does not know the truth (tattva); when the two errors (viparyāsa) are exhausted, one reaches the state of the fourth (turīya pada).

> anyathā gṛhṇataḥ svapno nidrā tattvam ajānataḥ / viparyāse tayoḥ kṣīṇe turīyaṃ padam aśnute

> anādimāyayā supto yadā jīvaḥ prabudhyate / ajam anidram asvapnam advaitaṃ budhyate tadā

### 1.26 <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-1-26"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

The praṇava is the lower (apara) brahman and the praṇava is remembered as the higher (para) as well; the praṇava is without a prior (apūrva), without an interior (anantara), without an exterior (abāhya), without an after (anapara), and imperishable (avyaya).

> praṇavo hy aparaṃ brahma praṇavaś ca paraḥ smṛtaḥ / apūrvo 'nantaro 'bāhyo 'naparaḥ praṇavo 'vyayaḥ

### thesis <a id="tea-mandukya-karika-thesis"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Thesis of the Māṇḍūkya Kārikā (Gauḍapāda), decided from the six marks of purport. The text teaches that nothing is born (ajāti): all dharmas and all jīvas are unborn, and duality is the appearing of mind through māyā, as in dream; the one self is threefold (viśva, taijasa, prājña) and its fourth (turya) is non-dual, unborn, sleepless and dreamless (1.1, 1.10–1.16, 2.32, 3.48, 4.71, 4.81). Opening and closing (upakrama-upasaṃhāra): it opens by restating the Upaniṣad's three and the fourth (1.1) and closes with a salutation to the unborn state, hard to see, very deep, of sameness, without multiplicity (4.100), after salutations at 4.1–4.2. Repetition (abhyāsa): non-birth is declared in each prakaraṇa (1.16–1.17, 2.32, 3.2, 3.19–3.28, 3.48, 4.4, 4.22, 4.71); the dream-waking argument returns (2.1–2.10 and 4.31–4.41, 4.61–4.67); and several verses recur nearly word for word (2.6 = 4.31; 2.7 = 4.32; 3.20–3.22 = 4.6–4.8; 3.29–3.30 = 4.61–4.62; 3.48 = 4.71). Novelty (apūrvatā): the text argues non-origination by reasoning in the manner of a treatise (4.11–4.23), gives the images of pot-space (3.3–3.9), the rope (2.17–2.18) and the firebrand (4.47–4.52), names asparśa-yoga (3.39, 4.2) and gives a counsel for the mind in restraint (3.40–3.46). Stated result (phala): fearlessness, ending of sorrow, awakening and imperishable peace depend on restraint of the mind (3.40); one who knows Oṃ is a muni (1.29) and does not grieve (1.28); the state free of sorrow, desire and fear (4.78); fitness for immortality (4.92); omniscience (4.85, 4.89). Praise (arthavāda): the honouring of the knower of the common feature of the three stations (1.22) and of those ascertained in the unborn sameness (4.95) are in the form of praise. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti): the unreality of dream and waking from dream itself (2.1–2.10), the immortal cannot become mortal (3.19–3.22, 4.6–4.8), the denial of birth from itself or another and as existent, non-existent or both (4.22) and the critique of cause and effect (4.11–4.21). The text also speaks at the practical level: worship taught out of compassion for the three kinds of āśrama (3.16), yoking the mind to Oṃ (1.25), fixing memory on non-duality (2.36), and the counsel on laya, distraction, kaṣāya and relishing of happiness (3.42–3.45). Main teaching: non-birth, taught from the ultimate standpoint (2.32), with the conventional teaching of birth given as a means (3.15, 4.42) and the practical counsel kept alongside it.
