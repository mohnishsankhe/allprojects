A compact verse synopsis of the whole hearing-lineage (snyan brgyud) path spoken in the voice of Vajradhara to the wisdom ḍākinī: the qualities of guru and disciple, the Cakrasaṃvara and Vajravārāhī empowerments, inner heat, illusory body, dream, clear light, transference and 'entering another's body', the consort practice (summarized here only), Mahāmudrā with an etymology of phyag rgya chen po, the three intermediate states with the dissolution at death, removal of obstacles by yantra exercises, and the fruit of Vajradhara in this life.

## Teachings (18: skeleton 18)

### 302b.6 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-302b-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Homage to Vajradhara. 'E ma ho!' The supreme true secret of all buddhas, the path of the wisdom-king, the supreme bindu, the meaning free of all verbal expression, the unborn self-sound of the Great Vehicle — thus it was heard: 'I, Vajradhara, explain it for the sake of the fortunate; bow with respect and listen, wisdom ḍākinī.'

> e ma ho/ sangs rgyas kun gyi yang dag gsang ba'i mchog /ye shes rgyal po thig le mchog gi lam/ /tshig tu brjod pa kun dang bral ba'i don/ /theg chen skye med rang sgra 'di skad thos/ /rdo rje 'chang ngas skal ldan don du bshad/ /gus btud nas ni ye shes mkha' 'gro nyon/

### 303a.1/2 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-303a-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 303a.2 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-303a-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

Inner heat (gtum mo) — self-blazing bliss-warmth — is the main trunk of the path. The verses name its key points — bodily posture, the deity's body visualized as an empty shell, the central, left and right channels and the four wheels, the seed-syllables, and the practices of breath and wind (not reproduced here); habituation to the four joys mixes channels, winds and drops; when wind and mind enter the central channel there is non-conceptuality, the afflictions subside by themselves, bliss and clarity are uninterrupted, and seeing the essence one abides in the dharmakāya.

### 303a.4 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-303a-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Enhancement (comes through) the illusory body, in which the eight worldly concerns are self-liberated: know all of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa as illusion, and illusion itself as like a rainbow or the moon in water. If present appearances were real and permanent, how could they change? Because they are deluded forms, they have no real existence. Look at forms as appearance-emptiness and sounds as echoes; when dualistic grasping collapses one is free of attachment and aversion; knowing this, act without clinging to variety, and the rainbow body, the dharmakāya, is attained.

> bogs 'don sgyu lus chos brgyad rang grol la/ /'khor 'das thams cad sgyu mar shes byas te/ /sgyu ma'ang 'ja' tshon chu zla lta bu'o/ /da ltar snang ba dngos po'i chos rnams ni/ /bden cing rtag na 'gyur bar ga la rigs/ /'khrul gzugs yin phyir don la grub pa med/ /gzugs rnams snang stong sgra rnams brag car ltos/ /gnyis 'dzin zhig pas chags sdang kun dang bral/ /de ltar shes pas sna tshogs zhen med spyod/ /'ja' lus chos sku thob par 'gyur ro kye/

### 303a.6 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-303a-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

At night the delusion of dreams clears by itself: with effort of the three doors, hold it with the iron hook of mindfulness; grasp (the dream), train, increase, transform and emanate, and clear away obstacles; ride the sun and moon and travel to all the pure lands; look at good and bad as illusion — whatever appears is self-liberated; brought to completion, one treads the ten grounds and attains the great aim.

### 303a.7 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-303a-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In particular, clear light, free of the darkness of ignorance: apply the body's key points and method to the four samādhis; look without grasping at the three poisons; seize (the clear light) in the gap between sleep, dream and thought; mix the four stages and day and night and grow familiar. That co-emergent wisdom, clarity-emptiness free of grasping, inexpressible, is the unbroken clear light; if it is not realized, train and look at (it as) Mahāmudrā.

### 303b.1 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-303b-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

Transference ('pho ba), the alchemy that gives buddhahood without meditation: when the signs of death appear, rejoice and give up attachment. The verses then summarize the method by which consciousness is sent out through the upper aperture into the pure land of the dharmakāya guru (method not reproduced here).

### 303b.6 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-303b-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mahāmudrā, which illuminates wisdom: the three doors unmoving, the five sense-doors left wide open; look, and see the characteristic of mind, which is not seen by looking; with no object, set (it) uncontrived in its own place; do not seek elsewhere — realize the essence. When it is set in the great way of the ordinary, uncontrived from the ground and spontaneously present from the start, then in the vast expanse of space without transition or change one sees the dharmakāya body beyond birth and death.

### 304a.1 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-304a-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

External objects and mind — all phenomena whatsoever — being one's own mind, are not established elsewhere; whatever arises as deluded thought and dual grasping, look: it is groundless, self-arisen, self-dissolving. Mind itself, unborn and empty, is the dharmakāya; its unceasing clear radiance, whatever arises, is the nirmāṇakāya; baseless great bliss, union, is the saṃbhogakāya.

### 304a.2 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-304a-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Phyag ('hand/seal') is being seized by the face of non-dual wisdom; rgya ('seal') is bliss through tearing apart the knot of saṃsāra; chen po ('great') is the self-liberated dharmakāya born from the lamp of union, not from another. The two obscurations are self-purified, free of the extremes of subject and object; perceptions, intellect and the phenomena of saṃsāra and nirvāṇa are exhausted; all qualities of knowledge are complete in oneself — buddha, beyond thought and expression, is self-born.

> phyag ni gnyis med ye shes ngo yis zin/ /rgya ni 'khor ba'i mdud pa dral bas bde/ /chen po zung 'jug sgron me bltams pa las/ / gzhan gyis ma yin rang grol chos sku'o/ /sgrib gnyis rang dag gzung 'dzin mtha' dang bral/ /'du shes blo dang 'khor 'das chos rnams zad/ /mkhyen tshad yon tan ji snyed rang la rdzogs/ /bsam brjod blo 'das sangs rgyas rang 'khrungs yin/

### 304a.3 <a id="tea-karnatantravajrapada-304a-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The introduction to the intermediate state: the three bardos — of birth-and-death, of dream, and of becoming. Look: self-awareness, clarity-emptiness, non-conceptual, free of obscuration, is the three kāyas arising of themselves. For one who has not realized, still in a body, in the three bardos generation stage, illusory body and clear light mix with the dharmakāya: earth, water, fire and wind dissolve in sequence; the eighty (conceptions) cease and the three appearances pass; white, red and mind meet within the lotus; the clear light is recognized and mother and child mix inseparably — for the sharpest faculties this is dharmakāya buddhahood. If it is not caught, (one is) bound by the three winds; the middling, like a fish in water, become the saṃbhogakāya; since attachment and aversion drive one into the womb, with the five blendings and the four seals carried onto the path, one trained before is liberated in the bardo; the least, freed from attachment and aversion, attain the nirmāṇakāya.
