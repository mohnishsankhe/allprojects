# Bṛhajjātaka

`src:brhat-jataka` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Horāśāstra, Bṛhat Jātaka
**Original title:** बृहज्जातक
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md)
**Genre:** jātaka
**Authors:** 
  - [Varāhamihira](../teachers/varahamihira.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: composed by Varāhamihira of Avanti, son and pupil of Ādityadāsa, who received a boon from the Sun at Kāpitthaka, after studying the views of the seers (28.9); scholarly: Varāhamihira, 6th c. CE; confidence: high
**Dates:** Scholarly account: mid 6th c. CE; (confidence high)
**Structure:** 28 chapters (GRETIL numbering; Kerala editions with Rudra's Vivaraṇa count 26)
**Availability:** digitized-original

Varāhamihira's classic of horoscopy: definitions of signs, planets and houses (with Greek-derived terms), conception and birth, infant mortality, longevity (with rival teachers' methods), planetary periods, aṣṭakavarga, livelihood, royal and nābhasa yogas, lunar and two-planet yogas, the yogas for renunciation (pravrajyā), character by signs and asterisms, aspects, houses, female nativities, the manner of death and the world one goes to next, lost horoscopes and the decans. It opens by saying the horā reveals the ripening of karma earned in a former birth.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Bṛhajjātakavivaraṇa of Rudra](brhajjataka-vivarana-rudra.md), [Daśādhyāyī](dasadhyayi.md), [Jagaccandrikā (Bhaṭṭotpala on the Bṛhajjātaka)](jagaccandrika.md)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

May the Sun give us speech — he who is held to give the moon its visible form, the path of those who are not reborn, the Self for the knowers of the Self, the sacrifice for sacrificers, lord of the immortal lights, the lord of the dissolution, origin and maintenance of the worlds spoken of in many ways in the Veda, the many-rayed lamp of the three worlds.

### 1.3 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Some take 'horā' as formed from 'aho-rātra' (day-and-night) by dropping the first and last syllables. The horā fully reveals the ripening of the karma, good and otherwise, earned in a former birth.

> horety ahorātravikalpam eke vāñchanti pūrvāparavarṇalopāt | karmārjitaṃ pūrvabhave sadādi yat tasya paktiṃ samabhivyanakti ||

### 1.4 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The limbs of Kāla are the head, face, chest, heart, belly, the part bearing the garment, the bladder, the genitals, the thighs, the two knees, the two shanks and the two feet: these are the signs beginning with Aries, each made of nine quarters of asterisms beginning with Aśvinī, set in the wheel. Rāśi, kṣetra, gṛha, ṛkṣa, bha and bhavana all mean the same.

### 1.13 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-1-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The exaltations of the Sun and the rest are Aries, Taurus, Capricorn, Virgo, Cancer, Pisces and Libra, at the 10th, 3rd, 28th, 15th, 5th, 27th and 20th degrees; their falls are in the seventh sign from these.

### 1.15-18 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-1-15-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The houses from the ascendant are named body (tanu), family, co-born, kin, son, enemy, wife, death, fortune (śubha), position, gain and loss (riḥpha); the 3rd, 6th, 10th and 11th are 'increasing' (upacaya). The 4th and 8th are 'quadrangular'; the 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th are the angles (kaṇṭaka, kendra, catuṣṭaya), in which planets are strong; after an angle comes the paṇaphara and then the āpoklima. The 4th is also hibuka, the 7th jāmitra, the 10th meṣūraṇa or karma.

### 2.1 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-2-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Of Kāla, the Sun is the self, the Moon the mind, Mars strength, Mercury speech, Jupiter knowledge and happiness, Venus desire, Saturn sorrow. The Sun and Moon are kings, Mars the general, Mercury the prince, Jupiter and Venus ministers, Saturn the servant.

### 2.6-7 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-2-6-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Venus and Jupiter are brahmins, Mars and the Sun kṣatriyas, the Moon a vaiśya, Mercury a śūdra, Saturn of the lowest; the Moon, Sun and Jupiter are of sattva, Mercury and Venus of rajas, Mars and Saturn of tamas.

### 7.9-13 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-7-9-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Jīvaśarman in his own view gives each planet an equal share of the maximum life; Satya's statement — reckoning by the navāṃśas traversed — is widely agreed. Satya's teaching is best here, but some make it unfit by many multiplications; where several apply, only the greatest multiplication should be made.

### 12.1-2 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-12-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Yavanas counted the yogas of the sky (nābhasa) as eighteen hundred; they are treated here in brief. When all planets occupy movable, fixed or dual signs the āśraya yogas rajju, musala and nala arise; when benefics or malefics occupy the angles, the two dala yogas, the garland (mālā) and the serpent (sarpa), which Parāśara described.

### 15.1 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-15-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When four or more strong planets are together in one sign, the native becomes a renunciant; according to the strongest of them — Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, the Moon, Venus, Saturn or the Sun — he becomes a Śākya (Buddhist), an Ājīvika, a bhikṣu, a vṛddha (elder ascetic), a caraka, a nirgrantha (Jain) or a forest-dweller living on wild food. If the lord of that renunciation is overcome by others, he falls from it.

> ekasthaiś caturādibhir balayutair jātāḥ pṛthag vīryagaiḥ śākyājīvikabhikṣuvṛddhacarakā nirgranthavanyāśanāḥ | māheyajñagurukṣapākarasitaprābhākarīnaiḥ kramāt pravrajyā balibhiḥ samāḥ parajitais tatsvāmibhiḥ pracyutiḥ ||

### 15.2-4 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-15-2-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

If the planets giving renunciation are overpowered by the Sun's rays, the persons are not initiated but are devoted to that order; if weak and aspected by others they are initiated only on request. One obtains initiation (dīkṣā) when the unaspected lord of the birth-sign looks at Saturn, or Saturn looks at the weak lord of the birth-sign, or the Moon in Saturn's decan or in a navāṃśa of Mars or Saturn is aspected by Saturn. With Jupiter, the Moon and the ascendant aspected by Saturn and Jupiter in the ninth, one born in a royal yoga becomes a founder of a holy place (tīrthakṛt); with Saturn in the ninth, a consecrated king.

### 25.14 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-25-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Jupiter; the Moon and Venus; the Sun and Mars; Saturn and Mercury lead respectively to the worlds of the gods, the fathers, animals and hell-beings: the world from which the native came is judged from the lord of the decan occupied by the stronger of Sun and Moon, and its high, middling or low place by that lord's exaltation, middle state or fall.

### 25.15 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-25-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The destination after death is judged likewise from the lord of the decan of the sixth or eighth, or from a planet in the seventh. If Jupiter is exalted in the sixth, an angle or the eighth, or if the twelfth house rises in a benefic navāṃśa while the remaining planets are without strength, there is liberation (mokṣa).

### 28.9-10 <a id="tea-brhat-jataka-28-9-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high
