The Brāhmaṇa of the Taittirīya school of the Black Yajurveda, supplementing the Saṃhitā's own prose: fire establishment, Agnihotra, Soma and royal rites, the nakṣatra offerings, the horse sacrifice, the cosmogony of Prajāpati diving as a boar to raise the earth (1.1.3), and the three Kāṭhaka chapters (3.10–12) on the Sāvitra, Nāciketa and Vaiśvasṛja fire-altars — including Naciketas' visit to Death (3.11.8), Bharadvāja's three lives of Vedic study (3.10.11) and the cosmic session of the world-creators (3.12.9).

### 3.10.11.3-5 <a id="tea-taittiriya-brahmana-3-10-11-3-5"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Bharadvāja lived three lives as a Vedic student. When, old and lying down, Indra asked what he would do with a fourth life, he said: 'I would live it as a student.' Indra showed him three mountain-like heaps, took a handful from each and said: 'These are the Vedas; the Vedas are endless; what you have learned in three lives is this. Come, know this: this is all knowledge' — and taught him the Sāvitra fire, by which he became immortal and reached the world of heaven, union with the sun.

### 3.11.8.1-6 <a id="tea-taittiriya-brahmana-3-11-8-1-6"></a>

Vājaśravasa gave away all he owned; faith entered his young son Naciketas, who asked 'To whom will you give me?' — and was told 'To Death'. In Death's house, unfed for three nights, he was granted three boons: to return alive to his father, the imperishability of sacrifice and gifts, and the conquest of re-death; Death taught him the Nāciketa fire, and by it he conquered re-death.

### 3.12.9.3-7 <a id="tea-taittiriya-brahmana-3-12-9-3-7"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The first world-creators sat in a sacrificial session for a thousand years: austerity was their householder, Brahman their brahmā priest, truth their hotṛ, immortality their udgātṛ, the past their prastotṛ, the future their pratihartṛ, the breath their adhvaryu, the seasons their assistants, death the slaughterer. From it was born the guardian of the world, the golden bird named Brahman, by whom the sun shines. 'One who does not know the Veda does not think of that great one'; this is the eternal greatness of the brāhmaṇa, which does not grow by action nor become less.
