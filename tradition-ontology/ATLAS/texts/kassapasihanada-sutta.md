## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 13-20 <a id="tea-kassapasihanada-sutta-13-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There is a path — the noble eightfold path — by which one sees for oneself that the Buddha speaks at the right time and truly. Going naked, food restrictions and other mortifications do not make one an ascetic or brahmin; one who develops a mind of love free of enmity and realises the liberation of mind and of wisdom is called an ascetic and a brahmin.

### 14 <a id="tea-kassapasihanada-sutta-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

The naked ascetic Kassapa lists the ascetic practices held to make one an ascetic or brāhmaṇa: going naked, licking the hands, refusing food brought or prepared, refusing food at certain times and places, restricted foods, eating at long intervals, wearing coarse garments, pulling out hair and beard, standing, squatting, lying on thorns, bathing thrice daily. The Buddha answers that one who does all this but has not developed ethics, mind and wisdom is far from being an ascetic or brāhmaṇa.
