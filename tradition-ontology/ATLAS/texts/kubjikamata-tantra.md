# Kubjikāmata Tantra

**Original title:** कुब्जिकामततन्त्र

The root scripture of the Kubjikā cult (the Kulālikāmnāya recension), a dialogue of Bhairava and the Goddess: her descent at Candradvīpa and emergence from the liṅga as the bent Kubjikā, her journey through the sacred seats and the founding of the 'western' transmission (1-2); the Mālinī and mantra system (3-10); the six centres with their deities and yoginīs (11-16); the haṃsa and the fourfold order (17-19); the island-transmission (20-21); knowledge of the three times and yogic departure (23); worship (24); the observance (25).

### 2.2-5 <a id="tea-kubjikamata-tantra-2-2-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Bhairava praises the Goddess as the fiery supreme power born of the supreme reality, knowledge, will and action, straight as a line and again coiled like a sleeping serpent, the power of sound; thus praised, the supreme Goddess splits the liṅga and comes forth — dark as collyrium, bent in form, pot-bellied, her face slightly fearsome, her hair standing up, both beautiful and deformed, of many forms.

### 2.14 <a id="tea-kubjikamata-tantra-2-14"></a>

### 2.17-18 <a id="tea-kubjikamata-tantra-2-17-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Bhairava answers: 'Why do you not understand in yourself, O Goddess who graces all? Nothing is without me, and nowhere is anything without you; by the mutual union of qualities, by the relation of cause and effect, you are my guru, O Goddess, and I am yours — without question.'

> na mayā rahitaṃ kiñcin na tvayā rahitaṃ kvacit / anyonyaguṇayogena kāryakāraṇayogataḥ / tvaṃ gurur mama deveśi ahaṃ te na vicāraṇāt

### 2.20-24 <a id="tea-kubjikamata-tantra-2-20-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Goddess agrees and names the path: this is the Western transmission (paścimāmnāya), 'the western (last) of all paths', to be given in full to the Siddhas; leaving Candradvīpa she goes to the Kula mountain in Bhārata, to Śrīparvata called Kumāra, where she stays as a shadow, unmanifest yet manifest.

### 2.36-42 <a id="tea-kubjikamata-tantra-2-36-42"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Going far to the west, to a Himalayan cave where the forest-dweller Olambikā lives, the Goddess is pleased by the yogins there and declares that because she 'flew up' (oḍḍitā) on her feet the place shall be Oḍḍiyāna; eight mind-born sons will arise, and she becomes mother of the eight Śākinīs, lords of the eight thrones.

### 11.6-7 <a id="tea-kubjikamata-tantra-11-6-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Know the primal yoni of the supreme as fourfold by the sequence 'beyond form' and the rest: beyond form (rūpātīta) is Kāmākhya, form (rūpa) is the great Pūrṇagiri, word (pada) is called Jālandhara, and body (piṇḍa) is Oḍra.

> rūpātītaṃ tu kāmākhyaṃ rūpaṃ pūrṇagirir mahān / padaṃ jālandharākhyaṃ tu piṇḍam oḍraṃ prakīrtitam

### 11.13-16 <a id="tea-kubjikamata-tantra-11-13-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Through the relation of support and supported, by the sixfold order, the Goddess brings about manifold creation: the self supports power, the self rests on the haṃsa, the haṃsa is within the breath, which moves in the channels; the channels are in the body with its karma, and the bound soul with māyā and impurity — this sixfold set awakens the Kula path; in it the whole world is born.

### 12.3-9 <a id="tea-kubjikamata-tantra-12-3-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Those who received the command hastily, before the appointed time, are called tāmasa: they do not honour the samayas, break the guru's command, love quarrels, look for faults, slander the guru and turn away from him; deluded and self-important, they go to the lowest state and are dead while living. The wise one should instead ask the lord's pardon and worship the transmission.
