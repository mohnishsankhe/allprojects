Reads the Nirvāṇa-sūtra line as 'all sentient beings — whole being — is buddha-nature' (shitsuu busshō); rejects buddha-nature as a seed or as a permanent self; impermanence is buddha-nature; discusses Zhaozhou's and Guishan's 'no buddha-nature'.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### impermanence <a id="tea-bussho-impermanence"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Quoting Huineng that impermanence is buddha-nature and permanence is the discriminating mind of good and evil, Dōgen teaches that impermanence itself — grasses, trees, bodies and minds — is buddha-nature.

### whole-being <a id="tea-bussho-whole-being"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Nirvāṇa-sūtra line 'all sentient beings without exception have buddha-nature' is read as 'all sentient beings — whole being — is buddha-nature' (shitsuu wa busshō nari): buddha-nature is not a seed possessed by beings nor a potential awaiting conditions.
