The prose auto-commentary to the Kośa. It reports the Vaibhāṣika positions (often marked with 'kila', 'so they say') and argues the Sautrāntika alternatives: seeds instead of possession, the present alone as existent, causeless destruction, the unreality of avijñapti and of the unconditioned. Its ninth chapter refutes the Vātsīputrīya person and the non-Buddhist self.

### 1.42 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-1-42"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Sautrāntikas say: why chew on empty space? Depending on the eye and visible forms visual consciousness arises; who then sees, and what is seen? This is a mere set of dharmas without activity, mere cause and effect; for practical purposes expressions are used at will — 'the eye sees', 'consciousness cognizes' — but one should not cling to them.

> cakṣurhi pratītya rūpāṇi cotpadyate cakṣurvijñānam | tatra kaḥ paśyati ko vā dṛśyate | nivryāpāraṃ hīdaṃ dharmamātraṃ hetuphalamātraṃ ca |

### 2.36 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-2-36"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Possession (prāpti) is not a separate real entity: what is called 'possession' of good or bad dharmas is the seed (bīja) in the series, not uprooted and not damaged; the seed is the name-and-form capable of producing a result, directly or mediately, through a specific transformation of the series (santati-pariṇāma-viśeṣa), a transformation being the series' becoming otherwise.

> kiṃ punaridaṃ bījaṃ nāma | yannāmarūpaṃ phalotpattau samartha sākṣāt pāramparyeṇa vā | santatipariṇāmaviśeṣāt |

### 2.46 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-2-46"></a>

### 2.55 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-2-55"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Sautrāntikas say all the unconditioned is unreal (adravya): it is not an entity like matter or feeling; space is mere absence of tangible resistance, as people call 'space' where in darkness they meet no obstruction; cessation through discernment is the non-arising of further defilements and births once the antidote has arisen.

### 4.2-3 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-4-2-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Destruction has no cause: if a thing did not perish as soon as it has arisen, it would not perish later either, being the same; the destruction of momentary things such as cognition, sound and flame is seen to be spontaneous; what seems destruction by fire (of wood) is only the non-arising of a similar series.

### 5.2 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-5-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

It is better as the Sautrāntikas hold: the latent defilement (anuśaya) is neither associated with nor dissociated from mind, since it is not a separate entity; the defilement asleep is called anuśaya, awake it is an envelopment (paryavasthāna); its sleep is the continuance of the seed-state of what is not manifest — the capacity, born of a defilement, to produce a defilement, as memory arises from a capacity born of experience and rice from a capacity born of rice.

> prasupto hi kleśo 'nuśaya ucyate | prabuddhaḥ paryavasthānam | kā ca tasya prasuptiḥ | asammukhībhūtasya bījabhāvānubandhaḥ |

### 5.25 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-5-25"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Those who say that everything exists — past, future and present — are Sarvāstivādins; those who, distinguishing, say that some exists (the present, and past karma that has not given its fruit) and some does not (past karma that has given its fruit, and the future) are Vibhajyavādins.

### 5.26 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-5-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The four masters: Dharmatrāta — change of mode of being, as a gold vessel is broken and remade, or milk becomes curd; Ghoṣaka — change of characteristic, a past dharma having the past characteristic without being severed from the others, as a man attached to one woman is not detached from others; Vasumitra — change of state, as a counting-token is 'one' in the units place and 'a hundred' in the hundreds place; Buddhadeva — change of relation, as one woman is daughter and mother. The third is best.

### 5.27 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-5-27"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Against three-time existence: a past thing is not seen as existing when remembered; if past and future existed as the present does they would be present; the conditioned 'comes to be having not been'. The Sautrāntikas do not teach that a result arises from past karma, but from the specific series preceded by it; if past and future existed as substances, the result would always exist, and what would karma do?

> naiva hi sautrāntikā atītāt karmaṇaḥ phalotpatti varṇayanti | kiṃ tarhi | tatpūrvakātsantānaviśeṣāt

### 6.58 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-6-58"></a>

### 9.p461 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-9-p461"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 9.p461/2 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-9-p461-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Vātsīputrīyas hold that there is a person. Is it real as a substance, like matter, or a designation, like milk (a collection)? If a substance, it would have to be different from the aggregates, with its own cause, or unconditioned — the outsiders' view. If a designation, we say the same. They reply: it is neither substance nor designation; the person is designated in dependence on the internal, appropriated, present aggregates.

> yattrhi vātsīputriyāḥ pudgalaṃ santimicchanti | vicāryaṃ tāvadetat | kiṃ te dravyata icchantyāhosvit prajñaptitaḥ |

### 9.p461/3 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-9-p461-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Vātsīputrīya illustration: as fire is designated in dependence on fuel — fire is not designated without fuel, yet cannot be designated as other than the fuel nor as not other; if other, the fuel would not be hot; if not other, what is burned would be the burner — so the person is neither other than nor the same as the aggregates.

> na hi vinendhanenāgniḥ prajñapyate na cānya indhanādagniḥ śakyate prajñapayituṃ nāpyananyaḥ | yadi hānyaḥ syādanuṣṇamindhanaṃ syāt | athānanyaḥ syāddāhyameva dāhakaṃ syāt |

### 9.p462 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-9-p462"></a>

### 9.p469 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-9-p469"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On the Burden sūtra ('I will teach you the burden, the taking up of the burden, the laying down of the burden and the bearer of the burden'): the bearer is not a real person apart from the burden; the earlier aggregates bring harm to the later, so the aggregates themselves are spoken of as burden and bearer, lest the bearer be taken as permanent or inexpressible.

> "bhāraṃ ca vo bhikṣavo deśayiṣyāmi bhārādānaṃ ca bhāranikṣepaṇaṃ ca bhārahāraṃ ce"ti

### 9.p477 <a id="tea-abhidharmakosabhasya-9-p477"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The result arises from the specific transformation of the series, as the fruit arises from the seed through shoot, stem and flower; as a citron flower dyed with lac yields red pulp in the fruit, so from karma arises ripening — but ripening does not produce further ripening.
