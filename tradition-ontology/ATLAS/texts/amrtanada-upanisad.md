Oṃ as chariot with Viṣṇu as charioteer; a six-limbed yoga (pratyāhāra, dhyāna, prāṇāyāma, dhāraṇā, tarka, samādhi); postures, breath-regulation, the doors of prāṇa, faults to avoid, month-by-month signs, seats and colours of the five prāṇas, and exit through the crown without rebirth.

### 1 <a id="tea-amrtanada-upanisad-1"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Having studied the scriptures and reflected on them again and again, the wise one who has known Brahman should abandon them like a torch after the goal is seen.

Mounting the chariot of Oṃ with Viṣṇu as charioteer, one goes to Rudra's world; as far as the chariot goes, then leaving it one reaches the place beyond.

`sourced` · confidence moderate

As impurities of ore are burnt by blowing, so faults born of the senses are burnt by restraining breath; sins by breath-control, faults by concentration, and so on.

### 15 <a id="tea-amrtanada-upanisad-15"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Like one blind, deaf and like a piece of wood, the yogin should abide unmoved (in practice).

### 17 <a id="tea-amrtanada-upanisad-17"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Tarka (the fifth limb) is reasoning not opposed to the āgamas; samādhi is that by which, when it is attained, one regards everything as equal.

_level: bridging · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: intermediate · types: practice, consciousness-mind_

`sourced` · confidence moderate

Seated in the lotus, svastika or bhadra posture, facing north, the yogin closes one nostril and draws breath through the other; he should meditate on Oṃ.

### 28 <a id="tea-amrtanada-upanisad-27"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The yogin should abandon fear, anger, sloth, too much sleep and too much waking, too much food and fasting.

### 29-30 <a id="tea-amrtanada-upanisad-29-30"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

If the rule is kept well, knowledge arises in three months; in four he sees the gods, in five he gains power equal to theirs, and in six he attains kaivalya.

Breathing goes on 1,13,680 times a day and night; prāṇa dwells in the heart, apāna in the anus, samāna in the navel, udāna in the throat, vyāna throughout the body; each has its colour.
