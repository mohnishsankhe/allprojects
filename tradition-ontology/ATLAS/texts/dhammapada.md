## Teachings (22: skeleton 22)

### 1-2 <a id="tea-dhammapada-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mind is the forerunner of all states; mind is chief, they are mind-made. If one speaks or acts with a corrupted mind, suffering follows as the wheel follows the ox's foot; if with a pure mind, happiness follows like a shadow that never leaves.

> Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā; Manasā ce paduṭṭhena, bhāsati vā karoti vā; Tato naṁ dukkhamanveti, cakkaṁva vahato padaṁ. Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā; Manasā ce pasannena, bhāsati vā karoti vā; Tato naṁ sukhamanveti, chāyāva anapāyinī.

### 5 <a id="tea-dhammapada-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Hatred is never appeased by hatred in this world; by non-hatred it is appeased: this is an eternal law.

> Na hi verena verāni, sammantīdha kudācanaṁ; Averena ca sammanti, esa dhammo sanantano.

### 21 <a id="tea-dhammapada-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Heedfulness is the path to the deathless, heedlessness the path to death; the heedful do not die, the heedless are as if dead already.

> Appamādo amatapadaṁ, pamādo maccuno padaṁ; Appamattā na mīyanti, ye pamattā yathā matā.

### 153-154 <a id="tea-dhammapada-153-154"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Through many births I wandered in saṃsāra seeking the house-builder; painful is birth again and again. House-builder, you are seen! You will not build a house again; all your rafters are broken, the ridge-pole shattered; the mind has gone to the unconditioned, it has reached the destruction of cravings.

> Anekajātisaṁsāraṁ, sandhāvissaṁ anibbisaṁ; Gahakāraṁ gavesanto, dukkhā jāti punappunaṁ. Gahakāraka diṭṭhosi, puna gehaṁ na kāhasi; Sabbā te phāsukā bhaggā, gahakūṭaṁ visaṅkhataṁ; Visaṅkhāragataṁ cittaṁ, taṇhānaṁ khayamajjhagā.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: experiential · path: knowledge · stage: realized · types: karma-liberation_

### 160 <a id="tea-dhammapada-160"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Oneself is one's own protector; what other protector could there be? With oneself well tamed one gains a protector hard to find.

> Attā hi attano nātho, ko hi nātho paro siyā; Attanā hi sudantena, nāthaṁ labhati dullabhaṁ.

### 165 <a id="tea-dhammapada-165"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

By oneself is evil done, by oneself one is defiled; by oneself evil is left undone, by oneself one is purified; purity and impurity are personal, no one purifies another.

> Attanā hi kataṁ pāpaṁ, attanā saṅkilissati; Attanā akataṁ pāpaṁ, attanāva visujjhati; Suddhī asuddhi paccattaṁ, nāñño aññaṁ visodhaye.

### 203-204 <a id="tea-dhammapada-203-204"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Hunger is the greatest illness, formations the greatest suffering; knowing this as it is, nibbāna is the highest happiness. Health is the highest gain, contentment the greatest wealth, trust the best kinsman, nibbāna the highest happiness.

> Jighacchāparamā rogā, saṅkhāraparamā dukhā; Etaṁ ñatvā yathābhūtaṁ, nibbānaṁ paramaṁ sukhaṁ. Ārogyaparamā lābhā, Santuṭṭhiparamaṁ dhanaṁ; Vissāsaparamā ñāti, Nibbānaṁ paramaṁ sukhaṁ.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: seeker · path: general · stage: all · types: ultimate, karma-liberation_

### 277-279 <a id="tea-dhammapada-277-279"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 282 <a id="tea-dhammapada-282"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

From meditation (yoga) wisdom arises, without it wisdom wanes; knowing this twofold path of gain and loss, one should so conduct oneself that wisdom grows.

> Yogā ve jāyatī bhūri, ayogā bhūrisaṅkhayo; Etaṁ dvedhāpathaṁ ñatvā, bhavāya vibhavāya ca; Tathāttānaṁ niveseyya, yathā bhūri pavaḍḍhati.

### 372 <a id="tea-dhammapada-372"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There is no jhāna for one without wisdom, no wisdom for one without jhāna; one in whom are both jhāna and wisdom is close to nibbāna.

> Natthi jhānaṁ apaññassa, paññā natthi ajhāyato; Yamhi jhānañca paññā ca, sa ve nibbānasantike.

> bāhitapāpoti brāhmaṇo, samacariyā samaṇoti vuccati

### 396 <a id="tea-dhammapada-396"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

I do not call one a brahmin because of birth or origin from a mother; he is just a 'bho-sayer' if he has possessions; the one who has nothing and takes nothing, him I call a brahmin.

> Na cāhaṁ brāhmaṇaṁ brūmi, yonijaṁ mattisambhavaṁ; Bhovādi nāma so hoti, sace hoti sakiñcano; Akiñcanaṁ anādānaṁ, tamahaṁ brūmi brāhmaṇaṁ.
