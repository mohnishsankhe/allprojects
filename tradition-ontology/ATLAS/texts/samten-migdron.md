Nupchen Sangye Yeshe's treatise distinguishing four approaches to contemplation — the gradual (tsen min, of Kamalaśīla), the simultaneous (ston mun, the Chan of Moheyan), Mahāyoga and Atiyoga — ranking each above the last and so distinguishing the Great Perfection from Chan.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### atiyoga <a id="tea-samten-migdron-atiyoga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Great Perfection is distinguished from the simultaneous approach: it does not rely on the effort of fixing on non-conceptuality but on the primordially present great self-arising wisdom in which there is nothing to cultivate or reject — so Atiyoga is placed highest.

### gradual <a id="tea-samten-migdron-gradual"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Nupchen presents the gradual approach (rim gyis pa) taught by Kamalaśīla — calm and analytical insight cultivated step by step — as authentic but the lowest of the four approaches he compares.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: meditation · stage: all · types: practice, dispute_

### simultaneous <a id="tea-samten-migdron-simultaneous"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The simultaneous approach (cig car ba) of the Chan master Moheyan — entering non-conceptuality directly without gradual steps — is presented with respect as higher than the gradual, yet below Mahāyoga and the Great Perfection.
