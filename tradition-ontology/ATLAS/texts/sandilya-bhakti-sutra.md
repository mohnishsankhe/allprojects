One hundred aphorisms attributed to the sage Śāṇḍilya, opening 'Now, therefore, the inquiry into devotion' and defining devotion as supreme attachment (parānurakti) to the Lord; it argues that devotion is not knowledge or an act, that it is primary and the others subordinate, distinguishes secondary (gauṇī) devotion leading to the supreme, reports Kāśyapa's and Bādarāyaṇa's views on devotion's object, and discusses eligibility, the Lord, the soul and creation.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Bhakticandrikā](bhakticandrika.md), [Śāṇḍilyabhaktisūtrabhāṣya (of Svapneśvara)](sandilya-bhakti-sutra-bhasya.md)

### 29-31 <a id="tea-sandilya-bhakti-sutra-29-31"></a>
