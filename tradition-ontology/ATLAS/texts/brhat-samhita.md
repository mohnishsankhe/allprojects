Varāhamihira's encyclopaedia of mundane astrology and omens in 106 chapters: the astrologer's qualifications; the courses of sun, moon, Rāhu (with the true cause of eclipses), planets, comets, Agastya and the Seven Seers; the tortoise-division of the land; rain-forecasting; portents (utpāta) and their pacification; bodily signs; site-lore (vāstu), finding water, plants, temples, images and their consecration; animals; the marks of the five great men; gems, pearls, ruby and emerald; perfumes; augury; and the qualities of asterisms, lunar days and karaṇas for action.

### 1.9 <a id="tea-brhat-samhita-1-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The science of the lights, with its many divisions and subjects, rests on three branches (skandha); the seers call the whole of it by one name. ### 5.1-7 <a id="tea-brhat-samhita-5-1-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Some say that the asura's head, though cut off, was not abandoned by its life-breaths because of the special taste of the nectar, and became a planet; that it has the form of the discs of sun and moon, and being black is not seen in the sky except at the junctions (parvan), by a boon from the lotus-born. Others teach that it is serpent-shaped with head and tail; others that it is formless, made of darkness, called Saiṃhikeya. (Against these:) if Rāhu were embodied, moving in the sky, a head or a disc, how would it seize (sun and moon) always half a circle apart with a fixed motion? If its motion were not fixed, how could it be computed? If it were a serpent seizing with head or tail, why does it not cover the half-circle between them? If there were two Rāhus, when the eclipsed moon had set or risen the sun would also be seen eclipsed by the other moving at the same speed.

### 5.8-13 <a id="tea-brhat-samhita-5-8-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In a lunar eclipse the moon enters the earth's shadow; in a solar eclipse the moon enters (before) the sun; hence the eclipse of the moon does not begin from its western half nor that of the sun from its eastern. As a tree's shadow lies long on one side, so night by night the earth's shadow falls opposite the sun. When the moon, in the seventh sign from the sun and not far north or south, moves east, it enters the earth's shadow; the moon below, coming like a cloud, covers the sun, so the solar eclipse differs from place to place by the observer's position. Thus the cause of eclipses has been told by the teachers of divine sight: Rāhu is not the cause here — this is the truth of the science.

> evam uparāgakāraṇam uktam idaṃ divyadṛgbhir ācāryaiḥ | rāhur akāraṇam asminn ity uktaḥ śāstrasadbhāvaḥ ||

### 8.23-29 <a id="tea-brhat-samhita-8-23-29"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The sixty-year cycle has twelve yugas of five years, ruled by Viṣṇu, Jupiter, Indra, Agni, Tvaṣṭṛ, Ahirbudhnya, the Fathers, the All-gods, Soma, Indra-Agni, the Aśvins and Bhaga; the five years of each are saṃvatsara (Agni), parivatsara (Sun), idāvatsara (Moon), anuvatsara (Prajāpati) and idvatsara (Umā's lord). ### 45.1-3 <a id="tea-brhat-samhita-45-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

I will tell the portents that Garga declared to Atri. In brief: a portent (utpāta) is a change of nature. Through men's misconduct an accumulation of sin brings calamity; the portents of sky, atmosphere and earth announce it. The deities, displeased by men's misconduct, create them; to counter them the king should have pacification (śānti) performed in the realm.

### 45.4-7 <a id="tea-brhat-samhita-45-4-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Heavenly portents are anomalies of planets and asterisms; atmospheric ones are meteors, thunderclaps, winds, halos, phantom cities, rainbows and the like; earthly ones arise from moving and fixed things. The earthly is calmed by pacification, the atmospheric softened; some say the heavenly is not calmed — but it too is calmed by abundant gifts of gold, food, cows and land, by milking a cow on the ground at a Rudra shrine, and by the ten-million oblation. The fate thus shown ripens for a king in eightfold ways: on himself, son, treasury, vehicles, city, wife, priest and people.

### 68.1-9 <a id="tea-brhat-samhita-68-1-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When the star-planets are strong, in their own or exaltation signs and in the angles, five excellent men are born: Haṃsa by Jupiter, Śaśa by Saturn, Rucaka by Mars, Bhadra by Mercury and Mālavya by Venus. Firmness of spirit comes from the Sun, bodily and mental (nature) from the Moon's strength. A sāttvika man has compassion, steadiness, truthfulness, uprightness and devotion to brahmins and gods; one with more rajas is attached to poetry, arts, sacrifice and women and is very brave; one with more tamas deceives others, is foolish, lazy, given to anger and much sleep; with mixed qualities there are seven kinds.
