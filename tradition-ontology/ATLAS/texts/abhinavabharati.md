Abhinavagupta's commentary on the Nāṭyaśāstra, the principal source for the debate on how rasa arises: it reports and criticises Lollaṭa, Śaṅkuka and Bhaṭṭa Nāyaka and sets out Abhinava's own view of rasa as the relish (carvaṇā) of a universalized emotion in the spectator's own consciousness, the seven obstacles to it, and śānta as a rasa whose stable basis is knowledge of the self.

## Teachings (7: skeleton 7)

### 6 <a id="tea-abhinavabharati-6-rasasutra-abhinavagupta"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Abhinavagupta's own view: rasa is not produced, inferred or merely enjoyed through special powers; it is manifested (suggested) and relished (carvaṇā). The stable emotion, already present as a latent impression in the sensitive spectator (sahṛdaya), is awakened by generalized (sādhāraṇīkṛta) determinants, freed from the limits of 'mine' and 'another's', time and place; its relish, lasting only as long as the relishing, is a supermundane (alaukika) wonder (camatkāra) in which consciousness rests in itself (viśrānti).

### 6 <a id="tea-abhinavabharati-6-rasasutra-bhatta-nayaka"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Bhaṭṭa Nāyaka's view: rasa is neither perceived, produced nor manifested; poetic language has, besides denotation, the power of 'making present' (bhāvakatva), which generalizes the determinants, and the power of 'making enjoyable' (bhojakatva), by which the rasa is enjoyed in a state of repose in one's own consciousness pervaded by sattva, akin to the tasting of the supreme Brahman.

### 6 <a id="tea-abhinavabharati-6-rasasutra-lollata"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Lollaṭa's view: rasa is the stable emotion intensified (upacita) by the determinants and the rest; it exists primarily in the character imitated (Rāma) and secondarily in the actor, because of the imitation.

### 6 <a id="tea-abhinavabharati-6-rasasutra-sankuka"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Śaṅkuka's view: rasa is the stable emotion inferred in the actor from artificially produced determinants and consequents; the actor is apprehended as Rāma as a painted horse is apprehended as a horse — a cognition neither true, false, doubtful nor similar; rasa is an 'imitation' of the stable emotion.

### 6 <a id="tea-abhinavabharati-6-rasasutra-vighnas"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Rasa arises when the obstacles (vighna) to relish are removed; there are seven: lack of credibility; being caught in the particular time and place of oneself or another; being overwhelmed by one's own pleasure or pain; lack of the means of perception; lack of clarity; the non-predominance (of the stable emotion); and doubt. The theatre's devices — preliminaries, music, conventions — serve to remove them.

### 6 <a id="tea-abhinavabharati-6-santa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On śānta: its stable emotion is knowledge of reality (tattvajñāna), which is the self (ātman) itself, pure and free of all other feelings; it underlies every other emotion as the thread under the gems, so that love and the rest become its transient states; its goal is liberation, the highest aim of man. Śānta is therefore a rasa and indeed the basis of all.
