Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja's Bengali verse biography and theology of Caitanya, interspersed with Sanskrit verses, in three parts: Ādi (17 chapters), Madhya (25) and Antya (20). It sets out who Caitanya is (Kṛṣṇa with Rādhā's mood), his teachings to Rūpa, Sanātana, Rāmānanda, Sārvabhauma and Prakāśānanda, and his last years of divine madness at Purī.

### 1.1.3 <a id="tea-caitanya-caritamrta-1-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

What the Upaniṣads call the non-dual Brahman is the effulgence of his body; the indwelling Self (Paramātman) is his partial expansion; the Bhagavān full of six opulences is he himself — there is no truth higher than Kṛṣṇa Caitanya.

### 1.1.5 <a id="tea-caitanya-caritamrta-1-1-5"></a>

### 1.7 <a id="tea-caitanya-caritamrta-1-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

At Kāśī Caitanya answers Prakāśānanda and the Advaita renouncers: the Brahma Sūtras teach transformation (pariṇāma) of the Lord's power, not unreal appearance; the Lord, like the touchstone that produces gold, remains unchanged while the world arises; the great saying is the syllable Oṃ, not 'that thou art'; Śaṅkara abandoned the direct sense of scripture for a secondary one; love of Kṛṣṇa (prema) is the fifth and highest human aim.
