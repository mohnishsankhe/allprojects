**Availability:** digitized-translation

Candrakīrti's 'Entry into the Middle Way', a supplement to Nāgārjuna's treatise arranged by the ten bodhisattva grounds, each paired with a perfection; its long sixth chapter on wisdom refutes arising from self, other, both and without cause, refutes mind-only, sets out the two truths, the sixteen (or twenty) emptinesses and the sevenfold analysis of the chariot, and ends with the qualities of the grounds and of buddhahood.

### 6.45-97 <a id="tea-madhyamakavatara-6-45-97"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Refutation of mind-only: consciousness does not exist without an object any more than an object without consciousness; the dream example does not prove a real mind; reflexive awareness is not established; the sūtra's 'the three worlds are mind only' teaches that mind, not a creator, is the principal cause, and statements denying external objects are of provisional meaning.
