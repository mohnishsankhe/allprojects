# Gorakṣaśataka

`src:goraksasataka` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Original title:** गोरक्षशतक
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md), [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md)
**Genre:** yoga manual
**Authors:** 
  - [Gorakṣanātha](../teachers/goraksanatha.md) — role: author; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: Spoken by Gorakṣa for the release from the noose of existence (v. 1).; scholarly: Several different texts carry this title. The text printed by Kuvalayananda and Shukla (and, in a longer form, by Nowotny) is identified by scholars as a recension of the Vivekamārtaṇḍa family; the 'original Gorakṣaśataka' is a different text (src:goraksasataka-mallinson).; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 13th c. (Vivekamārtaṇḍa family); (confidence low)
**Structure:** 101 verses in the Kuvalayananda–Shukla edition (GRETIL numbering, vv. 81 and 95 not numbered in the e-text)
**Availability:** digitized-original

'The hundred of Gorakṣa': a short haṭha manual of six-limbed yoga (posture, breath-control, withdrawal, concentration, meditation, samādhi). It teaches siddhāsana and padmāsana, the centres, ten channels and ten breaths, the awakening of kuṇḍalinī, five seals and locks, the sun and moon and the reversed practice, the five element-concentrations, meditation with and without qualities, and samādhi as the one taste of breath and mind.
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: original; name: Gorakṣaśataka, ed. Swami Kuvalayananda and S. A. Shukla (Lonavla: Kaivalyadhama); GRETIL e-text

### 1-2 <a id="tea-goraksasataka-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Gorakṣa will speak the Hundred for release from the noose of existence: it gives men knowledge of the self and is the key to the door of discrimination; it is the ladder to liberation and the cheating of time, by which the mind turned back from delusion is fixed on the supreme self.

### 5-7 <a id="tea-goraksasataka-5-7"></a>

### 38-39 <a id="tea-goraksasataka-38-39"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Because of the fear of time (death), Brahmā, yogīs and sages devote themselves to breath-control; therefore one should bind the breath. When the breath moves all moves; when it is still all is still, and the yogī becomes like a post — therefore one should restrain the breath.

> yataḥ kālabhayāt brahmā prāṇāyāmaparāyaṇaḥ / yogino munayaś caiva tataḥ prāṇaṃ nibandhayet cale vāte calaṃ sarvaṃ niścale niścalaṃ bhavet / yogī sthāṇutvam āpnoti tato vāyuṃ nibandhayet

### 57-59 <a id="tea-goraksasataka-57-59"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

At the navel dwells the one sun, of burning nature; at the root of the palate the moon, made of nectar.
