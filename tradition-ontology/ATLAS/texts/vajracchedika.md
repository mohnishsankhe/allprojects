# Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā

`src:vajracchedika` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Diamond Sūtra, Triśatikā (the 300-line), Jingang banruo boluomi jing 金剛般若波羅蜜經 (T235, Kumārajīva), Nengduan jingang (T237-T239, Bodhiruci, Paramārtha, Xuanzang, Yijing versions), Toh 16
**Original title:** वज्रच्छेदिका प्रज्ञापारमिता
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** ascetic

**Availability:** digitized-original

The Diamond Sūtra: in dialogue with Subhūti the Buddha teaches that the bodhisattva leads all beings to nirvāṇa while no being is led, gives without being supported by any thing, sees the Tathāgata in no-marks, treats the dharma like a raft, and should produce a mind supported nowhere. Its dialectic 'what is taught as A is not-A; therefore it is called A' runs throughout; it ends with the verse on conditioned things as stars, a fault of vision, a lamp, an illusion, dew, a bubble, a dream, lightning and a cloud.

### 3 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The bodhisattva should resolve: 'however many beings there are — egg-born, womb-born, moisture-born, spontaneously born, with form or without, with perception or without — all must be led by me to nirvāṇa without remainder'; yet though countless beings are so led, no being at all is led to nirvāṇa, for if the notion of a being, a self, a soul or a person occurs in a bodhisattva, he is not to be called a bodhisattva.

> te mayā sarve anupadhiśeṣe nirvāṇadhātau parinirvāpayitavyāḥ | evam aparimāṇāṃś ca satvān parinirvāpayitavyāḥ na ca kaścit satvaḥ parinirvāpito bhavati

### 4 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The bodhisattva should give a gift without being supported by any thing — not by form, sounds, smells, tastes, touchables or dharmas — so as not to rest on the notion of a sign; the merit of such unsupported giving is as immeasurable as space in the ten directions.

> na vastupratiṣṭhitena dānaṃ dātavyam | na kvacitpratiṣṭhitena dānaṃ dātavyam

### 5 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Tathāgata is not to be seen by the possession of marks, for the possession of marks he taught is no possession of marks; as far as there are marks there is falsehood, as far as there are no marks there is no falsehood; thus the Tathāgata is to be seen from no-marks as marks.

> yāvat subhūte lakṣaṇaṃ tāvan mṛṣā | yāvad alakṣaṇaṃ tāvad amṛṣā | iti hi lakṣaṇālakṣaṇataḥ tathāgato draṣṭavyaḥ

### 6 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Even in the last five hundred years there will be bodhisattvas of virtue and wisdom who find faith in these words, having planted roots under countless buddhas; they do not seize on a dharma or a non-dharma. Hence the saying that the dharma is like a raft: even dharmas must be abandoned, still more non-dharmas.

> 知我說法，如筏喻者，法尚應捨，何況非法。

### 7 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There is no dharma fully known as unsurpassed perfect awakening and no dharma the Tathāgata has taught: what he taught cannot be grasped or spoken, is neither dharma nor non-dharma, because the noble persons are distinguished by the unconditioned.

> agrāhyaḥ so 'nabhilapyaḥ | na sa dharmo nādharmaḥ | tat kasya hetoḥ | asaṃskṛtaprabhāvitā hy āryapudgalāḥ

### 8 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One who takes up even a four-line verse of this discourse and teaches it to others produces more merit than one who fills the trichiliocosm with the seven jewels as a gift, for the buddhas' awakening issues from it; yet 'the dharmas special to a buddha' are, as taught by the Tathāgata, not buddha-dharmas — that is why they are so called.

> 一切諸佛及諸佛阿耨多羅三藐三菩提法，皆從此經出。須菩提！所謂佛法者，即非佛法。

### 9 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The stream-enterer does not think 'I have attained the fruit of stream-entry', for he has entered nothing — neither form nor sound nor any object; likewise the once-returner, non-returner and arhat. If an arhat thought 'I am an arhat' he would seize on a self; Subhūti, foremost of those who dwell in peace, has no such thought.

### 10 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Tathāgata received no dharma from the buddha Dīpaṅkara; the 'harmonious arrays of buddha-fields' a bodhisattva speaks of creating are no arrays; therefore the bodhisattva should produce an unsupported thought, not supported by form, sound, smell, taste, touch or dharma, a thought supported nowhere.

> tasmāt tarhi subhūte bodhisatvena evaṃ cittam utpādayitavyaṃ apratiṣṭhitaṃ

### 13 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The sūtra is named 'the perfection of wisdom'; yet the perfection of wisdom taught by the Tathāgata is no perfection; the dust of the world-system is no dust, the world-system no system, the thirty-two marks of a great man no marks — therefore they are so called.

> yaiva subhūte prajñāpāramitā tathāgatena bhāṣitā | saivāpāramitā

### 14 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Subhūti weeps at the depth of the teaching: those who will have a true notion when it is taught are most wonderful — yet this true notion is no notion; in them the notion of self, being, soul or person will not occur, for buddhas are free of all notions; most wonderful are those who hear it without trembling.

> sarvasañjñāpagatā hi buddhā bhagavantaḥ

### 14 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-14-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The perfection of patience is no perfection: when the king of Kaliṅga cut the flesh from his limbs, the Buddha had no notion of self, being, soul or person — had he had one, he would have had a notion of ill-will; for five hundred births as the sage 'Preacher of Patience' he had none. The bodhisattva should raise the thought of awakening having given up all notions.

### 14 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-14-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Tathāgata speaks what is real, true, as it is, not otherwise; in the dharma he awakened to there is neither truth nor falsehood. ### 15 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This discourse is taught for those set out in the supreme and best vehicle; it cannot be heard by those of inferior resolve holding views of self, being, soul or person. The place where it is taught becomes a shrine (caitya) to be honored and circumambulated by gods, humans and asuras.

Those who take up this sūtra and are despised for it thereby exhaust, in this life, the evil deeds of former lives that would have led to bad destinies, and will attain awakening; the full merit of keeping it in the last time, if told, would drive people mad.

### 17 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There is no dharma called 'one set out in the bodhisattva-vehicle'; Dīpaṅkara predicted Śākyamuni's buddhahood precisely because there was no dharma by which he awakened; 'Tathāgata' is a synonym of true suchness; all dharmas are buddha-dharmas because all dharmas are no-dharmas; the bodhisattva who is intent on 'dharmas are without self' is called a bodhisattva.

### 18 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Tathāgata has the flesh eye, the divine eye, the wisdom eye, the dharma eye and the buddha eye; he knows the many streams of thought of all beings, but those streams are no streams: past thought cannot be got at, future thought cannot be got at, present thought cannot be got at.

> 過去心不可得，現在心不可得，未來心不可得。

### 23 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-23"></a>

That dharma is the same (sama); nothing in it is high or low; it is awakening through the absence of self, being, soul and person, fully known through all wholesome dharmas — which are no-dharmas.

### 25 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-25"></a>

The Tathāgata does not think 'I have set beings free': there is no being set free by the Tathāgata; were there one, he would seize a self. 'Self' was taught as no self, though foolish common people seize upon it; 'foolish common people' are taught as no people.

### 26 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-26"></a>

Those who saw me by my form, who followed me by my voice, engaged in wrong efforts; they will not see me. The Buddha is to be seen from the dharma, for the guides have the dharma for their body; and the nature of dharma (dharmatā) is not to be discerned, nor can it be discerned.

### 32 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-32"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One who takes up, teaches and explains even one four-line verse of this discourse gains more merit than one who fills immeasurable worlds with jewels. How should he explain it? So as not to reveal (yathā na prakāśayet). As stars, a fault of vision, a lamp, a mock show, dew, a bubble, a dream, lightning and a cloud — so should one see the conditioned.

> tārakā timiraṃ dīpo māyāvaśyāya budbudaḥ supinaṃ vidyud abhraṃ ca evaṃ draṣṭavya saṃskṛtaṃ

### 32 <a id="tea-vajracchedika-32-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Kumārajīva's closing verse: all conditioned dharmas are like a dream, an illusion, a bubble, a shadow, like dew and like lightning — thus should they be contemplated.

> 一切有為法，如夢、幻、泡、影，如露亦如電，應作如是觀。
