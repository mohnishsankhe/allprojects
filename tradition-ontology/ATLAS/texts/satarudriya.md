# Śatarudrīya (Śrī Rudram, Rudrādhyāya)

**Language:** Sanskrit

**Lineages:** [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md), [Taittirīya śākhā (Black Yajurveda)](../lineages/sakha-taittiriya.md), [Mādhyandina śākhā (White Yajurveda)](../lineages/sakha-madhyandina.md)

**Location in parent:** TS 4.5.1–11 (Taittirīya); = VS 16.1–66 (Mādhyandina)
**Availability:** digitized-original

The litany of homage to Rudra in his hundreds of forms: after asking Rudra to turn his wrath, bow and arrows away and to show his auspicious, non-terrible body (śivā tanūḥ aghorā), it pays homage (namaḥ) to Rudra as lord of paths, forests, hosts and animals, as present in thieves and craftsmen, in the small and the great, in waters, clouds and grass, and ends by placing the countless Rudras on earth far away. Its eighth section contains 'namaḥ śivāya ca śivatarāya ca', the source the Śaiva traditions give for the five-syllable formula.

_Notes: Used in the agnicayana to pacify Rudra; recited in the tradition as Rudra-japa (Ekādaśa-, Laghu-, Mahā-, Atirudra) and in Śaiva worship. The Jābāla Upaniṣad names śatarudrīya recitation as a means to immortality (reference only)._
