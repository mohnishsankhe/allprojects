Rājaśekhara's 'inquiry into poetry', of which only the first of eighteen planned books survives: the myth of the Poetry-Man (kāvyapuruṣa) born of Sarasvatī, the kinds of poetic genius (pratibhā) — creative (kārayitrī) and appreciative (bhāvayitrī) — and the poet's training and daily routine.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 3 <a id="tea-kavyamimamsa-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Poetry-Man (kāvyapuruṣa) was born as Sarasvatī's son: word and meaning his body, Sanskrit his mouth, rasa his self, metres his hair, figures his ornaments; he is wedded to the Poetics-Bride (sāhityavidyā).

### 4 <a id="tea-kavyamimamsa-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Poetic genius (pratibhā) is twofold: creative (kārayitrī), belonging to the poet, and appreciative (bhāvayitrī), belonging to the connoisseur; creative genius is innate, acquired by training, or given by instruction (mantra, deity).
