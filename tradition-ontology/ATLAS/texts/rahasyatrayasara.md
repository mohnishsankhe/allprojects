Deśika's Maṇipravāḷa summa of the Śrīvaiṣṇava way of surrender, in 32 chapters (adhikāras): the five things to be known, the three realities, the supreme deity, the qualified, prapatti with its limbs as an independent means, conduct after surrender, offences and their removal, departure and the path of light, the three secret mantras, and the duties of teacher and disciple.

### Adhikārivibhāgādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-adhikari-vibhaga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Prapatti is for those who, lacking the knowledge, ability or patience for bhakti-yoga, or being barred from it by birth, have no other means (ākiñcanya) and seek no other refuge (ananyagatitva); it is open to all, of every class.

### Aparādhaparihārādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-aparadha-parihara"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Offences committed unknowingly after surrender do not cling; for deliberate offences the surrendered must make expiation or repeat surrender as expiation, else the Lord corrects him by light punishment in this life, without cancelling liberation.

### Caramaślokādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-caramasloka"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The carama-śloka (Gītā 18.66): the Lord bids the one unable to follow other means to give them up and take him alone as the means through surrender; he will free him from all sins; he should not grieve. The surrender it enjoins is itself the means that is to be performed.

### Dvayādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-dvaya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The dvaya: its first half is the act of surrender — 'I take refuge at the feet of Nārāyaṇa, who is with Śrī'; its second half is the prayer for the goal — 'to Nārāyaṇa with Śrī, obeisance', i.e. service to the divine couple free of self-interest.

### Kṛtakṛtyādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-krtakrtya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Once prapatti is done the surrendered has done all that is to be done for liberation (kṛtakṛtya); he is free of anxiety about his salvation and lives in service while the body lasts.

### Mūlamantrādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-mula-mantra"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The tirumantra (oṃ namo nārāyaṇāya): the praṇava declares that the soul belongs to the Lord alone; 'namaḥ' that it does not belong to itself; 'nārāyaṇāya' asks for service to Nārāyaṇa, the abode of all souls and the one in whom all abide.

### Niryāṇādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-niryana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

At death the Lord himself leads the surrendered soul out through the channel of the head; the surrendered need not achieve a last remembrance, for the Lord remembers him; the distressed surrenderer (ārta) is freed at the end of this body, the patient one (dṛpta) when his begun karma is spent.

### Paradevatāpāramārthyādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-paradevata-paramarthya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The supreme deity is Nārāyaṇa together with Śrī: the two are inseparable, and the divine couple (mithuna) is the object of worship, the refuge and the goal; Brahmā, Rudra and the other gods are souls in his body.

### Parikaravibhāgādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-parikara-vibhaga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Prapatti has five limbs — the resolve to do what pleases the Lord (ānukūlya-saṅkalpa), avoidance of what displeases him (prātikūlya-varjana), great faith that he will protect (mahāviśvāsa), choosing him as protector (goptṛtva-varaṇa) and helpless humility (kārpaṇya) — with the principal act (aṅgin), laying down the burden of one's protection at his feet (ātma-nikṣepa, bharanyāsa).

### Sāṅgaprapadanādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-sanga-prapadana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Surrender with its limbs may be performed by oneself (svaniṣṭha), by repeating the words after the teacher (uktiniṣṭha), or by the teacher on one's behalf (ācāryaniṣṭha); in each case it is done once, with the sense that even this act is done by the Lord's grace and for his sake.

### Upāyavibhāgādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-upaya-vibhaga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Prapatti is an independent means (svatantra-upāya) to liberation, not a limb of bhakti; performed once, it achieves what bhakti-yoga achieves; the Lord, already accomplished (siddhopāya), is the one who grants, and the surrender is the means that is performed (sādhyopāya) and the occasion (vyāja) of his grace.

### Uttarakṛtyādhikāra <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-uttara-krtya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

After surrender, the obligatory and occasional duties of scripture must still be performed — as the Lord's command (ājñā) and as service, not as means; omitting them offends the Lord; in addition there are services done by his permission (anujñā).

### Ācāryakṛtya and Śiṣyakṛtya <a id="tea-rahasyatrayasara-acarya-sisya-krtya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

A true teacher (sadācārya) teaches out of compassion alone, without seeking gain, the doctrine received through the unbroken line; the disciple tests and approaches him with humility, serves him, keeps the teaching and hands it on only to the fit.
