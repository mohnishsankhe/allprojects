**Alternate titles:** Bhārata, Jaya, Śatasāhasrī Saṃhitā, Kārṣṇa Veda
**Original title:** महाभारत
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md)
**Genre:** itihāsa
**Authors:** 
  - [Vyāsa (Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana)](../teachers/vyasa.md) — role: author; attribution: traditional
  - [Vaiśampāyana](../teachers/vaisampayana.md) — role: compiler; attribution: traditional

**Attribution:** tradition: Composed by Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana Vyāsa, taught to his disciples, recited by Vaiśampāyana at Janamejaya's snake sacrifice, and retold by Ugraśravas Sauti to the sages of Naimiṣa forest at Śaunaka's twelve-year sacrifice.; scholarly: A composite work of many hands grown over several centuries around a narrative core; the frame narratives and the didactic books (esp. Śānti and Anuśāsana) are generally held to be later layers.; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: composed by Vyāsa at the close of the Dvāpara yuga, before the Kali yuga (traditionally reckoned from 3102 BCE); Scholarly account: composed and expanded in stages, c. 4th c. BCE – 4th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 18 books (parvans), traditionally 100 sub-books (upaparvans) as listed in the Parvasaṃgraha (1.2); the Harivaṃśa is its supplement (khila). The tradition speaks of 100,000 verses (śatasāhasrī saṃhitā); 1.1.61 says Vyāsa made a 24,000-verse Bhārata without the sub-stories (upākhyānas); 1.1.50 records three starting points used by reciters (Manu, Āstīka, Uparicara). The critical text as digitized locally has 2,097 chapters and about 73,800 verse units.
**Availability:** digitized-original

The great epic of the Bhārata war between the Pāṇḍavas and the Kauravas, calling itself a 'fifth Veda' (kārṣṇa veda) and a treatise on dharma, artha, kāma and mokṣa; besides the narrative it contains extended teaching sections (the Bhagavad Gītā, the Rājadharma, Āpaddharma and Mokṣadharma of the Śāntiparvan, the Anugītā, the Sanatsujātīya, Vidura's counsel, the Yakṣa's questions, the Nārāyaṇīya). It says of itself that what is found here on the four aims of life is found elsewhere, and what is not here is nowhere (1.56.33).
