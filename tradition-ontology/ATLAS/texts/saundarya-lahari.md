# Saundaryalaharī

`src:saundarya-lahari` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Ānandalaharī (verses 1-41), Saundaryalaharī-stotra
**Original title:** सौन्दर्यलहरी
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Śrīvidyā](../lineages/srividya.md), [Samaya school of Śrīvidyā (Samayācāra)](../lineages/samaya-srividya.md), [Śākta traditions](../lineages/sakta.md), [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
**Genre:** stotra
**Authors:** 
  - [Śaṅkara (Ādi Śaṅkarācārya)](../teachers/sankara.md) — role: author; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: Śaṅkara Bhagavatpāda. In the common legend Śaṅkara received a hymn of a hundred verses at Kailāsa; Nandin (in other versions Gaṇeśa) snatched it, leaving the first forty-one, and Śaṅkara composed the rest.; scholarly: Authorship by the Advaita teacher of the Brahma Sūtra commentary is doubted; the text is well established by the time of its 16th-c. commentators.; confidence: disputed
**Structure:** 100 verses in śikhariṇī metre (some editions 103); 1-41 doctrinal (Ānandalaharī), 42-100 descriptive (from crown to foot)
**Availability:** digitized-original

A hymn of one hundred verses to the Goddess. The first part (verses 1-41, called the Ānandalaharī) teaches the doctrine: Śiva can act only when joined to Śakti, the Goddess's ascent through the six cakras and return as kuṇḍalinī, the structure of the Śrīcakra, the coded Śrīvidyā mantra, the Goddess as Śiva's body, and her presence in each cakra; the second part describes her beauty from crown to foot and ends by offering the hymn made of her own words back to her.
