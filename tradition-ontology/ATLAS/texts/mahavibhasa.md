The great commentary on the Jñānaprasthāna and the authoritative text of the Kashmiri Vaibhāṣikas. It records the four masters' theories of existence in the three times, refutes the Dārṣṭāntikas, Vibhajyavādins and others, and gives the hostile Sarvāstivāda account of Mahādeva and the first schism.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### fasc.27 <a id="tea-mahavibhasa-fasc-27"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Vibhāṣā reports and rejects the view of the Vibhajyavādins that the mind is by nature pure and is defiled by adventitious defilements; for the Sarvāstivāda a defiled mind is not the same as a pure one.

### fasc.77 <a id="tea-mahavibhasa-fasc-77"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Vibhāṣā sets out the four great masters' explanations of how dharmas existing in all three times are distinguished — by mode of being (Dharmatrāta), characteristic (Ghoṣaka), state (Vasumitra) and relation (Buddhadeva) — and adopts the explanation by state, defined by activity.

### fasc.99 <a id="tea-mahavibhasa-fasc-99"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Vibhāṣā tells the story of Mahādeva — a merchant's son of Mathurā who killed his father, an arhat and his mother, became a monk at the Kukkuṭārāma, claimed arhatship and proclaimed the five points in a verse; the assembly divided, the king sided with the majority, and the arhats went to Kashmir.
