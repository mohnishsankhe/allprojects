### 2.2.37 <a id="tea-bhamati-2-2-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Māheśvaras are four - Śaivas, Pāśupatas, Kāruṇika-siddhāntins and Kāpālikas - all following doctrines taught by Maheśvara. For them the cause is the Lord; the effect is mahat and the rest derived from primal matter; yoga is meditation and fixation on Oṃ and the like; the rule runs from bathing thrice daily to the hidden conduct; the end of suffering is release, the loosing of the paśus' bond. Their reasoning: a conscious agent like a potter is only the efficient cause of a pot, so the Lord is only the efficient cause of the world.

> māheśvarāś catvāraḥ - śaivāḥ pāśupatāḥ kāruṇikasiddhāntinaḥ kāpālikāś ceti

### avaccheda <a id="tea-bhamati-avaccheda"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The jīva is Brahman delimited (avacchinna) by the inner organ, as space is delimited by a pot; Brahman itself is untouched by the delimitation.

### locus <a id="tea-bhamati-locus"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ignorance resides in the individual selves with Brahman as its object; the jīva and ignorance presuppose each other beginninglessly, like seed and sprout; there are as many ignorances as jīvas.

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind, dispute_

### manas <a id="tea-bhamati-manas"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The knowledge produced by the Upaniṣadic sentence is mediate; the mind, perfected by sustained contemplation and aided by hearing and reflection, is the instrument of direct realization, as scripture says 'it is to be seen by the mind alone'.

### mangala <a id="tea-bhamati-mangala"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Salutation to Brahman — immeasurable bliss and knowledge, deathless — which, accompanied by two indescribable ignorances, is the source whose apparent transformations are space, air, fire, water and earth, and from which this whole moving and unmoving world has come.

> anirvācyāvidyādvitayasacivasya prabhavato vivartā yasyaite viyadanilatejo'bavanayaḥ / yataś cābhūd viśvaṃ caram acaram uccāvacam idaṃ namāmas tad brahmāparimitasukhajñānam amṛtam

### vividisa <a id="tea-bhamati-vividisa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Sacrifice, gifts and austerity (BĀU 4.4.22) produce the desire to know (vividiṣā) by purifying the mind; they do not produce knowledge itself.
