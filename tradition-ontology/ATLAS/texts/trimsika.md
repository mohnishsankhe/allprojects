# Triṃśikā (Triṃśikāvijñaptikārikā)

`src:trimsika` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Thirty Verses, sum cu pa, Weishi sanshi lun song 唯識三十論頌 (T1586, Xuanzang)
**Original title:** त्रिंशिका
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** ascetic
**Lineages:** [Yogācāra](../lineages/yogacara.md)
**Genre:** kārikā
**Authors:** 
  - [Vasubandhu](../teachers/vasubandhu.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 4th–5th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 30 verses: 1–16 the threefold transformation of consciousness (store, mind, object-apprehension, with the mental factors); 17–19 cognition-only and the cause of rebirth; 20–25 the three natures and three naturelessnesses; 26–30 the path to the transformation of the basis
**Availability:** digitized-original

Vasubandhu's compact statement of the whole Yogācāra system: all talk of self and dharmas refers to the transformation of consciousness in three forms, so everything is cognition-only; the three natures and naturelessnesses; and the path on which, when cognition no longer apprehends an object, the yogin abides in cognition-only and attains the supramundane, non-apprehending wisdom and the transformation of the basis.

## Teachings (19: skeleton 19)

### 1 <a id="tea-trimsika-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Whatever various figurative talk of 'self' and 'dharmas' goes on, it goes on with reference to the transformation of consciousness; and that transformation is of three kinds.

> ātmadharmopacāro hi vividho yaḥ pravartate / vijñānapariṇāme 'sau pariṇāmaḥ sa ca tridhā

### 2 <a id="tea-trimsika-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The three transformations are the maturation, the one called 'mind' (manana) and the cognition of objects. Of these, the consciousness called ālaya is the maturation and holds all the seeds.

> vipāko mananākhyaśca vijñaptirviṣayasya ca / tatrālayākhyaṃ vijñānaṃ vipākaḥ sarvabījakam

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 3-4 <a id="tea-trimsika-3-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The store-consciousness has an unperceived appropriation and an unperceived cognition of a 'place' (the receptacle world); it is always accompanied by contact, attention, feeling, perception and volition; its feeling is neutral; it is unobscured and morally undetermined, as are its contact and the rest; and it flows on like a torrent.

> asaṃviditakopādisthānavijñaptikaṃ ca tat / sadā sparśamanaskāravitsañjñācetanānvitam // upekṣā vedanā tatrānivṛtāvyākṛtaṃ ca tat / tathā sparśādayastacca vartate srotasaughavat

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 5 <a id="tea-trimsika-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The store-consciousness ceases at arhatship. Dependent on it and taking it as its object there operates the consciousness called 'mind' (manas), whose nature is thinking of self.

> tasya vyāvṛtirarhatve tadāśritya pravartate / tadālambaṃ manonāma vijñānaṃ mananātmakam

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind, karma-liberation_

### 6-7 <a id="tea-trimsika-6-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

That mind is always accompanied by four afflictions, obscured but morally undetermined — self-view, self-delusion, self-conceit and self-love — and by contact and the other factors of its sphere of birth. It does not exist in the arhat, nor in the attainment of cessation, nor on the supramundane path.

> kleśaiścaturbhiḥ sahitaṃ nivṛtāvyākṛtaiḥ sadā / ātmaduṣṭyātmamohātmamānātmasnehasañjñitaiḥ

### 8-9 <a id="tea-trimsika-8-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The third transformation is the apprehension of the six kinds of object; it is wholesome, unwholesome or neither, and is associated with the universal, the particular and the wholesome mental factors, with the afflictions and secondary afflictions, and with the three feelings.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 10-14 <a id="tea-trimsika-10-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The mental factors are listed: the five universal (contact etc.); the five particular (desire-to-act, resolve, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom); eleven wholesome (faith, shame, embarrassment, the three roots non-greed etc., vigour, pliancy, heedfulness, equanimity, non-harming); the six afflictions (greed, aversion, delusion, conceit, views, doubt); the secondary afflictions (anger, enmity, concealment, spite, envy, avarice, deceit, dishonesty, pride, harmfulness, shamelessness, lack of embarrassment, torpor, agitation, lack of faith, laziness, heedlessness, forgetfulness, distraction, non-awareness); and four that may go either way (regret, sleep, initial and sustained thought).

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: meditation, knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 15-16 <a id="tea-trimsika-15-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The five sense-consciousnesses arise in the root consciousness according to their conditions, together or not, like waves on water. Mind-consciousness arises always, except in the state of the non-perceiving gods, in the two mindless attainments, in deep sleep and in fainting.

> pañcānāṃ mūlavijñāne yathāpratyayamudbhavaḥ / vijñānānāṃ saha na vā taraṅgāṇāṃ yathā jale

### 17 <a id="tea-trimsika-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This transformation of consciousness is imagination (vikalpa); what is imagined by it does not exist; therefore all this is cognition-only.

> vijñānapariṇāmo 'yaṃ vikalpo yadvikalpyate / tena tannāsti tenedaṃ sarvaṃ vijñaptimātrakam

### 18 <a id="tea-trimsika-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Consciousness holding all seeds transforms in this way and that, by their mutual influence, and from that such-and-such imagination arises.

> sarvabījaṃ hi vijñānaṃ pariṇāmastathā tathā / yātyanyonyavaśād yena vikalpaḥ sa sa jāyate

### 19 <a id="tea-trimsika-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The imprints of action, together with the imprints of the twofold grasping, produce another maturation when the former maturation is exhausted.

> karmaṇo vāsanā grāhadvayavāsanayā saha / kṣīṇe pūrvavipāke 'nyadvipākaṃ janayanti tat

### 20 <a id="tea-trimsika-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Whatever thing is imagined by whatever imagination is just the imagined nature (parikalpita-svabhāva); it does not exist.

> yena yena vikalpena yadyad vastu vikalpyate / parikalpita evāsau svabhāvo na sa vidyate

### 21 <a id="tea-trimsika-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The dependent nature is imagination arising from conditions; the perfected nature is its being always devoid of the former (the imagined).

> paratantrasvabhāvastu vikalpaḥ pratyayodbhavaḥ / niṣpannastasya pūrveṇa sadā rahitatā tu yā

### 22 <a id="tea-trimsika-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Therefore the perfected is neither other nor not other than the dependent — to be spoken of like impermanence and the like; when the perfected is not seen, the dependent is not (truly) seen.

> ata eva sa naivānyo nānanyaḥ paratantrataḥ / anityatādivad vācyo nādṛṣṭe 'smin sa dṛśyate

### 23-24 <a id="tea-trimsika-23-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

With the threefold nature in view, the threefold naturelessness was taught, and so the naturelessness of all dharmas: the first is natureless by its very characteristic; the second because it does not exist by itself; the third is naturelessness itself.

> trividhasya svabhāvasya trividhāṃ niḥsvabhāvatām / sandhāya sarvadharmāṇāṃ deśitā niḥsvabhāvatā

### 25 <a id="tea-trimsika-25"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

That (perfected nature) is the ultimate of dharmas; it is also suchness, because it is always thus; it is itself cognition-only-ness.

> dharmāṇāṃ paramārthaśca sa yatastathatāpi saḥ / sarvakālaṃ tathābhāvāt saiva vijñaptimātratā

### 26-27 <a id="tea-trimsika-26-27"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

As long as consciousness does not abide in cognition-only, the latent tendency of the twofold grasping does not cease. Even one who thinks 'all this is cognition-only' does not abide in it, because by that apprehension he places something before himself.

> yāvadvijñaptimātratve vijñānaṃ nāvatiṣṭhati / grāhadvayasyānuśayastāvanna vinivartate

### 28 <a id="tea-trimsika-28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When consciousness no longer apprehends any object, then it abides in cognition-only, because where there is nothing grasped there is no grasping.

> yadālambanaṃ vijñānaṃ naivopalabhate tadā / sthitaṃ vijñānamātratve grāhyābhāve tadagrahāt

### 29-30 <a id="tea-trimsika-29-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

That is without mind (acitta), non-apprehension; that is supramundane knowledge; it is the transformation of the basis through the abandonment of the twofold badness (dauṣṭhulya). That is the pure element, inconceivable, wholesome, constant, blissful; it is the liberation-body; it is called the dharma-body of the great sage.

> acitto 'nupalambho 'sau jñānaṃ lokottaraṃ ca tat / āśrayasya parāvṛttirdvidhā dauṣṭhulyahānitaḥ // sa evānasravo dhāturacintyaḥ kuśalo dhruvaḥ / sukho vimuktikāyo 'sau dharmākhyo 'yaṃ mahāmuneḥ
