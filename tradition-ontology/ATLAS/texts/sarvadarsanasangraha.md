## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 6 <a id="tea-sarvadarsanasangraha-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The end of suffering is twofold: impersonal - the absolute destruction of all sufferings - and personal - lordship consisting in the powers of seeing and acting. The power of seeing is fivefold (seeing, hearing, thinking, knowing all treatises, omniscience); the power of acting threefold (swiftness of mind, taking forms at will, acting without organs).

### 6 <a id="tea-sarvadarsanasangraha-6-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The dependent effect is threefold: vidyā (a quality of the paśu, cognitive or not), kalā (insentient, dependent on the sentient: ten 'effects' - five elements and five qualities - and thirteen 'instruments' - ten senses and the inner organs buddhi, ahaṃkāra and manas), and paśu (with or without body and organs). Yoga is the link of self and Lord through the mind, either as action (muttering, meditation) or as cessation of action. The rule is primary (the observance: vows - ash-bathing, lying in ash, offering, muttering, circumambulation - and the six 'doors': krāthana, spandana, mandana, śṛṅgāraṇa, avitatkaraṇa, avitadbhāṣaṇa) or secondary (re-bathing etc.).

### 6 <a id="tea-sarvadarsanasangraha-6-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Pāśupatas claim five distinctions: elsewhere the end of suffering is only cessation, here it is also supreme lordship; elsewhere the effect comes to be, here soul and the rest are eternal; elsewhere the cause depends on something, here the Lord alone is independent; elsewhere yoga yields isolation, here supreme lordship and the end of suffering; elsewhere [heaven etc.] brings return, here the fruit is non-return, proximity and the like.

### 6 <a id="tea-sarvadarsanasangraha-6-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Objection: if the Lord were an independent cause, karma would be fruitless and all effects would arise at once. Reply: karma favoured by the Lord's will bears fruit, and without it may be fruitless, as with Yayāti's; beings act because they depend on his will; he, whose desires are fulfilled, needs nothing that karma produces; and effects do not arise all at once, because his unimpeded power of action follows his will. As the knowers of the tradition say: 'Since he acts by his own will, independent of karma and the rest, he is called in scripture the cause of all causes.'

> karmādinirapekṣas tu svecchācārī yato hy ayam | tataḥ kāraṇataḥ śāstre sarvakāraṇakāraṇam

### 6 <a id="tea-sarvadarsanasangraha-6-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Liberation is not had from mere knowledge that Mahādeva is lord of the gods, nor can bound souls see him directly; right ascertainment of reality comes only from the Pāśupata scripture: 'In mere knowledge scripture suffices, but direct sight is hard to get; from nothing but the Pañcārtha comes true ascertainment of reality.'
