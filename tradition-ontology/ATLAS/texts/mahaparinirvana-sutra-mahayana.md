The Buddha's final Mahāyāna teaching on the day of his parinirvāṇa: the Tathāgata is permanent and unchanging; his parinirvāṇa is a skillful display; nirvāṇa has permanence, bliss, self and purity; 'self' means the tathāgatagarbha; all sentient beings have buddha-nature, obscured by defilements; buddha-nature is the emptiness of the highest meaning, wisdom, the middle way. The early part (Faxian's 6-fascicle version) excludes the icchantika; the later part (Dharmakṣema's 40-fascicle version, Lion's Roar and Kāśyapa chapters) affirms that icchantikas too have buddha-nature. It forbids eating meat.

## Teachings (11: skeleton 11)

### 2 <a id="tea-mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The monks claim to cultivate the perceptions of impermanence, suffering, no-self and impurity; the Buddha says these are inverted where the Tathāgata is concerned: 'no-self' is saṃsāra, 'self' is the Tathāgata; impermanence is the śrāvakas, permanence the Tathāgata's dharmakāya; suffering is the tīrthikas, bliss is nirvāṇa; impurity is conditioned things, purity the true dharma. There are worldly and supramundane permanence, bliss, self and purity.

> 無我者名為生死，我者名為如來

### 7 <a id="tea-mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'Self' means the tathāgatagarbha: all sentient beings have buddha-nature, and this is the meaning of self; from the beginning it is covered by countless defilements, so beings do not see it — like a poor woman who does not know that a store of true gold lies within her house, until a skilled man digs it out for her.

> 我者即是如來藏義。一切眾生悉有佛性，即是我義。

### 9 <a id="tea-mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana-9"></a>

All beings have buddha-nature; the icchantika too has buddha-nature, but it is bound by countless sins, like a silkworm in its cocoon, and he cannot produce the cause of awakening.

### 26 <a id="tea-mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Among icchantikas there is no buddha-nature (in the sense of wholesome qualities): buddha-nature is not the practice of good means, not vigor, mindfulness or concentration, which the icchantika lacks; yet buddha-nature is permanent, neither good nor bad; an icchantika who believes he has buddha-nature is no longer an icchantika.

> 一闡提中無有佛性

### 27 <a id="tea-mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana-27"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Buddha-nature is the emptiness of the highest meaning (paramārtha-śūnyatā), and that emptiness is wisdom; what is called empty sees both the empty and the non-empty; the wise see emptiness and non-emptiness, permanence and impermanence; buddha-nature is the middle way.

> 佛性者名第一義空，第一義空名為智慧。

### 27 <a id="tea-mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana-27-2"></a>

All sentient beings have buddha-nature — even the icchantikas; because they will surely attain unsurpassed awakening in the future, it is said that all beings have buddha-nature; defilements cover it so they do not see it.

> 一切眾生悉有佛性，乃至一闡提等亦有佛性。

### 32 <a id="tea-mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana-32"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The blind men and the elephant: a king has an elephant shown to blind men, who, touching tusk, ear, head, trunk, leg, back, tail, describe it as a radish, a winnowing basket, a stone, a pestle, a mortar, a bed, a rope; so beings describe buddha-nature by its parts — neither telling its whole body, nor not telling it.

### 36 <a id="tea-mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana-36"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Four alternatives: some buddha-nature the icchantika has and the one with good roots lacks; some the one with good roots has and the icchantika lacks; some both have; some neither has — one who understands these four should not insist that the icchantika definitely has, or definitely lacks, buddha-nature.

### 39 <a id="tea-mahaparinirvana-sutra-mahayana-39"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the Kauṇḍinya chapter the Buddha debates tīrthikas (Vasiṣṭha, Śreṇika and others) who object that Gautama now teaches permanence, bliss, self and purity; he answers that nirvāṇa's permanence, bliss, self and purity come from its being attained by the revealing cause, not the producing cause.
