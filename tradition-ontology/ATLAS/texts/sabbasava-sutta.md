## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 3-4 <a id="tea-sabbasava-sutta-3-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The destruction of the effluents is for one who knows and sees — who knows wise and unwise attention: by unwise attention unarisen effluents arise and arisen ones grow; by wise attention they do not arise and are abandoned. Some effluents are abandoned by seeing, some by restraint, using, enduring, avoiding, dispelling and developing.

### 5-11 <a id="tea-sabbasava-sutta-5-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Unwise attention asks: did I exist in the past? what was I? shall I exist in the future? am I? where did this being come from? — and one of six views arises: 'I have a self', 'I have no self', 'I perceive self by self' … 'this self of mine that speaks and feels … is permanent'. This is the thicket of views, the fetter of views. The noble disciple attends wisely to 'this is suffering … this is the way' and three fetters are abandoned.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: dispute, consciousness-mind_

### 12-21 <a id="tea-sabbasava-sutta-12-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Effluents abandoned by restraining the sense faculties; by wisely using robes, alms-food, lodging and medicine; by enduring cold, heat, hunger and hurtful words; by avoiding a wild elephant, bad places and bad friends; by dispelling thoughts of sensuality, ill will and cruelty; and by developing the seven factors of awakening.
