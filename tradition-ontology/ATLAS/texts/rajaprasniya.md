The second Upāṅga: the god Sūryābha's visit to Mahāvīra and his worship of Jina images in his celestial palace, and the dialogue in which Keśin, of Pārśva's line, convinces King Paesi (Pradeśin), who had tried to find the soul by experiments on criminals, that the soul is distinct from the body.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### paesi <a id="tea-rajaprasniya-paesi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

King Paesi holds that soul and body are one (tajjīva-taccharīra) and gives his proofs: his wicked grandfather, who should be in hell, never came to warn him; his pious grandmother never came from heaven; a thief shut in a sealed iron jar died without any hole appearing for the soul to leave, and worms later appeared in a sealed corpse without any hole for souls to enter; a man weighed alive and dead weighed the same; a man cut into pieces showed no soul.

### paesi/2 <a id="tea-rajaprasniya-paesi-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Kesi answers each proof with a simile — the hell-bound grandfather is held like a prisoner, the heavenly grandmother is detained by delight, sound escapes a sealed chamber without a hole, a filled and an empty skin weigh the same, and the woodcutter who chopped up the fire-sticks to find the fire found none — until the king accepts the lay vows.

### suryabha <a id="tea-rajaprasniya-suryabha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The god Sūryābha comes from his celestial palace to honour Mahāvīra and stages a divine dance; the text describes his palace, in which he worships the images of the Jinas with water, sandal, flowers, incense and lamps.
