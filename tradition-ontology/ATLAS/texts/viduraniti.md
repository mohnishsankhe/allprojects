In a sleepless night before the war, Vidura counsels Dhṛtarāṣṭra on wise and foolish conduct, the faults to be abandoned, policy, truth, forgiveness and non-violence, the golden rule, the self as a sacred river, and the fate of a man after death — his relatives turn back from the pyre and only his deeds follow him.

### 5.33.16-19 <a id="tea-viduraniti-5-33-16-19"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The marks of the wise (paṇḍita): he practises what is praiseworthy and avoids what is blamed, has faith and is not a denier; anger, joy, pride, shame, obstinacy and vanity do not draw him from his purpose; others know his deeds only when done; cold and heat, fear and love, prosperity and adversity do not obstruct his work.

### 5.33.66 <a id="tea-viduraniti-5-33-66"></a>

Six faults are to be abandoned by a person who seeks prosperity: sleep, drowsiness, fear, anger, sloth and procrastination.
