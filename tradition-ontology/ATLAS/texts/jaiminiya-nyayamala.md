Mādhava's verse digest of the topics (nyāyas, adhikaraṇas) of the Mīmāṃsā Sūtra, with his own prose expansion (vistara): each adhikaraṇa is set out through its members — topic, doubt, connection, prima facie view and conclusion. Written, by its own account, by the minister of King Bukka, after his commentary on the Parāśara Smṛti.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1.1.7-8 <a id="tea-jaiminiya-nyayamala-1-1-7-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Each topic (adhikaraṇa) has five members: the subject (viṣaya), the doubt (sandeha), the connection (saṅgati), the prima facie view (pūrvapakṣa) and the conclusion (siddhānta); the connection is threefold — with the whole śāstra, the chapter and the quarter — and can be worked out by the reader; usually one verse gives subject, doubt and prima facie view, and another the conclusion.

### 1.1.11-12 <a id="tea-jaiminiya-nyayamala-1-1-11-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dharma is expounded in (Jaimini's) work of twelve 'marks' (chapters), namely: means of knowledge, distinction, subsidiarity, prompting, sequence, eligibility, transfer in general and in particular, modification, cancellation, single performance (tantra) and incidental benefit (prasaṅga), in that order.

> dharmo dvādaśalakṣaṇyā vyutpādyastatra lakṣaṇaiḥ | pramāṇabhedaśeṣatvaprayuktikramasañjñakāḥ
