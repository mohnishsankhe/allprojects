# Tarkasaṅgraha

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Nyāya](../lineages/nyaya.md), [Vaiśeṣika](../lineages/vaisesika.md), [Navya-Nyāya](../lineages/navya-nyaya.md)
**Genre:** prakaraṇa
**Authors:** 
  - [Annambhaṭṭa](../teachers/annambhatta.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Dates:** Scholarly account: 17th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Availability:** digitized-original

Annambhaṭṭa's primer of syncretic Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika 'for the easy understanding of beginners': seven categories, nine substances, twenty-four qualities, five motions, two kinds of universal, endless particularities, one inherence and four absences; the self as the substrate of cognition (the one omniscient Lord and the many embodied selves); the four means of knowledge with the Navya definitions of pervasion, consideration, pseudo-reasons, the denotative power of words as the Lord's convention, and the conditions of verbal cognition.

## Teachings (21: skeleton 21)

### absence <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-abhava"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Prior absence is beginningless and has an end (of a product before it arises); destruction has a beginning and no end; absolute absence has a counterpositive delimited by a relation for all three times ('there is no pot on the ground'); mutual absence has a counterpositive delimited by identity ('a pot is not cloth').

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: world-fate_

### air (vāyu) <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-prana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The air moving within the body is breath (prāṇa); though one, it receives the names prāṇa, apāna and the rest from differences of adjunct.

> śarīrāntaḥsaṃcārī vāyuḥ prāṇaḥ sa caiko 'py upādhibhedāt prāṇāpānādisaṃjñā labhate

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: body-layers_

### cause <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-karana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A cause is what invariably precedes an effect; an effect is the counterpositive of a prior absence; causes are inherent (e.g. threads for cloth), non-inherent (e.g. the conjunction of threads) and efficient (e.g. the loom); the instrument (karaṇa) is the special cause.

> kāryaniyatapūrvavṛtti kāraṇam

### closing verse <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-colophon"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The learned Annambhaṭṭa composed the Tarkasaṅgraha to accomplish the instruction of beginners in the doctrines of Kaṇāda and of Nyāya.

> kāṇādanyāyamatayor bālavyutpattisiddhaye / annaṃbhaṭṭena viduṣā racitas tarkasaṃgrahaḥ

### cognition <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-buddhi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Cognition is the cause of all usage; it is memory or experience; valid experience (pramā) is the apprehension, in what has a property, of that property as its qualifier (e.g. 'this is silver' of silver); invalid experience apprehends a property as qualifier where it is absent (e.g. of mother-of-pearl).

> tadvati tatprakārako 'nubhavo yathārthaḥ

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: consciousness-mind_

### comparison <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-upamana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Comparison yields knowledge of the relation between a name and what it names, through cognition of similarity — as when one told 'a gavaya is like a cow' sees such an animal in the forest and knows 'this is what the word gavaya denotes'.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: consciousness-mind, sound-language_

terms: [upamāna](../terms/upamana.md)

### earth, water, fire, air <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-bodies-of-elements"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Each of earth, water, fire and air is eternal as atoms and non-eternal as products, and as product is threefold — body, sense faculty and object: the body of earth is that of beings like us, of water in the world of Varuṇa, of fire in the world of Āditya, of air in the world of Vāyu; the senses are smell (at the tip of the nose), taste (at the tip of the tongue), sight (at the tip of the black of the eye) and touch (spread over the whole body).

_level: unmarked · standpoint: cosmic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: body-layers, world-fate_

### enumeration of the categories <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-padartha-uddesa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Substance, quality, motion, universal, particularity, inherence and absence are the seven categories; the substances are only nine; the qualities twenty-four; the motions five; the universal is of two kinds, higher and lower; particularities, residing in eternal substances, are endless; inherence is one; absence is of four kinds — prior absence, destruction, absolute absence and mutual absence.

> dravyaguṇakarmasāmānyaviśeṣasamavāyābhāvāḥ sapta padārthāḥ

### impression <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-samskara"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Impression is of three kinds: momentum (vega), memory-impression (bhāvanā) produced by experience, the cause of memory, residing only in the self; and elasticity (sthitisthāpaka), which restores a thing to its former state.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: consciousness-mind_

### inference <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-anumana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Inferential cognition is produced by consideration (parāmarśa), the cognition of the subject as possessing (the probans) qualified by pervasion — e.g. 'this mountain has smoke pervaded by fire'; pervasion is the rule of co-presence 'wherever there is smoke there is fire'; inference is for oneself or for others, the latter stated with five members.

> yatra yatra dhūmas tatrāgnir iti sāhacaryaniyamo vyāptiḥ

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: consciousness-mind_

### invalid cognition <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-ayathartha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Invalid experience is of three kinds: doubt (e.g. 'a post or a man?'), error (e.g. 'this is silver' of mother-of-pearl) and tarka — superimposing the pervader by superimposing the pervaded ('if there were no fire, there would be no smoke').

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: consciousness-mind_

terms: [saṃśaya](../terms/samsaya.md), [viparyaya](../terms/viparyaya.md), [tarka](../terms/tarka.md)

### kinds of probans; subject <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-linga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The probans is of three kinds — with both positive and negative concomitance, with positive only (e.g. 'the pot is nameable, because knowable'), and with negative only (e.g. 'earth differs from the rest, because it has smell'); the subject is what possesses the doubted probandum, a similar instance what possesses it certainly, a dissimilar instance what certainly lacks it.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: consciousness-mind_

### mind <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-manas"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The mind is the sense faculty that is the instrument for apprehending pleasure and the rest; being restricted to each self, minds are endless in number, atomic and eternal.

> sukhādyupalabdhisādhanam indriyaṃ manaḥ

### perception <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-pratyaksa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Perception is cognition produced by the contact of sense and object; it is indeterminate ('this is something') or determinate (with a qualifier, e.g. 'this is Ḍittha'); the contact is of six kinds — conjunction, inherence in the conjoined, inherence in what inheres in the conjoined, inherence, inherence in what inheres, and the relation of qualifier and qualified (by which absence is perceived).

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: consciousness-mind_

terms: [pratyakṣa](../terms/pratyaksa.md), [nirvikalpaka](../terms/nirvikalpaka.md), [savikalpaka](../terms/savikalpaka.md), [sannikarṣa](../terms/sannikarsa.md), [viśeṣaṇatā](../terms/visesanata.md) · concepts: [Indeterminate and determinate perception](../concepts/nirvikalpaka-savikalpaka.md), [The six sense-object contacts](../concepts/six-sannikarsas.md)

### pseudo-reasons <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-hetvabhasa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The pseudo-reasons are five — inconclusive (common, uncommon, non-conclusive), contradictory, counterbalanced, unestablished (as to locus, as to itself, as to pervasion) and sublated; an adjunct (upādhi) is what pervades the probandum but does not pervade the probans (e.g. contact with wet fuel, when smoke is inferred from fire).

> savyabhicāraviruddhasatpakṣāsiddhabādhitāḥ pañca hetvābhāsāḥ

### qualities of the self <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-atma-gunas"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pleasure is what all experience as agreeable, pain as disagreeable; desire is wanting, aversion is anger, effort is volition; merit (dharma) is produced by enjoined acts, demerit by forbidden acts; these eight, beginning with cognition, are special qualities of the self alone; cognition, desire and effort are eternal in the Lord and non-eternal in the individual self.

> vihitakarmajanyo dharmaḥ

### self <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-atman"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The self is the substrate of cognition; it is of two kinds, the supreme self and the individual self; of these the Lord, omniscient, the supreme self, is one only; the individual self is distinct in each body, all-pervading and eternal.

> jñānādhikaraṇam ātmā sa dvividhaḥ paramātmā jīvātmā ca tatreśvaraḥ sarvajñaḥ paramātmaika eva jīvātmā pratiśarīraṃ bhinno vibhur nityaś ca

### universal, particularity, inherence <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-samanya-visesa-samavaya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The universal is eternal, one and present in many — highest being (sattā), lower e.g. substance-hood; particularities reside in eternal substances and distinguish them; inherence is the eternal relation of things that cannot exist apart (part and whole, quality and substance, motion and its substrate, universal and individual, particularity and eternal substance).

> nityam ekam anekānugataṃ sāmānyam

### verbal testimony <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-sabda"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Verbal testimony is the statement of a reliable person — one who speaks of things as they are; a sentence is a group of words; a word is what has denotative power (śakti), and that power is the Lord's convention: 'from this word this meaning is to be understood'; expectancy, compatibility and proximity are the causes of understanding a sentence; Vedic statements are all valid because spoken by the Lord, worldly ones only when spoken by a reliable person.

> asmāt padād ayam artho boddhavya itīśvarasaṃketaḥ śaktiḥ

### ākāśa, time, space <a id="tea-tarkasangraha-akasa-kala-dik"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ākāśa has sound as its quality and is one, all-pervading and eternal; time is the cause of usages such as 'past', and space of usages such as 'east'; each is one, all-pervading and eternal.
