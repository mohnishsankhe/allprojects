Vidyāraṇya's treatise on liberation while living in five chapters: scriptural proof and the two kinds of renunciation (of the seeker, vividiṣā; of the knower, vidvat); the destruction of latent tendencies (vāsanā-kṣaya); the dissolution of the mind (mano-nāśa); the five purposes of jīvanmukti; and the life of the paramahaṃsa. It draws heavily on the Yoga Vāsiṣṭha.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 1 <a id="tea-jivanmuktiviveka-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Renunciation is twofold: that of the seeker who desires to know (vividiṣā-sannyāsa), a means to knowledge, and that of the knower (vidvat-sannyāsa), a means to liberation while living.

### 2-3 <a id="tea-jivanmuktiviveka-2-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Knowledge of the truth, destruction of latent tendencies (vāsanā-kṣaya) and dissolution of the mind (mano-nāśa) are to be practised together, each supporting the others; without the latter two, knowledge does not bring the stable freedom of the jīvanmukta.

### 3 <a id="tea-jivanmuktiviveka-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The knower is graded by the seven stages of knowledge (from the Yoga Vāsiṣṭha): one at the fourth is a knower of Brahman; at the fifth, sixth and seventh, increasingly excellent knowers, distinguished by how far they rise from absorption by themselves or by others.

### 4 <a id="tea-jivanmuktiviveka-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The purposes of liberation while living are five: protection of knowledge, austerity, absence of conflict, cessation of suffering, and the manifestation of bliss.
