### 1 <a id="tea-ojoyoshu-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The first chapter describes the six paths to be loathed — the eight great hells with their torments in detail, hungry ghosts, animals, asuras, humans and gods — showing the human body as impure, suffering and impermanent and even heavenly life as ending in the five signs of decay, so that the practitioner will turn away from the three realms.

### 2 <a id="tea-ojoyoshu-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The second chapter sets out ten pleasures of the Pure Land: being welcomed by the holy assembly at death, the first opening of the lotus, the pleasure of the body and superknowledges, of the wondrous objects, of unending joy, of guiding one's connections, of the holy company, of seeing the Buddha and hearing the Dharma, of making offerings to the buddhas at will, and of advancing on the Buddha-path.

### 4 <a id="tea-ojoyoshu-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The right practice follows Vasubandhu's five gates — worship, praise, aspiration, contemplation and transfer — with contemplation at the centre: the particular contemplation of the Buddha's marks one by one, the general contemplation of his whole body, and the abbreviated contemplation for those who cannot manage these — contemplating the light of the white tuft between his brows, or simply thinking 'I too am within his embrace; though passions blind my eyes and I cannot see him, his great compassion never tires and always shines upon me'; and for those unable even to contemplate, single-mindedly calling the name.

### 6.deathbed <a id="tea-ojoyoshu-6-deathbed"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Deathbed practice: following the Jetavana custom the sick monk is moved to a separate hall of impermanence; an image of Amitābha is placed facing west with a five-coloured cord tied to its hand, the other end held by the dying; attendants burn incense, scatter flowers and keep the dying person's mind on the Buddha; they ask what he sees and help him repent evil signs; the dying one should call the name, and at the last moments the attendants help him complete ten recitations in right mindfulness. Genshin also gives exhortations for the attendant to speak at the bedside.

### preface <a id="tea-ojoyoshu-preface"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The teaching and practice for birth in the Land of Bliss are the eyes and feet for this defiled world in its last age; monks and lay, high and low, all should turn to it. The exoteric and esoteric teachings and the practices of principle and phenomena are many; for the wise and diligent they are not hard, but for a dull person like me they are difficult; therefore I have gathered passages on the single gate of the nenbutsu from the sūtras and treatises, so that they are easy to understand and practise.
