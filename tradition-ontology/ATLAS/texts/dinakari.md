## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### on Kārikāvalī 51–52 (anubhūti), opening of the second section <a id="tea-dinakari-pramana-sankhya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On the root text's division of cognition into experience and memory, the commentary reports the views on the kinds of experience (and so of the means of knowledge): the Cārvākas say experience is perception only; Kaṇāda and the Buddhist (Sugata) add inference; some partial Naiyāyikas add comparison; the Prābhākaras add presumption (arthāpatti); the Bhāṭṭas and the Vedāntins add non-apprehension (anupalabdhi); the Paurāṇikas add inclusion (sambhava) and tradition (aitihya). To show that all these are wrong, the root text says experience is fourfold.

> तत्रानुभूतिः प्रत्यक्षात्मिकैवेति चार्वाकाः। अनुमितिरपीति कणादसुगतौ। उपमितिरपीति केचिन्नैयायिकैकदेशिनः। अर्थापत्तिरपीति प्राभाकराः। अनुपलब्धिरपीति भट्टा वेदान्तिनश्च। सम्भवैतिह्यरूपेअपीति पौराणिकाः।
