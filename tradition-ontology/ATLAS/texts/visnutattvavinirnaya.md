one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); the tenth of the ten short treatises (daśa-prakaraṇa); it Madhva's chief independent treatise, in three chapters: the valid scriptures (sadāgama), their authorless and intrinsically valid character, and their teaching of difference (including the reading 'atat tvam asi'); the Lord's supremacy over the perishable (Brahmā, Śiva and the gods) and the imperishable (Lakṣmī); and Viṣṇu's freedom from all defects, even in his avatāras, and the identity of his qualities with his nature.

### 1 <a id="tea-visnutattvavinirnaya-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Homage to Nārāyaṇa, who is known through the true scriptures alone, who transcends the perishable and the imperishable, who is free of defects and has all good qualities.

### 1 <a id="tea-visnutattvavinirnaya-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Quoting the Brahmāṇḍa:) The Ṛg and the other Vedas, the Bhārata, the whole Pañcarātra, the original Rāmāyaṇa and the Purāṇas of that nature, and whatever follows them, are the true scriptures (sadāgama); others are false scriptures, by which Janārdana is not known. He is to be known by these, by the devoted and steadfast — not by reasoning alone, nor by the senses; he is known through scripture alone, by devotees only.

### 1 <a id="tea-visnutattvavinirnaya-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Veda is authorless (apauruṣeya); no author of it is known, and to assume one would be needless assumption. Its validity is intrinsic (svataḥ) — otherwise there would be an infinite regress; reasoning only removes the doubts of a defective intellect, and invalidity comes from extraneous defects.

### 1 <a id="tea-visnutattvavinirnaya-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The purport of all the scriptures cannot be the identity of soul and Supreme Self, since that contradicts every means of knowledge — no one experiences 'I am omniscient, lord of all, without sorrow, without defect', and the contrary is experienced. No scripture teaches non-difference; all teach difference. The teaching 'atat tvam asi' ('you are not that'), given nine times with examples (the bird tied by a string, the honey gathered from many trees, the rivers entering the sea ...), is not a teaching of non-difference.

### 2 <a id="tea-visnutattvavinirnaya-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Brahmā, Śiva and the gods are 'perishable' (kṣara) because their bodies perish; Lakṣmī is 'imperishable' (akṣara) because her body does not; Hari is beyond her. By the limitlessness of his independence, power, knowledge, bliss and all qualities, all of them are ever under his control. He alone brings about the creation, maintenance, destruction, control, illumination, covering and bondage of all the perishable, and the release of the sāttvika.

### 3 <a id="tea-visnutattvavinirnaya-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Quoting the Brahmāṇḍa, answering Nārada's doubt how the flawless Viṣṇu could be born among men and seem to suffer anxiety, fatigue, wounds and ignorance:) Viṣṇu's body is not born of union; he manifests his own eternal body of flawless consciousness and bliss, and that is his 'birth'. Yet to delude the asuras and others he sometimes shows sorrow, ignorance and confusion, so that liberation is hard to win (for the unfit); by the fault of seeing him falsely they do not reach liberation and go to darkness. His qualities, actions and the rest are his very nature, so there is no difference among them at any time.
