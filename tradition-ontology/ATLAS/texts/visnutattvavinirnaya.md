one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); the tenth of the ten short treatises (daśa-prakaraṇa); it Madhva's chief independent treatise, in three chapters: the valid scriptures (sadāgama), their authorless and intrinsically valid character, and their teaching of difference (including the reading 'atat tvam asi'); the Lord's supremacy over the perishable (Brahmā, Śiva and the gods) and the imperishable (Lakṣmī); and Viṣṇu's freedom from all defects, even in his avatāras, and the identity of his qualities with his nature.
