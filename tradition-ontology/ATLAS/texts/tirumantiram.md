# Tirumantiram

`src:tirumantiram` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Tirumantiramālai, Tamiḻ mūvāyiram (the Tamil three thousand)
**Language:** Tamil
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [The Nāyaṉmārs and the Tirumuṟai (Tamil Śaiva bhakti)](../lineages/nayanmar.md), [Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta (the Meykaṇṭa school)](../lineages/tamil-saiva-siddhanta.md), [The Tamil Siddhars (cittar)](../lineages/tamil-siddha.md)
**Genre:** yoga-theological verse (tantra)
**Part of:** [Tirumuṟai](tirumurai.md)
**Location in parent:** Tirumuṟai 10
**Authors:** 
  - [Tirumūlar](../teachers/tirumular.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: a siddha from Kailāsa who entered the body of the cowherd Mūlaṉ and sang one verse a year for 3,000 years; scholarly: a single or composite author; parts may be later additions; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Tirumūlar lived through 3,000 years of yoga; Scholarly account: disputed: proposals range from c. 5th to c. 10th c.; the 7th–8th c. is often given; (confidence low)
**Structure:** preface (pāyiram) + 9 tantras, c. 3,047 verses; the tradition links the nine tantras to nine Āgamas
**Availability:** digitized-original

Tirumūlar's c. 3,000 verses in nine sections (tantras) and a preface: ethics, impermanence and the guru; Śiva's greatness, his five acts and the classes of souls; eightfold yoga and its powers; mantras, cakras and the Goddess; the Śaiva schools and the four paths; the guru and grace; the centres, liṅga and the realized; the states of the soul and the tattvas; and the Dance, the five letters and the experience of Śiva. It declares 'love is Śiva' and 'one family, one God', and joins Vedānta and Siddhānta.
