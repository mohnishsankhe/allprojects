Dignāga's eight verses showing that the object-condition of cognition cannot be external atoms, singly or aggregated, since what causes a cognition does not appear in it and what appears in it is not causally real; the object is the knowable form inside cognition appearing as if outside, and the sense faculty is a potency within consciousness.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1-2 <a id="tea-alambanapariksa-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The object-condition of sense-cognition cannot be atoms: though atoms might be its cause, cognition does not bear their image, so they are not its object, like the sense faculty; nor can it be the aggregate of atoms, which bears the image but, not being a real substance, is not a cause — like the double moon.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: dispute, consciousness-mind_

### 6 <a id="tea-alambanapariksa-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The object is the knowable form that is internal and appears as if external, because it is of the nature of consciousness and because it is its condition.

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: consciousness-mind, ultimate_

concepts: [The examination of the object-condition](../concepts/alambana-pariksa.md), [Cognition-only (vijñaptimātratā)](../concepts/vijnaptimatrata.md) · teachers: [Dignāga](../teachers/dignaga.md)

### 7-8 <a id="tea-alambanapariksa-7-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The object is a condition as one part (of cognition), passing on a potency, either simultaneously or successively; the sense faculty is that potency (within consciousness), assisted by co-operating factors; object-form and potency condition each other from beginningless time.

_level: conventional · standpoint: causal · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: consciousness-mind_
