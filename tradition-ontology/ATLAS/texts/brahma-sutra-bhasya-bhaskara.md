Bhāskara's commentary on the Brahma Sūtras: Brahman really transforms into the world through its power; souls are parts of Brahman distinguished by real adjuncts (aupādhika bhedābheda); liberation comes after death through knowledge combined with lifelong ritual duty; it attacks Śaṅkara's doctrine of māyā as Mahāyāna Buddhist in origin.

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-bhaskara-1-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

On 'then, therefore, the inquiry into Brahman', Bhāskara takes 'then' to mean after the inquiry into ritual duty: the two Mīmāṃsās form one continuous teaching, and knowledge of Brahman presupposes and is joined with the knowledge and performance of works.

### 2.3.43 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-bhaskara-2-3-43"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On the soul as a part (aṃśa) of Brahman: the soul is a part of Brahman, different from it by its limiting adjuncts (upādhi) — the intellect and the rest, which are real — and non-different from it by nature (aupādhika-bhedābheda).

### 4.1 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-bhaskara-4-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Bhāskara holds that liberation is not attained while the body lasts: the embodied knower still bears real adjuncts; knowledge combined with prescribed works continues until death, after which the soul, freed of adjuncts, becomes non-different from Brahman.
