## Teachings (9: skeleton 9)

### 1.1.2 <a id="tea-sastradipika-1-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Validity is intrinsic: the injunctions produce undoubted cognition; the cognition that the object is otherwise, or the knowledge of a defect in the cause, would overturn it, but no such knowledge exists for the Veda, since defects of words depend on persons.

### 1.1.5 <a id="tea-sastradipika-1-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

By the three qualifiers in MS 1.1.5 the sūtrakāra indicated the definition of a means of knowledge: a cognition that apprehends what was not apprehended before, and is free from defects in its causes and from contradicting cognition.

> kāraṇadoṣabādhakajñānarahitamagṛhītagrāhijñānaṃ pramāṇamiti

### 1.1.5/2 <a id="tea-sastradipika-1-1-5-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In all errors only the relation (saṃsarga) that appears is unreal; the related things are real. This is what Mīmāṃsakas call viparītakhyāti. In dreams, objects of other places and times, remembered through impressions awakened by the unseen (adṛṣṭa), are taken by a sleep-affected mind as present.

> sarvatra saṃsargamātramasadevāvabhāsate | saṃsargiṇastu santa eva | seyaṃ viparītakhyātirucyate mīmāṃsakaiḥ |

### 1.1.5/3 <a id="tea-sastradipika-1-1-5-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Cognition itself is not perceived: the self's relation to the object, grasped by the mind, or a special property produced in the object by cognition (its being manifest), leads to the cognition being inferred; this property must be admitted even by those who hold that knower, known and knowing all appear together.

### 1.1.5/4 <a id="tea-sastradipika-1-1-5-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The world binds a person in three ways: the body as the seat of experience, the senses as its instruments, and sound and the other objects as what is experienced. Release is the absolute dissolution of this threefold bond: the destruction of body, senses and objects already produced (in relation to the self) and the absolute non-arising of new ones, through the complete exhaustion of the dharma and adharma that produce them.

> tredhā hi prapañcaḥ puruṣaṃ badhnāti bhogāyatanaṃ śarīraṃ bhogasādhanānīndriyāṇi bhogyāḥ śabdādayo viṣayāḥ |

### 1.1.5/5 <a id="tea-sastradipika-1-1-5-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Reported view (ānanda-mokṣa): the self's innate bliss, overpowered in saṃsāra, becomes manifest and enjoyed in release; the outer senses cease but the mind continues, so the released self abides experiencing supreme bliss by mental perception — citing śruti on bliss and on the imperishable seeing of the seer; they take Kumārila's verse (ŚV sambandhākṣepaparihāra 106–107) as stating another's view.

### 1.1.5/6 <a id="tea-sastradipika-1-1-5-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pārthasārathi's conclusion: in release there is no cognition, for the capacity to know remains but its instruments and objects are absent; release is like deep sleep, differing only in that saṃsāra does not resume; it is without bliss, and is a human goal because pain ceases. Release is the cutting off of all the specific qualities of the self, pleasure, pain and the rest. Only those averse to saṃsāra are eligible for the teaching on release.

> tasmānnirānando mokṣaḥ, duḥkhaparilopācca puruṣārthantvam

### 1.1.5/7 <a id="tea-sastradipika-1-1-5-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dharma and adharma cease thus: those already produced, by being experienced, by performing obligatory and occasional rites, and by self-knowledge, which opposes them; those not yet produced from desire-prompted rites, by not performing such rites; the demerit from omitting the enjoined or doing the forbidden, by doing the enjoined and avoiding the forbidden. When no new body begins and the former body falls, one becomes bodiless.

### 1.1.5/8 <a id="tea-sastradipika-1-1-5-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Knowledge of the self is both for the rite and for the person: knowing the self as eternal and distinct from the body enables rites whose fruits come in another world, and the Upaniṣads make that discrimination firm (citing ŚV ātmavāda 148); meditations with prescribed procedures have unseen fruits, some prosperity, some the highest good ('he does not return').
