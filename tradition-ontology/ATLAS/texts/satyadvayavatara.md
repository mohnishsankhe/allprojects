### v1 <a id="tea-satyadvayavatara-v1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

The dharma taught by the Buddhas rests entirely on the two truths: the conventional truth of the world and the ultimate truth.

> sangs rgyas rnams kyis chos bstan pa/ / bden pa gnyis la yang dag brten/ / 'jig rten kun rdzob bden pa dang / / de bzhin don dam bden pa'o/ /

### v10-13 <a id="tea-satyadvayavatara-v10-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Buddhists accept two means of knowledge, perception and inference; to say that emptiness is realized by these two is the talk of the near-sighted and confused. (If it were,) non-Buddhists and śrāvakas too would realize dharmatā, to say nothing of the Vijñānavādins, and there would be no disagreement with the Mādhyamikas; all tenets would agree because they are measured by valid cognition. Since all logicians disagree, would not dharmatā measured by valid cognition become many? Perception and inference are not needed (for this); the learned made them to refute the non-Buddhist disputants.

### v15-17 <a id="tea-satyadvayavatara-v15-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Who realizes emptiness? Candrakīrti, disciple of Nāgārjuna, who was prophesied by the Tathāgata and saw the truth of dharmatā; by the instruction transmitted from him, the truth of dharmatā will be realized. All the eighty-four thousand collections of dharma that were taught incline and flow toward this dharmatā; one is liberated by realizing emptiness; the rest of the practice is for that purpose.

### v17-20 <a id="tea-satyadvayavatara-v17-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

If, disparaging the correct conventional, one becomes habituated to emptiness, one is deceived about conventional cause and effect, virtue and vice, and the world beyond. Relying on a little learning, not knowing the true meaning, those who do no merit are ruined; if emptiness is wrongly viewed, those of little wisdom are destroyed.

### v2-3 <a id="tea-satyadvayavatara-v2-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

The conventional is held to be of two kinds, false and correct. The first is twofold: (appearances like) the moon in water, and the conceptions of bad tenet systems. What, unexamined, is pleasing when taken just as it appears, has the nature of arising and perishing and is able to perform functions, is held to be the correct conventional.

### v21-22 <a id="tea-satyadvayavatara-v21-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

The master Candrakīrti says: the conventional truth is the method and the ultimate truth is what arises from the method; those who do not know the distinction between the two go to lower realms through wrong understanding. Without relying on conventions, the ultimate will not be realized; it is not fitting for the learned to try to climb to the top of the great mansion of reality without the ladder of the correct conventional.

### v23-25 <a id="tea-satyadvayavatara-v23-25"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

This conventional, as it appears, is not found at all when examined by reasoning; that very not-finding is the ultimate, the dharmatā abiding from the beginning. Because it is produced by causes and conditions, the conventional as it appears is established; if it were not fit to be established, who produced the moon in water and the like? Therefore all appearances are established as produced by various causes and conditions; when the continuum of conditions is cut, they do not arise even conventionally.

### v28-30 <a id="tea-satyadvayavatara-v28-30"></a>

Though I, near-sighted and confused, cannot settle the two truths, relying on the words of my teachers I have set out the two truths of Nāgārjuna's system. Written at the request of the king of Suvarṇadvīpa, it should be accepted after careful examination, not out of faith and respect alone, even by those who today have faith. ### v5-9 <a id="tea-satyadvayavatara-v5-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

In terms of the words of the teaching, it is indicated as unarisen, unceasing and so on; in the ultimate, undifferentiated, there is neither the bearer of qualities nor the quality. In emptiness there is not the slightest differentiation; because it is realized non-conceptually it is conventionally called 'seeing emptiness'. The profound sūtras say that not seeing is seeing it; there, there is neither seeing nor seer; without beginning or end, peace, free of thing and non-thing, non-conceptual, without object, without abiding and without place, without coming or going, beyond example, inexpressible, not to be looked at, unchanging, unconditioned. When the yogin realizes it, the obscurations of affliction and of the knowable are abandoned.
