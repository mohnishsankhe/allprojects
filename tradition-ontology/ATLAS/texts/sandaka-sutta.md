# Sandaka Sutta

**Language:** Pali

**Lineages:** [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
**Part of:** [Majjhima Nikāya](majjhima-nikaya.md)
**Location in parent:** MN 76

Ānanda teaches the wanderer Sandaka four ways that negate the holy life (the doctrines later tied to Ajita, Pūraṇa, Gosāla and Pakudha) and four unreliable holy lives (the teacher who claims constant omniscience, the oral transmitter, the reasoner, the dull one); Sandaka remarks that the Ājīvakas recognize only three who have made an end: Nanda Vaccha, Kisa Saṅkicca and Makkhali Gosāla.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 7-19 <a id="tea-sandaka-sutta-7-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ānanda sets out four ways that negate the holy life, each of which a sensible person would not follow: (1) the teacher who says there is no gift or fruit of deeds and that the person made of four elements is annihilated at death; (2) the teacher who says the doer does no evil; (3) the teacher who says there is no cause for defilement or purification; (4) the teacher who says there are seven unmade bodies and that fool and wise alike make an end of suffering after 8,400,000 great aeons. Each would make the holy life pointless: 'under this teacher it is as if I had already done it without doing it'.

### 21-33 <a id="tea-sandaka-sutta-21-33"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Four unreliable holy lives: (1) under a teacher who claims constant all-knowing and all-seeing knowledge, yet enters an empty house, gets no alms, is bitten by a dog, meets a wild elephant, asks people's names and the way, and explains 'I had to'; (2) under an oral transmitter who takes tradition for truth; (3) under a reasoner, whose reasoning may be well or badly done; (4) under one who is dull and confused and wriggles like an eel when questioned.

### 53 <a id="tea-sandaka-sutta-53"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sandaka says that in the Buddha's teaching many hundreds are emancipated without self-praise, whereas 'these Ājīvakas, sons of a mother whose sons are dead', praise themselves and disparage others and recognize only three who have made an end: Nanda Vaccha, Kisa Saṅkicca and Makkhali Gosāla.
