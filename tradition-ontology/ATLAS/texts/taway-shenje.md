Gorampa Sönam Senge's treatise dividing Tibetan Madhyamaka into three: the 'eternalist' Madhyamaka of Dolpopa, the 'nihilist' Madhyamaka of Tsongkhapa, and the Madhyamaka free from extremes (spros bral) that he defends as Nāgārjuna's intent.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### critique-qualifier <a id="tea-taway-shenje-critique-qualifier"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Tsongkhapa's addition of the qualifier 'inherently' or 'truly' to the object of negation is not found in Nāgārjuna and Candrakīrti, who negate arising, existence and the rest without qualification; by leaving conventional things unnegated at the level of ultimate analysis, that system clings to a subtle existence.

### freedom-from-extremes <a id="tea-taway-shenje-freedom-from-extremes"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The ultimate is freedom from all elaborations — from the four extremes of existence, non-existence, both and neither; the negation of true existence alone is only a step, and clinging to emptiness as a mere negation is itself an extreme to be abandoned.

### three-views <a id="tea-taway-shenje-three-views"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In Tibet three views are held to be Madhyamaka: the Madhyamaka of the eternalist extreme (Dolpopa: the ultimate is empty of other but not of itself), the Madhyamaka of the nihilist extreme (Tsongkhapa: the ultimate is the mere negation of true existence), and the Madhyamaka free from extremes, which is the view of the Sakya and of Nāgārjuna.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: dispute, ultimate_

### ultimate-not-object <a id="tea-taway-shenje-ultimate-not-object"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The ultimate truth is not an object of knowledge in the ordinary sense: it is beyond the scope of mind and words; ultimate truth is 'known' only in the sense that, in meditative equipoise, subject and object are no longer divided.
