# Amṛtasiddhi

`src:amrtasiddhi` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Alternate titles:** 'Chi med grub pa (Tibetan translation)
**Original title:** अमृतसिद्धि
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** shared
**Lineages:** [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md), [Vajrayāna (Mantranaya) in India](../lineages/vajrayana.md), [The Mahāsiddhas (the eighty-four great adepts)](../lineages/mahasiddha.md)
**Genre:** yoga manual
**Authors:** 
  - [Virūpa](../teachers/virupa.md) — role: author; attribution: disputed
**Attribution:** tradition: ascribed in its colophon/Tibetan transmission to Virūpākṣa (identified with the Buddhist mahāsiddha Virūpa); scholarly: anonymous Buddhist tantric (Vajrayāna) milieu with Śaiva elements; authorship uncertain; confidence: low
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 11th c. CE; a manuscript dated 1160 CE gives a terminus ante quem; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 36 short chapters (viveka) — c. 300 verses (as recalled; check the Mallinson–Szántó edition)
**Availability:** unknown

The earliest known text to teach the methods later called haṭha: bindu, identified with the moon in the head, is the essence of life and is consumed by the sun (fire) in the belly; by mahāmudrā, mahābandha and mahāvedha the breath is forced into the central channel, bindu is made steady and the yogin attains the 'nectar of immortality'. It also first teaches the four stages (ārambha, ghaṭa, paricaya, niṣpatti). Its setting is Buddhist tantric, with Śaiva elements.

### topic.avasthas <a id="tea-amrtasiddhi-topic-avasthas"></a>

Four stages of the practice are taught — ārambha, ghaṭa, paricaya and niṣpatti — each with its signs, culminating in the yogin's perfection.

### topic.bindu <a id="tea-amrtasiddhi-topic-bindu"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Bindu is the essence of the body and the source of life; it is the moon; as long as bindu is stable the body lives, and its falling is death.

_level: conventional · standpoint: causal · path: body-breath · stage: all · types: body-layers, death-dying_

### topic.madhyama <a id="tea-amrtasiddhi-topic-madhyama"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The central channel (madhyamā) is where sun and moon are to be united; when the breath enters it the yogin attains the state of immortality.

### topic.sun-moon <a id="tea-amrtasiddhi-topic-sun-moon"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The moon in the head continually drips nectar, which the sun (fire) in the belly consumes; this consumption is the cause of ageing and death; the yogin's task is to prevent it.

_level: conventional · standpoint: causal · path: body-breath · stage: all · types: body-layers, death-dying_

### topic.three-mudras <a id="tea-amrtasiddhi-topic-three-mudras"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The text teaches three practices — mahāmudrā, mahābandha and mahāvedha — by which the breath is forced into the central channel and bindu is made steady; it is the first known text to teach them.
