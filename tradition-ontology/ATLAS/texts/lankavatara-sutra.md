**Availability:** digitized-original

The Buddha, on Mount Malaya in Laṅkā, answers the bodhisattva Mahāmati: all that appears is perceived as one's own mind (svacittadṛśya); the eight consciousnesses arise from the ālayavijñāna like waves from the ocean stirred by the wind of objects; the five dharmas, three natures and two selflessnesses; the tathāgatagarbha, called the ālayavijñāna, is not the self of the tīrthikas; the self-realized noble knowledge (pratyātmāryajñāna) beyond words — 'the Buddha spoke not a single syllable' — the finger and the moon; four kinds of dhyāna; the gradual and the sudden purification of the mind-stream; and a chapter forbidding the eating of meat.

### 2 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The five dharmas (sign, name, discrimination, right knowledge, suchness), the three natures and the two kinds of no-self (of persons and of dharmas) contain the whole teaching of the Buddhas; the world is nothing but what is seen of one's own mind.

### 2.p20 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-2-p20"></a>

From the ālayavijñāna, like an ocean stirred by the wind of objects, the waves of the evolving consciousnesses arise without interruption; the five sense-consciousnesses with the mind-consciousness arise simultaneously or in sequence, like images in a mirror.

### 2.p25 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-2-p25"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Is the stream of what is seen as one's own mind purified gradually or all at once? Gradually — as mangoes ripen, pots are made, grasses grow and arts are learned step by step; and also all at once — as a mirror shows all forms at once, the sun and moon light all forms at once, the ālaya displays its world at once, and the dharmatā-buddha shines at once.

> kramavṛttyā mahāmate svacittadṛśyadhārā viśudhyati na yugapat

### 2.p28 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-2-p28"></a>

### 2.p33 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-2-p33"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mahāmati asks whether the tathāgatagarbha — described as luminous, pure, within all beings, permanent, covered by the aggregates — is not the same as the self taught by the tīrthikas. The Buddha answers that it is not: the Tathāgatas teach the tathāgatagarbha meaning emptiness, the reality-limit, nirvāṇa, the unborn, the signless and wishless, so that the foolish may not fear no-self; bodhisattvas should not cling to it as a self.

> na hi mahāmate tīrthakarātmavādatulyo mama tathāgatagarbhopadeśaḥ

### 2.p33/2 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-2-p33-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Buddha answers: my teaching of the tathāgatagarbha is not like the tīrthakaras' doctrine of self. The Tathāgatas teach the tathāgatagarbha as a name for emptiness, the limit of reality, nirvāṇa, the unborn, the signless and the wishless, so that the foolish may give up their fear of selflessness; bodhisattvas should not cling to it as a self. As a potter makes many vessels from one lump of clay, the Tathāgatas teach the one selflessness of dharmas by various means — as the 'womb' or as selflessness — and they teach the tathāgatagarbha to attract the tīrthakaras who cling to a self.

> na hi mahāmate tīrthakarātmavādatulyo mama tathāgatagarbhopadeśaḥ

### 2.p41 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-2-p41"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Four kinds of dhyāna: that practiced by the foolish (bālopacārika — śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha yogis contemplating no-self of persons, impermanence, suffering and impurity); that which examines meanings (arthapravicaya — the no-self of dharmas); that which takes suchness as its object (tathatālambana); and the Tathāgata's dhyāna (tāthāgata), abiding in the self-realized noble knowledge and working for beings.

> yaduta bālopacārikaṃ dhyānam, arthapravicayaṃ dhyānam, tathatālambanaṃ dhyānam, tāthāgataṃ caturthaṃ dhyānam

### 2.p52 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-2-p52"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Buddha has established eight consciousnesses; when the mind-consciousness (manovijñāna) ceases, the other seven cease with it, since it is their cause and support.

### 3.p59 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-3-p59"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'From the night the Tathāgata awakened to the night he will enter parinirvāṇa he has not uttered a single syllable, nor will he; non-speech is the Buddha's speech': said with reference to two dharmas — the self-realized dharma and the eternally abiding dharma-nature, which the buddhas do not make.

### 6.p90 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-6-p90"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The tathāgatagarbha, called the ālayavijñāna, perfumed by beginningless habit-energy, evolves together with the seven consciousnesses like the ocean and its waves, yet is by nature free of impermanence, free of the doctrine of self and utterly pure; bodhisattvas seeking the higher should purify this tathāgatagarbha called ālayavijñāna; were it absent there would be neither saṃsāra nor liberation.

> tasmāttarhi mahāmate tathāgatagarbhaḥ ālayavijñānasaṃśabdito viśodhayitavyo viśeṣārthibhirbodhisattvairmahāsattvaiḥ

### 8 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The chapter on meat-eating: the bodhisattva, who regards all beings as his only child and as having been his relatives in past lives, must not eat meat; eating meat causes killing for trade, breeds fear in beings and obstructs liberation; the permission of 'threefold pure' meat is not given here; alcohol, meat, onions and garlic are forbidden.

### 10.256-258 <a id="tea-lankavatara-sutra-10-256-258"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Mounting to mind-only, one should not imagine an external object; abiding in the object that is suchness, one should pass beyond mind-only; passing beyond mind-only, one should pass beyond the imageless; the yogin abiding in the imageless sees the Mahāyāna. The effortless state is peaceful, purified by vows; the supreme selfless wisdom is not seen in the imageless.

> cittamātraṃ samāruhya bāhyamarthaṃ na kalpayet / tathatālambane sthitvā cittamātramatikramet // cittamātramatikramya nirābhāsamatikramet / nirābhāsasthito yogī mahāyānaṃ sa paśyati
