46 verses discriminating the seer from the seen: form is seen by the eye, the eye by the mind, the mind's states by the witness, which is never seen; the two powers of māyā; the five aspects of every thing (is, shines, dear; name, form); six kinds of samādhi; three kinds of jīva.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 1 <a id="tea-drg-drsya-viveka-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Form is seen and the eye is its seer; the eye is seen and the mind is its seer; the mind's modes are seen and the witness is their seer — the witness is never seen.

> rūpaṃ dṛśyaṃ locanaṃ dṛk tad dṛśyaṃ dṛk tu mānasam / dṛśyā dhīvṛttayaḥ sākṣī dṛg eva na tu dṛśyate

### 20 <a id="tea-drg-drsya-viveka-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Every thing has five aspects: it is, it shines, it is dear, and it has a name and a form; the first three belong to Brahman, the last two to the world.

> asti bhāti priyaṃ rūpaṃ nāma cety aṃśapañcakam / ādyatrayaṃ brahmarūpaṃ jagadrūpaṃ tato dvayam

### 23-30 <a id="tea-drg-drsya-viveka-23-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Six samādhis: within the heart, savikalpa attended by objects of the mind, savikalpa attended by words ('I am unattached consciousness'), and nirvikalpa; and the same three applied to external objects (separating being-shining-dear from name and form); one should spend one's time in these.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation, knowledge · stage: advanced · types: practice_

### 32-37 <a id="tea-drg-drsya-viveka-32-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The jīva is threefold: the absolute (pāramārthika) jīva delimited by the body but in truth Brahman; the empirical (vyāvahārika) jīva, the reflection in the intellect imagined by māyā; and the illusory (prātibhāsika) jīva, the dream-self; each superimposed on the former.
