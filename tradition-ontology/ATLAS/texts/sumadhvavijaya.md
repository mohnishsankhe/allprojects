The principal hagiography of Madhva, a mahākāvya in sixteen cantos by Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍitācārya, son of Trivikrama Paṇḍitācārya: Vāyu's earlier avatāras as Hanumān and Bhīma; Madhva's birth at Pājaka to Madhyageha Bhaṭṭa; his renunciation under Acyutaprekṣa with the name Pūrṇabodha (Pūrṇaprajña) and his installation as Ānandatīrtha; his tours, debates, journeys to Badarī and meeting with Vyāsa; the conversion of Trivikrama; and his final glorification, the gods hailing him as Hanumān, Bhīma and Madhva and showering him with flowers.

### 4.32-33 <a id="tea-sumadhvavijaya-4-32-33"></a>

(At his renunciation) he became an ascetic entitled to the japa of the praṇava, whose three measures the wise say end in the endless; and he was given the name Pūrṇabodha, befitting the height of his qualities.

### 5.1-2 <a id="tea-sumadhvavijaya-5-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Resolved upon by his teacher for the rule of the kingdom of Vedānta learning, he was consecrated with waters; and the name 'Ānandatīrtha' spoken by the teacher became fitting for him who was a vessel of the supreme Bliss and made the blissful śāstra.

### 10.51; 16.6-7 <a id="tea-sumadhvavijaya-10-51-16-7"></a>

The hagiography tells of feats: he ate a thousand plantains that a king offered to test him (10.51); his toenail shone to light his work (16.6); he set in place, for a bathing ghāṭ, a huge rock that many men had brought with difficulty (16.7).
