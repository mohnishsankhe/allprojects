The seventh Aṅga: ten chapters on ten wealthy lay disciples (upāsaka) of Mahāvīra — Ānanda, Kāmadeva, Culanīpitā, Surādeva, Cullaśataka, Kuṇḍakolika, Saddālaputra, Mahāśataka, Nandinīpitā and Sālihīpitā — who take the twelve lay vows, progress through the eleven lay stages (pratimā), withstand divine tests and die peacefully; the Saddālaputra chapter records Mahāvīra's argument against the Ājīvika fatalism.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 1 <a id="tea-upasakadasa-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ānanda, a wealthy householder of Vāṇijagrāma, takes before Mahāvīra the five small vows and seven supplementary vows, limiting his wealth, fields, cattle and trade; later he performs the eleven lay stages (pratimā), gains clairvoyance and dies a peaceful death. When Gautama doubted him, Mahāvīra told Gautama that he, not the layman, must ask forgiveness.

### 6 <a id="tea-upasakadasa-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

A god appears to the Jain layman Kuṇḍakoliya and praises the teaching of Gosāla Maṅkhaliputta — that there is no exertion, action, strength, energy or human effort, and all things are fixed (niyayā savvabhāvā) — over Mahāvīra's. Kuṇḍakoliya asks how the god gained his divine state: by exertion or without? If without, why are all beings not gods? The god falls silent; Mahāvīra praises the layman.

### 7 <a id="tea-upasakadasa-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Saddālaputra, a potter and follower of Gośāla, believed that all happens by fate without effort. Mahāvīra asked him: if a man stole or smashed your pots or assaulted your wife, would you punish him? He said yes — then, said Mahāvīra, you do not really hold that there is no effort or exertion. Saddālaputra became Mahāvīra's lay disciple.
