# The oral and popular corpus of Kabīr (sākhīs, dohās, padas and bhajans)

`src:kabir-oral-corpus` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Alternate titles:** Kabīr ke dohe (popular), Kabīr Vacanāvalī (Hariaudh's anthology, 1916) and similar anthologies, Kṣitimohan Sen's collection 'Kabīr' (Bengali script, 1910–11), basis of Tagore's Songs of Kabir (1915)
**Language:** Hindi
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Sant tradition (nirguṇa bhakti of North India)](../lineages/sant.md), [Kabīr Panth](../lineages/kabir-panth.md)
**Genre:** oral song and proverb corpus
**Authors:** 
  - [Kabīr](../teachers/kabir.md) — role: author; attribution: disputed
**Attribution:** tradition: all ascribed to Kabīr by singers, panths and schoolbooks; scholarly: a living, growing corpus; many famous dohās are absent from the oldest recensions, and much of Kṣitimohan Sen's collection (hence Tagore's Songs of Kabir) is of doubtful authenticity; confidence: disputed
**Dates:** Scholarly account: 15th c. to the present (oral accretion); (confidence low)
**Availability:** oral

The couplets and songs everyone quotes as Kabīr's — on reading and love, the musk-deer, the guru and Govind, the mill-stones, the mind, stone idols and the mullā's call — sung in bhajan and Kabīr Panth gatherings and printed in anthologies. The ontology records them as the tradition's Kabīr, with the attribution marked disputed.
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: translation; name: R. Tagore (with E. Underhill), Songs of Kabir (1915); licence: public domain
