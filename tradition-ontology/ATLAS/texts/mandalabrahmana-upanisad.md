### 1.3 <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-1-3"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Tāraka is twofold — the earlier tāraka and the later amanaska; tāraka with form and without; śāmbhavī, the gaze on the inner target with eyes open, is secret in all the tantras.

`sourced` · confidence high

Views of the inner target differ — water-like light in the thousand-petalled lotus; the person in the cave of the intellect; the five-faced blue-throated Lord with Umā; the thumb-sized person — all these are the Self alone.

### 1.1 (eight limbs) <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-1-1-eight-limbs"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Yama: conquering cold, heat, food and sleep, calm, steadiness and sense-restraint; niyama: devotion to the guru, love of the path of truth, enjoying what comes unsought, contentment, non-attachment, solitude, cessation of mind, no desire for fruits, dispassion; a comfortable posture; breath-control; withdrawal; dhyāna is one-pointedness on the consciousness in all bodies; samādhi is the forgetting of meditation.

_level: bridging · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: intermediate · types: practice, ethics_

### 1.1 (frame) <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-1-1-frame"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Yājñavalkya goes to the solar world and asks the Person there about the Self; Nārāyaṇa teaches the eight-limbed yoga joined with knowledge.

### 1.2 (five faults) <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-1-2-five-faults"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The body has five faults — desire, anger, (disturbed) breathing, fear and sleep; their remedies are freedom from willing, forbearance, light food, vigilance and devotion to truth.

_level: conventional · standpoint: ethical-social · path: general · stage: beginner · types: practice, ethics_

### 1.2 (tāraka) <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-1-2-taraka"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

To cross the ocean of saṃsāra whose waves are sleep and fear, one takes the subtle path, meditating on the tāraka light between the brows, with the three targets.

### 2.3 <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-2-3"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Deep sleep and samādhi both dissolve the mind, yet differ: in sleep mind dissolves in tamas and it is not the cause of liberation; in samādhi the world dissolves into the witness-consciousness.

_level: bridging · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: intermediate · types: consciousness-mind_

### 2.4 <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-2-4"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The five states — waking, dream, deep sleep, the fourth and beyond the fourth; mind with intention is bondage.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 2.1 (śāmbhavī forms) <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-2-1-sambhavi-forms"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The gaze has three forms — amā (eyes closed), pratipat (half open) and pūrṇimā (open) — and the full-moon gaze is to be practised; signs follow: a star, a diamond mirror, the full moon, nine gems, the midday sun, a flame.

### 2.2 (lights) <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-2-2-lights"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Lights appear in the west — crystal, smoke, bindu, nāda, kalā, stars, fireflies, lamps, eyes, gold, gems; they are the form of the praṇava.

### 2.2 (mental worship) <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-2-2-mental-worship"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Worship is mapped onto the state beyond mind: meditation is absence of thought; invocation abandoning action; the seat firm knowledge; water for the feet the unmanī state; the offering the amanaska state; the bath radiance; perfume seeing all; the lamp the sun of consciousness; salutation 'so'ham'; praise silence; dismissal total contentment.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: absolute · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: practice_

### 2.2 (no karma) <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-2-2-no-karma"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

For one who has seen, there are no rites, since the sun of consciousness neither rises nor sets.

### 3.1-2 <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-3-1-2"></a>

Amanaska: the mind becomes like an udder after milking; 'you are I, I am you'; the guru embraces the disciple and imparts the knowledge.

### 4.1 <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-4-1"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The five voids: ākāśa (dark within and without), parākāśa (like the fire of dissolution), mahākāśa (immeasurable light), sūryākāśa (like the sun) and paramākāśa (indescribable, blissful). 'One who does not know the nine cakras, six supports, three targets and five voids is a yogin in name only.'

### 5.1 <a id="tea-mandalabrahmana-upanisad-5-1"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

When the anāhata sound is heard, its resonance becomes light and the mind dissolves there — the supreme state of Viṣṇu; the knower behaves like a child, madman or goblin; he is a paramahaṃsa avadhūta whose sight purifies the world and who saves a hundred and one generations.
