# Yogabīja

`src:yogabija` · `skeleton` · confidence moderate

**Original title:** योगबीज
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md), [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md)
**Genre:** yoga dialogue
**Authors:** 
  - [Gorakṣanātha](../teachers/goraksanatha.md) — role: author; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: a dialogue of Īśvara (Śiva) and Devī, ascribed to Gorakṣa; scholarly: anonymous, Nāth milieu, c. 14th c.; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Śiva's teaching to the Goddess; Scholarly account: c. 14th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** c. 190 verses (recensions vary)
**Availability:** unknown

Argues that knowledge alone does not liberate: the body must be 'ripened' (pakva) in the fire of yoga, for an unripe body is subject to karma and rebirth. It teaches the great yoga (mahāyoga) that unites mantra, laya, haṭha and rāja, and gives what is probably the earliest statement that 'ha' is the sun and 'ṭha' the moon, their union being haṭha.
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: original; name: Yogabīja, ed. Rāmlāl Śrīvāstav (Gorakhpur: Gorakhnath Mandir 1982); licence: unknown

### topic.ha-tha <a id="tea-yogabija-topic-ha-tha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

'Ha' is said to be the sun and 'ṭha' the moon; the union of sun and moon is called haṭhayoga.

### topic.knowledge-and-yoga <a id="tea-yogabija-topic-knowledge-and-yoga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Knowledge without yoga does not bring liberation: even one devoted to knowledge, dispassionate, knowing dharma and with senses conquered — even a god — does not gain liberation without yoga; yoga and knowledge together liberate.

### topic.mahayoga <a id="tea-yogabija-topic-mahayoga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

### topic.ripe-body <a id="tea-yogabija-topic-ripe-body"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The body is of two kinds, unripe and ripe; the unripe body, not fired by yoga, is subject to karma, disease and death; the ripe body is fired by yoga, and only the one whose body is ripened is liberated.
