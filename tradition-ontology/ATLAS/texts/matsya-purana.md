### 53.3-11 <a id="tea-matsya-purana-53-3-11"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The Purāṇa was remembered by Brahmā first of all śāstras, and after it the Vedas came from his mouths; there was once one Purāṇa of a hundred crore verses; in every Dvāpara (Viṣṇu says) I take the form of Vyāsa and condense it into four lakh verses in eighteen Purāṇas, while the full hundred crore remain in the world of the gods.

> purāṇaṃ sarvaśāstrāṇāṃ prathamaṃ brahmaṇā smṛtam / anantaraṃ ca vaktrebhyo vedās tasya vinirgatāḥ

### 53.20-22 <a id="tea-matsya-purana-53-20-22"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

That is called the Bhāgavata in which dharma is expounded taking the Gāyatrī as its basis and which contains the slaying of Vṛtrāsura; it tells of the Sārasvata kalpa; it has eighteen thousand verses and one who gives it on the full moon of Bhādrapada reaches the highest goal.

> yatrādhikṛtya gāyatrīṃ varṇyate dharmavistaraḥ / vṛtrāsuravadhopetaṃ tad bhāgavatam ucyate

### 53.60-64 <a id="tea-matsya-purana-53-60-64"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Upapurāṇas: the Nārasiṃha told in the Padma, of eighteen thousand verses; the Nandī Purāṇa in which Kārttikeya tells the glory of Nandā; the Sāmba, centred on Sāmba; and likewise the Āditya; know that any Purāṇa taught apart from the eighteen has issued from them.

### 53.65-69 <a id="tea-matsya-purana-53-65-69"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

A Purāṇa has five marks (sarga, pratisarga, vaṃśa, manvantara, vaṃśānucarita) and tells the glory of Brahmā, Viṣṇu, the Sun and Rudra and the four aims of life; in sāttvika Purāṇas the glory of Hari predominates, in rājasa ones that of Brahmā, in tāmasa ones that of Agni and Śiva, and in mixed (saṅkīrṇa) ones that of Sarasvatī and the ancestors.
