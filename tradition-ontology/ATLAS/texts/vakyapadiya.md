**Availability:** digitized-original

Bhartṛhari's treatise on 'sentence and word' in three books. Book 1 (Brahmakāṇḍa) teaches that Brahman is the Word-principle appearing as the world, that all cognition is word-permeated, the sphoṭa–dhvani distinction, the three levels of speech and grammar as a path to liberation; book 2 (Vākyakāṇḍa) argues that the sentence is the indivisible unit of meaning, grasped as pratibhā; book 3 (Padakāṇḍa or Prakīrṇaka) analyses universals, substance, relation, quality, direction, means, action, time, person, number, gender and compounds in fourteen sections (samuddeśa).

### 1 <a id="tea-vakyapadiya-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The word (sphoṭa) is ascertained in the intellect when the sounds have planted their seeds in it and it has matured through repetition, together with the last sound; the sphoṭa has no sequence, but appears as if sequential because the sounds that manifest it come in sequence.
