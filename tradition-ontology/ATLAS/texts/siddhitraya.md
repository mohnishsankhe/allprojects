Yāmuna's three 'proofs': Ātmasiddhi (the self is distinct from body, senses, mind and cognition and is known as the 'I'), Īśvarasiddhi (on establishing the Lord) and Saṃvitsiddhi (against Advaita's objectless consciousness); the Īśvarasiddhi and Saṃvitsiddhi survive only partly.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### Saṃvitsiddhi <a id="tea-siddhitraya-samvitsiddhi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Against the Advaitins, consciousness without subject or object is never found; consciousness is always someone's and of something, and the non-dual texts do not deny the plurality of real things.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: consciousness-mind, dispute_

### Ātmasiddhi <a id="tea-siddhitraya-atmasiddhi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The self is distinct from the body, the senses, the mind, the vital breath and cognition; it is established in the awareness 'I', is self-luminous, eternal, atomic and many, and is the knower of its cognitions.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

concepts: [The 'I' is the self (aham-artha)](../concepts/aham-artha.md), [The individual living self (jīva)](../concepts/jiva.md) · teachers: [Yāmunācārya](../teachers/yamuna.md) · disputes: `dsp:is-there-a-self`

### Īśvarasiddhi <a id="tea-siddhitraya-isvarasiddhi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Yāmuna argues for the existence of a Lord who is the maker of the world; whether he accepts the Naiyāyika inference or rests the proof on scripture alone (as Rāmānuja does) is not settled from memory here.
