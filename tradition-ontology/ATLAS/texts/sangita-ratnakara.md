# Saṅgītaratnākara of Śārṅgadeva

**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Saṅgītaśāstra (the science of music: song, instrument and dance)](../lineages/sangita.md)
**Genre:** saṅgītaśāstra
**Authors:** 
  - [Śārṅgadeva](../teachers/sarngadeva.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 1210–1247 CE (Yādava court of Siṅghaṇa, Devagiri); (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 7 adhyāyas: svaragata, rāgaviveka, prakīrṇaka, prabandha, tāla, vādya, nartana
**Availability:** digitized-original

The standard treatise of Indian music in seven chapters (svara, rāga, miscellany, compositions, tāla, instruments, dance). Its first chapter opens with the worship of nāda-brahman and a section on the 'origin of the body' (piṇḍotpatti): Brahman and the jīvas, creation, embryology, the channels and ten centres of the body — the jīva's place on certain petals bringing success or failure in song — and the teaching that the struck nāda, delighting people, breaks the bondage of existence; then the origin of nāda from breath and fire, the twenty-two śrutis and seven svaras.

Opening praise: I worship Śaṅkara whose body is nāda (nādatanu), who shines of himself in the heart-lotus of the wise, reached by the mind that follows the breath arising at the knot of Brahmā, the source of the scale, the grāmas, the melodic patterns and ornaments, whose song fills the world — for joy.

### 1.1.21-23 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-1-21-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Song, instrumental music and dance — these three are called saṅgīta. It is twofold, mārga and deśī: mārga is that which was sought out by Brahmā and the gods and practised by Bharata and others, which, performed before the god Śambhu, gives assured well-being; deśī is what delights the hearts of people region by region.

> गीतं वाद्यं तथा नृत्तं त्रयं सङ्गीतमुच्यते। मार्गो देशीति तद् द्वेधा

### 1.2.1-3 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-2-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Song is made of nāda; instrumental music is praised as the manifestation of nāda; dance follows both — so all three depend on nāda. By nāda the letter is manifested, from letters the word, from words the sentence, and from sentences all this dealing: the world depends on nāda. Nāda is said to be twofold, struck (āhata) and unstruck (anāhata); it becomes manifest in the body (piṇḍa), which is therefore described.

> गीतं नादात्मकं वाद्यं नादव्यक्त्या प्रशस्यते। तद्द्वयानुगतं नृत्तं नादाधीनमतस्त्रयम्॥ नादेन व्यज्यते वर्णः पदं वर्णात्पदाद्वचः। वचसो व्यवहारोऽयं नादाधीनमतो जगत्॥ आहतोऽनाहतश्चेति द्विधा नादो निगद्यते। सोऽयं प्रकाशते पिण्डे तस्मात्पिण्डोऽभिधीयते॥

### 1.2.4-7 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-2-4-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There is Brahman — consciousness and bliss, self-luminous, stainless, called Īśvara and liṅga, without a second, unborn, all-pervading, changeless, formless, lord of all, imperishable, all-powerful, all-knowing. Its parts are called jīvas, conditioned by beginningless ignorance like sparks of a fire divided by the adjunct of wood; governed by beginningless karmas of merit and demerit giving pleasure and pain, they obtain bodies of their species, life-span and experience born of karma.

### 1.2.8-10 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-2-8-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In each birth the jīvas also have a subtle body (liṅga-śarīra), lasting until liberation, made of the subtle elements, senses and breaths. For the jīvas' experience the Unborn creates this world, and as the supreme self withdraws it again for rest; this creation and dissolution is held to be a beginningless stream.

### 1.2.11-12 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-2-11-12"></a>

The jīvas are not different from the self, nor is the world different from it: creating by its power, it remains undivided, as gold in earrings. Others say it creates through ignorance, as a rope appears as a snake. From the self came space, from space wind, from wind fire, from fire water, from water earth.

### 1.2.14-17 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-2-14-17"></a>

Brahman created Brahmā and gave him the Vedas; from the Vedic words he fashioned the elemental world; of the four kinds of bodies (born of sweat, sprouts, wombs and eggs) the human body is described here because it is useful for nāda.

### 1.2.120-139 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-2-120-139"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The centres of the body: the four-petalled ādhāra between anus and genitals, seat of kuṇḍalinī, the power of Brahman, which, straightened up to the brahmarandhra, bestows nectar; the six-petalled svādhiṣṭhāna at the root of the genitals; the ten-petalled maṇipūra at the navel; the twelve-petalled anāhata in the heart, place of worship of Śiva in the form of the praṇava; the sixteen-petalled viśuddhi in the throat, seat of Bhāratī (speech), whose petals bear the praṇava, the udgītha, huṃ-phaṭ, vaṣaṭ, svadhā, svāhā, namaḥ, amṛta, the seven notes and poison; the twelve-petalled lalanā at the uvula; the three-petalled ājñā between the brows; the six-petalled centre of mind; the sixteen-petalled centre of the moon with sixteen kalās (compassion, forbearance, uprightness, fortitude, dispassion and the rest); and the thousand-petalled lotus in the brahmarandhra, holding nectar that nourishes the body. When the jīva rests on each petal a particular state of mind arises.

### 1.2.140-144 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-2-140-144"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When the jīva dwells on the first, eighth, eleventh and twelfth petals of the heart centre it gains success in song and music; on the fourth, sixth and tenth, music is lost; the eight petals of the throat centre from the eighth give success, the sixteenth destroys it; in the lalanā the tenth and eleventh give success, the first, fourth and fifth destroy it; dwelling in the brahmarandhra, bathed in nectar and content, the jīva accomplishes music excellently; on the other petals and centres it never succeeds.

### 1.2.163-167 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-2-163-167"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In this body, covered with the accumulation of impurities, the wise attain enjoyment and liberation by the right means: enjoyment from meditation on the qualified, liberation from the unqualified. But meditation, attainable only by one-pointed mind, is not easy for men; therefore here the easy means is the glorious unstruck nāda, which sages worship by the path taught by the guru. That too, lacking charm (rakti), does not delight the minds of people; therefore (we shall describe) the struck nāda, which, extending all that is to be sung through the śrutis and the rest, delights the world and breaks worldly existence (bhava-bhañjana).

> तस्मादत्र सुखोपायं श्रीमन्नादमनाहतम्। गुरूपदिष्टमार्गेण मुनयः समुपासते॥ सोऽपि रक्तिविहीनत्वान्न मनोरञ्जको नृणाम्। तस्मादाहतनादस्य श्रुत्यादिद्वारतोऽखिलम्। गेयं वितन्वतो लोकरञ्जनं भवभञ्जनम्॥

### 1.3.1-2 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-3-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

We worship nāda-brahman, the consciousness of all beings unfolded as the world, bliss without a second. By worship of nāda (nādopāsana) the gods Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheśvara are truly worshipped, since they are of its nature.

> चैतन्यं सर्वभूतानां विवृत्तं जगदात्मना। नादब्रह्म तदानन्दमद्वितीयमुपास्महे॥ नादोपासनया देवा ब्रह्मविष्णुमहेश्वराः। भवन्त्युपासिता नूनं यस्मादेते तदात्मकाः॥

### 1.3.3-5 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-3-3-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The self, wishing to speak, impels the mind; the mind strikes the fire in the body; the fire impels the wind; the wind, located at the knot of Brahmā, rising gradually along the upward path, manifests sound in the navel, heart, throat, head and mouth; so nāda takes five names by these five places — very subtle, subtle, full, not full and artificial.

### 1.3.6 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-3-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

They know the syllable na as breath (prāṇa) and da as fire; because it is born of the union of breath and fire, it is called nāda.

### 1.3.7-10 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-3-7-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In practice nāda is threefold: low (mandra) in the heart, middle in the throat, high (tāra) in the head, each double the previous. Its twenty-two divisions are called śrutis because they are heard: twenty-two channels are attached obliquely to the upward channel at the heart, and struck by the wind they give śrutis of successively higher pitch; so also in throat and head.

### 1.3.11-22 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-3-11-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The twenty-two śrutis are demonstrated on two identical vīṇās of twenty-two strings, one kept fixed (dhruva) and one movable (cala), by four successive lowerings (sāraṇā) of the movable one, which show the number of śrutis in each note.

### 1.3.23-25 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-1-3-23-25"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

From the śrutis arise the seven notes (svara) — ṣaḍja, ṛṣabha, gāndhāra, madhyama, pañcama, dhaivata and niṣāda, also called sa ri ga ma pa dha ni. That is called a svara which arises right after the śruti, smooth and resonant, and of itself delights the listener's mind.

### 3.24-26 <a id="tea-sangita-ratnakara-3-24-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Singers are censured for twenty-five faults: biting the teeth (sandaṣṭa), shouting, hissing, fear, hesitation, trembling, gaping, weakness, a crow-like voice, losing the tāla, a camel-like neck, goat-like bleating, swelling veins, gourd-like cheeks, a crooked neck, sprawling limbs, closing the eyes, tastelessness, off-pitch notes, indistinctness, missing the register, disorder, mixing styles, inattention and nasality.
