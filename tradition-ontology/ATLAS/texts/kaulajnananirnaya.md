A dialogue of Bhairava and the Goddess in twenty-four chapters, 'brought down' by Macchaghnapāda (Matsyendra) from Candradvīpa as the Yoginīkaula. It teaches creation and dissolution within the body, the body-liṅga worshipped with inner flowers, the eleven centres of the body, nectar-meditations that conquer death and old age, the Kula and Akula, the eight-by-eight yoginīs, the qualifications of disciple, Śakti and hero, Kaula rites, and the story of how Bhairava as a fisherman recovered the scripture from a fish's belly.
