### adhikara <a id="tea-arthasangraha-adhikara"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The injunction of eligibility conveys ownership of the fruit of the act, i.e. being its enjoyer, as in 'one desiring heaven should sacrifice'.

> karmajanyaphalasvāmyabodhako vidhiradhikāravidhiḥ

### arthavada <a id="tea-arthasangraha-arthavada"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Arthavāda is a sentence whose purport is praise or blame; it is meaningful by indicating the excellence of what is enjoined or the badness of what is forbidden, which moves a lazy person to act. It supplements an injunction or a prohibition; (in the commentary) it is praise, blame, another's deed or ancient narrative; and 'it is threefold: figurative where it conflicts (with another means of knowledge), restating where (the fact) is already known, and stating a fact where both are absent'.

> virodhe guṇavādaḥsyādanuvādo'vadhārite| bhūtārthavādastaddhānādarthavādastridhā mataḥ

### bhavana <a id="tea-arthasangraha-bhavana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The verbal suffix expresses bhāvanā. Śābdī bhāvanā is the prompter's operation conducive to a person's activity, expressed by the injunctive (liṅ) element; ārthī bhāvanā is the activity directed to an act, produced by the desire for a purpose. Each expects three things: what is to be brought about, by what means, and how (the procedure) — heaven, the sacrifice, and the subsidiaries such as the prayājas.

> puruṣapravṛttyanukūlo bhāvayiturvyāpāraviśeṣaḥ śābdībhāvanā

### dharma <a id="tea-arthasangraha-dharma"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dharma is the sacrifice and the like; its definition: an object with a purpose (prayojana) made known by the Veda.

> vedapratipādyaḥ prayojanavadartho dharma iti

### krama <a id="tea-arthasangraha-krama"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sequence is fixed by six means: direct statement, purpose (artha), textual order (pāṭha), position (sthāna), the order of principals (mukhya) and the order once begun (pravṛtti).

> ṣaṭ pramāṇāni-śruti-artha-pāṭha-sthāna-mukhya-pravṛttyākhyāni

### namadheya <a id="tea-arthasangraha-namadheya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Names are meaningful by delimiting what is enjoined: a word is a name (not a quality) for four reasons — fear of secondary meaning (udbhid), fear of splitting the sentence (citrā), another text already supplying the quality (agnihotra), and comparison (śyena).

### nisedha <a id="tea-arthasangraha-nisedha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A prohibition is a sentence that turns a person away (from an act that brings harm). Where 'not' cannot attach to the injunctive (e.g. 'he shall not look at the rising sun', introduced as 'his vow'; or to avoid an option), it is an exclusion (paryudāsa) instead: a positive resolve not to look, whose fruit is freedom from sin.

> puruṣasya nivartakaṃ vākyaṃ niṣedhaḥ

### niyama-parisankhya <a id="tea-arthasangraha-niyama-parisankhya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'An injunction proper (apūrva-vidhi) is for what is not at all otherwise obtained; a restrictive injunction (niyama) where something would be obtained only optionally; where it is obtained both there and elsewhere, it is called an exclusive specification (parisaṅkhyā).' E.g. 'five five-nailed animals are to be eaten' excludes the others; parisaṅkhyā is explicit or by implication.

> vidhiratyantamaprāptau niyamaḥ pākṣike sati| tatra cānyatra ca prāptau parisaṃkhyeti gīyate

### upasamhara <a id="tea-arthasangraha-upasamhara"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This dharma, performed with the aim for which it is enjoined, is the cause of that aim; performed with the thought of offering it to Īśvara, it is the cause of the highest good — the Bhagavad Gītā ('whatever you do ... do it as an offering to me') being the authority for such dedication.

> so'yaṃ dharmo yaduddiśya vihitastaduddeśena kriyamāṇastaddhetuḥ| īśvarārpaṇabuddhyā kriyamāṇastu niḥśreyasahetuḥ

### veda <a id="tea-arthasangraha-veda"></a>

### vidhi <a id="tea-arthasangraha-vidhi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

An injunction is the part of the Veda that makes known an unknown purposeful object. It is fourfold: originative (utpatti-vidhi, e.g. 'he offers the Agnihotra'), which conveys only the form of the act; applicatory (viniyoga-vidhi, e.g. 'he offers with curd'), which conveys the relation of subsidiary and principal; performative (prayoga-vidhi), which conveys the promptness of performance; and eligibility (adhikāra-vidhi, e.g. 'one desiring heaven should sacrifice'), which conveys ownership of the fruit.

> vidhiścaturvidhaḥ - utpattividhi-viniyogavidhi-radhikāravidhiḥ-prayogavidhiśceti

### viniyoga <a id="tea-arthasangraha-viniyoga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The applicatory injunction is assisted by six means: direct statement (śruti), indicative power (liṅga), syntactic connection (vākya), context (prakaraṇa, major and minor), position (sthāna) and name (samākhyā); śruti is threefold (by case-ending, by a common expression, by one word). Subsidiaries are 'proximate helpers' that act on a material (like husking or sprinkling) or 'remote helpers' enjoined by themselves (like the prayājas), which serve only toward the final apūrva.
