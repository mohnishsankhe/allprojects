# Rāmcaritmānas

`src:ramcaritmanas` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Mānas, Tulsī Rāmāyaṇa
**Original title:** रामचरितमानस
**Language:** Awadhi
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Rāmānandī sampradāya](../lineages/ramanandi.md)
**Genre:** epic poem (dohā–caupāī)
**Authors:** 
  - [Tulsīdās](../teachers/tulsidas.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: Begun by Tulsīdās at Ayodhyā on Rāmanavamī of Saṃvat 1631 (1574 CE) (Bāla-kāṇḍa, near dohā 34) and completed in about two years and seven months; Śiva approved it.; scholarly: Accepted; draws on the Vālmīki and Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇas, the Bhāgavata, the Hanumannāṭaka and Prasannarāghava.; confidence: high
**Dates:** Tradition's account: begun 1574 CE (Saṃvat 1631); Scholarly account: 1574 – c. 1577 CE; (confidence high)
**Structure:** 7 kāṇḍas of caupāīs grouped under dohās, with Sanskrit invocatory ślokas at the head of each kāṇḍa; citations here are kāṇḍa.dohā in the Gita Press numbering
**Availability:** digitized-original

Tulsīdās's Awadhi retelling of the Rāma story in seven kāṇḍas (Bāla, Ayodhyā, Araṇya, Kiṣkindhā, Sundara, Laṅkā, Uttara), framed by four nested dialogues (Śiva–Pārvatī, Kākabhuśuṇḍi–Garuḍa, Yājñavalkya–Bharadvāja, the poet and his hearers) likened to the four ghāṭs of a lake. It teaches that Rāma is the supreme Brahman, formless and with form; that his name is greater than either form; that devotion (bhakti) with the saints' company is the easiest and surest path in the Kali age; and it depicts dharma embodied in Rāma and Rāma-rājya.
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: original; name: Gita Press, Gorakhpur (standard numbering)

### 7.115–120 (kāṇḍa.dohā, Gita Press numbering; caupāī-level position approximate) <a id="tea-ramcaritmanas-7-115-120"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

### 7.117–118 (Gita Press; line positions approximate) <a id="tea-ramcaritmanas-7-117-118"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The lamp of knowledge in detail: when sāttvika faith, a cow, comes to dwell in the heart by Hari's grace, she grazes on japa, austerity, vows, restraints and observances and the good conduct the Veda teaches; the calf of loving disposition makes her give milk, which a pure mind milks into the vessel of trust; boiled on the fire of desirelessness, cooled by contentment and forgiveness, set with fortitude and equanimity, it is churned with joy by the churn of reflection — self-control the post, truth and kind speech the rope — yielding the butter of pure dispassion; melted in the fire of yoga, burning good and bad karma, it becomes the ghee of knowledge, the dross of 'mine-ness' burnt away; poured into the lamp of the mind set firm on the stand of equanimity, with a wick of the fourth state spun from the cotton of the three states and three qualities, it is lit: the unbroken awareness 'I am That' (so'ham asmi) is its flame, the Self's experience shines and the root of existence — the delusion of difference — is destroyed. Then māyā sends the powers to tempt the intellect, and the senses' gods open the doors for the wind of objects to blow the lamp out.

### 7.121–122 (kāṇḍa.dohā, Gita Press numbering; caupāī-level position approximate) <a id="tea-ramcaritmanas-7-121-122"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Answering Garuḍa's seven questions (the human body is the rarest; poverty the greatest pain; meeting saints the greatest joy…), Bhuśuṇḍi names the diseases of the mind: delusion (moha) is the root of them all; lust is wind, greed phlegm, anger the bile that burns the breast; jealousy, pride and the rest follow.
