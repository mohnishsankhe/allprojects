### 8-9 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-8-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The seven consecrations at the beginning are for introducing children; three (higher ones) are by worldly convention, the fourth is ultimate; my teaching of the dharma is by the two truths, conventional and ultimate.

> ādau saptābhiṣeko yo bālānāmavatāraṇam | trividho lokasaṃvṛtyā caturthaḥ paramārthataḥ || satyadvayena dharmāṇāṃ deśanā vajriṇo mama | lokasaṃvṛtisatyena satyena paramārthataḥ ||

### 10-14 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-10-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The sevenfold consecration is water, crown, silk ribbon, vajra and bell, the great vow, the name and the permission; they purify, in turn, the elements, the aggregates, the perfections, sun and moon (vajra and bell), the senses and objects, the four immeasurables and the buddha; these seven are given after drawing the maṇḍala.

> udakaṃ mukuṭaḥ paṭṭo vajraghaṇṭā mahāvratam | nāmānujñāsamāyuktaḥ sekaḥ saptavidho nṛpa ||

### 15-22 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-15-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

The higher consecrations — vase, secret, wisdom-gnosis and again great wisdom — correspond to the purification of body, speech, mind and gnosis; the recipients are called child, youth, adult and lord of beings; the fourth makes one Vajrasattva, the progenitor of all protectors.

### 24-27 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-24-27"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One should accomplish this by unimagined appearances — the signs beginning with smoke, images of wisdom like the sky: smoke, mirage, fireflies, lamp, flame, moon, sun, darkness, the digit (kalā) and the great drop — the universal image, clear light; with eyes closed or open, the image seen in the empty, unimagined, like a dream — meditate always on that image.

> dhūmamarīcikhadyotadīpajvālendubhāskaraiḥ | tamaḥ kalā mahābindurviśvabimbaṃ prabhāsvaram ||

### 28 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Meditation on an existent (image) is not meditation for yogins; the mind has neither existence nor non-existence in the image, because it is seen without imagining.

> abhāve bhāvanā bimbe yogināṃ sā na bhāvanā | bhāvo 'bhāvo na cittasya bimbe 'kalpitadarśanāt ||

### 43-45 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-43-45"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The breath moves fifty-six and a quarter times in each period, and twenty-one thousand six hundred times in a day and night; the left and right channels carry them, the middle one only for a small part in a hundred years.

> adhikān pañcasaptatyā ṣaṭśatāṃśca divāniśam | ekaviṃśatsahasrāṃśca ṣaṭśatairadhikāṃśca yān ||

### 46-50 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-46-50"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The left channel is the moon, lalanā (iḍā), the right the sun, rasanā (piṅgalā), the middle the avadhūtī; above the navel the avadhūtī is called suṣumṇā, carrying Rāhu (darkness); below, the śaṅkhinī; the channels are of the natures of the elements and carry faeces, urine and semen below.

> yā nābhyūrdhve 'vadhūtī sā suṣumnā tamovāhinī | yādhaḥ khagamukhā sā ca śaṅkhinī śukravāhinī ||

### 70-79 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-70-79"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _restricted: summary only_

Omens of death are read from the excess flow of wind in the left or right channel over days and months; knowing the omen, the yogin places the life-wind in the drop and, relying on the avadhūtī, meditates on the supreme imperishable — this is the yogins' cheating of death through the joys.

### 80-82 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-80-82"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _restricted: summary only_

The joys arise as the drop descends from the crown: joy at the head, supreme joy at throat and heart, the joy of cessation, and at the jewel the innate joy, unchanging; non-abiding nirvāṇa is great passion, the imperishable lord; the bliss that falls away through dispassion is nirvāṇa 'abiding'.

### 129-133 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-129-133"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The stain is not adventitious to mind nor long-lasting in it, not born without mind nor abiding in mind unchanged; as the tarnish of copper is destroyed by mercury while the copper's nature, abiding as purity, is not destroyed, so mind's stain is destroyed by union with emptiness while its gnosis-nature, abiding as purity, is not destroyed.

> nāgantuko malaścitte na cittāccirakālikaḥ | na cittena vinā jāto na citte saṃsthito 'vyayaḥ || tadvaccittamalaḥ śūnyatāyogena praṇaśyati | na tasya jñānatā naśyennirmalatvena yā sthitā ||

_level: ultimate · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: consciousness-mind, ultimate_

concepts: [Beings are buddhas obscured by adventitious stains](../concepts/adventitious-stains-vajrayana.md)

### 135-141 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-135-141"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _restricted: summary only_

There is no evil greater than dispassion and no merit greater than bliss; as one cannot describe sexual bliss to a girl who has not known it, the bliss of samādhi cannot be told to one without samādhi; from emission comes dispassion, from dispassion suffering, from suffering loss of the elements, and from that death and rebirth; therefore the yogin abandons the passion of emission and attains imperishable bliss, free from the bonds of saṃsāra.

### 146 <a id="tea-sekoddesa-146"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The image arisen from emptiness is the cause; the bliss born of the imperishable is the result; the cause is sealed by the result and the result by the cause.

> bimbaṃ śūnyodbhavaṃ hetuḥ phalamakṣarajaṃ sukham | phalena mudrito heturhetunā mudritaṃ phalam ||
