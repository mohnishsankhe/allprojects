Śiva tells Pārvatī of his descent between the Dvāpara and Kali ages: he is born at Ulkāgrāma to the brahmin Viśvarūpa of Atri's line and his wife Sudarśanā; the infant secretly performs the fire-offering; he dies at seven months, is laid in the sacred pool, and Śiva enters the body, which is found alive; as Lakulīśa he comes to Kāyāvarohaṇa in the Bhṛgu region and merges into the Brahmeśvara liṅga. The tīrtha bears a different name in each age.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1 <a id="tea-karavana-mahatmya-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Śiva tells Pārvatī of his descent between the Dvāpara and Kali ages: in the line of the sage Atri the brahmin Viśvarūpa and his devoted wife Sudarśanā lived at Ulkāgrāma; Śiva was conceived and born to her on the fourteenth of the bright half of Caitra, a tawny-eyed, golden child, and the birth rites were performed.

### 3 <a id="tea-karavana-mahatmya-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The infant was found each day to have performed the fire-offering; when his parents watched and questioned him he fainted and died at seven months; his mother laid him in the sacred pool, and Śiva, having stilled the body, entered it; the child was seen playing in the water, and the sages praised him as Mahādeva in the form of a child.

### 4 <a id="tea-karavana-mahatmya-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Lakulīśa came to Kāyāvarohaṇa in the Bhṛgu region and merged into the Brahmeśvara liṅga; because the Lord descended there in bodily form the place is called Kāyāvarohaṇa. His image holds a club in the left hand and a citron in the right. The tīrtha was called Icchāpurī in the Kṛta age, Māyāpurī in the Tretā, Meghavatī in the Dvāpara and Kāyāvarohaṇa in the Kali; in the terrible Kali age the Lord is known as Lakulīśa, for the liberation of beings.
