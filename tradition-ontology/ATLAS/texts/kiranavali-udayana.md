Udayana's commentary on Praśastapāda's Padārthadharmasaṅgraha; it explains why absence is not listed among the categories there (because it is determined through its counterpositive, not because it is unreal) and gives the six impediments to a genuine universal (jātibādhaka).

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### padārtha-uddeśa (on Praśastapāda's enumeration) <a id="tea-kiranavali-udayana-abhava-noddista"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

These (six) categories are enumerated as the principal ones; absence, though it has its own nature, is not enumerated because its determination depends on the determination of its counterpositive — not because it is a mere nothing.

### verse on the impediments to a universal (location not checked) <a id="tea-kiranavali-udayana-jatibadhaka"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Six impediments exclude a genuine universal: the individual being only one; two terms having exactly the same extension; cross-division (saṅkara); infinite regress; loss of (the thing's) own nature; and absence of the relation (of inherence).
