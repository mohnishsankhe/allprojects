The 'Awakening of Faith': the one mind has two aspects — mind as suchness and mind as arising and ceasing; the tathāgatagarbha and the ālayavijñāna; original awakening (benjue), non-awakening and actualized awakening; suchness as empty and not empty; five practices and calm-insight; recollection of Amitābha for the timid. The most influential buddha-nature treatise in East Asia.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### benjue <a id="tea-dasheng-qixin-lun-benjue"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Original awakening (benjue) is the mind's nature free of thought, equal to space, the dharmakāya of the Tathāgata; because of it one speaks of non-awakening, and actualized awakening (shijue) is the return to it; when actualized awakening is complete it is identical with original awakening.

### one-mind <a id="tea-dasheng-qixin-lun-one-mind"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The one mind has two aspects: the mind as suchness (xin zhenru), neither born nor perishing, the one dharmadhātu, which is 'empty' as free of all deluded marks and 'not empty' as full of pure qualities; and the mind as arising and ceasing, which depends on the tathāgatagarbha — the ālaya consciousness in which non-arising and arising combine.
