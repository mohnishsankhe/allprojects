One of the three gems of the Pāñcarātra, in 33 paṭalas. Sages ask Śāṇḍilya, who relates Bhagavān's teaching to Nārada. It distinguishes Brahmā's, primordial matter's and the pure creation (paṭalas 2–4), and treats mantras, initiation, worship, meditation and yoga (paṭala 33). An additional passage in the printed text names the three gems and their expansions (Pādma, Pārameśvara, Īśvara) and assigns them to Kāñcī, Śrīraṅgam and Melkote.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1.adhika.1-14 <a id="tea-jayakhya-samhita-1-adhika-1-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Nārāyaṇa himself is the speaker of the whole Pañcarātra. The Sātvata, the Pauṣkara and the Jayākhya are known as the three gems, issued directly from the Lord's mouth; the other tantras, also from his mouth, expand their essence by abridgement and expansion, as explanations do. Among the hundred and eight tantras, the Pārameśvara Saṃhitā explains the Pauṣkara, the Īśvara the Sātvata, and the Pādma the Jayākhya. Among the hundred and eight self-manifest places of the Lord four are chief — Śrīraṅgam, Veṅkaṭādri, Hastiśaila and Nārāyaṇādri; except at Veṅkaṭādri the three gems hold sway in the three others — the Sātvata at Yaduśaila, the Pauṣkara at Śrīraṅgam and the Jayākhya at Hastiśaila — while in practice the Pādma is followed at Hastiśaila, the Pārameśvara at Śrīraṅgam and the Īśvara at Yādavādri; of these Hastiśaila is pre-eminent.

> सात्त्वतं पौष्करं चैव जयाख्यं तन्त्रमुत्तमम् । रत्नत्रयमिति ख्यातं तद्विशेष इहोच्यते ॥

### 2.73-75 <a id="tea-jayakhya-samhita-2-73-75"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Thus the creation of Brahmā (brāhma sarga) has been told; many other gross creations of many forms have passed, beyond counting. Prior to it is the creation from primordial matter (prādhānika), and prior to that the highest, the Vaiṣṇava creation.
