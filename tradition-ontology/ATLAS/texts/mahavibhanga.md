### pj1.5.11 <a id="tea-mahavibhanga-pj1-5-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The first training rule is laid down on account of Sudinna for ten reasons: the excellence of the Saṅgha, its comfort, the restraint of shameless monks, the ease of well-behaved monks, the restraint of effluents in this life and their prevention in the next, the faith of those without faith, the increase of those with faith, the long endurance of the true Dhamma, and the support of the discipline.

> “tena hi, bhikkhave, bhikkhūnaṁ sikkhāpadaṁ paññapessāmi dasa atthavase paṭicca— saṅghasuṭṭhutāya, saṅghaphāsutāya, dummaṅkūnaṁ puggalānaṁ niggahāya, pesalānaṁ bhikkhūnaṁ phāsuvihārāya, diṭṭhadhammikānaṁ āsavānaṁ saṁvarāya, samparāyikānaṁ āsavānaṁ paṭighātāya, appasannānaṁ pasādāya, pasannānaṁ bhiyyobhāvāya, saddhammaṭṭhitiyā, vinayānuggahāya.

### pj3.1.1 <a id="tea-mahavibhanga-pj3-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

At Vesālī, after the Buddha praised the meditation on foulness, monks devoted to it became repelled by their bodies; some took their own lives, some had one another killed, and a sham ascetic, Migalaṇḍika, killed many for their bowls and robes before being remorseful. The Buddha then taught mindfulness of breathing, and laid down the third pārājika: intentionally depriving a human being of life, or praising or inciting death, entails defeat.

### pj4.1.1 <a id="tea-mahavibhanga-pj4-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

During a famine monks on the Vaggumudā praised each other's superhuman states to householders to get alms; the Buddha rebuked them and laid down the fourth pārājika: falsely claiming a superhuman state (jhāna, the fruits, the knowledges) entails defeat.
