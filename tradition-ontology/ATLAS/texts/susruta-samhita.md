**Availability:** digitized-original

The foundational compendium of the surgeons' school: the origin and eight limbs of Āyurveda, surgery and its instruments, the doṣas, dhātus and malas and the definition of health (Sū 15.41), the stages of disease (Sū 21), a Sāṃkhya account of creation and of the person (Śā 1), embryology, anatomy and the 107 marmas (Śā 6), therapeutics, rejuvenation, toxicology, and in the Uttaratantra eye, ear and head diseases, children's diseases, the seizing spirits (Utt 60) and the devices of exposition (Utt 65).

### sa.1.3-9 <a id="tea-susruta-samhita-sa-1-3-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The cause of all beings, itself uncaused, marked by sattva, rajas and tamas, eightfold in form, the source of the whole world, is the unmanifest (avyakta); it is the one support of many knowers of the field (kṣetrajña), as the ocean of watery things. From it arise the great principle, then ego-sense in three forms; from the sāttvika ego with the taijasa the eleven senses; from the elemental ego the five subtle elements and from them the five great elements — twenty-four principles. The person is the twenty-fifth, conscious when joined with effect and cause; prakṛti and puruṣa share beginninglessness, endlessness, eternity and all-pervasiveness, but prakṛti is one, unconscious and threefold, while the persons are many.

### sa.1.16-18 <a id="tea-susruta-samhita-sa-1-16-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In the Āyurveda treatises the knowers of the field are not taught as all-pervading and eternal; rather, knowers of the field that are not all-pervading are eternal, (born) among animals, men and gods. Their qualities are pleasure and pain, desire and aversion, effort, in- and out-breath, blinking, intellect, mind, resolve, reflection, memory, understanding, determination and perception of objects. Sāttvika qualities are kindness, sharing, forbearance, truth, dharma, faith, knowledge, intellect, memory, steadiness and non-attachment; rājasa and tāmasa their opposites.

### su.1.18-21 <a id="tea-susruta-samhita-su-1-18-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Surgery is held foremost among the eight tantras for its quick action and its use of instruments, knives, caustics and fire, and because it shares in all the others; it is eternal, meritorious, leading to heaven, fame, long life and livelihood. ### su.15.19-24 <a id="tea-susruta-samhita-su-15-19-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The supreme essence (tejas) of the dhātus from rasa to semen is ojas; that is called strength (bala). Ojas is of the nature of Soma, unctuous, white, cool, stable, flowing, clear, soft and smooth, the best seat of life; the whole body with its parts is pervaded by it, and without it bodies decay. It is diminished by injury, wasting, anger, grief, worry, fatigue and hunger. Its disorders are three — displacement, vitiation and loss — the last ending in fainting, delusion, delirium and death.

Vāta, pitta and śleṣman alone are the causes of the body's origin; unimpaired, set below, in the middle and above, they uphold the body as three pillars uphold a house — hence some call it 'three-pillared'; impaired, they cause its dissolution. ### su.21.8-10 <a id="tea-susruta-samhita-su-21-8-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

As Soma, the Sun and Wind uphold the world by releasing, taking and moving, so kapha, pitta and vāta uphold the body. There is no fire in the body other than pitta: being fiery, pitta is treated like fire in burning and digesting and is called the inner fire. The pitta between the stomachs digests the four kinds of food and separates doṣa, rasa, urine and faeces — the 'digesting fire' (pācaka); that in liver and spleen colours rasa (rañjaka); that in the heart accomplishes what is desired (sādhaka); that in the eye sees (ālocaka); that in the skin gives lustre (bhrājaka).

> visargādānavikṣepaiḥ somasūryānilā yathā | dhārayanti jagaddehaṃ kaphapittānilāstathā

### su.21.18-36 <a id="tea-susruta-samhita-su-21-18-36"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The doṣas accumulate in their seats (first time for treatment), are provoked (second), spread — singly, in combination or with blood, like a great flood breaking a dike (third), lodge in a particular region (fourth, the prodromal stage), become manifest as a disease (fifth), and become chronic or break open (sixth); untreated they become incurable. 'He who knows accumulation, provocation, spreading, lodging, manifestation and differentiation of the doṣas is a physician'; doṣas removed at accumulation do not go on to the later stages. Blood is never provoked apart from the doṣas.

> saṃcayaṃ ca prakopaṃ ca prasaraṃ sthānasaṃśrayam | vyaktiṃ bhedaṃ ca yo vetti doṣāṇāṃ sa bhavedbhiṣak
