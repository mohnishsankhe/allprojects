The hymn of the cosmic Person: thousand-headed, thousand-eyed, thousand-footed, covering the earth and exceeding it by ten fingers; all this is Puruṣa, past and future; one quarter of him is all beings, three quarters the immortal in heaven; from him Virāj was born and from Virāj he; the gods sacrificed Puruṣa as the oblation, and from that sacrifice came the verses, chants, metres and formulas, the animals, the four varṇas from his mouth, arms, thighs and feet, the moon from his mind, the sun from his eye, Indra and Agni from his mouth, wind from his breath, and the worlds; 'with the sacrifice the gods sacrificed to the sacrifice; these were the first dharmas'.

_Notes: Also Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā 31.1–16 (with the six 'Uttaranārāyaṇa' verses 31.17–22), Atharvaveda (Śaunaka) 19.6 and Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 3.12 (last two from memory). Its sixteen verses are used for the sixteen-fold worship in later temple and domestic ritual._
