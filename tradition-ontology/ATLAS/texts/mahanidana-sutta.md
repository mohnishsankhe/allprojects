# Mahānidāna Sutta

The Buddha rebukes Ānanda for thinking dependent origination plain: the chain of conditions (with the mutual dependence of consciousness and name-and-form), ways of declaring and not declaring a self, the seven stations of consciousness and two spheres, and the eight liberations.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 1.4-1.8 <a id="tea-mahanidana-sutta-1-4-1-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ānanda says dependent origination seems perfectly clear; the Buddha answers that it is deep and appears deep, and that through not understanding it this generation is tangled like a ball of string and does not pass beyond the states of misery and saṃsāra.

> “acchariyaṁ, bhante, abbhutaṁ, bhante. Yāva gambhīro cāyaṁ, bhante, paṭiccasamuppādo gambhīrāvabhāso ca, atha ca pana me uttānakuttānako viya khāyatī”ti. “Mā hevaṁ, ānanda, avaca, mā hevaṁ, ānanda, avaca. Gambhīro cāyaṁ, ānanda, paṭiccasamuppādo gambhīrāvabhāso ca. Etassa, ānanda, dhammassa ananubodhā appaṭivedhā evamayaṁ pajā tantākulakajātā kulagaṇṭhikajātā muñjapabbajabhūtā apāyaṁ duggatiṁ vinipātaṁ saṁsāraṁ nātivattati.

### 2-22 <a id="tea-mahanidana-sutta-2-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ageing-and-death has birth as its condition, birth becoming, becoming clinging, clinging craving, craving feeling, feeling contact, contact name-and-form, name-and-form consciousness, and consciousness has name-and-form as its condition; from craving also arise seeking, gain, possessiveness, guarding, and the taking up of sticks and swords. Had consciousness not descended into the mother's womb, name-and-form would not take shape there.

### 23-32 <a id="tea-mahanidana-sutta-23-32"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ways of describing a self: as having form or formless, limited or infinite; and ways of regarding self: 'feeling is my self', 'my self is without feeling', 'my self feels'. Each is examined: feeling is impermanent, conditioned and liable to cease, so taking it as self leads to 'my self has vanished'. A monk who regards no self clings to nothing in the world and is personally extinguished; of him it does not fit to say that he exists or does not exist after death.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: ultimate, dispute_

terms: [attā](../terms/atta.md), [vedanā](../terms/vedana.md) · concepts: [Not-self (anattā)](../concepts/anatta.md), [The Tathāgata after death](../concepts/tathagata-after-death.md) · disputes: [Is there a self (ātman, puruṣa, jīva) — an enduring conscious subject distinct from body and changing cognitions — or only a stream of impersonal factors designated 'person'?](../debates/is-there-a-self.md), [Should the questions about the eternity and extent of the world, soul and body, and the Tathāgata after death be answered?](../debates/avyakata.md)

### 33-36 <a id="tea-mahanidana-sutta-33-36"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Seven stations of consciousness and two spheres (the non-percipient beings and neither-perception-nor-non-perception); one who understands their origin, passing, gratification, danger and escape is freed by wisdom. The eight liberations; one who enters and emerges from them and has destroyed the effluents is 'freed both ways'.
