# Sāmaññaphala Sutta

**Alternate titles:** The Fruits of the Contemplative Life
**Language:** Pali
**Family:** ascetic
**Lineages:** [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md), [The śramaṇa movement](../lineages/sramana.md)

**Location in parent:** DN 2
**Availability:** digitized-original

King Ajātasattu asks the fruit of the contemplative life visible here and now; he reports the answers of the six rival teachers; the Buddha answers with the whole gradual training from going forth, virtue and sense restraint through the four jhānas to the six higher knowledges and the destruction of the effluents.

### 2-7 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-2-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Six ministers each recommend a teacher to King Ajātasattu with the same praise: he leads an order and a community and tutors a community, is well known and famous, a ford-maker (titthakara) esteemed by many, long gone forth, advanced in years. The six are Pūraṇa Kassapa, Makkhali Gosāla, Ajita Kesakambalī, Pakudha Kaccāyana, Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta and Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta.

> saṅghī ceva gaṇī ca gaṇācariyo ca ñāto yasassī titthakaro sādhusammato bahujanassa rattaññū cirapabbajito addhagato vayoanuppatto

### 16-32 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-16-32"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ajātasattu reports the answers of the six teachers: Pūraṇa Kassapa's doctrine that there is no evil or merit in deeds (non-action), Makkhali Gosāla's purification through wandering on in saṃsāra by fate, Ajita Kesakambalī's annihilationism (nothing survives death), Pakudha Kaccāyana's seven unmade bodies, the Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta's fourfold restraint and Sañjaya Belaṭṭhaputta's evasion; each answered a question about fruits with something else.

### 17 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pūraṇa Kassapa, asked for a visible fruit of the ascetic life, says: one who acts or makes others act — mutilating, tormenting, killing, stealing, housebreaking, robbing, committing adultery, lying — does no evil. Were one to make all living beings on earth into one heap of flesh with a razor-edged wheel, no evil would come of it; killing on the south bank of the Ganges brings no evil, giving and sacrificing on the north bank brings no merit; there is no merit from giving, self-control, restraint or truthful speech. The king reports this as a doctrine of non-action (akiriya).

> karoto kho, mahārāja, kārayato … karoto na karīyati pāpaṁ

### 20 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Makkhali Gosāla says: there is no cause or condition for the defilement of beings or for their purification; there is no doing by oneself or by another, no human doing, no power, no energy, no human strength or vigour. All beings, all living things, all creatures, all souls are without control, power or energy; transformed by destiny, circumstance and nature (niyati-saṅgati-bhāva-pariṇatā), they experience pleasure and pain in the six classes (abhijāti). There is no ripening of unripe karma or wearing out of ripe karma by conduct, vows, austerity or the holy life; pleasure and pain are measured out as with a measure and saṃsāra is fixed in its limits, with no increase or decrease. As a ball of string, thrown, runs out unwinding, so fool and wise alike, having run and wandered through saṃsāra, will make an end of suffering. The king calls this 'purification through transmigration' (saṃsāra-suddhi).

> sabbe sattā sabbe pāṇā sabbe bhūtā sabbe jīvā avasā abalā avīriyā niyatisaṅgatibhāvapariṇatā chasvevābhijātīsu sukhadukkhaṁ paṭisaṁvedenti

_level: unmarked · standpoint: causal · path: general · stage: unmarked · types: karma-liberation, world-fate, dispute_

### 20.7 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-20-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 23 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ajita Kesakambalī says: there is no gift, no sacrifice, no offering, no fruit or result of good and bad deeds, no this world, no other world, no mother, no father, no spontaneously born beings, no ascetics or brahmins in the world who, rightly practising, declare this world and the other from their own direct knowledge. This person is made of the four great elements; at death earth goes to the mass of earth, water to water, fire to fire, air to air, and the faculties pass into space. Four men with the bier as fifth carry the corpse; the footprints show as far as the burning-ground; the bones turn the colour of doves; offerings end in ashes. Giving is a doctrine of fools; the talk of those who assert existence is empty lying. Fool and wise alike are annihilated and destroyed at the breaking of the body and do not exist after death. The king calls this annihilation (uccheda).

> cātumahābhūtiko ayaṁ puriso

### 26 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pakudha Kaccāyana says: there are seven bodies (kāya), unmade, not of a made kind, uncreated, without a creator, barren, steady as a mountain peak, standing firm as a pillar: the bodies of earth, water, fire and air, pleasure, pain, and the soul (jīva) as seventh. They do not move, change or harm one another and cannot cause one another pleasure or pain. There is no killer or one who causes killing, no hearer, no knower; one who cuts off a head with a sharp sword takes no one's life — the sword passes through the gap between the seven bodies. The king says he answered 'one thing by another'.

> sattime, mahārāja, kāyā akaṭā akaṭavidhā animmitā animmātā vañjhā kūṭaṭṭhā esikaṭṭhāyiṭṭhitā

### 29 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-29"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta says: a Nigaṇṭha is restrained by the fourfold restraint (cātuyāma-saṃvara): he is curbed by all waters, yoked to all waters, cleansed by all waters, pervaded by all waters (sabbavāri-vārito … phuṭo); so restrained he is called 'one whose self is gone to the goal, controlled and steady' (gatatto yatatto ṭhitatto).

> idha, mahārāja, nigaṇṭho cātuyāmasaṁvarasaṁvuto hoti

### 32 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-32"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta says: 'If you ask me whether there is another world — if I thought there was, I would say so. But I do not say it is so, I do not say it is thus, I do not say it is otherwise, I do not say it is not, I do not say it is not not.' He answers the same way about there being no other world, both, and neither; about spontaneously born beings; about the fruit of good and bad deeds; and about whether the Tathāgata exists after death, does not, both, or neither. The king calls this evasion (vikkhepa).

> evantipi me no, tathātipi me no, aññathātipi me no, notipi me no, no notipi me no

### 33 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-33"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ajātasattu tells the Buddha that each teacher, asked for a visible fruit of the ascetic life, answered off the point, 'as if asked about a mango one answered about a breadfruit'; he neither approved nor rejected them, not wishing to disparage an ascetic or brahmin living in his realm; Sañjaya he judged 'the most foolish and confused of all these ascetics'.

### 35-36 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-35-36"></a>

The first visible fruit: a slave or servant who goes forth and lives restrained is no longer treated as a slave by the king but is honoured and protected.

### 40-42 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-40-42"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A Tathāgata arises in the world and teaches; a householder hears the Dhamma, gains faith, reflects that household life is crowded and the holy life hard to live at home 'like a polished shell', and goes forth; he lives restrained by the Pātimokkha, seeing danger in the slightest fault.

### 43-63 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-43-63"></a>

### 67-74 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-67-74"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Resorting to a secluded dwelling, sitting cross-legged with mindfulness established, he cleanses his mind of covetousness, ill will, dullness and drowsiness, restlessness and remorse, and doubt. The five hindrances are like a debt, a disease, a prison, slavery and a desert journey; their abandoning is like freedom from debt, health, release, emancipation and reaching safety — and on seeing them abandoned, gladness and joy arise.

### 75-82 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-75-82"></a>

### 83-84 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-83-84"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

With the mind concentrated, purified and malleable he directs it to knowledge and vision: he knows 'this body of mine is material, made of the four elements … and this consciousness of mine is supported here and bound up here', as one sees a thread strung through a clear beryl gem.

### 85-96 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-85-96"></a>

### 97-98 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-97-98"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

He directs the mind to the knowledge of the destruction of the effluents: he knows as it really is 'this is suffering … its origin … its cessation … the way', and 'these are the effluents …'; his mind is freed from the effluents of sensuality, becoming and ignorance, and he knows 'birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, done is what was to be done, there is no more coming back to any state of being' — like one who sees shells, pebbles and fish in a clear lake.

### 99-102 <a id="tea-samannaphala-sutta-99-102"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ajātasattu confesses the killing of his father; the Buddha accepts his confession as restraint for the future, and afterwards says that had the king not killed his father, the stainless eye of the Dhamma would have arisen in him on that very seat.
