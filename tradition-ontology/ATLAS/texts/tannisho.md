Sayings of Shinran and the compiler's laments: the moment one entrusts oneself to the inconceivable vow and the thought of saying the nenbutsu arises, one is embraced never to be abandoned; even a good person attains birth, how much more an evil person; Shinran has not one disciple; the nenbutsu is the single path free of hindrance, neither practice nor good; 'in the nenbutsu, no working is true working'; the vow was 'for me, Shinran, alone'; only the nenbutsu is true.

## Teachings (14: skeleton 14)

### 1 <a id="tea-tannisho-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When, entrusting ourselves to the inconceivable working of Amida's vow and believing we will be born, the thought of saying the nenbutsu arises within, we are at that moment brought to share in the benefit of being embraced, never to be abandoned. Amida's vow does not discriminate between young and old, good and evil; only shinjin is essential, for the vow is to save beings heavy with evil and ablaze with passions. So no other good is needed, for no good surpasses the nenbutsu, and no evil need be feared, for no evil obstructs Amida's vow.

### 2 <a id="tea-tannisho-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

To followers who had come from the Kantō at the risk of their lives to ask about the way to birth: 'As for me, Shinran, there is nothing but to receive and entrust myself to the teaching of my master that we are saved by Amida simply by saying the nenbutsu. Whether the nenbutsu is the seed of birth in the Pure Land or the act that will send me to hell, I do not know. Even if deceived by Hōnen I would have no regret, for I am incapable of any other practice and hell is my dwelling anyway. If Amida's vow is true, Śākyamuni's teaching cannot be false ... Beyond this, whether you take up the nenbutsu or abandon it is for each of you to decide.'

### 3 <a id="tea-tannisho-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Even a good person attains birth in the Pure Land, how much more an evil person. People commonly say the reverse, but that goes against the intent of the other-power vow: those who do good by self-power, lacking the mind of total reliance on other-power, are not the object of Amida's vow; when they turn from self-power and entrust themselves to other-power they attain the true land. Amida made the vow out of pity for us who, full of passions, cannot free ourselves by any practice; its intent is the evil person's attaining buddhahood, so the evil person who entrusts himself to other-power is the true object (shōki) of birth.

### 4 <a id="tea-tannisho-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Compassion differs between the path of sages and the Pure Land path: the former is to pity, sympathise with and care for beings, but one can hardly save them as one wishes; the latter is quickly to become a buddha by the nenbutsu and with great compassion benefit beings as one wishes. In this life, however much we pity others, we cannot fully save them; only the nenbutsu is the thoroughgoing compassion.

### 5 <a id="tea-tannisho-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Shinran has never said the nenbutsu even once as a memorial service for his father and mother, for all beings have been parents and siblings through many lives; the nenbutsu is not a good one does and transfers by one's own power; only after becoming a buddha can one save them.

### 6 <a id="tea-tannisho-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

It is absurd for followers of the exclusive nenbutsu to quarrel over 'my disciples' and 'others' disciples'; Shinran does not have a single disciple, for people say the nenbutsu through Amida's working, not his; we meet and part according to conditions.

### 7 <a id="tea-tannisho-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The nenbutsu is the single path free of hindrance: the gods of heaven and earth bow before the practitioner of shinjin, the demons and non-Buddhists cannot obstruct him, his karmic evil cannot bear fruit, and no good acts equal it.

### 8 <a id="tea-tannisho-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

For the practitioner the nenbutsu is neither a practice nor a good act: it is not practice because it is not done by one's own designs, not good because it is not produced by one's own designs; being wholly other-power and free of self-power, it is neither practice nor good.

### 9 <a id="tea-tannisho-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Yuien asks why, though he says the nenbutsu, he feels no dancing joy nor any wish to go quickly to the Pure Land. Shinran answers that he has the same doubt; that we do not rejoice at what should make us dance with joy is the working of blind passions — and the Buddha, knowing this, made the vow for such foolish beings, so we can be all the more sure. We cling to this old home of suffering and do not long for the Pure Land; when our karmic bond with this world is exhausted and we die, we will go there; the Buddha pities especially those without longing to go quickly.

### 13 <a id="tea-tannisho-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Asked whether he would do whatever Shinran said, Yuien agreed; told to kill a thousand people to be sure of birth, he said he could not kill even one. Shinran: then you see that it is not by a good mind that one does not kill, nor by an evil mind that one kills — if karmic conditions so arise, one might kill a hundred or a thousand. To fear doing evil and think that thereby one is fit or unfit for the vow is not to entrust oneself to it; yet Shinran also rebuked those who boasted of doing evil because the vow saves the evil ('do not take poison because there is an antidote').

### 15 <a id="tea-tannisho-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The compiler laments the view that one attains awakening in this very body while still full of passions: that is the teaching of the Shingon and Lotus schools, attainable by few; in the Pure Land teaching one entrusts oneself to the vow in this life and attains awakening in the Pure Land.

### postscript <a id="tea-tannisho-postscript"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Shinran often said: when I consider deeply the vow Amida pondered for five kalpas, it was for me, Shinran, alone; how grateful I am for the vow that resolved to save me, burdened with so much karma. ... I know nothing of what is good or evil; for the foolish being full of passions in this burning house of an impermanent world, all things are empty and false, without any truth; only the nenbutsu is true.
