# Alagaddūpama Sutta

The simile of the snake: grasping the teaching wrongly; the raft to be let go of; the six grounds for views; no self or what belongs to a self is found; 'I teach suffering and its cessation'; give up what is not yours.

## Teachings (7: skeleton 7)

### 10-12 <a id="tea-alagaddupama-sutta-10-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Some learn the Dhamma but do not examine its meaning with wisdom and learn it for criticising others and winning debates; wrongly grasped it leads to their harm, like a snake seized by the tail; rightly grasped it leads to welfare, like a snake pinned with a forked stick and seized by the neck.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission, practice_

### 13-14 <a id="tea-alagaddupama-sutta-13-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A man crossing a great river on a raft of grass and branches should, once across, leave it on the shore, not carry it on his head; understanding the Dhamma as similar to a raft, you should abandon even good states, how much more bad ones.

_level: bridging · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission, ultimate_

### 13.26 <a id="tea-alagaddupama-sutta-13-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 15-17 <a id="tea-alagaddupama-sutta-15-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Six grounds for views: regarding form, feeling, perception, formations, what is seen, heard, sensed, cognised, and the view 'that which is the world is the self; after death I shall be permanent, everlasting…' as 'this is mine, I am this, this is my self'. The noble disciple regards them 'this is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self', and so is not agitated about what is non-existent.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: ultimate, dispute_

terms: [anattā](../terms/anatta.md), [attā](../terms/atta.md) · concepts: [Not-self (anattā)](../concepts/anatta.md) · disputes: [Is there a self (ātman, puruṣa, jīva) — an enduring conscious subject distinct from body and changing cognitions — or only a stream of impersonal factors designated 'person'?](../debates/is-there-a-self.md)

### 25-27 <a id="tea-alagaddupama-sutta-25-27"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

If there were a self there would be what belongs to a self; since neither self nor what belongs to a self is found as true and established, is not the view 'the world is the self … permanent' an utterly foolish teaching? What is impermanent is suffering; what is suffering is not fit to be regarded as 'mine, I, my self'.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: ultimate, dispute_

### 37.5 <a id="tea-alagaddupama-sutta-37-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Formerly and now, what I teach is suffering and the cessation of suffering.

> Pubbe cāhaṁ, bhikkhave, etarahi ca dukkhañceva paññāpemi, dukkhassa ca nirodhaṁ.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: seeker · path: general · stage: all · types: karma-liberation, teacher-transmission_

### 40-41 <a id="tea-alagaddupama-sutta-40-41"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Give up what is not yours; giving it up will lead to your welfare and happiness. What is not yours? Form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness are not yours — as people carrying off the grass and sticks of Jeta's Grove would not be carrying off you.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: ultimate, practice_
