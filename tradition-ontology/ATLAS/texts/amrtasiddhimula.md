In the Derge Tengyur (Tōh 2285, among the siddhas' works of vol. zhi) the text opens with homage to Śrī Heruka and to a three-eyed, head-severed, red deity who dispels the fear of death, then announces the 'great seals' (phyag rgya chen po) and teaches the seated seal with the heel at the perineum — the mahāmudrā of the early haṭha texts — in a Buddhist tantric frame.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### colophon <a id="tea-amrtasiddhimula-colophon"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The colophon reads: 'The Amṛtasiddhi composed by the great master Virūpa is complete', and says it was translated by an Indian master, a yogin who had attained siddhi.

> bdud rtsi grub pa zhes bya ba slob dpon chen po bi rU pas mdzad pa rdzogs so

### homage <a id="tea-amrtasiddhimula-homage"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Tibetan opens: 'Homage to Śrī Heruka' and praises a red, three-eyed, head-severed deity who dispels the fear of death and holds the seal (mudrā) of yogins and yoginīs; it then announces that the 'great seals' (phyag rgya chen po) will be explained, whose mere hearing makes a person honoured by all the gods, and begins with the seated practice in which the left heel blocks the anus and the right leg is extended.

> dpal he ru ka la phyag 'tshal lo/ /spyan gsum rab ldan dbu bcad sku mdog dmar/
