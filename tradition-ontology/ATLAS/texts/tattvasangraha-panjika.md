Kamalaśīla's commentary on Śāntarakṣita's Tattvasaṅgraha. It preserves Lokāyata fragments: Purandara's statement that the Cārvākas accept inference as known in the world and reject only inference that goes beyond the worldly path; the sūtras 'tatsamudāye viṣayendriyasañjñā' and Kambalāśvatara's 'kāyād eva'; and it names the deniers of inference as 'Bārhaspatyas and others'.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 1-6.gold-test <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-panjika-1-6-gold-test"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Kamalaśīla, explaining that the Buddha's teaching is established by the two means of valid cognition, cites the Blessed One's words: as the wise accept gold after testing it by heating, cutting and rubbing, so, monks, my word is to be accepted after examination, not out of respect.

> tāpācchedācca nikaṣātsuvarṇamiva paṇḍitaiḥ | parīkṣya bhikṣabo grāhyaṃ madvaco natu gauravāt

### 1481-1482 <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-panjika-1481-1482"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Purandara says: 'The Cārvākas too accept inference as it is known in the world; what is rejected is the inference that some state by transgressing the worldly path.' Śāntarakṣita answers that the world too infers causes from effects, and in truth that is all the logicians claim.

> lokaprasiddham anumānaṃ cārvākair apīṣyata eva, yat tu kaiścil laukikaṃ mārgam atikramyānumānam ucyate tan niṣidhyate

### 1860 <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-panjika-1860"></a>

Kamalaśīla: 'and so their sūtra: in the aggregate of these are the designations object and sense' — for sense-organs and the rest are nothing apart from the great elements, being particular arrangements of them.
