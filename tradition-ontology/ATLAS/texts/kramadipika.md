An anonymous commentary on the Tattvasamāsa (its colophon: 'tattvasamāsākhyā sūtravṛttiḥ'), reckoned at 300 ślokas: a brahmin afflicted by the threefold suffering approaches Kapila, who explains each topic — the five functions of buddhi, the five sources of action, the five winds, the five agents, the five-jointed avidyā, the sixty topics, threefold bondage and liberation.

## Teachings (11: skeleton 11)

### 8 <a id="tea-kramadipika-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The five functions of buddhi are: resolve (of buddhi), self-assertion (of ahaṃkāra), desire (of manas), the task of awareness (of the sense capacities) and action (of the action capacities).

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 9 <a id="tea-kramadipika-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The five sources of action are steadfastness, faith, (seeking) happiness, the wish not to know and the wish to know; the first four lead to bondage, the wish to know (vividiṣā), womb of wisdom for meditators, to liberation.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: karma-liberation, practice_

### 10 <a id="tea-kramadipika-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The five winds: prāṇa at mouth and nose, apāna at the navel, samāna at the heart, udāna at the throat, vyāna at the joints, named from leading forth, leading down, leading together, rising and spreading.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: body-layers_

### 11 <a id="tea-kramadipika-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The five agents: the vaikārika does good actions, the taijasa bad ones, the bhūtādi deluded ones; the sānumāna good-and-deluded ones, the niranumāna bad-and-deluded ones.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: karma-liberation_

terms: [karmātman](../terms/karmatman.md)

### 12 <a id="tea-kramadipika-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The five-jointed avidyā: darkness (taking the eight non-selves, from the unmanifest to the subtle elements, as self), delusion (self-assertion for the eight powers), great delusion (thinking 'I am liberated' amid the ten seen and heard objects), gloom (aversion when the powers and objects are obstructed) and blind gloom (despair at death when they have been attained) — sixty-two divisions.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind, karma-liberation_

terms: [tamas](../terms/tamas.md), [moha](../terms/moha.md), [mahāmoha](../terms/mahamoha.md), [tāmisra](../terms/tamisra.md), [andhatāmisra](../terms/andhatamisra.md) · obstacles: [Error (viparyaya): the five-jointed avidyā](../obstacles/viparyaya.md)

### 16 <a id="tea-kramadipika-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The ten fundamental topics — existence (of pradhāna and puruṣa), oneness, purposiveness, being for another, otherness, non-agency, conjunction, separation, the plurality of puruṣas and the persistence of the body — are each proved by a Kārikā verse; with the fifty cognitions they are the sixty topics called Ṣaṣṭitantra.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission_

terms: [mūlikārtha](../terms/mulikartha.md), [ṣaṣṭitantra](../terms/sastitantra.md) · concepts: [The sixty topics of the Ṣaṣṭitantra](../concepts/sastitantra-sixty-topics.md)

### 18-19 <a id="tea-kramadipika-18-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The fourteenfold creation: eight divine (daiva, paiśāca, rākṣasa, yākṣa, gāndharva, aindra, prājāpatya, brāhma), five animal (domestic animals, birds, wild beasts, reptiles, plants) and one human; the threefold creation of bodily constituents: subtle (the subtle bodies), born of mother and father (hair, blood and flesh from the mother; sinew, bone and marrow from the father) and the gross elements.

_level: conventional · standpoint: cosmic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: world-fate, body-layers_

concepts: [The creation of beings and the worlds by guṇa](../concepts/fourteenfold-creation.md), [The subtle body (liṅga-śarīra)](../concepts/linga-sarira.md)

### 20 <a id="tea-kramadipika-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Bondage is threefold: bondage to prakṛti (absorption in prakṛti by taking the eight prakṛtis as highest); bondage to the modifications (of renunciants and worldly people ruled by the senses and attached to objects); and bondage through gifts (of students, householders, mendicants and forest-dwellers who give gifts with self-conceit, their minds struck by desire and delusion).

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: karma-liberation, ethics_

concepts: [Threefold bondage and threefold release](../concepts/threefold-bondage-and-release.md) · obstacles: [The three bonds (prakṛti-, vaikārika-, dakṣiṇā-bandha)](../obstacles/threefold-bondage.md), [Absorption into prakṛti mistaken for liberation](../obstacles/prakrtilaya.md)

### 21 <a id="tea-kramadipika-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Liberation is threefold: by the increase of knowledge, by the calming of the senses' passion, and by total exhaustion (of virtue and non-virtue), from which comes isolation.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: karma-liberation_

concepts: [Threefold bondage and threefold release](../concepts/threefold-bondage-and-release.md), [Isolation (kaivalya)](../concepts/kaivalya.md)

### 22 <a id="tea-kramadipika-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The means of knowledge are three; a reliable person (āpta) is one devoted to his own work, free of passion and aversion, knowing and virtuous; through the three, the principles are measured as grain by a measure.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### intro <a id="tea-kramadipika-intro"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A brahmin overwhelmed by the threefold suffering took refuge with the Sāṃkhya teacher, the great sage Kapila, told his family, name, lineage and Vedic study, and asked: 'What is highest here, what is true, by doing what would I be fulfilled?' Kapila answered with the aphorisms.
