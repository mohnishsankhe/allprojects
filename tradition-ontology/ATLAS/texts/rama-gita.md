After one's duties have purified the mind, one should approach a true guru: ignorance is the root of rebirth and only knowledge removes it, not action, so knowledge cannot be combined with ritual action; the meaning of 'that thou art' is grasped by partial implication; the self is distinct from the three bodies and three states; superimposition is explained; one meditates on the Self, dissolves the syllables of Oṃ into it, lives out prārabdha free of ego and merges in Rāma. Until all is seen as Rāma, one should worship him.

### 7.5.3-7 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-3-7"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Lakṣmaṇa takes refuge and asks how to cross the ocean of ignorance; Rāma: first, performing the actions prescribed for one's class and stage of life, one purifies the mind; then, giving up those means, one should resort to a true guru for self-knowledge.

### 7.5.8-10 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-8-10"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Action causes embodiment; from attachment come the agreeable and disagreeable, merit and demerit, then body again — existence turns like a wheel. Ignorance is its root cause and its removal is the aim; knowledge alone is able to destroy it, not action, which, born of ignorance, is opposed to it; so the wise should inquire into knowledge.

### 7.5.11-16 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-11-16"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Objection: action is enjoined by the Veda as knowledge is; it is a duty and assists knowledge; scripture declares a fault in omitting it; as a sacrifice needs its accessories, knowledge needs prescribed acts. Reply: knowledge, the final mental mode lit by pure awareness, destroys the whole apparatus of agent, act and instrument on which action depends; so the wise should give up action entirely — knowledge and action cannot be combined, being opposed — and remain devoted to contemplating the Self.

### 7.5.17-23 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-17-23"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

As long as the notion of self persists in the body through māyā, the injunctions of ritual apply; having negated everything by 'not this, not this' and known the supreme Self, one gives them up; when the luminous knowledge that removes the difference between Self and supreme dawns, māyā with all its apparatus dissolves at once and cannot arise again; the Taittirīya (not by action, offspring or wealth, by renunciation some attained immortality) and the Bṛhadāraṇyaka ('this much is immortality') declare it.

### 7.5.24-27 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-24-27"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

With faith, from the saying 'that thou art' and by the teacher's grace, the pure-minded knows the identity of Self and jīva and becomes unshakeable as Meru; understanding the words comes first: 'that' and 'thou' signify the supreme Self and the jīva, 'art' their oneness; setting aside the opposition of mediate and immediate, one grasps their common consciousness by implication — neither exclusive nor non-exclusive implication fits, but partial implication (bhāga-lakṣaṇā), as in 'this is that Devadatta'.

### 7.5.29-33 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-29-33"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Distinct from the gross body is the subtle body — mind, intellect, ten senses and breaths, made of the unquintuplicated elements — and the causal body, beginningless, indescribable māyā; the Self, though appearing in the sheaths like a crystal coloured by what is near it, is unattached, unborn, non-dual. The intellect's threefold states of waking, dream and sleep, excluding one another, are unreal in the eternal supreme.

### 7.5.34-41 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-34-41"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Having negated all by 'not this' and tasted the nectar of consciousness in the heart, give up the world as one gives up the fruit's rind after drinking its juice. The Self is never born, never dies, neither wanes nor grows. Superimposition (adhyāsa) is imagining one thing in another, as a snake in a rope; ego is the first superimposition on the Self; the jīva is consciousness reflected in the intellect born of beginningless ignorance, while the Self stands apart as the intellect's witness; by their mutual superimposition, like fire and iron in a heated ball, the insentient seems conscious and the conscious insentient.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: consciousness-mind, ultimate_

### 7.5.42-47 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-42-47"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Having gained knowledge from the teacher and the Veda, one should give up all insentient things and contemplate: I am light, unborn, non-dual, ever-shining, pure, a mass of consciousness, complete, blissful, actionless, ever free; such constant contemplation soon destroys ignorance as medicine a disease; seated in solitude with senses withdrawn, one should dissolve the world into the Self, the cause of all, and remain full, knowing nothing outside or inside.

### 7.5.48-52 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-48-52"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Before samādhi, one should contemplate the whole world as Oṃ alone: A is the waking person (viśva), U the dreaming (taijasa), M the sleeping (prājña); dissolve viśva, A, into U; taijasa, U, into M; and M, prājña, the cause, into the Self, dense consciousness — 'I am the supreme Brahman, ever free'. So abiding, content in one's own bliss, forgetting all, one remains like a still ocean.

### 7.5.53-57 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-53-57"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

To the yogin constantly practising samādhi, with senses withdrawn and inner enemies and six waves conquered, I am always visible; meditating thus day and night, free of all bonds, he lives out his prārabdha without ego and merges in me; knowing existence to be the source of fear and grief, he worships the Self of all, becoming one with me as water in the ocean, milk in milk, space in space, air in air.

### 7.5.58-62 <a id="tea-rama-gita-7-5-58-62"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Until one sees everything as myself, one should be devoted to my worship; to the faithful with intense devotion I am visible in the heart day and night. Whoever serves me, with or without attributes, purifies the three worlds like the sun. This knowledge, the one essence of all revelation, was sung by me; whoever reads it with faith and devotion to the teacher attains my form.
