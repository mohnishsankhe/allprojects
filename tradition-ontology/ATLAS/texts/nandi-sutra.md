A Cūlikāsūtra (a Mūlasūtra for Sthānakavāsīs): opening hymns to Mahāvīra, the saṅgha and the line of elders, then a treatise on the five kinds of knowledge (mati, śruta, avadhi, manaḥparyaya, kevala), with the classification of scripture (aṅga-praviṣṭa and aṅga-bāhya, kālika and utkālika), the contents of the twelve Aṅgas, and the teaching that the twelve-limbed canon is eternal.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### dvadasanga <a id="tea-nandi-sutra-dvadasanga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This twelve-limbed basket of the gaṇin never was not, never is not, never will not be; it was, is and will be — fixed, permanent, eternal, imperishable, unchanging, established, everlasting.

### jnana <a id="tea-nandi-sutra-jnana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Knowledge is of five kinds: sensory-mental (mati), scriptural (śruta), clairvoyant (avadhi), mind-reading (manaḥparyaya) and omniscient (kevala). The last three, gained by the soul directly, are 'direct' (pratyakṣa); the first two, gained through the senses and mind, are 'indirect' (parokṣa), though sensory knowledge may be called direct in the conventional sense.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### mithyasruta <a id="tea-nandi-sutra-mithyasruta"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Mahābhārata, the Rāmāyaṇa, the Vedas, Kauṭilya's treatise, the Buddhist and other scriptures are 'wrong scripture' (mithyā-śruta) for one of wrong view, but 'right scripture' for one of right view, since he uses them rightly; likewise the twelve Aṅgas are right scripture for the right-viewed and wrong scripture for the wrong-viewed.
