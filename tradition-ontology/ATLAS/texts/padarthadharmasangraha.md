Praśastapāda's systematic exposition of Vaiśeṣika (often called Praśastapādabhāṣya, though not a line-by-line commentary): after saluting Īśvara and Kaṇāda it declares knowledge of the similarities and dissimilarities of the six categories the cause of the highest good, arising from dharma manifested by the Lord's command; it fixes twenty-four qualities, gives the account of cosmic dissolution and creation by Maheśvara's will, divides cognition into knowledge (perception, inference, memory, seers' knowledge) and its opposites, treats yogic perception, dharma and adharma, and liberation.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Kiraṇāvalī (Udayana)](kiranavali-udayana.md), [Nyāyakandalī](nyayakandali.md), [Vyomavatī](vyomavati.md)

## Teachings (10: skeleton 10)

### dravya section (account of dissolution and creation) <a id="tea-padarthadharmasangraha-srsti-samhara"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

At the end of a Brahmā's lifespan, Maheśvara, wishing to give rest to beings wearied by transmigration, desires dissolution; the unseen powers of selves cease to operate, bodies and elements separate down to their atoms, and the atoms remain with the selves' merit and demerit. For their experience, Maheśvara then desires creation: motion arises in the atoms of air through the selves' unseen powers, and through dyads and so on the great elements arise in order; from atoms of fire and earth arises a great egg, in which Brahmā is born and appointed to create the world of beings.

### dravya section (self) <a id="tea-padarthadharmasangraha-atman"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The self, being subtle, is not perceived (by ordinary perception) but inferred from instruments such as the senses, which need an agent, and from the marks listed in the sūtra (breath, eye-movements, desire etc.); selves are distinct in each body.

### guṇa section (buddhi) <a id="tea-padarthadharmasangraha-buddhi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Cognition is of two kinds: knowledge (vidyā) — perception, inference, memory and the seers' knowledge (ārṣa) — and non-knowledge (avidyā) — doubt, error, indefinite cognition (anadhyavasāya) and dream.

### guṇa section (dharma: liberation) <a id="tea-padarthadharmasangraha-moksa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Knowledge of the truth of the categories, arising from dharma of the kind marked by withdrawal (nivṛtti), brings detachment; with attachment and aversion gone no new merit or demerit accrues, and when the accumulated merit and demerit are exhausted the self, without seed, no longer takes a body — a quiescence like that of a fire whose fuel is burnt, which is liberation.

### guṇa section (dharma) <a id="tea-padarthadharmasangraha-samanya-dharma"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The common means of dharma for all are faith, non-injury, benevolence to beings, truthfulness, non-stealing, celibacy, purity of intention, freedom from anger, ablution, use of pure things, devotion to a chosen deity, fasting and heedfulness; besides these there are the particular duties of each class and life-stage.

### guṇa section (enumeration) <a id="tea-padarthadharmasangraha-guna-uddesa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Seventeen qualities are named in the sūtra; seven more — heaviness, fluidity, viscidity, impression, merit, demerit and sound — are included by its word 'ca', making twenty-four.

### guṇa section (perception of yogins) <a id="tea-padarthadharmasangraha-yogipratyaksa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Yogins in the state of concentration (yukta) perceive their own and others' selves, ākāśa, space, time, atoms, air and mind with their qualities through a particular conjunction of self and mind aided by yoga-born merit; those out of concentration (viyukta) perceive subtle, hidden and distant things through the fourfold contact aided by that merit.

### guṇa section (seers' knowledge) <a id="tea-padarthadharmasangraha-arsa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

In the seers who compose the sacred texts there arises, from a particular merit, an intuitive knowledge of past, future, present and supersensible things, of dharma and the like; occasionally it arises in ordinary people too, as when a girl says 'tomorrow my brother will come'.

### opening (maṅgala and statement of purpose) <a id="tea-padarthadharmasangraha-mangala"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Having saluted Īśvara, the cause, and the sage Kaṇāda, the author sets out the compendium of the properties of the categories; knowledge of the truth of the similarities and dissimilarities of the six categories — substance, quality, motion, universal, particularity, inherence — is the cause of the highest good, and it arises from dharma manifested by the Lord's command.
