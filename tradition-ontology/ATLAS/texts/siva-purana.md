**Availability:** digitized-original

The great Śaiva Purāṇa: Śiva as niṣkala (worshipped in the liṅga) and sakala (in the image); the means of hearing, singing and reflecting; the liṅga of fire; Satī and Pārvatī; the jyotirliṅgas; the pañcākṣara and Śaiva initiation; and Upamanyu's five kinds of yoga, their obstacles and powers.

Hearing (śravaṇa), singing (kīrtana) and reflection (manana) on Śambhu are the great means (mahāsādhana), agreeable to the Veda; reflection with the mind is the greatest; one should first hear, then practise singing and reflection.

### 1.5.10-13 <a id="tea-siva-purana-1-5-10-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Śiva alone, being Brahman, is called partless (niṣkala) and, having form, also with parts (sakala); because he is niṣkala his formless liṅga is worshipped, because he is sakala his image with form; other gods, being only sakala, are worshipped only in images, never in the formless liṅga.

### 7.1.33.1-27 <a id="tea-siva-purana-7-1-33-1-27"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Vāyu teaches the Vedic (śrauta) Pāśupata vow heard in the Atharvaśiras: on the full moon of Caitra, with the teacher's leave, one vows it for life, twelve years or a shorter term, performs the virajā fire-offering for purification of the principles, takes the ash, and - matted-haired, shaven or with one lock, naked or in ochre, bark or a single cloth - smears the limbs with the six Atharvaṇa mantras beginning 'agni', applies the three lines, and practises Śiva-yoga at the three junctures; this vow gives enjoyment and release and removes the paśu-state.

### 7.2.37.6-11 <a id="tea-siva-purana-7-2-37-6-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Yoga, in brief, is the unwavering activity of the mind fixed on Śiva, other activities being restrained; it is fivefold: mantra-yoga (the mind resting on the meaning of the mantra through practice of the mantra), sparśa-yoga (the same beginning with breath-control), bhāva-yoga (free of mantra and sparśa), abhāva-yoga (the universe contemplated with its parts dissolved, the real appearing as nothing) and mahā-yoga, the highest, in which Śiva's own nature alone, without adjuncts, is contemplated.

### 7.2.37.12-20 <a id="tea-siva-purana-7-2-37-12-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Only one whose mind is dispassionate toward seen and scriptural objects is qualified for yoga; yoga has eight limbs (yama, niyama, āsana, prāṇāyāma, pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna, samādhi) or six (without yama and niyama); yama is non-harming, truth, non-stealing, celibacy and non-possession; niyama is purity, contentment, austerity, japa and devotion to the Lord (praṇidhi); posture is eightfold: svastika, padma, ardhendu, vīra, yoga, prasādhita, paryaṅka and as one likes.

### 7.2.37.21-30 <a id="tea-siva-purana-7-2-37-21-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Prāṇāyāma is the restraint of the body's own breath, in three parts - exhalation, inhalation and retention - done neither fast nor slow, preceded by purification of the channels and practised gradually; its grades bring sweat and trembling, and at the highest bliss, horripilation and tears, and babbling, whirling and swooning.

### 7.2.38.9-17 <a id="tea-siva-purana-7-2-38-9-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When these are calmed, divine upasargas arise that indicate powers: pratibhā (knowing the subtle, hidden, past, distant and future), śravaṇa (hearing all sounds effortlessly), vārtā (knowing the speech of all beings), darśana (seeing divine forms), āsvāda (tasting divine flavours) and vedanā (divine touch); these six are powers that, when yoga is spent, become losses; seeing even a part of yoga's powers one should think liberation will likewise come.

### 7.2.39.4-9 <a id="tea-siva-purana-7-2-39-4-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Meditation is at first with an object (saviṣaya) and then without (nirviṣaya); some hold there is no objectless meditation, since meditation is a continuity of cognition; the meditation with object rests on a form (like the rays of the rising sun), the objectless is awareness of the formless self; they are also called with seed and without seed; one should begin with the seeded, objective meditation and end with the seedless, objectless one.

### 7.2.39.10-13 <a id="tea-siva-purana-7-2-39-10-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Through prāṇāyāma the divine states arise in order: śānti (the calming of all calamities), praśānti (the destruction of darkness within and without), dīpti (light within and without) and prasāda (the clarity of the intellect), by which all instruments inner and outer become clear.
