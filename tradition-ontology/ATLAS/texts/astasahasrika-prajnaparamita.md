# Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā

`src:astasahasrika-prajnaparamita` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Perfection of Wisdom in 8,000 Lines, Daoxing banruo jing 道行般若經 (T224, Lokakṣema), Xiaopin banruo jing 小品般若經 (T227, Kumārajīva), 'phags pa shes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa brgyad stong pa (Toh 12)
**Original title:** अष्टसाहस्रिका प्रज्ञापारमिता
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** ascetic
**Lineages:** [Mahāyāna](../lineages/mahayana.md), [Madhyamaka](../lineages/madhyamaka.md), [Vajrayāna (Mantranaya) in India](../lineages/vajrayana.md), `lin:newar-vajrayana`
**Genre:** sūtra (prajñāpāramitā)
**Location in parent:** Nepal's nine dharmas (navadharma); Tibetan Kangyur Sher phyin section
**Attribution:** tradition: Spoken by the Buddha Śākyamuni (tradition's account: buddhavacana); much of it spoken by Subhūti through the Buddha's power (adhiṣṭhāna); in Tibetan and Nepalese accounts the Perfection of Wisdom was guarded by the nāgas until Nāgārjuna; scholarly: Anonymous; the earliest dated Prajñāpāramitā text; core usually placed c. 1st c. **Dates:** Tradition's account: the Buddha's lifetime (taught on the Vulture Peak); Scholarly account: c. 1st c. BCE-1st c. CE (core); Gāndhārī Bajaur fragment c. 1st-2nd c. CE; Lokakṣema's Chinese 179 CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 32 chapters (parivarta) in Vaidya's ed.; ch. 30-31 Sadāprarudita and Dharmodgata
**Availability:** digitized-original

The Perfection of Wisdom in 8,000 lines: in 32 chapters Subhūti, empowered by the Buddha, teaches that the bodhisattva does not apprehend any dharma, not even 'bodhisattva' or 'perfection of wisdom', and so courses in wisdom; all dharmas are like illusions and dreams, even nirvāṇa. It praises the perfection of wisdom as the mother of the buddhas and the book as an object of worship, describes Māra's deeds that obstruct it, the signs of the irreversible bodhisattva, skillful means that keep him from realizing the reality-limit too soon, and ends with the story of Sadāprarudita's search for the teaching from Dharmodgata.

## Teachings (25: skeleton 25)

### 1 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Subhūti says he does not find, apprehend or see any dharma called 'bodhisattva', nor any called 'perfection of wisdom'; if, hearing this, a bodhisattva's mind is not cowed or frightened, that bodhisattva is to be instructed in the perfection of wisdom — this very non-apprehension is his coursing in it.

### 1 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The bodhisattva coursing in the perfection of wisdom should train so as not even to conceive of (manyeta) the thought of awakening, because that thought is no-thought: the essential nature of thought is luminous.

> tathā hi - taccittamacittam | prakṛtiścittasya prabhāsvarā ||

### 1 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The bodhisattva is 'great being' (mahāsattva) because he is armed with the great armor: he thinks 'I must lead countless beings to nirvāṇa', and yet there are none who lead or are led; he leads them all to nirvāṇa and no being at all has been led to nirvāṇa, because that is the true nature of dharmas — like a magician who conjures a great crowd and makes it vanish.

### 1 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The bodhisattva does not take hold of form or the other aggregates and does not even think of nirvāṇa; yet he does not enter nirvāṇa midway, before completing the ten powers of a Tathāgata and the other buddha-qualities.

### 1 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Hearing that consciousness is like an illusion, bodhisattvas new to the vehicle may be frightened; they tremble if they are in the hands of bad friends; the good friends of the great-armored bodhisattva are those who teach the perfections and expose Māra's deeds.

### 2 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Subhūti tells the gods that beings are like an illusion, like a dream; all dharmas, the fruits of the path, perfect buddhahood and even nirvāṇa are like an illusion and a dream — and if there were any dharma more distinguished than nirvāṇa, that too would be like an illusion and a dream.

> nirvāṇam api devaputrā māyopamaṃ svapnopamamiti vadāmi, kiṃ punaranyaṃ dharmam

### 2 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-2-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Śāriputra answers where the Tathāgata stands: nowhere, for the Tathāgata's mind is unsupported (apratiṣṭhita-mānasa); he stands neither in the conditioned nor the unconditioned element, nor has he emerged from them. So too should the bodhisattva stand.

> apratiṣṭhitamānaso hi tathāgato 'rhan samyaksambuddhaḥ

### 3 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Whoever writes, honors and worships a copy of the perfection of wisdom with flowers, incense and garlands gains more merit than one who fills the world with stūpas of the Tathāgata's relics, for the relics are honored because they are pervaded by the perfection of wisdom; the gods guard the place where it is kept.

### 6 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Rejoicing (anumodanā) in all merit and dedicating (pariṇāmanā) it to awakening is supreme when done without apprehending the merit, the dedicator or awakening as things; dedication made while seizing on marks is like good food mixed with poison — 'poisoned and with a thorn'.

> saviṣaḥ saśalyo hyeṣaḥ pariṇāmaḥ

### 7 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

### 8 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The purity of form is the purity of the fruit, and the purity of the fruit is the purity of form; the two are not two, not divided, not cut apart — likewise for the other aggregates and all-knowledge; this purity is 'deep'.

> yā subhūte rūpaviśuddhiḥ, sā phalaviśuddhiḥ, yā phalaviśuddhiḥ, sā rūpaviśuddhiḥ

### 11 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Māra's deeds (mārakarma) that obstruct the perfection of wisdom: eloquence that comes slowly or is scattered; copying it while yawning, laughing or distracted; losing interest; turning from it to seek sūtras that lead to the śrāvaka and pratyekabuddha levels — like a builder who wants to build a palace the size of Vaijayanta but measures it by the orbit of the sun and moon.

### 12 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

As sons protect and cherish the mother who bore them, so the Tathāgatas cherish the perfection of wisdom: she is their mother, their genetrix, who shows them the world; understanding suchness they know the world's suchness, non-falseness and unaltered suchness, and thus are called Tathāgata.

> prajñāpāramitā tathāgatānāmarhatāṃ samyaksambuddhānāṃ mātā jananī janayitrī

### 14 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A bodhisattva with faith and zeal but without the perfection of wisdom and skillful means will fall short, like a man crossing water with an unbaked pot or a merchant launching an unrepaired ship laden with goods: both are lost; with wisdom and skillful means he is like a baked pot or a well-fitted ship that reaches its destination.

### 16 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The suchness of the Tathāgata and the suchness of all dharmas are one suchness, undivided, unaltered; Subhūti is 'born after' (anujāta) the Tathāgata because he follows this suchness; the suchness is not past, future or present.

A bodhisattva without the perfection of wisdom and skillful means is like a huge bird without wings that jumps from the heaven of the Thirty-Three to reach Jambudvīpa: it cannot turn back and is broken on the ground — he falls to the level of the śrāvakas or pratyekabuddhas.

### 17 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Signs, marks and tokens of the irreversible (avinivartanīya) bodhisattva: he does not look to other teachers or doubt; he establishes others in the ten wholesome ways and does not break them even in dreams; he is not moved when Māra, even in the Buddha's form, tells him the teaching is not the Buddha's word.

### 19 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Does karma done in a dream accumulate? As all dharmas are like dreams, a dream-deed does not accumulate in itself; but when one wakes and dwells on it with discrimination (e.g. 'I killed'), it does accumulate. The bodhisattva may cultivate the three doors of liberation even in dreams. The goddess of the Ganges receives the prediction that she will become the buddha Suvarṇapuṣpa.

_level: bridging · standpoint: causal · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: intermediate · types: karma-liberation, powers-experiences_

### 20 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Endowed with skillful means and upheld by the perfection of wisdom, the bodhisattva dwells in emptiness and the signless yet does not realize the reality-limit (bhūtakoṭi) until his roots of merit are ripe for full awakening — like a bird that moves through space without falling and without resting anywhere, or an archer who keeps arrows in the air by shooting each into the last; his resolve not to abandon beings is his protection.

> prajñāpāramitayā ca parigṛhīto bhūtakoṭiṃ na sākṣātkaroti

The good friends (kalyāṇamitra) of the bodhisattva are the buddhas and the six perfections; the perfection of wisdom goes before the other five as their leader, guide, mother and nurse — without it the other five do not even receive the name 'perfection'.

### 24 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-24"></a>

A bodhisattva who, predicted or not, quarrels with, despises or is angry at other bodhisattvas must remain in saṃsāra as many aeons as the moments of that anger; he should resolve never to be angry even at those who wrong him and to confess such thoughts.

### 26 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The bodhisattva coursing in the perfection of wisdom does not discriminate that awakening is near or the śrāvaka level far, as a man conjured by a magician does not think 'the magician is near, the crowd far', because the perfection of wisdom is free of discrimination.

### 30 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sadāprarudita, told by a voice in the forest to go east without regard for body, life or gain, seeks the perfection of wisdom; to honor the teacher Dharmodgata he tries to sell his own body in the market, and when Māra blocks buyers he cuts his flesh and blood for Śakra, who is testing him; his resolve is rewarded.

### 31 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-31"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dharmodgata teaches that the Tathāgatas neither come from anywhere nor go anywhere, for non-arising, the reality-limit, emptiness and suchness neither come nor go, and the Tathāgata is these; like the water of a mirage that a thirsty man runs after, which came from no ocean and goes to none.

### 32 <a id="tea-astasahasrika-prajnaparamita-32"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Buddha entrusts the perfection of wisdom to Ānanda: it brings forth the knowledge of the all-knowing; it is to be heard, learned, kept, recited, mastered, copied and honored; while it is honored the Buddha is present.
