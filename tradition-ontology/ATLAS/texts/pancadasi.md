Vidyāraṇya's fifteen-chapter verse manual in three pentads — five 'discriminations' (viveka: of reality, the elements, the sheaths, duality, the great sayings), five 'lamps' (dīpa: the picture, contentment, the immutable, meditation, the theatre) and five 'blisses' (ānanda: of yoga, of the self, of non-duality, of knowledge, of objects). It distinguishes māyā (pure sattva) as the adjunct of Īśvara from avidyā (impure sattva) as the adjunct of the jīva, and gives the canvas, four-space, tenth-man and theatre-lamp illustrations and the seven states of the jīva.

### 1.3 <a id="tea-pancadasi-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Sound, touch and the other objects known in waking differ from one another; distinct from them, the consciousness of them, being uniform, does not differ.

### 1.15-17 <a id="tea-pancadasi-1-15-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Prakṛti, with the reflection of Brahman in it, is twofold: with pure sattva it is māyā, and Brahman reflected in it, controlling it, is the omniscient Īśvara; with impure sattva it is avidyā, and the reflection subject to it is the jīva, many owing to its variety; this avidyā is the causal body, and the jīva identifying with it is prājña.

### 6 <a id="tea-pancadasi-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

As a canvas is washed, starched, outlined and coloured, so the supreme self is pure consciousness, the inner controller (antaryāmin), the cosmic subtle being (Hiraṇyagarbha) and the cosmic gross being (Virāj); the painted figures are the beings, and the painted clothes on them the semblance of consciousness (cidābhāsa).

### 7 <a id="tea-pancadasi-7-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The tenth man: ten men who crossed a river count themselves, each forgetting to count himself, and grieve for the 'lost' tenth; told 'there is a tenth', they know it mediately; told 'you are the tenth', each knows it immediately and grief ends — so the mahāvākya gives immediate knowledge of the self.

### 8 <a id="tea-pancadasi-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The immutable (kūṭastha) consciousness, the substratum of both bodies, is distinct from the semblance of consciousness (cidābhāsa) that animates the intellect; the jīva is the two taken together, and discrimination separates them.

### 9 <a id="tea-pancadasi-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Meditation on the attributeless Brahman, though an act of mind, leads to liberating knowledge, as mistaking the light of a gem for the gem leads one to the gem (a 'consistent error', saṃvādi-bhrama); inquiry is best, but those unable to inquire should meditate.
