The eighth Aṅga: stories of those who 'made an end' (antakṛt) of rebirth in that very life — among them Gajasukumāla, Kṛṣṇa's young brother, who kept equanimity while a brāhmaṇa set burning coals on his head, and the gardener Arjuna, a murderer who became a monk and bore abuse patiently.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 3.8 <a id="tea-antakrddasa-3-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Gajasukumāla, Kṛṣṇa's young brother, became a monk under Ariṣṭanemi and stood in meditation in a cremation ground; the brāhmaṇa Somila, his would-be father-in-law, set a rim of clay on his head and filled it with burning coals. Without anger, regarding Somila as a helper in shedding karma, he attained omniscience and liberation that very night.

### 6.3 <a id="tea-antakrddasa-6-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Arjuna the gardener, possessed by a yakṣa, killed many people in Rājagṛha; stopped by the layman Sudarśana's fearless faith, he became a monk of Mahāvīra, bore the beatings and curses of the townspeople with equanimity for six months, and attained liberation.
