Citsukha's dialectical treatise (four chapters): defines self-luminosity as 'being fit for immediate usage while not being an object of cognition', proves the self self-luminous, defends indescribable ignorance, refutes Nyāya's categories and defines falsity as being the counterpositive of an absolute absence in its own locus.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1 <a id="tea-tattvapradipika-citsukha-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Self-luminosity (svaprakāśatva) is being fit to be referred to as immediate while not being an object of cognition; consciousness alone has this character.

> avedyatve sati aparokṣavyavahārayogyatvam

### falsity <a id="tea-tattvapradipika-citsukha-falsity"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Falsity (mithyātva) is being the counterpositive of an absolute absence in the very locus where the thing appears (as the snake is absent in all three times in the rope where it appears).
