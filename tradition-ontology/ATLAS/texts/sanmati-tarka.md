# Sanmatitarka (Sammaisutta)

**Dates:** Tradition's account: contemporary of king Vikramāditya of Ujjain; Scholarly account: c. 5th c. CE (debated: 4th–7th c.); (confidence low)
**Structure:** 3 kāṇḍas, c. 167 gāthās
**Availability:** digitized-original

Siddhasena Divākara's Prakrit treatise in three books (kāṇḍa) on the standpoints (nayas), on knowledge and perception (defending their identity in the omniscient), and on the object as substance and modes; it holds that the two root standpoints are the substance-view and the mode-view, that each standpoint taken alone is false and all together true, and that there are as many rival doctrines as there are standpoints. Revered by both sects.

### 1.3 <a id="tea-sanmati-tarka-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The root expounders of the Tīrthaṅkara's word, in its collective and particular aspects, are the substance-standpoint and the mode-standpoint; the others are their subdivisions.

### 3.53 <a id="tea-sanmati-tarka-3-53"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Time, own-nature, destiny, former deeds and human effort, each held one-sidedly as the cause, are wrong views; taken together they are right view.

### kanda-1 <a id="tea-sanmati-tarka-kanda-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Each standpoint taken by itself, excluding the others, is false; the standpoints joined together and mutually dependent are right view — like separate gems that become a necklace only when strung together.

### kanda-2 <a id="tea-sanmati-tarka-kanda-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the omniscient, knowledge and perception are not two cognitions occurring in succession or at once, but one: when all obstruction is gone, the distinction of general and particular grasping falls away.
