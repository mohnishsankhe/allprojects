# Nāradaparivrājaka Upaniṣad

`src:naradaparivrajaka-upanisad` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md), [Saṃnyāsa — the renunciate ideal of the Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣads](../lineages/sannyasa.md), [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
**Genre:** upaniṣad
**Attribution:** tradition: Śruti: revealed, without human author (apauruṣeya); the Muktikā presents it as one of the 108 Upaniṣads that Rāma teaches Hanumān, each belonging to a Vedic śākhā; scholarly: anonymous; assigned to a Veda by the Muktikā list, not by any surviving śākhā transmission; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Śruti: eternal and authorless; no human date; Scholarly account: Olivelle (1992) separates an older group (Āruṇi, Laghu-Saṃnyāsa/Kuṇḍikā, Kaṭhaśruti, Paramahaṃsa, Jābāla, Brahma), probably from around the turn of the common era, from a later medieval group (c. 12th–15th c.), largely composed in Advaita milieus in South India.; (confidence low)
**Availability:** digitized-original

Nine upadeśas in which Nārada and Brahmā teach the wandering ascetic's life: eligibility, the rite, conduct, the four and six kinds of renouncer and their marks, dress, food and worlds attained, the paramahaṃsa's rules, the sixteen-measured praṇava and Brahman; the most comprehensive Saṃnyāsa Upaniṣad.

## Teachings (30: sourced 30)

### 3.1-11 (eligibility) <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-3-1-11-eligibility"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Those ineligible to renounce include the impotent, the fallen, the deformed, the effeminate, the deaf, children, the mute, heretics, emperors, sectarian mark-bearers, Vaikhānasas, Hara-dvijas, paid teachers and those without fires; but if they have renounced they are fit for the great sayings; the sick (ātura) may renounce when breath is departing.

### 3.12-15 <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-3-12-15"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

One should renounce when dispassion arises, otherwise one falls; a renouncer still full of passion goes to hell; one who guards tongue, genitals, belly and hand may renounce even unmarried.

### 3.56-59 <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-3-56-59"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

One ascetic alone is proper; two make a couple, three a village, more a city — then comes gossip; asked anything, he answers only 'Nārāyaṇa'.

### 3.60-61 <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-3-60-61"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

### 3.62-68 <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-3-62-68"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Six marks: he is tongueless (speaks little), a eunuch (unmoved by a newborn, a girl of sixteen or a woman of a hundred alike), lame (goes no further than a yojana for alms), blind (looks only a short distance ahead), deaf (does not listen to praise or abuse), and foolish (unmoved by objects).

`sourced` · confidence low

Six things he must never see, six never think of, and six that cause his fall — among them a couch, white clothes, talk of women, sleep by day and riding in vehicles.

### 4.1-37 (conduct) <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-4-1-37-conduct"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

He does not announce his name or lineage; does not converse with women; does not give medicine, mantras or blessings; stays one night in a village and five in a town, four months in the rains; the haṃsa bathes once, the paramahaṃsa not at all; outer marks are useless — knowledge alone is the cause; from the absence of marks comes kaivalya.

### 4.38 (rite) <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-4-38-rite"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The rite: penances; eight śrāddhas (to gods, seers, divine beings, humans, elements, fathers, mothers and oneself); the virajā homa; placing the fires in the self; dissolving the Sāvitrī into the vyāhṛtis; the declaration 'I have risen above the desire for wife, wealth and worlds', said softly, middling and loud; granting fearlessness; plucking the topknot, casting the thread into water; then he departs north, naked or with his marks.

### 7.1 <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-7-1"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

He should regard ghee as blood, food from a single house as flesh, perfume as filth, salt as an outcaste, oil-massage as sex, women as snakes, gold as poison, an assembly as a cremation ground, a capital as the Kumbhīpāka hell.

### 7.3-4 <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-7-3-4"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Rules differ by kind: bathing thrice, twice, once, mentally, with ash or with air; marks from ūrdhvapuṇḍra and tripuṇḍra to none; clothing from two garments to nakedness; worship external for kuṭīcaka and bahūdaka, mental for haṃsa and paramahaṃsa, 'so'ham' for turīyātīta and avadhūta; hearing, reflection, contemplation respectively.

### 8 <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-8"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The praṇava has sixteen measures — a, u, m, the half-measure, nāda, bindu, kalā, kalātīta, śānti, śāntyatīta, unmanī, manonmanī, purī, madhyamā, paśyantī and parā — and four quarters.

### 9 <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-9"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Brahman is taught with verses of the Śvetāśvatara and 'not this, not this'; the wanderer lives like a frightened deer, by the bee's way, knowing 'I am He'.

### 1 (frame) <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-1-frame"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Nārada, visiting the sages led by Śaunaka at Naimiṣa, teaches the renunciant's way: a life of twenty-five years in each stage and the saṃskāras, culminating in renunciation.

### 5 (four kinds) <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-5-four-kinds"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Renunciation is truly one, but is spoken of as fourfold: from dispassion (vairāgya), from knowledge (jñāna), from knowledge-and-dispassion, and ritual (karma) renunciation; ritual renunciation is occasioned (for the sick) or unoccasioned (in due order).

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: general · stage: advanced · types: ethics, karma-liberation_

concepts: [Four kinds of renunciation](../concepts/four-kinds-of-sannyasa.md), [Emergency and regular renunciation](../concepts/atura-krama-sannyasa.md)

### 5 (other śāstras) <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-5-other-sastras"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

For the paramahaṃsa, turīyātīta and avadhūta other studies are useless — like a camel carrying saffron or ornaments on a corpse; they study only praṇava; not yoga-śāstra, Sāṃkhya or mantra-tantra. Liberation is not by staff, shaving, garb or show.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: general · stage: advanced · types: ethics, dispute_

### 5 (six kinds) <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-5-six-kinds"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Six kinds of renouncer: kuṭīcaka (topknot, thread, staff, water-pot; eats in one place), bahūdaka (eight mouthfuls of alms from many houses), haṃsa (matted hair, alms unplanned), paramahaṃsa (no topknot or thread; alms from five houses, hand as bowl; one loincloth; bamboo staff; ash), turīyātīta (like a cow, takes food in the mouth; naked; body as corpse) and avadhūta (no rule; takes food like the python from all castes except the fallen).

_level: conventional · standpoint: ethical-social · path: general · stage: advanced · types: ethics_

concepts: [The kinds of renouncer](../concepts/six-kinds-of-renunciant.md)

### 5 (worlds) <a id="tea-naradaparivrajaka-upanisad-5-worlds"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The sick and the kuṭīcaka reach the earthly worlds, the bahūdaka heaven, the haṃsa the world of austerity, the paramahaṃsa the world of truth; the turīyātīta and avadhūta attain kaivalya in the Self, as the insect becomes the wasp; one's last thought decides.

_level: conventional · standpoint: cosmic · path: general · stage: advanced · types: karma-liberation, death-dying_
