**Availability:** digitized-original

Jayarāśi Bhaṭṭa's 'Lion that upsets all principles', the only surviving work of the Lokāyata current. It examines in turn the definitions of the means of knowledge given by the Naiyāyikas, Mīmāṃsakas, Buddhists and Sāṃkhyas and argues that none can be established; the Bṛhaspati sūtra's list of four elements was stated only 'to reflect' (pratibimbana) what the world accepts, and even those 'do not stand when examined'. Worldly practice goes on unexamined; in it the fool and the wise are alike.

### 0.1 <a id="tea-tattvopaplavasimha-0-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Even those who know the highest truth must follow the worldly path; with regard to worldly conduct the fool and the wise are alike.

> lokavyavahāraṃ prati sadṛśau bālapaṇḍitau

### 0.2 <a id="tea-tattvopaplavasimha-0-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Objection: if all principles are upset, why does the sūtra say 'Now we shall explain the principle: earth, water, fire and air are the principles; their aggregate is called body, senses and objects'? Answer: it is said for another purpose — to reflect (pratibimbana) what is accepted in the world; even earth and the rest, well known in the world, do not stand when examined — how much less the others?

> pṛthivyādīni tattvāni loke prasiddhāni / tāny api vicāryamāṇāni na vyavatiṣṭhante

### 0.3 <a id="tea-tattvopaplavasimha-0-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The establishing of objects of knowledge depends on the means of knowledge, and the means depend on correct definitions; where these are lacking, neither can be said to exist.

> sallakṣaṇanibandhanaṃ mānavyavasthānam / mānanibandhanā ca meyasthitiḥ

### 1 <a id="tea-tattvopaplavasimha-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Nyāya definition of perception — cognition arising from contact of sense and object, non-verbal, non-deviating, determinate — fails: its non-deviation cannot be known, neither from the faultlessness of its causes (which are beyond the senses), nor from absence of later contradiction (which may simply not have arisen yet), nor from success in practice, without circularity.

### 2-4 <a id="tea-tattvopaplavasimha-2-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The same examination overturns the Mīmāṃsā definition of a means of knowledge as cognition of what was not known before, and the Buddhist definition of perception as free of conceptual construction and non-erroneous: each qualifier either excludes nothing or cannot be ascertained.

### 4.25a <a id="tea-tattvopaplavasimha-4-25a"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

If cognition were produced from form as its material cause, it would have the nature of form; then there would be no self that goes to another world, and so no other world: 'having this in mind, the venerable Bṛhaspati said: because there is no one who goes to the other world, there is no other world'.

> idam eva cetasi samāropyāha bhagavān bṛhaspatiḥ paralokino 'bhāvāt paralokābhāvaḥ

### 5 <a id="tea-tattvopaplavasimha-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Inference too cannot be established: the connection (between mark and inferred) cannot be grasped for particulars, and for universals it proves what is already known; and the universal 'smokeness' itself does not exist to be grasped.
