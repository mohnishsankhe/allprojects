The Buddha displays countless lotuses whose closing petals reveal buddhas seated within, and explains: all beings, though in the midst of defilements, have within them the Tathāgata's knowledge, eyes and body, seated in meditation, unmoving — the tathāgatagarbha. Nine similes: a buddha in a withered lotus, honey amid bees, a kernel in its husk, gold in filth, a treasure beneath a poor man's house, a seed within a fruit, a golden image wrapped in rags, a future king in the womb of a destitute woman, a golden statue inside a clay mould.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1 <a id="tea-tathagatagarbha-sutra-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Buddha makes countless lotuses appear in the sky, each holding a seated buddha; the petals wither and the buddhas are revealed. So, he says, with the buddha-eye he sees that all beings, amid greed, anger and delusion, have within them the Tathāgata's knowledge, eyes and body, seated in meditation, unmoving; though they wander in defilements, the tathāgatagarbha is unstained, full of virtues, no different from his own.

### 2 <a id="tea-tathagatagarbha-sutra-2"></a>

Nine similes for the tathāgatagarbha hidden in defilements: a buddha inside a withered lotus; honey surrounded by a swarm of bees; a kernel within its husk; gold fallen into a pit of filth; a treasure buried under a poor man's house; a seed inside a fruit that will grow into a great tree; a golden image wrapped in rags left on the road; a poor, ugly woman carrying in her womb a future universal monarch; a golden statue inside a blackened clay mould.

### 3 <a id="tea-tathagatagarbha-sutra-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Whether or not buddhas appear in the world, the tathāgatagarbha of all beings is eternally unchanging; the buddhas teach so that beings may remove their defilements and reveal it.
