# Siddhāntaśikhāmaṇi

`src:siddhantasikhamani` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Reṇukāgastyasaṃvāda, Vīraśaivadharmanirṇaya (colophon)
**Original title:** सिद्धान्तशिखामणि
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md), [Pañcācārya (Pañcapīṭha) tradition](../lineages/pancacarya.md)
**Genre:** śāstra (dialogue)
**Authors:** 
  - [Śivayogi Śivācārya](../teachers/sivayogi-sivacarya.md) — role: compiler; attribution: accepted
  - [Reṇukācārya (Revaṇasiddha)](../teachers/renukacarya.md) — role: revealer; attribution: traditional
  - [Śivayogi Śivācārya](../teachers/sivayogi-sivacarya.md) — role: author; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: Śiva's teaching to the Goddess and Nandin, transmitted by Reṇuka to Agastya and set down by Śivayogi Śivācārya (SSM 1.28–31); scholarly: a Sanskrit compilation by Śivayogi Śivācārya; its date is debated (placed variously between the 12th and 15th c.); confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: the teaching is anādi; Reṇuka's descent in the Kali age; Scholarly account: debated: 12th–15th c.; (confidence low)
**Structure:** 21 chapters (pariccheda); ch. 1 frame and author's lineage; 2–4 descent of Reṇuka and meeting with Agastya; 5–14 the 44 aṅga-sthalas under bhakta (15), māheśvara (9), prasādi (7), prāṇaliṅgi (5), śaraṇa (4), aikya (4); 15–20 the 57 liṅga-sthalas; 21 Reṇuka's dialogue with Vibhīṣaṇa and his return into the liṅga
**Availability:** digitized-original

The principal Sanskrit scripture of the Pañcācārya strand: the teaching of the gaṇa Reṇuka (Revaṇasiddha), who emerged from the Someśvara liṅga at Kollipāki, to the sage Agastya on the Malaya mountain. It sets out Vīraśaiva doctrine as Śivādvaita consistent with Veda and Āgama and maps the path as 101 sthalas (44 on the side of the soul, 57 on the side of the liṅga) under the six great sthalas from bhakta to aikya.

  - kind: original; name: Śaivabhāratī Śodhapratiṣṭhāna, Vārāṇasī 2006, with Maritoṇṭadārya's Tattvapradīpikā (Muktabodha e-text M00207)

### 1.3 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-1-3"></a>

`skeleton` · confidence high

### 4.11-12 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-4-11-12"></a>

### 5.3-8 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-3-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Many siddhāntas are renowned, differing in conduct and meaning according to taste — Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Pāñcarātra, the Vedas and Pāśupata — and are not to be struck down by argument; the Veda is chief among them and they are authoritative by following it; Pāñcarātra, Sāṃkhya and Yoga follow part of the Veda, while the Śaiva is made of the Veda and excels because it follows the whole Veda; the Śaiva tantra called Siddhānta, spoken by Śiva, is always authoritative like the Veda.

### 5.10-13 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-10-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Śaiva tantra is fourfold — vāma (centred on Śakti), dakṣiṇa (Bhairava), miśra (the seven Mothers) and siddhānta; the Siddhānta Āgama is Veda-consonant because it teaches Vedic dharma and opposes those outside the Veda; Veda and Siddhānta are one because they teach one meaning, and their authority is equal.

### 5.15-16 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-15-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Because they delight especially in knowledge (vidyā) whose form is Śiva, these fortunate ones are called Vīraśaivas; the syllable 'vī' denotes the knowledge that awakens the oneness of Śiva and the jīva, and the Śaivas who delight (ramante) in it are held to be Vīraśaivas.

> विद्यायां शिवरूपायां विशेषाद् रमणं यतः । तस्मादेते महाभागा वीरशैवा इति स्मृताः ॥ १५ ॥ वीशब्देनोच्यते विद्या शिवजीवैक्यबोधिका । तस्यां रमन्ते ये शैवा वीरशैवास्तु ते मताः ॥ १६ ॥

### 5.17-18 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-17-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One who delights in (Śiva-)knowledge and keeps away māyā, which is to be abandoned, as one keeps away a dog, is by this derivation called Vīramāheśvara; the knowledge born of Vedānta is vidyā, and one who delights in it is called vīra.

> विद्यायां रमते यस्मान्मायां हेयां श्ववद् रहेत् । अनेनैव निरुक्तेन वीरमाहेश्वरः स्मृतः ॥ १७ ॥ वेदान्तजन्यं यज्ज्ञानं विद्येति परिकीत्यते । विद्यायां रमते तस्यां वीर इत्यभिधीयते ॥ १८ ॥

### 5.20-21 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-20-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Śaivas and Māheśvaras are not ultimately different: those devoted to the sacrifice of knowledge (worship within) are Śaivas, those devoted on earth to the sacrifice of action are Māheśvaras; therefore Śaivas should worship within and Māheśvaras without.

> यथा तथा न भिद्यन्ते शैवा माहेश्वरा अपि । शिवाश्रितेषु ते शैवा ज्ञानयज्ञरता नराः ॥ २० ॥ माहेश्वराः समाख्याताः कर्मयज्ञरता भुवि । तस्मादभ्यन्तरे कुर्युः शैवा माहेश्वरा बहिः ॥ २१ ॥

### 5.22-23 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-22-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Vīraśaivas are of six kinds according to the difference in the dharma of the sthalas, named bhakta and so on by those who have mastered the śāstra; the śāstra of the Vīraśaivas is sixfold by the sthalas, by the conjunction of their dharmas, and by the difference of qualified aspirants (adhikārin).

> वीरशैवास्तु षड्भेदाः स्थलधर्मविभेदतः । भक्तादिव्यवहारेण प्रोच्यन्ते शास्त्रपारगैः ॥ २२ ॥ शास्त्रं तु वीरशैवानां षड्विधं स्थलभेदतः । धर्मभेदसमायोगादधिकारिविभेदतः ॥ २३ ॥

`skeleton` · confidence high

One in whom devotion to Śiva has arisen is called a bhakta; the teaching of the dharmas he is to practise is the bhakta-sthala.

### 5.27-30 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-27-30"></a>

### 5.34-36 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-34-36"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Śiva is without conceptual distinction, formless, attributeless and beyond the world; through connection with beginningless ignorance a part of him is named jīva; Śiva abides in the self as water in the moonstone, fire in the sunstone and the sprout in the seed.

### 5.55-60 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-55-60"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Cārvākas call the body the self, others the senses, the Bauddhas the intellect; but the self is none of these, being known through the cognition 'I' and through memory of what was experienced; one who discerns the eternal self as distinct from body, senses and intellect, and the Lord as the impeller distinct from the selves, is called piṇḍajñānin.

### 5.61-67 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-5-61-67"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

For one whose heart is cleansed and who discerns the eternal from the non-eternal, the resolve to abandon saṃsāra arises: who that discerns would want fleeting pleasures from son and wife, or a heaven that perishes, when even Viṣṇu met death in his births as fish, tortoise, boar, man-lion and man? Born by karma into castes beginning with brāhmaṇa, the creature burns in the fire of the threefold suffering — bodily-mental, from beings, and from the gods.

### 6.1-6 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-6-1-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The disciple — discerning, dispassionate, pure-minded, seeking — approaches a guru who is renowned, free of greed and delusion, skilled in inquiry into the self, knower of the Śiva-siddhānta with doubts cut, versed in all tantric practice, righteous and truthful, of hereditary good conduct, avoiding wrong ways, devoted to meditation on Śiva, peaceful, expert in ash and tripuṇḍra, wearing rudrākṣa and the liṅga, devoted to liṅga worship, knowing the yoga of liṅga and aṅga, with a firm disposition toward non-duality.

### 6.19-21 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-6-19-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 10.44-47 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-10-44-47"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 15.30-36 <a id="tea-siddhantasikhamani-15-30-36"></a>
