Śaṅkara's independent treatise ('a thousand teachings') in a metrical part (19 chapters) and a prose part (3 chapters): the prose part describes how a teacher should instruct a qualified disciple, gives a model teacher–disciple dialogue, and sets out a meditation (parisaṃkhyāna) on the self as distinct from all objects; the metrical part includes a long chapter on 'tat tvam asi' that rejects the need for repeated meditation (prasaṅkhyāna) after the sentence is understood.

### gadya.1.2 <a id="tea-upadesa-sahasri-gadya-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The knowledge of Brahman, the means to liberation, is to be taught repeatedly until firmly grasped, to a disciple who is calm, self-controlled, free from faults, obedient, devoted to the teacher, and longing for liberation, who has approached in the proper way.

### gadya.1.6 <a id="tea-upadesa-sahasri-gadya-1-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The teacher is one able to argue and to answer, retentive, calm, self-controlled, compassionate, learned in scripture, unattached to enjoyments, having renounced all works, a knower of Brahman established in Brahman, free of pretence and pride, whose sole aim is to help others.

### gadya.2 <a id="tea-upadesa-sahasri-gadya-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

A model dialogue: the disciple, asked who he is, answers with his birth and caste; the teacher shows that body, caste and rites belong to the not-self, and leads him to know himself as eternal, pure awareness in which the cognitions of the intellect appear and which is never itself an object.

### gadya.3 <a id="tea-upadesa-sahasri-gadya-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Parisaṃkhyāna, for seekers who wish to destroy merit and demerit and to avoid new ones: one reflects that sound and the other objects are not-self, change and depart, while 'I' am of the nature of pure awareness, unconnected, changeless, not an object — thus detaching from them.

### padya.1 <a id="tea-upadesa-sahasri-padya-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Action cannot remove ignorance, since it is not opposed to it; only knowledge removes ignorance; hence knowledge alone is the means to liberation and the renunciation of works follows.

### padya.18 <a id="tea-upadesa-sahasri-padya-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the chapter on 'tat tvam asi', Śaṅkara holds that once the meaning of the sentence is understood, the self is known as free from suffering; he rejects the view that one must further practise repeated meditation (prasaṅkhyāna) on the sentence for knowledge to become effective.
