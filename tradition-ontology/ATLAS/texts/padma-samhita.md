# Pādma Saṃhitā

**Availability:** digitized-original

Pāñcarātra saṃhitā in the four pādas (jñāna, yoga, kriyā, caryā), presented as the expansion of the Jayākhya and followed in practice at Hastiśaila (Kāñcī). Its jñānapāda praises the Pāñcarātra above all śāstras, explains the name (other systems become 'night' before it) and divides it into four siddhāntas (1.60–83).

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### jnana.1.63-65 <a id="tea-padma-samhita-jnana-1-63-65"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

As the brahmin among the classes, the renouncer among the stages of life, the Gaṅgā among sacred fords and Acyuta among the gods, so the Pañcarātra is praised among all śāstras; whoever worships Hari by the Pañcarātra's rules attains supreme isolation (kaivalya), does not return to saṃsāra and gains imperishable fruit.

_level: conventional · standpoint: devotional · path: ritual, devotion · stage: all · types: karma-liberation_

### jnana.1.72-75 <a id="tea-padma-samhita-jnana-1-72-75"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The five other śāstras, however great, become like night (rātrīyante) in its presence; hence this name is current in the world. As the moon and stars do not shine by day, the five others do not shine near it; as nights come to an end when the sun blazes, so do the others before it. This śāstra is holy, destroying sin and becoming, a great ship carrying across the ocean of existence those sunk in it and bound by the noose of karma.

### jnana.1.76-83 <a id="tea-padma-samhita-jnana-1-76-83"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

As the Veda is divided fourfold as Ṛg and the rest, so the Pañcarātra is fourfold by siddhānta, and as each Veda divides into many branches, each siddhānta divides by its speakers. It is called 'siddhānta' because those who have reached settled conclusions in Mīmāṃsā and the other śāstras become eligible for it at the end. That in which one form predominates is the Mantra-siddhānta; that in which four forms predominate is the Āgama-siddhānta; that in which nine forms predominate is the Tantra-siddhānta; that in which worship is of a god with four or three faces is the Tantrāntara. (V. 83 names Vāsudeva and the other three, and Nārāyaṇa, Hayagrīva, Viṣṇu, Nṛhari and the Boar.)
