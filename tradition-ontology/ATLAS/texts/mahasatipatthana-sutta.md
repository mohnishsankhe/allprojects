## Teachings (26: skeleton 2, text-verified 24)

### 18-21 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-18-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Under the contemplation of dhammas the four noble truths are analysed: suffering (birth, ageing, death, sorrow … in brief the five aggregates of clinging); its origin (craving for sensual pleasure, becoming and non-becoming, arising wherever there is the pleasant and agreeable in the world); its cessation (the fading and ending of that craving); and the path (the noble eightfold path, each factor defined).

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: meditation, knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: practice, karma-liberation_

### 22 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Whoever develops the four establishments of mindfulness in this way for seven years — or even seven days — can expect one of two fruits: final knowledge in this very life or, if there is a residue, non-return.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation · stage: all · types: practice, karma-liberation_

terms: [anāgāmī](../terms/anagami.md), [aññā](../terms/anna.md) · practices: [The four establishments of mindfulness (satipaṭṭhāna)](../practices/satipatthana.md)

### ch1 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-ch1"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

DN 22 in its own order: frame and theme (dn22:1); contemplation of the body in fourteen sections (dn22:2-10: breath, postures, clear comprehension, repulsiveness, elements, nine charnel-ground contemplations); of feelings (dn22:11); of mind (dn22:12); of dhammas: hindrances, aggregates, sense bases, awakening factors (dn22:13-16) and the four noble truths (dn22:17, the short form, then the expanded expositions of the four truths in dn22:18-21, which MN 10 lacks); the promised fruit and the closing (dn22:22). It is the same instruction as MN 10 with the four noble truths expanded; the refrain is written out in full more often.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation · stage: all · types: practice_

### dn22:1 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-1"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence low

Frame and theme. As in MN 10: thus have I heard; the Blessed One was staying among the Kurus at Kammāsadhamma and addressed the monks, who answered 'Bhaddante'. He declared this the one-way path (ekāyana) for the purification of beings, for overcoming sorrow and lamentation, for ending pain and displeasure, for reaching the right method (ñāya) and realising nibbāna: the four establishments of mindfulness (contemplating body in body, feelings in feelings, mind in mind, dhammas in dhammas; ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having put away covetousness and displeasure for the world). The statement of the theme ends. Here frame and theme are one segment; in MN 10 they are three (mn10:1-3).

> Evaṁ me sutaṁ— ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā kurūsu viharati kammāsadhammaṁ nāma kurūnaṁ nigamo. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi: “bhikkhavo”ti. “Bhaddante”ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṁ. Bhagavā etadavoca: “Ekāyano ayaṁ, bhikkhave, maggo sattānaṁ visuddhiyā, sokaparidevānaṁ samatikkamāya dukkhadomanassānaṁ atthaṅgamāya ñāyassa adhigamāya nibbānassa sacchikiriyāya, yadidaṁ cattāro satipaṭṭhānā. Katame cattāro? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ, vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ, citte cittānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ, dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ. Uddeso niṭṭhito.

### dn22:2 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-2"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

First section on the body: the breath (ānāpāna), as in MN 10. A monk, gone to the forest, a tree-root or an empty hut, sits folding his legs crosswise, body upright, mindfulness established in front; mindful he breathes in, mindful he breathes out. He knows long and short breaths, trains 'experiencing the whole body' and 'calming the bodily formation', as a skilled turner knows a long or short turn. The refrain follows in full (internal, external, both; arising, passing away; bare knowledge and recollection; independent; clinging to nothing), and the breath section ends.

> 1. Kāyānupassanā 1.1. Kāyānupassanāānāpānapabba Kathañca pana, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā nisīdati pallaṅkaṁ ābhujitvā ujuṁ kāyaṁ paṇidhāya parimukhaṁ satiṁ upaṭṭhapetvā. So satova assasati, satova passasati. Dīghaṁ vā assasanto ‘dīghaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, dīghaṁ vā passasanto ‘dīghaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti. Rassaṁ vā assasanto ‘rassaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, rassaṁ vā passasanto ‘rassaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti. ‘Sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati. ‘Passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, dakkho bhamakāro vā bhamakārantevāsī vā dīghaṁ vā añchanto ‘dīghaṁ añchāmī’ti pajānāti, rassaṁ vā añchanto ‘rassaṁ añchāmī’ti pajānāti; evameva kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dīghaṁ vā assasanto ‘dīghaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, dīghaṁ vā passasanto ‘dīghaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti, rassaṁ vā assasanto ‘rassaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, rassaṁ vā passasanto ‘rassaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti. ‘Sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati. Iti ajjhattaṁ vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati, bahiddhā vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati, ajjhattabahiddhā vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati. Samudayadhammānupassī vā kāyasmiṁ viharati, vayadhammānupassī vā kāyasmiṁ viharati, samudayavayadhammānupassī vā kāyasmiṁ viharati. ‘Atthi kāyo’ti vā panassa sati paccupaṭṭhitā hoti yāvadeva ñāṇamattāya paṭissatimattāya anissito ca viharati, na ca kiñci loke upādiyati. Evampi kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati. Ānāpānapabbaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ.

### dn22:3 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-3"></a>

Second section on the body: the postures (iriyāpatha). A monk knows 'I am walking', 'I am standing', 'I am sitting', 'I am lying down', and knows the body however it is disposed. The refrain follows in full and the section ends.

Kāyānupassanāiriyāpathapabba Puna caparaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu gacchanto vā ‘gacchāmī’ti pajānāti, ṭhito vā ‘ṭhitomhī’ti pajānāti, nisinno vā ‘nisinnomhī’ti pajānāti, sayāno vā ‘sayānomhī’ti pajānāti, yathā yathā vā panassa kāyo paṇihito hoti tathā tathā naṁ pajānāti. Iti ajjhattaṁ vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati, bahiddhā vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati, ajjhattabahiddhā vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati. Samudayadhammānupassī vā kāyasmiṁ viharati, vayadhammānupassī vā kāyasmiṁ viharati, samudayavayadhammānupassī vā kāyasmiṁ viharati. ‘Atthi kāyo’ti vā panassa sati paccupaṭṭhitā hoti yāvadeva ñāṇamattāya paṭissatimattāya anissito ca viharati, na ca kiñci loke upādiyati. Evampi kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati. Iriyāpathapabbaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ.

### dn22:4 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-4"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Third section on the body: clear comprehension (sampajāna). A monk acts with clear comprehension in going forward and back, looking ahead and away, bending and stretching, wearing robes and carrying bowl, eating, drinking, chewing and tasting, defecating and urinating, and in walking, standing, sitting, sleeping, waking, speaking and keeping silent. The refrain is abbreviated (…pe…) and the section ends.

> 1.3. Kāyānupassanāsampajānapabba Puna caparaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu abhikkante paṭikkante sampajānakārī hoti, ālokite vilokite sampajānakārī hoti, samiñjite pasārite sampajānakārī hoti, saṅghāṭipattacīvaradhāraṇe sampajānakārī hoti, asite pīte khāyite sāyite sampajānakārī hoti, uccārapassāvakamme sampajānakārī hoti, gate ṭhite nisinne sutte jāgarite bhāsite tuṇhībhāve sampajānakārī hoti. Iti ajjhattaṁ vā …pe… Evampi kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati. Sampajānapabbaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ.

### dn22:5 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-5"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Fourth section on the body: attention to repulsiveness (paṭikūlamanasikāra). A monk reviews this body from the soles upward and the hair-tips downward, bounded by skin, full of various impurities, listing the parts from head hairs to urine (thirty-one in this edition), as a man with eyes opening a sack of mixed grains reviews each kind. The refrain is abbreviated (…pe…) and the section ends.

> 1.4. Kāyānupassanāpaṭikūlamanasikārapabba Puna caparaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu imameva kāyaṁ uddhaṁ pādatalā adho kesamatthakā tacapariyantaṁ pūraṁ nānappakārassa asucino paccavekkhati: ‘atthi imasmiṁ kāye kesā lomā nakhā dantā taco, maṁsaṁ nhāru aṭṭhi aṭṭhimiñjaṁ vakkaṁ, hadayaṁ yakanaṁ kilomakaṁ pihakaṁ papphāsaṁ, antaṁ antaguṇaṁ udariyaṁ karīsaṁ, pittaṁ semhaṁ pubbo lohitaṁ sedo medo, assu vasā kheḷo siṅghāṇikā lasikā muttan’ti. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, ubhatomukhā putoḷi pūrā nānāvihitassa dhaññassa, seyyathidaṁ—sālīnaṁ vīhīnaṁ muggānaṁ māsānaṁ tilānaṁ taṇḍulānaṁ. Tamenaṁ cakkhumā puriso muñcitvā paccavekkheyya: ‘ime sālī, ime vīhī ime muggā ime māsā ime tilā ime taṇḍulā’ti. Evameva kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu imameva kāyaṁ uddhaṁ pādatalā adho kesamatthakā tacapariyantaṁ pūraṁ nānappakārassa asucino paccavekkhati: ‘atthi imasmiṁ kāye kesā lomā …pe… muttan’ti. Iti ajjhattaṁ vā …pe… Evampi kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati. Paṭikūlamanasikārapabbaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ.

### dn22:6 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-6"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Fifth section on the body: attention to the elements (dhātumanasikāra). A monk reviews this body, however placed, by elements: earth, water, fire and air, as a skilled butcher or his apprentice, having slaughtered a cow, sits at a crossroads with it divided into portions. The refrain is abbreviated (…pe…) and the section ends.

> 1.5. Kāyānupassanādhātumanasikārapabba Puna caparaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu imameva kāyaṁ yathāṭhitaṁ yathāpaṇihitaṁ dhātuso paccavekkhati: ‘atthi imasmiṁ kāye pathavīdhātu āpodhātu tejodhātu vāyodhātū’ti. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, dakkho goghātako vā goghātakantevāsī vā gāviṁ vadhitvā catumahāpathe bilaso vibhajitvā nisinno assa; evameva kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu imameva kāyaṁ yathāṭhitaṁ yathāpaṇihitaṁ dhātuso paccavekkhati: ‘atthi imasmiṁ kāye pathavīdhātu āpodhātu tejodhātu vāyodhātū’ti. Iti ajjhattaṁ vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati …pe… Evampi kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati. Dhātumanasikārapabbaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ.

### dn22:7 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-7"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Sixth section on the body: the nine charnel-ground contemplations, first. As if he saw a body thrown down in a charnel ground, one, two or three days dead, swollen, blue-black and festering, a monk compares this very body with it: 'this body too is of such a nature, will become like that, and is not exempt from it'. The refrain is abbreviated (…pe…).

> 1.6. Kāyānupassanānavasivathikapabba Puna caparaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu seyyathāpi passeyya sarīraṁ sivathikāya chaḍḍitaṁ ekāhamataṁ vā dvīhamataṁ vā tīhamataṁ vā uddhumātakaṁ vinīlakaṁ vipubbakajātaṁ. So imameva kāyaṁ upasaṁharati: ‘ayampi kho kāyo evaṁdhammo evaṁbhāvī evaṁanatīto’ti. Iti ajjhattaṁ vā …pe… Evampi kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati.

### dn22:8 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-8"></a>

Charnel-ground contemplation, second. As if he saw a body in a charnel ground eaten by crows, kites, vultures, herons, dogs, tigers, leopards, jackals or various creatures, he compares this very body with it as before. The refrain is abbreviated (…pe…).

### dn22:9 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-9"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Charnel-ground contemplations, third to sixth. As if he saw a skeleton with flesh and blood held by sinews; a skeleton smeared with blood, without flesh, held by sinews; a skeleton without flesh and blood, held by sinews (each abbreviated with …pe…); then bones disconnected and scattered in all directions (hand-bone, foot-bone, ankle, shin, thigh, hip, rib, back, shoulder, neck, jaw, tooth, skull), he compares this very body with it: 'this body too is of such a nature, will become like that, and is not exempt from it'. The refrain is abbreviated (…pe…).

### dn22:18 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-18"></a>

Exposition of the truth of suffering (dukkha). Birth is suffering, ageing is suffering, death is suffering; sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure and despair are suffering; association with the disliked is suffering, separation from the liked is suffering, not getting what one wants is suffering; in brief, the five aggregates of clinging are suffering. The text then defines each: birth (jāti) as the being-born of beings in their various orders, their coming to be, the manifestation of the aggregates and the acquisition of the sense bases; ageing (jarā) as decrepitude, broken teeth, grey hair, wrinkled skin, decline of life-span and maturing of the faculties; death (maraṇa) as the passing away and dissolution of beings from their order, the breaking up of the aggregates, the laying down of the body and the cutting off of the life faculty; sorrow, lamentation, bodily pain, mental displeasure and despair as what befalls one struck by some misfortune or painful state; association with the disliked and separation from the liked as meeting or parting with unwelcome or welcome objects and with those who wish one ill or well (mother, father, brother, sister, friends, colleagues, relatives); not getting what one wants as the wish, arising in beings subject to birth, ageing, sickness, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure and despair, 'oh, that we were not subject to these, that they might not come to us', which cannot be attained by wishing; and the five aggregates of clinging as form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness as objects of clinging. 'This is called the noble truth of suffering.'

It is the complete fading away and cessation (asesavirāganirodha) of that same craving, its giving up (cāga), relinquishment (paṭinissagga), release (mutti) and non-reliance (anālaya). Where is this craving abandoned and where does it cease? Wherever in the world there is something dear-looking and pleasant-looking: there it is abandoned and there it ceases. The text then repeats the same list as in the origin exposition (eye, ear … up to the six sustained thinkings), each time naming it as the place where craving is abandoned and ceases (abbreviated with …pe…). 'This is called the noble truth of the cessation of suffering.'

### dn22:21 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-21"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Exposition of the truth of the way (dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā): just this noble eightfold path (ariya aṭṭhaṅgika magga). Right view (sammādiṭṭhi) is knowledge of suffering, of its origin, of its cessation and of the way to its cessation. Right intention (sammāsaṅkappa) is intention of renunciation (nekkhamma), of non-ill-will and of non-harming. Right speech (sammāvācā) is abstaining from false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech and idle chatter. Right action (sammākammanta) is abstaining from killing, from taking what is not given and from sexual misconduct (kāmesumicchācāra). Right livelihood (sammāājīva) is that the noble disciple abandons wrong livelihood and makes a living by right livelihood. Right effort (sammāvāyāma) is generating desire, making effort, arousing energy, exerting the mind and striving for the non-arising of unarisen evil unwholesome states, for the abandoning of arisen ones, for the arising of unarisen wholesome states, and for the persistence, non-confusion, increase, expansion, development and fulfilment of arisen wholesome states. Right mindfulness (sammāsati) is that a monk abides contemplating body in body, feelings in feelings, mind in mind, dhammas in dhammas, ardent, clearly comprehending and mindful, having put away covetousness and displeasure for the world. Right concentration (sammāsamādhi) is the four jhānas: the first, secluded from sensual pleasures and unwholesome states, with thought and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of seclusion; the second, with the stilling of thought and sustained thought, inner confidence and unification of mind, without thought and sustained thought, with rapture and pleasure born of concentration; the third, with the fading of rapture, equanimous, mindful and clearly comprehending, feeling pleasure with the body, of whom the noble ones say 'equanimous, mindful, dwelling in pleasure'; the fourth, with the abandoning of pleasure and pain and the earlier passing away of joy and grief, neither painful nor pleasant, with purity of mindfulness through equanimity. 'This is called the noble truth of the way.' The refrain follows in full, and the sections on the truths and on dhammas end.

> 4.5.4. Maggasaccaniddesa Katamañca, bhikkhave, dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā ariyasaccaṁ? Ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo seyyathidaṁ— sammādiṭṭhi sammāsaṅkappo sammāvācā sammākammanto sammāājīvo sammāvāyāmo sammāsati sammāsamādhi. Katamā ca, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi? Yaṁ kho, bhikkhave, dukkhe ñāṇaṁ, dukkhasamudaye ñāṇaṁ, dukkhanirodhe ñāṇaṁ, dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya ñāṇaṁ. Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammādiṭṭhi. Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo? Nekkhammasaṅkappo abyāpādasaṅkappo avihiṁsāsaṅkappo. Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo. Katamā ca, bhikkhave, sammāvācā? Musāvādā veramaṇī pisuṇāya vācāya veramaṇī pharusāya vācāya veramaṇī samphappalāpā veramaṇī. Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāvācā. Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammākammanto? Pāṇātipātā veramaṇī adinnādānā veramaṇī kāmesumicchācārā veramaṇī. Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammākammanto. Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo? Idha, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako micchāājīvaṁ pahāya sammāājīvena jīvitaṁ kappeti. Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo. Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāvāyāmo? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu anuppannānaṁ pāpakānaṁ akusalānaṁ dhammānaṁ anuppādāya chandaṁ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṁ ārabhati cittaṁ paggaṇhāti padahati; uppannānaṁ pāpakānaṁ akusalānaṁ dhammānaṁ pahānāya chandaṁ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṁ ārabhati cittaṁ paggaṇhāti padahati; anuppannānaṁ kusalānaṁ dhammānaṁ uppādāya chandaṁ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṁ ārabhati cittaṁ paggaṇhāti padahati; uppannānaṁ kusalānaṁ dhammānaṁ ṭhitiyā asammosāya bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya bhāvanāya pāripūriyā chandaṁ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṁ ārabhati cittaṁ paggaṇhāti padahati. Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāvāyāmo. Katamā ca, bhikkhave, sammāsati? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ; vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ; citte cittānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ; dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ. Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāsati. Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāsamādhi? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṁ savicāraṁ vivekajaṁ pītisukhaṁ paṭhamaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati. Vitakkavicārānaṁ vūpasamā ajjhattaṁ sampasādanaṁ cetaso ekodibhāvaṁ avitakkaṁ avicāraṁ samādhijaṁ pītisukhaṁ dutiyaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati. Pītiyā ca virāgā upekkhako ca viharati, sato ca sampajāno, sukhañca kāyena paṭisaṁvedeti, yaṁ taṁ ariyā ācikkhanti ‘upekkhako satimā sukhavihārī’ti tatiyaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati. Sukhassa ca pahānā dukkhassa ca pahānā pubbeva somanassadomanassānaṁ atthaṅgamā adukkhamasukhaṁ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṁ catutthaṁ jhānaṁ upasampajja viharati. Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāsamādhi. Idaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā ariyasaccaṁ. Iti ajjhattaṁ vā dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati, bahiddhā vā dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati, ajjhattabahiddhā vā dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati. Samudayadhammānupassī vā dhammesu viharati, vayadhammānupassī vā dhammesu viharati, samudayavayadhammānupassī vā dhammesu viharati. ‘Atthi dhammā’ti vā panassa sati paccupaṭṭhitā hoti yāvadeva ñāṇamattāya paṭissatimattāya anissito ca viharati, na ca kiñci loke upādiyati. Evampi kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati catūsu ariyasaccesu. Saccapabbaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ. Dhammānupassanā niṭṭhitā.

### dn22:22 <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-dn22-22"></a>

The stated result and the close. Whoever would develop these four establishments of mindfulness in this way for seven years may expect one of two fruits: final knowledge in this very life or, if there is a residue of clinging, non-return. The same is said for six years down to one year, for seven months down to half a month, and for seven days (abbreviated with …pe…). The Buddha then repeats the opening words: this is the one-way path for the purification of beings … the four establishments of mindfulness; it was said on account of this. The monks rejoiced. The colophon reads: the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta, ninth.

### thesis <a id="tea-mahasatipatthana-sutta-thesis"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Thesis by the six marks. The sutta teaches the four establishments of mindfulness as the one-way path to purification and nibbāna, and, beyond MN 10, sets out the four noble truths in full within the contemplation of dhammas. Opening and closing (upakrama-upasaṃhāra): the same words open (dn22:1) and close (dn22:22) it. Repetition (abhyāsa): the refrain recurs after each section (written out in dn22:2, 3, 10-16 and 21; abbreviated with …pe… in dn22:4-9) and the closing formula 'thus too he abides contemplating' ends each. Novelty (apūrvatā): the sutta does not itself mark anything as new; what it adds to MN 10 is the expanded definition of each of the four truths, including where craving arises and ceases (dn22:18-21) and the definition of the eightfold path, whose right-mindfulness factor is the four establishments themselves (dn22:21). Stated result (phala): one of two fruits, final knowledge in this life or non-return, within seven years down to seven days (dn22:22). Praise (arthavāda): the opening claim that this is the one-way path is repeated at the close (dn22:1, 22); the sutta gives no argument for it. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti): none beyond the similes (turner, sack of grains, butcher) and the definitional exposition of the truths.
