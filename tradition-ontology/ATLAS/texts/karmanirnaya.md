one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); the ninth of the ten short treatises (daśa-prakaraṇa); it interprets the ritual Mahānāmnī verses to show that the 'great names' of the ritual texts (Indra and the rest) designate the supreme Brahman, full of all qualities, and rejects the view that Brahman is without qualities — i.e., that even the ritual portion of the Veda has Viṣṇu as its purport.
