Dakpo Tashi Namgyal's great Mahāmudrā manual: a general presentation of śamatha and vipaśyanā common to sūtra and tantra, then the special Mahāmudrā — preliminaries, śamatha with and without support, vipaśyanā by examining the mind in stillness and movement, recognition, the deviations and strayings, the four yogas with their subdivisions and their relation to the paths and grounds, and the fruition.
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: translation; name: Lobsang P. Lhalungpa, Mahāmudrā: The Quintessence of Mind and Meditation (1986); Elizabeth Callahan, Moonbeams of Mahāmudrā (2019)

### pt.2/4 <a id="tea-moonbeams-of-mahamudra-pt-2-4"></a>

Vipaśyanā: the meditator examines the mind at rest and in movement — its colour, shape and location, where it arises, stays and goes, whether the still and the moving mind are the same or different — and, not finding it, recognizes its empty and luminous nature.

The four ways of deviating from emptiness — deviating from its essence, deviating into sealing (stamping things as empty), deviating into the antidote, and deviating into the path — and the three strayings, attachment to bliss, clarity and non-thought, which lead to rebirth in the desire, form and formless realms.

The four yogas — one-pointedness, simplicity (freedom from elaboration), one taste and non-meditation — each divided into lesser, middling and greater, twelve in all; at each, meditative experience (nyams) is distinguished from realization (rtogs pa).
