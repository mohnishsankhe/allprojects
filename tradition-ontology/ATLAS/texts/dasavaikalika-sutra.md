The second Mūlasūtra, the first text learned by a new Śvetāmbara monk: dharma as non-harm, restraint and austerity; the bee that takes nectar without hurting the flower; the six classes of living beings and the vows; the rules of alms-seeking and speech; conquering the four passions; and the marks of the true monk.

### 1.1 <a id="tea-dasavaikalika-sutra-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dharma is the highest auspiciousness: non-harm, self-restraint and austerity. Even the gods bow to one whose mind is always set on dharma.

> dhammo maṃgalamukkiṭṭhaṃ ahiṃsā saṃjamo tavo / devā vi taṃ namaṃsaṃti jassa dhamme sayā maṇo

### 1.2-5 <a id="tea-dasavaikalika-sutra-1-2-5"></a>

### 4 <a id="tea-dasavaikalika-sutra-4"></a>

The six classes of living beings (earth, water, fire, wind, plants, mobile beings) are described, and the monk takes the five great vows and the sixth, not eating at night, renouncing harm to them in thought, word and deed, doing, causing and approving, for life.

### 8.36-38 <a id="tea-dasavaikalika-sutra-8-36-38"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Anger, pride, deceit and greed increase evil; one who desires his own good should vomit out these four faults. Anger destroys affection, pride destroys humility, deceit destroys friendship, greed destroys everything. Conquer anger by calm, pride by gentleness, deceit by straightforwardness, greed by contentment.

### 10 <a id="tea-dasavaikalika-sutra-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The true monk (sa bhikkhū): one who, having left home at the Jina's word, harms no being, keeps his vows, begs pure food, is not angered by abuse, does not cling to his body, and is free from the pride of birth, of beauty, of gain and of learning.
