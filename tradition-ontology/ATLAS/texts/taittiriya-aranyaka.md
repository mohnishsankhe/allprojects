The Āraṇyaka of the Taittirīya Black Yajurveda: the Āruṇaketuka fire-altar (1); the book of Vedic self-study with the twilight worship, the wind-girdled seers who were śramaṇas (2.7) and the five great sacrifices (2.10); the cāturhotra altar (3); the Pravargya mantras and explanation (4–5); the funeral and ancestral mantras (6); the Taittirīya Upaniṣad (7–9); and the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad (10), which contains the mantras of the food-offering into the breaths.

## Teachings (4: sourced 4)

### 2.2 <a id="tea-taittiriya-aranyaka-2-2"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

At the twilights the knowers of Brahman, facing east, throw up water consecrated with the Gāyatrī; the waters become thunderbolts and hurl the demons called Mandehas onto the island Aruṇa; walking around sunwise they shake off evil; meditating on the rising and setting sun as 'yonder sun is Brahman', the knowing brāhmaṇa attains all good, and being Brahman goes to Brahman.

### 2.7.1 <a id="tea-taittiriya-aranyaka-2-7-1"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The wind-girdled seers (vātaraśana ṛṣayaḥ) were śramaṇas who kept their seed drawn upward; the other seers came to them for their purpose; they hid in the kūṣmāṇḍa formulas, and the seers found them there by faith and austerity.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation, body-breath · stage: advanced · types: practice, teacher-transmission_

### 2.10 <a id="tea-taittiriya-aranyaka-2-10"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

These five great sacrifices are spread out and completed continually: to the gods, the ancestors, beings, men and Brahman. When one offers in the fire, even a stick of fuel, that is the sacrifice to the gods; when one offers svadhā to the ancestors, even water, the sacrifice to the ancestors; and so on for beings, men and Brahman (self-study).

### 6 <a id="tea-taittiriya-aranyaka-6"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The funeral chapter (pitṛmedha): mantras for laying out and cremating the dead, sending him by the ancient paths to the ancestors and to Yama, collecting the bones and the concluding rites of the survivors.
