Amṛtānanda's commentary on the Yoginīhṛdaya, explaining the three codes in terms of prakāśa and vimarśa, the thirty-six principles and the stages of speech, quoting the Vijñānabhairava, Svacchanda and Upaniṣads; it identifies Tripurā with the fusion of light and awareness.

## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-yoginihrdaya-dipika-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The dialogue itself is Paramaśiva, whose form is light, dividing himself by his aspect of self-awareness and questioning himself through the stages of speech from paśyantī onward: 'I, Śiva, extending as far as vaikharī by my vimarśa aspect, have asked.'

### 1.6 <a id="tea-yoginihrdaya-dipika-1-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Saṅketa means a tryst: as lovers who have agreed on a meeting place meet there, so Śiva and Śakti meet in the cakra, the mantra and the worship. Tripurā is she who exists before (purā) the three — abodes, principles, seats, liṅgas, letters — the supreme, whose nature is the fusion (sāmarasya) of light (prakāśa) and self-awareness (vimarśa).

### 1.86 <a id="tea-yoginihrdaya-dipika-1-86"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Liberation while living is release from bondage by the eight fetters beginning with disgust; the one who has grasped the cakra-code through the guru forcibly destroys all fetters and sets going the contemplation 'I am Śiva'.

### 3.2 <a id="tea-yoginihrdaya-dipika-3-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Supreme worship is not the daily offering of outer flowers and substances; it is abiding in one's own great non-dual abode (quoting the Saṅketapaddhati). Wherever the mind goes, outside or inside, there is the supreme state (quoting the Vijñānabhairava). The parāparā worship is meditative, dissolving the outer into the inner non-dual abode of consciousness.

### 3.3 <a id="tea-yoginihrdaya-dipika-3-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The aparā worship — worship of the enclosure-deities of the Śrīcakra from the square to the bindu — is done daily even by the all-knowing, because it produces the conviction of non-difference; all knowers should perform it, otherwise non-duality would be a mere denial of what was never in question.
