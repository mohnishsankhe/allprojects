# Viṃśatikā (Viṃśatikāvijñaptimātratāsiddhi)

**Structure:** 22 verses (numbered 1–22 in the DCS e-text) with Vasubandhu's prose vṛtti

Vasubandhu's defence of cognition-only against objections: the regularity of place and time, intersubjectivity and causal efficacy are explained without external objects (dream, hungry ghosts, hell guardians); the Buddha's teaching of the sense-fields had a purpose; atoms can be neither one nor many; and one mind can affect another directly.

### 1 <a id="tea-vimsatika-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

All this is cognition-only, because non-existent objects appear, as a person with an eye disease sees non-existent hairs, moons and the like.

> vijñaptimātramevedamasadarthāvabhāsanāt / yadvat taimirikasyāsatkeśoṇḍrakādidarśanam

### 2 <a id="tea-vimsatika-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Objection: if cognition arises without an object, then the restriction of place and time, the lack of restriction to one mind-stream, and the causal efficacy of things would be unaccounted for.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: dispute, consciousness-mind_

teachers: [Vasubandhu](../teachers/vasubandhu.md) · disputes: [Are there objects external to cognition?](../debates/external-objects.md)

### 3-4 <a id="tea-vimsatika-3-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Reply: restriction of place and time is established as in a dream; non-restriction to one mind-stream is established as when hungry ghosts all see a river of pus; causal efficacy as in a wet dream; and all of these as in hell, where the hell-guardians are seen and inflict torment though they are not beings.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: dispute, world-fate_

terms: [preta](../terms/preta.md), [naraka](../terms/naraka.md) · teachers: [Vasubandhu](../teachers/vasubandhu.md) · disputes: [Are there objects external to cognition?](../debates/external-objects.md)

### 6-7 <a id="tea-vimsatika-6-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

If you accept that, through beings' actions, elements arise in hell and transform, why not accept that consciousness transforms? You imagine the imprint of action in one place and its fruit in another; what reason is there not to accept the fruit where the imprint is?

> karmaṇo vāsanānyatra phalamanyatra kalpyate / tatraiva neṣyate yatra vāsanā kiṃ nu kāraṇam

_level: conventional · standpoint: causal · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: karma-liberation, dispute_

### 8-10 <a id="tea-vimsatika-8-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Buddha taught the existence of the sense-fields (form etc.) with a particular intention for people to be trained, just as he spoke of beings of spontaneous birth; cognition arises from its own seed with the appearance of an object, and he called seed and appearance its two fields. Taught in this way, one enters the selflessness of persons; taught otherwise (as cognition-only), one enters the selflessness of dharmas — with respect to an imagined self (of dharmas).

> rūpādyāyatanāstitvaṃ tadvineyajanaṃ prati / abhiprāyavaśāduktamupapādukasattvavat

### 11-14 <a id="tea-vimsatika-11-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The object is neither one (a whole), nor many atoms, nor atoms combined, because an atom is not established: joined with six others at once it would have six parts; if the six occupied the same place, the aggregate would be the size of an atom; whatever has spatial divisions cannot be one; and without parts there could be no shadow or obstruction.

> na tadekaṃ na cānekaṃ viṣayaḥ paramāṇuśaḥ / na ca te saṃhatā yasmātparamāṇurna sidhyati

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: dispute, world-fate_

terms: [paramāṇu](../terms/paramanu.md) · teachers: [Vasubandhu](../teachers/vasubandhu.md) · disputes: [Is the material world composed of eternal partless atoms?](../debates/atomism.md), [Are there objects external to cognition?](../debates/external-objects.md)

### 16-17 <a id="tea-vimsatika-16-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Perceptual awareness occurs as in a dream; and when it occurs, the object is no longer seen — how then can it be called perception? Cognition arises with that appearance, and memory follows from it; one not yet awakened does not understand that the objects seen in the dream do not exist.

> uktaṃ yathā tadābhāsā vijñaptiḥ smaraṇaṃ tataḥ / svapnadṛgviṣayābhāvaṃ nāprabuddho 'vagacchati

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: consciousness-mind, ultimate_

terms: [svapna](../terms/svapna.md), [pratyakṣa](../terms/pratyaksa.md) · concepts: [Cognition-only (vijñaptimātratā)](../concepts/vijnaptimatrata.md) · teachers: [Vasubandhu](../teachers/vasubandhu.md)

### 18 <a id="tea-vimsatika-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Cognitions are determined by one another through mutual dominant influence; in dream the mind is overpowered by sleep, and so the fruit (of dream actions) is unequal.

_level: conventional · standpoint: causal · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: karma-liberation, consciousness-mind_

terms: [adhipati-pratyaya](../terms/adhipati-pratyaya.md) · teachers: [Vasubandhu](../teachers/vasubandhu.md)

### 19-20 <a id="tea-vimsatika-19-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Death is a transformation produced by a particular cognition of another, as loss of memory and the like are produced by the minds of goblins and others. Otherwise how was the Daṇḍaka forest emptied by the sages' anger? How would mental punishment be the gravest fault?

> kathaṃ vā daṇḍakāraṇyaśūnyatvamṛṣikopataḥ / manodaṇḍo mahāvadyaḥ kathaṃ vā tena sidhyati

_level: conventional · standpoint: causal · path: action, knowledge · stage: all · types: karma-liberation, ethics, death-dying_

terms: [mano-daṇḍa](../terms/manodanda.md), [karma](../terms/karma.md) · concepts: [The primacy of mental action](../concepts/mental-karma-primacy.md) · teachers: [Vasubandhu](../teachers/vasubandhu.md)

### 21 <a id="tea-vimsatika-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The knowledge of those who know others' minds is not in accord with its object, just as knowledge of one's own mind is not, because of ignorance — unlike the domain of a buddha.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: powers-experiences, consciousness-mind_

terms: [paracitta-jñāna](../terms/paracittajnana.md) · concepts: [Higher knowledges and their limits (Yogācāra)](../concepts/abhijna-yogacara.md) · teachers: [Vasubandhu](../teachers/vasubandhu.md)

### 22 <a id="tea-vimsatika-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

I have composed this proof of cognition-only according to my ability; in all its aspects it is inconceivable, the domain of the buddhas.

> vijñaptimātratāsiddhiḥ svaśaktisadṛśī mayā / kṛteyaṃ sarvathā sā tu na cintyā buddhagocaraḥ
