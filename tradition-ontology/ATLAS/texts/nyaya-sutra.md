**Availability:** digitized-original

The root text of Nyāya. It opens by promising the highest good (niḥśreyasa) from knowledge of the truth of sixteen categories (NS 1.1.1), states the chain from false knowledge to liberation (1.1.2), defines the four means of knowledge, the twelve objects of knowledge beginning with the self, the five-membered demonstration, debate, pseudo-reasons, quibbles, futile rejoinders and points of defeat (book 1); examines the means of knowledge (book 2), the self, body, senses, cognition and mind (book 3), faults, rebirth, fruit, pain, liberation, the whole and atoms, rival views such as momentariness and idealism, and the yogic means to knowledge of truth (book 4); and lists the twenty-four futile rejoinders and twenty-two points of defeat (book 5).

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-nyaya-sutra-1-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The highest good (niḥśreyasa) is attained from knowledge of the truth of: the means of knowledge, the objects of knowledge, doubt, purpose, example, established tenet, the members (of a demonstration), hypothetical reasoning (tarka), ascertainment, discussion (vāda), wrangling (jalpa), cavilling (vitaṇḍā), pseudo-reasons, quibbles, futile rejoinders (jāti) and points of defeat.

> pramāṇaprameyasaṃśayaprayojanadṛṣṭāntasiddhāntāvayavatarkanirṇayavādajalpavitaṇḍāhetvābhāsacchalajātinigrahasthānānām tattvajñānāt niḥśreyasādhigamaḥ

### 1.1.33-39 <a id="tea-nyaya-sutra-1-1-33-39"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high
