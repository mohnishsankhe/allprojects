one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); a compilation of verses (largely from Purāṇas) on devotion to Kṛṣṇa, the necessity of receiving Hari's initiation and worship, the name, and the observance of vows, especially the Ekādaśī fast in both fortnights and its rules when the day is overlapped (viddhā).

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1-3 <a id="tea-krsnamrtamaharnava-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

May Keśava protect me, who grants immortality when worshipped, remembered, meditated on, praised and spoken of. For the peace of this world scorched by the three afflictions, the Kṛṣṇāmṛtamahārṇava is told. Those who have not received Hari's initiation (dīkṣā) nor worshipped Janārdana are beasts among men; what is the fruit of their life?

### 126-152 <a id="tea-krsnamrtamaharnava-126-152"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Ekādaśī of both fortnights is to be fasted; an Ekādaśī touched by the tenth day is to be given up; when the Ekādaśī is overlapped, or with Śravaṇa, the fast is kept on the Dvādaśī (and broken on the Trayodaśī); if the Ekādaśī is not overlapped, the Dvādaśī must not be neglected; for breaking the fast and for death rites the lunar day current at that time is taken.

### 221-225 <a id="tea-krsnamrtamaharnava-221-225"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One on whose forehead the straight, gentle upright mark (ūrdhvapuṇḍra) is seen is pure and worthy of honour, even if an outcaste; the face without it is like a cremation ground; sacrifice, gifts, austerity, study and offerings to the ancestors done without it are fruitless; whatever one whose body is marked with gopīcandana looks upon becomes pure.
