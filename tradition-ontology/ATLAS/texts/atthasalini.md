Buddhaghosa's commentary on the Dhammasaṅgaṇī; its introduction (Nidānakathā) defends the Abhidhamma as the Buddha's word and tells how it was taught in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven; the body explains consciousness, the mental factors, matter and the five niyāmas.

## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### cittuppada <a id="tea-atthasalini-cittuppada"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Consciousness (citta) is so called because it thinks of (cinteti) its object, and because it is variegated (citta); it is the forerunner of the mental factors.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: consciousness-mind_

terms: [citta](../terms/citta.md)

### five-niyamas <a id="tea-atthasalini-five-niyamas"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Five orders (niyāma) are distinguished: of seasons (heat), of seeds, of kamma, of the Dhamma, and of mind — so that not every event is a result of kamma.

_level: conventional · standpoint: causal · path: general · stage: all · types: world-fate, karma-liberation_

### nidanakatha <a id="tea-atthasalini-nidanakatha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Abhidhamma was taught by the Buddha in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven during the rains retreat to the gods, with his mother (reborn as a deity) as chief listener; each day he gave the method to Sāriputta at Lake Anotatta, who passed it to his five hundred pupils; the Abhidhamma is thus the Buddha's word.

_level: conventional · standpoint: divine · path: general · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission_

### nidanakatha <a id="tea-atthasalini-nidanakatha-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the fourth week after the awakening, when the Buddha contemplated the Paṭṭhāna with its infinite method, rays of six colours issued from his body — for only there did his omniscient knowledge find full scope.

_level: conventional · standpoint: experiential · path: general · stage: realized · types: powers-experiences, teacher-transmission_

### nidanakatha <a id="tea-atthasalini-nidanakatha-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

To the objection that the Kathāvatthu was spoken by Moggaliputta Tissa and so is not the Buddha's word, the commentary answers that the Buddha laid down its outline, foreseeing future controversy, and the elder expanded it according to that outline, as a disciple fills out a teacher's summary.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: general · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission, dispute_
