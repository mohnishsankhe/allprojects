## Teachings (8: skeleton 8)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-mahavastu-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There are four conducts of the bodhisattvas: the natural conduct, the conduct of the vow, the conforming conduct and the conduct of not turning back. Under Aparājitadhvaja the Blessed One, as a universal king in natural conduct, first planted roots of merit; under a former Śākyamuni, as a merchant, he first made the vow to become a Buddha of the same name.

> catvāri imāni bodhisatvānāṃ bodhisatvacaryāṇi || katamāni catvāri || prakṛticaryā praṇidhānacaryā anulomacaryā anivartanacaryā

### 1.76 <a id="tea-mahavastu-1-76"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There are ten grounds of the bodhisattvas: the first is called Hard to Enter (durārohā), the second Binding (baddhamānā), the third Adorned with Flowers, the fourth Charming, the fifth Expansion of Mind, the sixth Beautiful, the seventh Hard to Conquer, the eighth Ascertainment of Birth, the ninth Installation as Heir-Apparent, and the tenth Consecration.

> durāroheti prathamā bhūmī samupadiśyate | dvitīyā baddhamānā nāma tṛtīyā puṣpamaṇḍitā || caturthī rucirā nāma pañcamī cittavistarā | ṣaṣṭī rūpavatī nāma saptamī durjayā smṛtā || aṣṭamā janmanideśo navamī yauvarājyato | daśamī tv abhiṣekāto iti etā daśa bhūmayaḥ ||

### 1.158-159 <a id="tea-mahavastu-1-158-159"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Perfect Buddhas are endowed with five eyes; even their fleshly eye cannot be fathomed in its qualities in an aeon, for nothing about the perfect Buddhas is like the world — everything about the great seers is supramundane.

> nahi kiñcit samyaksambuddhānāṃ lokena samaṃ || atha khalu sarvam eva maharṣiṇāṃ lokottaraṃ ||

### 1.167-168 <a id="tea-mahavastu-1-167-168"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The conduct of the Blessed One is supramundane, his root of merit is supramundane; the Sage's walking, standing, sitting and lying are supramundane; the Sugata's body, which destroys the bonds of existence, is supramundane — of this there is no doubt; his wearing of robes and his taking of food are supramundane.

> lokottarā bhagavato caryā lokottaraṃ kuśalamūlaṃ | gamanaṃ sthitaṃ niṣaṇṇaṃ śayitaṃ lokottaraṃ munino ||

### 1.168-169 <a id="tea-mahavastu-1-168-169"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Buddhas conform to the world's conventions (lokānuvartanā): they follow worldly designation as well as the supramundane; they display the four postures without fatigue; they wash their feet though no dust clings to them, bathe though they have no dirt, clean their teeth, eat though hunger does not afflict them, drink without thirst, take medicine though they have no illness, show old age though they do not age — this is conformity with the world.

> lokānuvartanāṃ buddhā anuvartanti laukikīṃ | prajñaptim anuvartanti yathā lokottarām api || … pādāṃ ca nāma dhovanti na caiṣāṃ sajjate rajaḥ | pādāḥ kamalapatrābhā eṣā lokānuvartanā ||

### 3.331-333 <a id="tea-mahavastu-3-331-333"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

At Ṛṣivadana the Buddha tells the five monks that there are two extremes for one gone forth — devotion to sensual pleasure and self-torment — and teaches the four noble truths, the knowledge and vision that arose in him regarding each, and their three turnings; Ājñāta-Kauṇḍinya obtains the pure dharma-eye.

> dvāv imau bhikṣavaḥ pravrajitasya antau |

### 3.335 <a id="tea-mahavastu-3-335"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Matter is not self, feeling is not self, perception, formations and consciousness are not self; if matter were self it would not lead to affliction and one could have it as one wishes: 'let my matter be thus, let it not be thus'.

> rūpaṃ bhikṣavo anātmā vedanā anātmā sañjñā anātmā saṃskārā anātmā vijñānaṃ anātma |
