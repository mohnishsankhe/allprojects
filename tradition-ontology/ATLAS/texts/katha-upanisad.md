# Katha Upaniṣad

`src:katha-upanisad` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Kathaka Upaniṣad, Kathavalli
**Original title:** कठोपनिषद्
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md), [Kaṭha śākhā (Black Yajurveda)](../lineages/sakha-katha.md)
**Genre:** upanisad

**Dates:** Tradition's account: Revealed scripture (śruti): in the Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta view without human author and beginningless; the texts themselves trace their teaching through lineages of teachers back to Brahma/Prajāpati and brahman, the self-existent; Scholarly account: c. 5th-3rd c. **Structure:** 2 adhyayas of 3 vallis each (6 vallis; 29, 25, 17 / 15, 15, 19 verses = 120 in Śaṅkara's text; often cited with continuous valli numbers 1-6)
**Availability:** digitized-original

Naciketas, given to Death by his father, receives three boons from Yama: his father's welcome, the fire leading to heaven, and the answer to whether one exists after death. Refusing all worldly gifts he is taught the difference of the good and the pleasant, Om, the unborn self, the chariot image, the ladder of principles up to the person, yoga as the firm holding of the senses and the 101 channels.

### 1.1.1-4 <a id="tea-katha-upanisad-1-1-1-4"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Vājaśravasa, desiring (heaven), gave away all his possessions; he had a son named Naciketas. Seeing the gifts — cows that had drunk their water, eaten their grass, given their milk and were barren — the boy thought 'joyless are the worlds to which one goes by giving these', and asked his father 'to whom will you give me?' Asked a second and a third time, the father said: 'to Death I give you.'

### 1.1.12-19 <a id="tea-katha-upanisad-1-1-12-19"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Second boon: the fire that leads to heaven, where there is no fear, old age, hunger or thirst. Yama teaches the fire that is the beginning of the world, the bricks, their number and how they are laid; Naciketas repeats it, and Yama names it the Nāciketa fire and gives him a many-coloured chain. One who has piled the Nāciketa fire three times, joined with the three, doing the three acts, crosses over birth and death.

### 1.1.21-29 <a id="tea-katha-upanisad-1-1-21-29"></a>

### 1.2.1-2 <a id="tea-katha-upanisad-1-2-1-2"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The good (śreyas) is one thing, the pleasant (preyas) another; both bind a person with different aims. Good comes to one who takes the good; one who chooses the pleasant misses the goal. Both come to a person; the wise one examines and distinguishes them; the wise prefers the good to the pleasant; the fool chooses the pleasant for the sake of getting and keeping (yogakṣema).

> śreyaś ca preyaś ca manuṣyam etas tau samparītya vivinakti dhīraḥ | śreyo hi dhīro 'bhi preyaso vṛṇīte preyo mando yogakṣemād vṛṇīte

### 1.2.4-6 <a id="tea-katha-upanisad-1-2-4-6"></a>
