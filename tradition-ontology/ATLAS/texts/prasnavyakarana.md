The tenth Aṅga. The extant text treats the five 'doors of inflow' (āsrava: violence, falsehood, theft, unchastity, possessiveness) and the five 'doors of stopping' (saṃvara: the five great vows), with a celebrated list of names and praises of non-harm (ahiṃsā). The Sthānāṅga, Samavāyāṅga and Nandī describe a different original content (questions and answers, including on spells and prognostication); the commentators note the difference.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### asrava.1 <a id="tea-prasnavyakarana-asrava-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The first door of inflow is violence (hiṃsā), described by its names, its forms, those who commit it and the terrible fruit it bears in hell and animal births; the other four doors are falsehood, theft, unchastity and possessiveness.

### samvara.1 <a id="tea-prasnavyakarana-samvara-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Non-harm (ahiṃsā) is praised under sixty names: it is the island for those in the sea, the refuge, the support, the mother of all beings, the rest and nourishment of the world; it is what the Tīrthaṅkaras teach and the protector of all beings, moving and unmoving.
