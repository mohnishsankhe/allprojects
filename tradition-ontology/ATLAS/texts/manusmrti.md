# Manusmṛti (Mānava Dharmaśāstra)

`src:manusmrti` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Mānava Dharmaśāstra, Manusaṃhitā
**Original title:** मनुस्मृतिः
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Dharmaśāstra (the science of dharma; Dharmasūtra and Smṛti)](../lineages/dharmasastra.md)
**Genre:** smṛti
**Authors:** 
  - [Manu Svāyambhuva](../teachers/manu-svayambhuva.md) — role: revealer; attribution: traditional
  - [Bhṛgu Vāruṇi](../teachers/bhrgu.md) — role: compiler; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: taught by Manu Svāyambhuva, who received it from Svayambhū, and recited to the seers by Bhṛgu (1.58–60); Nārada's preface tells of an original of 100,000 verses successively abridged; scholarly: a single composition or redaction by an unknown brāhmaṇa author (or school) drawing on the Dharmasūtras; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: from the beginning of creation (the first Manu's teaching); Scholarly account: c. 2nd c. BCE – 2nd c. CE (one recent estimate: 2nd c. CE); (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 12 adhyāyas, c. 2,700 ślokas (verse numbers cited in Kullūka's vulgate numbering as in the GRETIL e-text)
**Availability:** digitized-original

The most authoritative metrical code of dharma, spoken by Bhṛgu on behalf of Manu Svāyambhuva. Book 1 tells of creation from the self-existent, the four classes from the mouth, arms, thighs and feet (1.31), the Manus, the ages and their declining dharma (1.68–86); book 2 the sources of dharma (2.6, 2.12), the sacraments, the syllable Oṃ, the Sāvitrī and recitation (2.76–87), control of the senses and the student's life with the teacher; book 3 marriage, the five great sacrifices and the ancestral rites; book 4 the householder and the snātaka; book 5 food, death-impurity and purification, and women; book 6 the forest-dweller and the renouncer, breath control and meditation, and the tenfold dharma; books 7–9 kingship and law; book 10 the classes and mixed castes and dharma in distress; book 11 gifts and expiation; book 12 the fruits of action, the three guṇas, rebirth, the highest good and the vision of the self in all beings.

### 12.118-125 <a id="tea-manusmrti-12-118-125"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Concentrated, one should see all in the self, the real and the unreal; seeing all in the self, one does not set the mind on wrong. The self alone is all the gods; all rests in the self. One should know the supreme Person, ruler of all, subtler than the subtle, bright as gold, graspable by a mind as if in sleep. Some call him Agni, others Manu, Prajāpati; some Indra, others the breath, others the eternal Brahman. He, pervading all beings with the five elements, makes them revolve like a wheel through birth, growth and decay.
