## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1.2.1 <a id="tea-prabhavali-1-2-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Where the fruit — happiness, hell and the like — is by nature liked or disliked, praise has no purpose; so a sentence such as 'that happiness, unmixed with sorrow, not swallowed up afterwards, brought by longing, is the meaning of the word svar' does not indicate praise but conveys the nature of heaven and hell that injunctions and prohibitions need.

### 1.2.1/2 <a id="tea-prabhavali-1-2-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Upaniṣads that teach the self as eternal agent and enjoyer, distinct from the perishable body, serve the injunctions of rites with other-worldly fruit; those that teach the non-transmigrating self with or without qualities have their own purport, being required by the injunctions of the two kinds of meditation.
