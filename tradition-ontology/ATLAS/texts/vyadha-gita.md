Within Mārkaṇḍeya's discourses: the brāhmaṇa Kauśika, humbled by a devoted housewife (pativratā), is sent to a righteous meat-seller (dharmavyādha) of Mithilā, who teaches him that one's own inherited work done rightly, service of parents, truth, and self-restraint are dharma; that no one lives without harming some beings; and gives teaching on karma, the elements, the guṇas, the senses and liberation, sending Kauśika home to serve his parents.

### 3.198 <a id="tea-vyadha-gita-3-198"></a>

The meat-seller explains that he follows the occupation inherited from his forefathers, sells meat of animals he has not killed himself, gives, speaks truth, honours gods and guests and serves his parents; one's own work, done rightly, is dharma.
