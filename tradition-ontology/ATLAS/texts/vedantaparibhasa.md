Dharmarāja Adhvarīndra's manual of Advaita epistemology in eight chapters: perception, inference, comparison, verbal testimony, presumption and non-cognition (the six means of knowledge, following Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā for empirical matters), then the object (Brahman's defining marks, creation and dissolution) and the purpose (liberation and its means).

## Teachings (6: skeleton 6)

### ch.1 <a id="tea-vedantaparibhasa-ch-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Valid cognition (pramā) is cognition of an object not already known and not contradicted; the means of knowledge are six — perception, inference, comparison, verbal testimony, presumption and non-cognition.

> anadhigatābādhitārthaviṣayakajñānatvaṃ pramātvam

### ch.1 <a id="tea-vedantaparibhasa-ch-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In perception the inner organ goes out through the sense to the object and takes its form (a vṛtti); then the consciousness delimited by the inner organ (the knower), by the vṛtti (the means) and by the object become one, and the object is perceived.

### ch.4 <a id="tea-vedantaparibhasa-ch-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Verbal testimony gives knowledge through expectancy, compatibility, proximity and intended purport; the Veda is not composed by a person but reproduced by Īśvara at each creation in the same order as before.

### ch.7 <a id="tea-vedantaparibhasa-ch-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Brahman has a defining mark of its own nature (svarūpa-lakṣaṇa: existence, consciousness, bliss) and an incidental mark (taṭastha-lakṣaṇa: being the cause of the world's origin, sustenance and dissolution), as a house is pointed out by the crow sitting on it.

### ch.7 <a id="tea-vedantaparibhasa-ch-7-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Dissolution (pralaya) is fourfold: daily (deep sleep), occasional (at the end of Brahmā's day), elemental (of all effects into prakṛti at the end of Brahmā's life) and final (ātyantika, liberation through knowledge).

### ch.8 <a id="tea-vedantaparibhasa-ch-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Liberation is the attainment of bliss and the removal of sorrow, which is Brahman itself; its means is the knowledge of Brahman, arising from hearing and the rest in one endowed with the four means; worship of the qualified Brahman leads to the Brahma-world and gradual liberation.
