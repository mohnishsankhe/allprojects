**Availability:** digitized-original

The foundational compendium of internal medicine: Ātreya's teaching to Agniveśa in dialogues and assemblies of sages. It sets out the aim and definition of Āyurveda, the doṣas, dhātus and malas, diet, daily and seasonal routine and good conduct, the causes and diagnosis of disease, the examination of patient and physician, debate, the person and its constituents, embryology, the signs of death, therapeutics including rejuvenation and virility, and purificatory therapies; and it teaches a doctrine of the self, rebirth, yoga and liberation (Sū 11; Śā 1, 5).

### ci.1.1.3-8 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-ci-1-1-3-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Medicine is twofold: some gives vigour to the healthy, some cures the diseased. What gives vigour to the healthy is rasāyana or the aphrodisiac. From rasāyana one gains long life, memory, intelligence, health, youth, excellence of lustre, complexion and voice, great strength of body and senses, success of speech, respect and radiance; rasāyana is the means of obtaining the excellent rasa and other dhātus.

> dīrghamāyuḥ smṛtiṃ medhāmārogyaṃ taruṇaṃ vayaḥ | prabhāvarṇasvaraudāryaṃ dehendriyabalaṃ param

### ci.1.1.16-24 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-ci-1-1-16-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The sages know two modes of rasāyana: entering a hut (kuṭīprāveśika) and 'wind-and-sun' (vātātapika, in ordinary life). For the first, a hut with three chambers is built in a safe town with provisions, away from disturbing sounds and women; at an auspicious time the person, head shaved, having made firm his resolve, memory and strength, faithful and collected, casting off the mental doṣas and cultivating friendliness to beings, worships the gods and brahmins and enters; purified by cleansing therapies and restored in strength, he then takes the rasāyana.

### ci.1.4.30-35 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-ci-1-4-30-35"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### ni.7.10-23 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-ni-7-10-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Exogenous insanity arises from the gods, sages, ancestors, gandharvas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, piśācas, teachers, elders and siddhas. Some hold past unwholesome karma to be its cause; Ātreya says its cause is prajñāparādha alone: through it a man disregards the gods and the venerable and does unwholesome acts, and they strike one already struck by himself. Its remedies are mantra, herbs, gems, auspicious rites, offerings, oblations, observances, vows, expiation, fasting, benedictions, prostration and pilgrimage. Neither gods nor gandharvas, piśācas nor rākṣasas afflict a man not already afflicted by himself; for a disease born of prajñāparādha, one's own karma, the wise should blame neither gods nor ancestors nor demons but know himself as the agent of his happiness and suffering, and take the path of the good without fear.

### sa.1.3-14 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-sa-1-3-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Agniveśa asked: into how many dhātus is the person divided? Why is the person a cause, what is its origin? Is it ignorant or a knower, eternal or not? What is prakṛti, what are the modifications, what the mark of the person? The knowers of the self call it inactive, independent, self-controlled, all-pervading, witness — then how does it act, why is it born in undesired wombs, why does it not know all feelings? Which comes first, the field or its knower? Which feeling — past, present or future — does the physician treat? Where do all feelings cease entirely, and by what marks is the one who knows all, has renounced all and is at peace, known?

### sa.1.18-23 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-sa-1-18-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The mark of the mind is the presence or absence of knowledge: when self, sense and object are in contact, knowledge does not arise without the mind's presence. Atomicity and oneness are the two qualities of mind. Its objects are what is to be thought, considered, reasoned, meditated and resolved; its acts are directing the senses, restraining itself, reasoning and consideration; then buddhi, which is determinate, operates, and by it one decides to speak or act.

### sa.1.35-38 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-sa-1-35-38"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The aggregate of twenty-four is called the person; this conjunction, when joined with rajas and tamas, is endless; when they are removed by the increase of sattva it ceases. Here are established action and its fruit, knowledge, delusion, pleasure and pain, life and death, ownership; one who truly knows this knows dissolution and arising, the succession, treatment, and whatever is to be known.

### sa.1.46-51 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-sa-1-46-51"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Some hold that the aggregate of momentary things, without a lord, called sattva, is the agent and enjoyer, not a person; for them what is done by some would be reaped by others that are new — they who teach no self. The instruments are seen to be other, and the agent the same; the agent joined with instruments is the cause of all actions. ### sa.1.52-55 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-sa-1-52-55"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ego-sense, fruit, action, passage to another body and memory exist only when there is a cause (the self) apart from the body. The supreme self, being beginningless, has no origin; the person called the aggregate (rāśi) is born of delusion, desire, aversion and action. The self is the knower; its knowledge operates through connection with instruments and does not operate when they are impure or absent — as one looking into a dirty mirror or muddy water sees nothing, so when the mind is afflicted.

### sa.1.59-65 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-sa-1-59-65"></a>

### sa.1.86-93 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-sa-1-86-93"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The physician treats feelings of all three times: a returning headache or fever is a past feeling come again, and medicine given at its time allays the past; seeing prodromes and acting beforehand destroys the future one, as one builds a dike against waters that once ruined the crop; and the continuing chain of suffering is stopped and happiness set going by attending to its causes — like produce like: the balanced do not become unbalanced nor the unbalanced balanced of themselves.

### sa.1.94-97 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-sa-1-94-97"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Thus the physician removes feelings of the three times; but the final (naiṣṭhikī) cure is that which is without upadhā. Upadhā (craving-grasping) is the supreme cause that gives suffering and the seats of suffering; renunciation of all upadhās removes all suffering. As the silkworm takes up the threads that bring its death, so the ignorant, ever afflicted, takes up craving from objects; the knower, knowing objects to be like fire, turns away from them, and through not starting and not joining, suffering does not reach him.

### su.25.5-25 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-su-25-5-25"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In an assembly of sages King Vāmaka of Kāśī asked: are the person and his diseases born of the same source or not? Pārīkṣi Maudgalya: both are born of the self, for the self gathers karma and reaps its fruits. Śaraloman: no — the self would not yoke itself to suffering; the mind pervaded by rajas and tamas is the cause. Vāryovida: not the mind alone; beings and diseases arise from rasa (water). Hiraṇyākṣa: the self is not born of rasa; person and disease arise from the six dhātus, as the first Sāṃkhyas taught. Kauśika: without mother and father how could the six dhātus make a person? — they are the cause. Bhadrakāpya: the blind are not born of the blind; the creature and his diseases are born of karma. ### vi.8.14 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-vi-8-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There is no easy crossing to the far shore of Āyurveda; so one should apply oneself to it constantly and without heedlessness; and one should learn good conduct and freedom from envy even from others, for to the wise the whole world is a teacher, to the unwise an enemy; a word that brings wealth, fame, life and welfare should be heard and followed even from an enemy.

> kṛtsno hi loko buddhimatāmācāryaḥ śatruścābuddhimatām

### vi.8.15-23 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-vi-8-15-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A physician should converse with physicians, for discussion brings zeal for knowledge, confidence, eloquence and fame, removes doubts and brings to hearing what one had not heard; what a pleased teacher reveals step by step as secret, two disputants eager to win bring out together. Discussion is friendly (with a patient, knowledgeable, unpretentious partner — ask and answer with confidence, do not fear defeat or exult in victory, do not cling one-sidedly) or hostile (examine the opponent, the audience and one's own strength first). The wise do not delight in quarrels in an assembly of the good; an angry man will do or say anything.

### vi.8.27-28 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-vi-8-27-28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

To know the course of debate among physicians these terms are to be learnt: debate (vāda), substance, quality, action, general, particular, inherence, proposition, establishment, counter-establishment, reason, example, application, conclusion, rejoinder, tenet, word, perception, inference, tradition, analogy, doubt, purpose, the inconclusive, desire to know, determination, presumption, probability, questionable and unquestionable, question and counter-question, faults and merits of speech, quibble, pseudo-reason, the untimely, censure, defence, abandoning the proposition, admission, shifting the reason and the topic, and the point of defeat. Vāda is disputation with another based on the science; it is jalpa (each side establishing its thesis, e.g. 'rebirth exists' against 'it does not') or vitaṇḍā (only finding fault with the other side).

### vi.8.84 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-vi-8-84"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The ten things to be examined before action: the cause (the physician), the instrument (the medicine), the source of the effect (imbalance of the dhātus), the effect (their balance), the fruit of the effect (happiness), its continuation (life), the place (land and patient), the time (the year and the patient's state), the undertaking (the start of treatment) and the means (the excellence of physician and the rest).

### vi.8.94 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-vi-8-94"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The patient should be examined by constitution, morbid state, excellence of tissues (sāra), compactness, measure, suitability (sātmya), mind (sattva), capacity for food, capacity for exercise, and age — to know the measure of strength; for a strong medicine given without examination to a weak patient can kill him, and a weak one given to a strong patient with a strong disease is useless.

> tasmādāturaṃ parīkṣeta prakṛtitaśca vikṛtitaśca sārataśca saṃhananataśca pramāṇataśca sātmyataśca sattvataśca āhāraśaktitaśca vyāyāmaśaktitaśca vayastaśceti

### vi.8.95-100 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-vi-8-95-100"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Constitution arises from the state of semen and ovum, the season and womb, the mother's diet and conduct, and the elements; the doṣa predominating there marks the embryo, so some are kapha-, some pitta-, some vāta-natured, some mixed, some balanced. The kapha-natured, from kapha's unctuous, smooth, soft, sweet, firm, dense, slow, stable, heavy, cold, slimy and clear qualities, are strong, wealthy, learned, vigorous, calm and long-lived; the pitta-natured are of middling strength, life and knowledge; the vāta-natured have little strength, life, wealth and memory; those balanced in all qualities are best.

### vi.8.119 <a id="tea-caraka-samhita-vi-8-119"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sattva is called the mind; it governs the body through its connection with the self. It is of three grades: superior, middling and inferior. Those of superior mind, even with small bodies, are not distressed by great pains; the middling sustain themselves by seeing others or are sustained by others; the inferior can be sustained neither by themselves nor by others and, even with large bodies, cannot bear small pains.
