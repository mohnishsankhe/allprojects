one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); a verse work in thirty-two chapters: the first sets out the purport of all scripture (Viṣṇu's supremacy, the five differences, the gradation of souls, bhakti as the means); the second the principles of interpretation, including the threefold meaning of the Mahābhārata; the third creation; and the rest retell the Rāmāyaṇa and the Mahābhārata, including Kṛṣṇa's life, in the light of that purport. It closes with the author naming himself the third form of Vāyu, born when 4,300 years of Kali had passed.
