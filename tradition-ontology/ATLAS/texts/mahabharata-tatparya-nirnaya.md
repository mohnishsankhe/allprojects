**Availability:** digitized-original

one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); a verse work in thirty-two chapters: the first sets out the purport of all scripture (Viṣṇu's supremacy, the five differences, the gradation of souls, bhakti as the means); the second the principles of interpretation, including the threefold meaning of the Mahābhārata; the third creation; and the rest retell the Rāmāyaṇa and the Mahābhārata, including Kṛṣṇa's life, in the light of that purport. It closes with the author naming himself the third form of Vāyu, born when 4,300 years of Kali had passed.

### 1.1 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Salutation to Nārāyaṇa, ocean of perfect qualities, who gives the rise, maintenance, dissolution and control of the world, gives knowledge, and is the true cause of the happiness of the gods and the sorrow of the asuras, the all-pervading.

### 1.2-5 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-1-2-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In the beginning Nārāyaṇa alone existed, supreme, an ocean of qualities, holding in his belly all (souls) with their awareness quiescent, within Lakṣmī's embrace, delighting in himself. Seeing the conscious beings lying in him — the liberated, those absorbed in meditation, those in transmigration, those in deep sleep, from Brahmā down to Kali and to humans — he resolved to create them so that the embodied would reach happiness, sorrow or the mean according to their nature; this is his sport (vihāra), and it serves the welfare of those who depend on him.

### 1.68-71 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-1-68-71"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Quoted as scripture:) All Viṣṇu's qualities are real; the difference of soul and Lord is real; the mutual difference of souls is real; and the world is real. Only (the notion of) internal difference in Viṣṇu is unreal. This real flow of the world has the five differences — of soul and Lord, of souls from one another, of matter and Lord, of material things from one another, and of matter and soul. These five differences are eternal, in every state and in every way; they are not lost even for the liberated, and gradation holds always.

> jīveśayor bhidā caiva jīvabhedaḥ parasparam | jaḍeśayor jaḍānāṃ ca jaḍajīvabhidā tathā || pañcabhedā ime nityāḥ sarvāvasthāsu sarvaśaḥ | muktānāṃ ca na hīyante tāratamyaṃ ca sarvadā ||

### 1.76-77 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-1-76-77"></a>

### 1.78-80 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-1-78-80"></a>

### 1.81-84 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-1-81-84"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Liberation comes only from knowing the five differences, Viṣṇu's non-difference within himself, his flawlessness and abundance of qualities; from rightly knowing Hari's avatāras, those who are not his avatāras, and his indwellings (āveśa); from knowing that creation, protection, destruction, knowledge, control, ignorance, bondage and release are from Viṣṇu alone; and from knowing the Vedas, the Pañcarātras, the Itihāsas and Purāṇas to have Viṣṇu as their purport — not otherwise.

### 1.86-90 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-1-86-90"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The groups of souls are three — gods, humans and dānavas. The gods and the best of humans are fit for liberation; middling humans are always fit only for transmigration; the lowest are for hell, and the dānavas for dissolution in darkness. Liberation and (that) darkness are eternal, with no return from either; gods never go to hell or darkness, asuras are never liberated, and middling humans reach neither. The asuras necessarily reach darkness when, though the wise are present, they do not accept the Supreme.

### 1.96-99 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-1-96-99"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A person who desires what he is unfit for surely falls; so Viṣṇu is always to be served according to one's fitness. By flawless service, by desirelessness and according to fitness Hari can be seen by all (the fit), not otherwise; this law of Hari cannot be transgressed by any conscious being, and by his true resolve Viṣṇu will not act otherwise. (Quoted:) gifts, pilgrimage, austerity, sacrifice and the rest are all limbs in the service of Hari; bhakti alone is for liberation.

### 2.129-133 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-2-129-133"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Lord himself declared the meaning of the Bhārata threefold, for 'some say it begins with Manu, others with Āstīka, others with Uparicara': the meaning that follows the Pāṇḍavas with Kṛṣṇa, with its wonders of reversal and the like, is called āstīka; where dharma, devotion and the rest, learning, conduct and humility, together with Brahmā, are taught, the wise call it manvādi; and where all words are names of Nārāyaṇa expressing his power, that is auparicara.

### 32.162-166 <a id="tea-mahabharata-tatparya-nirnaya-32-162-166"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This third body of Pavana (Vāyu) made this book to bring out the true meaning of the Bhārata, in which devotees of Hari's feet delight; this is what the Vedic words ('the third of this bull ... daśapramati', Ṛgveda 1.141.2–3), the Purāṇas and the Pañcarātra declare. Of him whose three divine forms are declared in the Veda — Vāyu who carried Rāma's word, Pṛkṣa (Bhīma) the second, and Madhva the third — by this (third) the book was made for Keśava.
