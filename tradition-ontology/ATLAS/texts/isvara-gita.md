# Īśvara Gītā

**Dates:** Tradition's account: spoken by Īśvara in a former age; Scholarly account: c. 6th-9th c. CE (Pāśupata redaction of the Kūrma Purāṇa); (confidence low)
**Structure:** 11 chapters of the Kūrma Purāṇa's second part: the secret knowledge of the Self (2), the unmanifest, time, pradhāna and puruṣa (3), the Lord's greatness (4-7) with Śiva's dance (5), knowledge (8), the Lord's cosmic form question (9), the liṅga as Brahman (10), yoga and the Pāśupata observance (11)
**Availability:** digitized-original

Śiva teaches the Self as one pure consciousness distinct from body and mind, the Sāṃkhya categories under the Lord, his own supremacy (displayed in a cosmic dance), devotion and knowledge, and an eight-limbed yoga ending in the Pāśupata yoga of smearing with sacred ash and meditating on Īśāna as the supreme light, which frees the bound soul (paśu) from its bonds (pāśa).
