Nityanātha's compendium in five sections — mercury (for physicians), the lord of mercury (for the sick), metallurgy (for alchemists), rejuvenation (for the aged) and mantra (for mantra-practitioners) (Rasa-khaṇḍa 1.3-6). It warns that impure mercury is poison and that a physician skilled in practice but ignorant of the śāstra is death in human form. RESTRICTED: summary and warnings only.

### rasa.1.3-6 <a id="tea-rasaratnakara-rasa-1-3-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

This five-part science benefits five kinds of practitioner: the section on rasa serves physicians, the section on the lord of rasa the sick, the section on metallurgy the alchemists, the section on rejuvenation the aged, and the section on mantra those seeking mantra-success; for those who study each section, success in rasa arises without doubt.

### rasa.1.20-22 <a id="tea-rasaratnakara-rasa-1-20-22"></a>

### rasa.1.26-30 <a id="tea-rasaratnakara-rasa-1-26-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

A physician skilled in application who has not understood the meaning of the science is to be known as Yama himself, death in the form of mortals. Mercury carries grave natural impurities, each causing its own affliction — swellings, skin disease, burning, loss of seed, death, eruptions and delusion; when mercury is free of impurities it removes death and old age; mercury with its impurities, though nectar itself, is poison. Therefore purification of mercury is enjoined for removing the impurities.

> sākṣādamṛtamapyeṣa doṣayukto raso viṣam

### rasa.5-7 <a id="tea-rasaratnakara-rasa-5-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate · _restricted: summary only_

The minerals and gems used with mercury are also declared deadly when unpurified: impure sulphur causes severe skin disease, burning, giddiness and pitta disorders; an unpurified 'diamond' destroys life; impure mica destroys life and increases vāta and kapha; impure orpiment and realgar cause wasting and urinary disease; impure pyrite (mākṣika) causes death without doubt.
