Kundakunda's 'essence of the teaching' in three parts: knowledge (the omniscient's knowledge and its bliss beyond the senses), the knowable (substance, quality and mode; the soul and matter), and conduct (the ascetic's discipline, nudity and the pure state of equanimity). Conduct is dharma, and dharma is equanimity free of delusion and agitation.

### 1.7 <a id="tea-pravacanasara-1-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Conduct is indeed dharma; that dharma is declared to be equanimity (sama); equanimity is the state of the self free from delusion and agitation.

### 3 <a id="tea-pravacanasara-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The third part, on conduct: the renunciant takes the sign 'like the newborn' — naked, with hair plucked out, without possessions — and keeps the twenty-eight root qualities; he is equal to friend and foe, to praise and blame, to clod and gold. In Jayasena's longer recension this part includes verses stating that women cannot take this sign in its fullness and are not liberated in that birth; these verses are absent from Amṛtacandra's recension.
