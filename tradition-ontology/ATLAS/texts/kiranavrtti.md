Rāmakaṇṭha's Siddhānta commentary on the Kiraṇatantra; among much else it defines liberation as the manifestation of the soul's own Śiva-nature and rejects the rival accounts of transfer, possession and production of the Lord's qualities.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1 <a id="tea-kiranavrtti-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When a soul has been initiated by the teacher through an intense descent of power, it is filled with the manifestation of Śiva-nature and is no longer transmigrant. Liberation here is the manifestation of [the soul's own] Śiva-nature, not a transfer [of the Lord's qualities] into the perfected soul, nor possession, nor production; and release comes through initiation, the Lord's act, not through knowledge, yoga or renunciation.

> śivatvasya vyaktir iha mokṣaḥ na tu siddhe saṅkrāntir āveśaḥ samutpattir vā

### 1-6 <a id="tea-kiranavrtti-1-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Rāmakaṇṭha argues that because mala is a substance (dravya) and not ignorance, knowledge cannot remove it — as knowing a cataract does not cure it; it is removed by Śiva's power applied in initiation, which is therefore the direct cause of liberation.
