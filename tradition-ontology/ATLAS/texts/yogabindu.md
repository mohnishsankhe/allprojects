Haribhadra's Sanskrit treatise (c. 527 verses) on the qualification for yoga (the last turning, caramāvarta; the one who will not again bind the maximum karma, apunarbandhaka), the preliminaries (pūrvasevā), five kinds of religious performance (poison, venom, non-performance, its cause, nectar) and five yogas: adhyātma, bhāvanā, dhyāna, samatā and vṛttisaṃkṣaya.

### 31 <a id="tea-yogabindu-31"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Adhyātma, cultivation (bhāvanā), meditation, equanimity (samatā) and the ending of mental activity (vṛttisaṃkṣaya) — this yoga, because it joins [the soul] to liberation, is better in each successive stage.

### anusthana <a id="tea-yogabindu-anusthana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Religious performance is of five kinds: 'poison' (done for this-worldly gain), 'venom' (for heavenly reward), mechanical non-performance, performance that is a cause [of the true], and 'nectar' (done with right understanding and longing for liberation); the first three are to be avoided.

### caramavarta <a id="tea-yogabindu-caramavarta"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Only a soul in its last cycle of worldly existence (caramāvarta), who no longer binds karma of the most intense kind (apunarbandhaka), is fit for yoga; for others the teaching does not take hold.

### vrttisanksaya <a id="tea-yogabindu-vrttisanksaya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

In the ending of mental activity the soul's activities born of the body and mind cease at their root; from it come omniscience and liberation.
