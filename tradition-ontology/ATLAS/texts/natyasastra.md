### 1.106-107 <a id="tea-natyasastra-1-106-107"></a>

### 1.108-113 <a id="tea-natyasastra-1-108-113"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In drama there is sometimes dharma, sometimes play, wealth, peace, laughter, battle, love or killing: dharma for those given to dharma, love for lovers, restraint for the unruly, discipline for the disciplined, courage for cowards, learning for the ignorant; it is an imitation of the ways of the world (lokavṛttānukaraṇa), with many moods and situations, based on the deeds of high, low and middling people, giving counsel, steadiness, play and happiness.

### 6 (prose after v. 31) <a id="tea-natyasastra-6-31-prose"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Rasa arises from the union of the determinants (vibhāva), the consequents (anubhāva) and the transient states (vyabhicāribhāva). As a taste arises from the combination of various condiments, herbs and substances, so the stable emotions, joined with the various bhāvas, attain the state of rasa. Why is it called rasa? Because it is relished (āsvādyatva).

> vibhāvānubhāvavyabhicārisaṃyogād rasaniṣpattiḥ

### 6 (śānta passage after v. 82) <a id="tea-natyasastra-6-82-santa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Now the peaceful (śānta), whose stable emotion is calm (śama), leading to liberation: it arises from the determinants knowledge of reality, dispassion and purity of mind, and is acted through restraints and observances, contemplation of the self, meditation, concentration, worship and compassion for all beings; where there is neither pain nor pleasure, neither hatred nor envy, and one is equal to all beings — that is known as śānta. Love and the other bhāvas are modifications, śānta is the natural state (prakṛti): each bhāva arises from śānta on meeting its cause and dissolves back into it when the cause is gone. Thus nine rasas are seen.

> न यत्र दुःखं न सुखं न द्वेषो नापि मत्सरः। समः सर्वेषु भूतेषु स शान्तः प्रथितो रसः॥
