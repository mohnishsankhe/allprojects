Guṇaratna's commentary on Haribhadra's Ṣaḍdarśanasamuccaya. On the Naiyāyikas it describes them as Śaiva ascetics (staff, loincloth, matted hair, ash, sacred thread), lists the eighteen avatāras/tīrtheśas of their god from Nakulīśa to Vidyāguru, divides the ascetics into four kinds - Śaivas, Pāśupatas, Mahāvratadharas and Kālamukhas - and says Naiyāyikas are called Śaivas and Vaiśeṣikas Pāśupatas.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### naiyayika-section <a id="tea-tarkarahasyadipika-naiyayika-section"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Naiyāyikas, also called Yaugas, carry staffs, wear a loincloth and blanket, have matted hair, smear ash, wear the sacred thread and carry water-pots, live mostly in forests on roots and fruits, some with wives and some without (the latter being best), and the most advanced go naked; they greet with 'oṃ namaḥ śivāya'. Their god Īśvara, omniscient maker and destroyer, has eighteen avatāras worshipped as tīrtheśas: Nakulīśa, Kauśika, Gārgya, Maitrya, Kauruṣa, Īśāna, Pāragārgya, Kapilāṇḍa, Manuṣyaka, Kuśika, Atri, Piṅgala, Puṣpaka, Bṛhadārya, Agasti, Santāna, Rāśīkara and Vidyāguru. They say: 'Whoever serves the Śaiva initiation for twelve years and then leaves it - even a slave woman or slave - attains nirvāṇa.'

### naiyayika-section <a id="tea-tarkarahasyadipika-naiyayika-section-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

These ascetics are of four kinds by their conduct - Śaivas, Pāśupatas, Mahāvratadharas and, fourth, Kālamukhas - with subgroups such as bharaṭas, bhaktas, laiṅgikas and tāpasas; for bharaṭas and the like there is no rule of varṇa in taking the vow - whoever has devotion to Śiva may take it. In the treatises the Naiyāyikas are called Śaivas and the Vaiśeṣikas Pāśupatas.

> śaivāḥ pāśupatāś caiva mahāvratadharās tathā | turyāḥ kālamukhā mukhyā bhedā ete tapasvinām
