The largest and probably oldest Upanisad: the cosmic horse; the self that was alone in the beginning and said 'I am'; 'I am brahman'; Ajātaśatru's teaching of the sleeping person; the two forms of brahman and neti neti; Yājñavalkya's dialogues with Maitreyī, with the brahmins at Janaka's court (Gārgī, Uddalaka, Śākalya) and with Janaka on light, dream, sleep, death and karma; da da da; the contest of the breaths; the five fires and the two paths; lineages of teachers.

## Teachings (88: sourced 88)

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-1-1"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The head of the sacrificial horse is the dawn, its eye the sun, its breath the wind, its open mouth the fire common to all men, its body the year; its back the sky, belly the intermediate space, the quarters its flanks; its limbs the seasons and its joints the months and fortnights; the stars its bones, the rivers its veins, plants and trees its hair; when it yawns it lightens, when it shakes it thunders; its voice is speech.

### 1.2.1-7 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-2-1-7"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

In the beginning there was nothing here; this was covered by death, by hunger, for hunger is death. Death thought 'let me have a body (ātman)' and, worshipping, produced water, fire and the worlds; he desired a second body, united with speech and brought forth the year; the horse sacrifice is explained from this.

### 1.3.1-7 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-3-1-7"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The gods and the asuras, both children of Prajāpati, contended. The gods asked speech, breath (smell), sight, hearing and mind in turn to sing the udgītha for them; the asuras pierced each with evil (pāpman), which is why one speaks, smells, sees, hears and thinks what is improper. Then they asked the breath in the mouth; striking it, the asuras were scattered like a clod thrown against a rock.

### 1.3.28 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-3-28"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

When the prastotṛ begins the pavamāna praise, the sacrificer should mutter the ascents: 'From the unreal lead me to the real; from darkness lead me to light; from death lead me to immortality.' The text explains: the unreal is death, the real immortality; darkness is death, light immortality; each means 'make me immortal'. With the rest of the chant he may win for himself whatever he desires.

> asato mā sad gamaya | tamaso mā jyotir gamaya | mṛtyor māmṛtaṃ gamaya

### 1.4.1 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-4-1"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

In the beginning this was the self alone, in the form of a person. Looking around he saw nothing other than himself. He first said 'I am' (so 'ham asmi); hence the name 'I'. Even now, when addressed, one first says 'It is I' and then another name.

### 1.4.2 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-4-2"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

He was afraid; that is why one who is alone is afraid. He considered: 'since there is nothing other than me, of what am I afraid?' His fear left him, for of what should he be afraid? Fear arises from a second.

`sourced` · confidence high

He found no delight; one who is alone finds no delight. He desired a second; he was as large as a woman and a man embracing; he split himself into two, and from that husband and wife came to be; from their union human beings were born (and in 1.4.4 all pairs of creatures down to the ants).

### 1.4.7 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-4-7"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

This was then unmanifest; it became manifest by name and form. He entered here up to the nail-tips, like a razor in its case. Breathing he is called breath, speaking speech, seeing sight, hearing hearing, thinking mind — these are only the names of his actions. Whoever venerates one of them does not know, for he is incomplete in each. One should venerate him as the self (ātmety evopāsīta), for in it all these become one; this self is the track (padanīya) of all this.

### 1.4.8 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-4-8"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

This self is dearer than a son, dearer than wealth, dearer than all else, for it is more inward. If someone who holds anything other than the self dear were told 'he will lose what he holds dear', it may happen. One should venerate the self alone as dear; what he holds dear will not perish.

### 1.4.10 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-4-10"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

In the beginning this was brahman; it knew only itself: 'I am brahman' (ahaṃ brahmāsmi); therefore it became all. Whoever among the gods, the seers and humans awoke to it became that. Seeing this, the seer Vāmadeva declared 'I was Manu and the sun'. Even now whoever knows 'I am brahman' becomes this all; even the gods cannot prevent it, for he becomes their self. But whoever venerates another deity, thinking 'he is one, I am another', does not know; he is like a domestic animal for the gods.

> brahma vā idam agra āsīt tad ātmānam evāvet ahaṃ brahmāsmīti tasmāt tat sarvam abhavat

### 1.4.11-15 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-4-11-15"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Brahman, being alone, was not fully developed; it created the ruling power (kṣatra), then the commoners (viś), then the śūdra class, and still not fully developed, it created dharma, the ruling power of the ruling power; nothing is higher than dharma; through dharma a weak man prevails over a strong as through a king. Dharma is truth: of one who speaks the truth they say 'he speaks dharma'.

### 1.4.17 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-4-17"></a>

In the beginning the self alone was, and it desired: 'may I have a wife, offspring, wealth, and may I perform rites'; one who lacks any of these considers himself incomplete. There are three worlds: the world of men, the world of the fathers and the world of the gods. ### 1.5.17 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-5-17"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The transfer (sampratti): when a father thinks he is about to depart he says to his son 'You are brahman, you are the sacrifice, you are the world'; the son replies 'I am brahman, I am the sacrifice, I am the world'. A son so instructed is said to secure the world; when the father departs he enters the son together with his breaths, and whatever he has left undone the son frees him from.

### 1.5.21-23 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-1-5-21-23"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

An inquiry into vows (vratamīmāṃsā): speech, sight, hearing and the others each vowed to keep working; death, as weariness, seized them, but did not seize the breath in the middle; the functions took its form, so they are called 'breaths'. Therefore one should keep a single vow: to breathe in and to breathe out, lest the evil one, death, seize him.

### 2.1.1-15 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-2-1-1-15"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The proud Gārgya Bālāki offers to explain brahman to Ajātaśatru of Kāśī and proposes in turn the person in the sun, the moon, lightning, space, wind, fire, water, a mirror, the sound that follows one who walks, the quarters, the shadow and the person in the self (ātmani, i.e. in the body); to each Ajātaśatru replies that he already venerates it as such, with a limited result. ### 2.5.16-19 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-2-5-16-19"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

### 6.3.1-13 <a id="tea-brhadaranyaka-upanisad-6-3-1-13"></a>

The mantha rite for one who desires greatness: after a period of observance he prepares a mixture in a cup, offers ghee into the fire to the breaths and other deities, pours residues into the mixture, recites and drinks it.
