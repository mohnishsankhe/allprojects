The Hindi (old Hindi / Sadhukkaḍī) sayings ascribed to Gorakhnāth: sabadīs (couplet-sayings), pads (songs) and short works such as the Machhīndra Gorakh Bodh, Ātma Bodh, Narvai Bodh, Abhai Mātrā Jog, Sisṭ Purāṇ, Pandrah Tithi, Sapt Vār and Romāvalī. They teach the yogī's restraint and middle way, the guru, the unstruck sound, the sky-circle and its inverted well of nectar, the retention of bindu, the conquest of death by 'dying the death Gorakh died', and the rejection of temple, mosque and caste.
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: original; name: Pītāmbaradatta Baṛthvāl (ed.), Gorakh-bānī (Prayag: Hindi Sahitya Sammelan, 1942)

### sabadi/1 <a id="tea-gorakhbani-sabadi-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Laugh, play, keep meditation; day and night speak the knowledge of Brahman; those who laugh and play yet do not break the mind abide ever steady with the Nāth.

### sabadi/2 <a id="tea-gorakhbani-sabadi-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Die, O yogī, die — death is sweet; die that death by which Gorakh died and saw.

`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the circle of the sky is an upside-down well where the nectar dwells; one who has a guru drinks it to the full, one without a guru goes away thirsty.
