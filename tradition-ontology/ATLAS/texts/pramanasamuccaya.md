Dignāga's culminating work, which founded the Buddhist pramāṇa school: two means of knowledge because two kinds of object; perception free of conceptual construction; reflexive awareness as the result; the triple character of the reason; the meaning of words as exclusion of others; and criticism of the Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Sāṃkhya and Mīmāṃsā accounts.

## Teachings (8: skeleton 8)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-pramanasamuccaya-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dignāga salutes the Buddha, who has become a means of valid cognition (pramāṇa-bhūta), seeks the welfare of the world, is the teacher, the Sugata and the protector, and resolves to compile, for the establishment of valid cognition, a compendium of his own scattered views.

_level: conventional · standpoint: devotional · path: knowledge, devotion · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission_

### 1.2 <a id="tea-pramanasamuccaya-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There are only two means of valid cognition, perception and inference, because the object to be known has two characteristics — its own particular character and the general character; there is no further object, so there is no further means.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: ultimate, dispute_

### 1.3 <a id="tea-pramanasamuccaya-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Perception is free of conceptual construction; construction is the joining of a name, a genus and the like (to what is perceived).

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind_

### 1.6-7 <a id="tea-pramanasamuccaya-1-6-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Mental perception includes the awareness of an object and the self-awareness of desire and other feelings, which is free of construction; so is the yogins' vision of the object alone, unmixed with the teacher's instruction. Erroneous cognition, conventional cognition, inference and memory are pseudo-perceptions.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind, powers-experiences_

### 1.8-10 <a id="tea-pramanasamuccaya-1-8-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The means of knowledge is the cognition itself as having the form of the object, and its result is that same cognition as self-awareness; a cognition arises with two appearances, of itself and of the object, and its self-awareness is the result — so the distinction of means, object and result is only figurative, for cognition is one.

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: consciousness-mind_

### 2 <a id="tea-pramanasamuccaya-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Inference for oneself is seeing the object through a reason having three characteristics; the reliable word (āptavāda) is a form of inference, because it is like inference in not deceiving.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: sound-language, dispute_

### 3 <a id="tea-pramanasamuccaya-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Inference for others is making known to another the object one has seen oneself, by stating the triple-marked reason; a thesis is what one intends to prove oneself, not contradicted by perception, inference, one's own doctrine or common usage.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: sound-language_

terms: [parārthānumāna](../terms/pararthanumana.md), [pakṣa](../terms/paksa.md) · teachers: [Dignāga](../teachers/dignaga.md) · disputes: [How many members does a demonstration (inference for others) need?](../debates/members-of-inference.md)

### 5 <a id="tea-pramanasamuccaya-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

A word does not denote the particulars, which are infinite, nor a real universal; it expresses its object through the exclusion of other objects (anyāpoha) — as 'tree' denotes by excluding the non-tree.
