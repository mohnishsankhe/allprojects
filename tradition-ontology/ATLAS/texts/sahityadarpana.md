Viśvanātha's comprehensive manual in ten chapters: poetry is a sentence whose soul is rasa (1.3); rasa is the stable emotion made relishable by determinants, consequents and transient states (3.1), undivided, self-luminous, bliss and consciousness, twin of tasting Brahman (3.2); śānta (3.245–250) and vatsala ('parental affection', 3.251) are also rasas.

### 3.1 <a id="tea-sahityadarpana-3-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The stable emotion, love and the rest, of sensitive people, manifested by the determinants, the consequents and the transient states, becomes rasa.

### 3.2-3 <a id="tea-sahityadarpana-3-2-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Arising from the predominance of sattva, undivided, self-luminous, made of bliss and consciousness, free from contact with any other object of knowledge, the twin brother of tasting Brahman, whose life is supermundane wonder, rasa is relished by certain knowers as not different from their own form.

> sattvodrekād akhaṇḍasvaprakāśānandacinmayaḥ / vedyāntarasparśaśūnyo brahmāsvādasahodaraḥ // lokottaracamatkāraprāṇaḥ kaiścit pramātṛbhiḥ / svākāravad abhinnatvenāyam āsvādyate rasaḥ //

### 3.4-5 <a id="tea-sahityadarpana-3-4-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Even in the compassionate and similar rasas supreme happiness arises; the experience of the sensitive is the only proof; were they painful, no one would turn to them, and the Rāmāyaṇa and the like would be causes of pain.

### 3.245-250 <a id="tea-sahityadarpana-3-245-250"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Śānta, whose stable emotion is calm (śama), belongs to the best characters, is white as jasmine and moon, and has Śrī Nārāyaṇa as its deity; its object is the worthlessness of all things through impermanence, or the nature of the supreme self; holy hermitages, places of Hari, pilgrimage places and lovely forests, and the company of great souls, are its stimulants; horripilation its consequents; despondency, joy, remembrance and compassion for beings its transient states. It is not 'compassion-heroism', being free of ego; calm, present in the states of union and separation, becomes rasa.

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge, devotion · stage: all · types: consciousness-mind, karma-liberation_

### 3.251 <a id="tea-sahityadarpana-3-251"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Because it is clearly a source of wonder, parental affection (vatsala) is also known as a rasa; its stable emotion is affection (sneha) and its object a son or the like.

### 3.3 vṛtti <a id="tea-sahityadarpana-3-3-vrtti"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Viśvanātha's commentary: wonder (camatkāra), an expansion of the mind, is the essence of rasa; therefore his great-grandfather Nārāyaṇa declared that the marvellous (adbhuta) alone is rasa.
