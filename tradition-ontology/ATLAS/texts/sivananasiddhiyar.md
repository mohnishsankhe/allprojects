Aruṇanti Civācāriyār's great exposition of the Civañāṉa Pōtam in two parts: Parapakkam, stating and refuting fourteen rival schools (Lokāyata, four Buddhist schools, Jains, Ājīvikas, Mīmāṃsakas, Māyāvāda Vedānta, Pāñcarātra and others), and Cupakkam, the Siddhānta's own doctrine arranged under the twelve sūtras (God, soul, bonds, grace, the kinds of initiation, the four paths, liberation).

## Teachings (9: skeleton 9)

### cupakkam.1 <a id="tea-sivananasiddhiyar-cupakkam-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Lord performs the five acts — creation, maintenance, destruction, concealment and grace — not for himself but out of compassion, so that souls may exhaust their karma and ripen their mala; even destruction is rest for the souls.

_level: conventional · standpoint: divine · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: ultimate, world-fate_

terms: [pañcakṛtya](../terms/pancakrtya.md) · concepts: [Śiva's five acts (pañcakṛtya)](../concepts/five-acts-of-siva.md)

### cupakkam.2 <a id="tea-sivananasiddhiyar-cupakkam-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

God's non-duality with souls means not identity but inseparability: he is one with them, different from them and together with them, as the vowel is in the consonants; the Vedic sayings of oneness are to be understood so.

_level: bridging · standpoint: divine · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: ultimate_

### cupakkam.8 <a id="tea-sivananasiddhiyar-cupakkam-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

When the two deeds come into balance and the soul's mala has ripened, Śiva's power descends (catti nipātam) in four grades — very mild, mild, intense and very intense — fitting the soul for caryā, kriyā, yoga or jñāna; Śiva then comes as the guru and initiates.

_level: conventional · standpoint: divine · path: general · stage: all · types: karma-liberation, teacher-transmission_

### cupakkam.8 <a id="tea-sivananasiddhiyar-cupakkam-8-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The guru initiates in seven ways — by look, touch, word, thought, instruction in the scriptures, yoga, and the fire rite (auttiri) — destroying the accumulated karma; the prārabdha that formed the body is exhausted by experience.

_level: conventional · standpoint: ritual · path: ritual, knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: teacher-transmission, karma-liberation_

### cupakkam.8 <a id="tea-sivananasiddhiyar-cupakkam-8-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Caryā, kriyā, yoga and jñāna are four steps; the first three give the lower liberations (sālokya, sāmīpya, sārūpya) and ripen the soul; only jñāna, given by the guru's grace, leads to union (sāyujya). Jñāna itself proceeds by hearing, reflection, clarity and abiding (kēṭṭal, cintittal, teḷital, niṭṭai).

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: action, ritual, meditation, knowledge · stage: all · types: practice, karma-liberation_

### cupakkam.11 <a id="tea-sivananasiddhiyar-cupakkam-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In liberation the soul does not become Śiva nor perish; it abides inseparable from him, enjoying his bliss, as the malas, which are eternal, lie powerless.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: experiential · path: knowledge · stage: realized · types: karma-liberation_

### parapakkam <a id="tea-sivananasiddhiyar-parapakkam"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The opponents' section sets out in their own terms and then refutes the Lokāyata, the four Buddhist schools, the Jains, the Ājīvikas, the Mīmāṃsakas, the grammarians' word-Brahman, Māyāvāda Vedānta, Bhāskara's view, the Pāñcarātra, the Sāṃkhya and others, as a preparation for the Siddhānta.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: dispute_

concepts: [The Siddhānta doxography of rival schools](../concepts/four-circles-of-schools.md) · disputes: [Is the world real, or an appearance (mithyā) that is neither real nor unreal, superimposed on the one reality — and is māyā an indeterminable ignorance or the Lord's real power?](../debates/world-real-or-appearance.md), [Is there a self (ātman, puruṣa, jīva) — an enduring conscious subject distinct from body and changing cognitions — or only a stream of impersonal factors designated 'person'?](../debates/is-there-a-self.md)

### parapakkam.lokayata <a id="tea-sivananasiddhiyar-parapakkam-lokayata"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Parapakkam opens with the Lōkāyatam: only perception is valid, the four elements alone exist, consciousness arises from their combination, and there is no other world — and refutes it.
