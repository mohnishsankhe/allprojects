An eight-limbed yoga for Vedic householders and renouncers taught by Yājñavalkya to Gārgī: ten yamas and ten niyamas, postures, the channels and the ten breaths, purification of the channels, breath-control with Vedic mantra, a fivefold withdrawal including the movement of breath through the eighteen vital points, the five element concentrations, meditation and samādhi. It allows women and śūdras to practise with non-Vedic mantras (recalled).

### 1.topic.frame <a id="tea-yoga-yajnavalkya-1-topic-frame"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Gārgī asks Yājñavalkya for the yoga that leads to liberation, and he teaches the eight-limbed yoga: yama, niyama, āsana, prāṇāyāma, pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna and samādhi.

### 1.topic.yamas <a id="tea-yoga-yajnavalkya-1-topic-yamas"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The ten restraints are non-harming, truthfulness, non-stealing, continence, compassion, straightforwardness, forbearance, fortitude, moderate diet and purity; the ten observances (in the next chapter) include austerity, contentment, faith, giving, worship of the Lord, listening to doctrine, modesty, intelligence, repetition and vows.

_level: conventional · standpoint: ethical-social · path: general · stage: beginner · types: ethics_

### topic.pratyahara-marmas <a id="tea-yoga-yajnavalkya-topic-pratyahara-marmas"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

One form of withdrawal is to draw the breath from one vital point (marma) to the next along the eighteen vital points of the body, holding it at each; this is taught among five kinds of withdrawal.

### topic.women-sudras <a id="tea-yoga-yajnavalkya-topic-women-sudras"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Women and śūdras too may practise breath-control, using a non-Vedic (e.g. Śaiva or Vaiṣṇava) mantra in place of the Vedic praṇava/Gāyatrī.
