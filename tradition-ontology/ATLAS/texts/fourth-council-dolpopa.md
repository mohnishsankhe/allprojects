Dolpopa's short verse work (with his own commentary) presenting his teaching as a 'fourth council': it reckons the ages of the doctrine and holds that the teaching of the age of perfection (kṛtayuga), preserved in the Kālacakra and the definitive sūtras, is the Buddha's final intent — the ultimate empty of other.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### whole <a id="tea-fourth-council-dolpopa-definitive"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

In the Dharma of the age of perfection the ultimate is taught as empty of other and endowed with qualities, while all conventional phenomena are empty of self; the three wheels culminate in this teaching.

### whole <a id="tea-fourth-council-dolpopa-four-ages"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The doctrine is reckoned by the four ages — perfection (kṛta), the three-portioned, the two-portioned and the strife age. The teaching of the age of perfection, the Buddha's final and definitive intent, had declined; Dolpopa's exposition is presented as a 'fourth council' that restores it.
