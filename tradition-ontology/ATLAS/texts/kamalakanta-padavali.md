The Bengali songs of Kamalākānta Bhaṭṭācārya to Kālī/Śyāmā and to Umā, in which the Mother's blackness is questioned and affirmed as the colour of the formless, and the devotee's mind is urged to rest in her.

### sadanandamayi-kali <a id="tea-kamalakanta-padavali-sadanandamayi-kali"></a>

Kālī, ever full of bliss, enchantress of Mahākāla: you dance by yourself, sing by yourself and clap your own hands in time — the world is the Mother's own play.

### syama-ma-ki-amar-kalo <a id="tea-kamalakanta-padavali-syama-ma-ki-amar-kalo"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Is my Mother Śyāmā black? People call Kālī black, but my heart does not: the black-formed, sky-clad one lights up the lotus of the heart; she is sometimes white, sometimes yellow, blue or red — the singer cannot say what she is.
