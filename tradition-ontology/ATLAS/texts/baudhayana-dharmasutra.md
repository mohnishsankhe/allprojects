The Dharmasūtra of the Baudhāyana Kalpasūtra (with later books). It defines the cultured (śiṣṭa) whose practice is authoritative (1.1.1.5–6), records the five practices peculiar to the south and the north and Gautama's rejection of them (1.1.2.1–8), sets out the four orders and the teachers' view that there is only one, telling that the divisions were made by the asura Kapila, son of Prahlāda, in rivalry with the gods (2.6.11.27–28), and treats expiations, the householder's livelihoods, the forest-dweller and the renouncer.

### 1.1.1.5-6 <a id="tea-baudhayana-dharmasutra-1-1-1-5-6"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The cultured (śiṣṭa) are those free from envy and pride, content with a pot of grain, without greed, free from hypocrisy, arrogance, avarice, delusion and anger, who have learned the Veda with its supplements according to dharma and can draw inferences from it.

### 1.1.2.1-8 <a id="tea-baudhayana-dharmasutra-1-1-2-1-8"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

There is disagreement about five practices in the south and in the north (e.g. in the south, eating with an uninitiated person or with one's wife, eating stale food, marrying a maternal uncle's or paternal aunt's daughter; in the north, trading in wool, drinking spirits, dealing in animals with two rows of teeth, taking up arms, going to sea); each is followed in its own region. 'That is false', says Gautama; one should respect neither, since they conflict with the tradition of the cultured.

### 2.6.11.27-28 <a id="tea-baudhayana-dharmasutra-2-6-11-27-28"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The teachers hold that there is only one order, since the others do not produce offspring. On this they quote: 'There was an asura named Kapila, son of Prahlāda; he made these divisions in rivalry with the gods; a wise man should not heed them.'
