Bhāskararāya's commentary on the Lalitā Sahasranāma. It explains each name with several meanings drawn from the tantras, Purāṇas, Upaniṣads and Mīmāṃsā, sets out the Śrīvidyā tradition behind the names (the kūṭas, the cakras, the kuṇḍalinī ascent, the Samaya and Kaula modes), counts and divides the text, and gives the ritual uses (prayoga) of the hymn.

## Teachings (8: skeleton 8)

### 36-37 <a id="tea-saubhagyabhaskara-36-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On 'Kaulinī' and 'intent on Samaya conduct': kula is Śakti and akula Śiva, and their relation is kaula; worship on a cakra drawn in external space is conventionally called Kaula; imagining the cakra in the inner space of the heart (dahara) and worshipping there is called Samaya; the five tantras of Vasiṣṭha, Śuka, Sanaka, Sanandana and Sanatkumāra are also called Samaya; or Samaya is Śiva, who has fivefold equality with the Goddess (of seat, action, state, name and form), as the commentator on the Subhagodaya (Lalla) set out.

### phalasruti-prayoga <a id="tea-saubhagyabhaskara-phalasruti-prayoga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On the concluding section: recitation is a daily duty yet may by explicit statement also serve desired ends, as with the Agnihotra; the offerings are subsidiaries that share the fruit of the main act; and in worship with the names the principal thing is to contemplate the identity of oneself and the deity.

### rudrayamala-ladder <a id="tea-saubhagyabhaskara-rudrayamala-ladder"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Quoting the Rudrayāmala: after many births of merit one is initiated; then by great fortune one becomes devoted to Śiva or Viṣṇu; then the disposition toward Śakti arises; then by great good fortune the disposition toward Sundarī; then the fourth is reached within; and then, rarest of all, the singing of her names — in that birth the eternal Goddess, pleased by the recitation, grants liberation while living and nothing more remains to be done.

### turyatita <a id="tea-saubhagyabhaskara-turyatita"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On 'devoid of all states': one who has passed beyond the fourth state has passed through the first three and is free of all four; this state beyond the fourth (turyātīta) comes from firmness in the fourth, as the saying 'the state beyond the fourth is gained by holding firmly to the fourth' shows.

### twelve-upasakas <a id="tea-saubhagyabhaskara-twelve-upasakas"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The twelve kinds of Śrīvidyā are those of its twelve worshippers — Manu, Candra, Kubera, Lopāmudrā, Manmatha, Agastya, Agni, Sūrya, Indra, Skanda, Śiva and Krodhabhaṭṭāraka; their derivation is to be seen in the Jñānārṇava.
