Briggs (1938) and found as the first hundred verses of the Gorakṣapaddhati: after praising Mīnanātha it teaches the six limbs, the two postures, the knowledge of the six centres, sixteen supports, three aims and five voids of one's own body, the channels and breaths, and ends with the purification of the channels by alternate breathing.

### 2 <a id="tea-goraksasataka-briggs-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Homage to Śrī Mīnanātha, the yogī praised through ages and aeons for holding the lamp-flame of the self motionless by the root lock and other means, in whom Ādinātha himself became a great ocean of the fragrance of knowledge.

> अन्तर्निश्चलितात्मदीपकलिकास्वाधारबन्धादिभिः यो योगी युगकल्पकालकलनात् त्वं जजेगीयते । ज्ञानामोदमहोदधिः समभवद् यत्रादिनाथः स्वयं व्यक्ताव्यक्तगुणाधिकं तम् अनिशं श्रीमीननाथं भजे

### 13-14 <a id="tea-goraksasataka-briggs-13-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

How can yogīs succeed who do not know in their own body the six centres, the sixteen supports, the three aims and the five voids — who do not know their own body as a house of one pillar and nine doors with five presiding deities?

> षट्चक्रं षोडशाधारं त्रिलक्षं व्योमपञ्चकम् । स्वदेहे ये न जानन्ति कथं सिध्यन्ति योगिनः एकस्तम्भं नवद्वारं गृहं पञ्चाधिदैवतम् । स्वदेहं ये न जानन्ति कथं सिध्यन्ति योगिनः

### 42-46 <a id="tea-goraksasataka-briggs-42-46"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The breath goes out with the sound 'ha' and enters again with 'sa'; the jīva always repeats this mantra 'haṃsa haṃsa', twenty-one thousand six hundred times in a day and night; this is the gāyatrī called ajapā, which gives yogins liberation; by merely resolving on it one is freed from all sins; there is no knowledge, repetition or wisdom equal to it; this gāyatrī arises from kuṇḍalinī and sustains life — the one who knows this science of the breath knows yoga.

### 100-101 <a id="tea-goraksasataka-briggs-100-101"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

By alternately breathing through iḍā and piṅgalā while meditating on the discs of sun and moon, the channels of the restrained one become pure; from purification of the channels come holding the breath at will, kindling of the fire, manifestation of the inner sound (nāda) and health.
