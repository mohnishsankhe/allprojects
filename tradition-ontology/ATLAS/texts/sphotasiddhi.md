Maṇḍana Miśra's defence of the sphoṭa in kārikās with his own commentary: the letters, each perishing, cannot convey meaning singly or together; the word is grasped as one entity manifested by the sounds, against Kumārila's view that the letters themselves (through impressions) convey meaning.

## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### 1-37 <a id="tea-sphotasiddhi-1-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Maṇḍana argues that the letters cannot convey meaning, neither singly (for then the other letters would be useless) nor together (for they perish in sequence and never co-exist), nor through the impression of earlier letters (for impressions reproduce only their own objects); the meaning-bearer is the unitary word, grasped as one in a cognition that the sounds progressively clarify.
