Mataṅga's treatise on 'regional' (deśī) music: the teaching on nāda — without nāda there is no song, note or dance; the world is made of nāda; Brahmā, Janārdana, the supreme Śakti and Maheśvara are nāda-forms (16–18); nāda arises in the 'place of Brahmā' from the union of breath and fire (18–22) and is fivefold (23–25) — then śrutis, svaras, grāmas, jātis and the first definition of rāga as the sound-pattern that colours the minds of people (280–281).

### 16-19 <a id="tea-brhaddesi-16-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Now the supreme mark of nāda: without nāda there is no song, without nāda no notes, without nāda no dance — therefore the world is made of nāda. Brahmā is remembered as nāda in form, Janārdana is nāda in form, the supreme Śakti is nāda in form, Maheśvara is nāda in form. In the place called Brahmā's, the knot of Brahmā, dwells breath; from breath fire arises; from the union of fire and wind nāda is born.

> न नादेन विना गीतं न नादेन विना स्वराः। न नादेन विना नृत्तं तस्मान्नादात्मकं जगत्॥ नादरूपः स्मृतो ब्रह्मा नादरूपो जनार्दनः। नादरूपा परा शक्तिर्नादरूपो महेश्वरः॥

### 20-21 <a id="tea-brhaddesi-20-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'From nāda arises bindu, from nāda all that is made of speech' — so some say; others say it arises from the region of the bulb (kanda) and is fivefold, the wind moving subtly and very subtly downward and upward producing the whole course of nāda.

### 22-25 <a id="tea-brhaddesi-22-25"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Na is breath and da fire: this is the correct meaning of the two parts of 'nāda'. Nāda, from the root nad, is fivefold: subtle (in the cave), very subtle (in the heart), manifest (in the throat), unmanifest (in the palate) and artificial (in the mouth).

### 280-281 <a id="tea-brhaddesi-280-281"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

That by which one is coloured (rajyate), through a particular arrangement of notes and melodic movement or a particular sound, is agreed by the good to be a rāga; or, that particular sound adorned with notes and melodic movement which colours (delights) the minds of people is called a rāga.

> योऽसौ ध्वनिविशेषस्तु स्वरवर्णविभूषितः। रञ्जको जनचित्तानां स च राग उदाहृतः॥
