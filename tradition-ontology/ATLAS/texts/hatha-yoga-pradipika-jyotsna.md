The standard Sanskrit commentary: it glosses haṭha as the union of 'ha' (sun, prāṇa) and 'ṭha' (moon, apāna), i.e. prāṇāyāma, and rājayoga as asamprajñāta yoga, the cessation of all mental activity; it discusses who is eligible (the dispassionate, per the Vāyu Saṃhitā, Sureśvara and Yājñavalkya), explains the practices, and reads the text through Pātañjala yoga and Advaita.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-hatha-yoga-pradipika-jyotsna-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Commenting on HYP 1.1: 'ha' and 'ṭha' are the sun and moon, i.e. prāṇa and apāna; their union — prāṇāyāma — is haṭhayoga, as Gorakṣanātha says in the Siddhasiddhāntapaddhati ('ha is the sun, ṭha the moon; from the union of sun and moon it is called haṭhayoga'). Ādinātha taught haṭhavidyā to Girijā (Pārvatī), as is known from the Mahākālayogaśāstra and elsewhere. Rājayoga is 'lofty' because it is the higher stage beyond mantrayoga, haṭhayoga and the rest; rājayoga is asamprajñāta yoga, marked by the cessation of all mental activity.

### 1.11 <a id="tea-hatha-yoga-pradipika-jyotsna-1-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

On who may receive haṭha: it is to be given to one who has conquered the senses, is calm, attached to liberation, free of faults and doing what is enjoined — not to others; Yājñavalkya lists the qualities (performing prescribed duties, free of desire, with yamas and niyamas, without attachments, learned, free of anger, devoted to truth and dharma, serving the guru, devoted to parents, in his own āśrama, of good conduct); it is not to be given to a garb-wearer devoted to penis and belly. The author of the Yogacintāmaṇi says that though Purāṇic verses grant every living being eligibility for yoga, the fruit, liberation, comes only to the dispassionate, so he alone is the proper adhikārin — as the Vāyu Saṃhitā and Sureśvara also say.
