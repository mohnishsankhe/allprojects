Uddyotakara's sub-commentary defending the Nyāya Sūtra and Bhāṣya against Dignāga and other Buddhist critics ('bad reasoners'); it enumerates the six kinds of sense-object contact, defends the self, the whole, universals and Īśvara, and criticizes Buddhist definitions of perception and inference.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1.1.4 <a id="tea-nyaya-varttika-1-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Sense-object contact is of six kinds: conjunction, inherence in the conjoined, inherence in what inheres in the conjoined, inherence, inherence in what inheres, and the relation of qualifier and qualified.

What Akṣapāda, the foremost of sages, taught as a śāstra for the peace (śama) of the world — on that I shall compose a treatise, a cause of removing the ignorance of bad reasoners.

### 3.1.1 (section on the self) <a id="tea-nyaya-varttika-3-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Uddyotakara defends the self as distinct from body, senses, mind and cognition against the Buddhist doctrine of no-self, arguing among other things from the unity of the one who recognizes and remembers, and appealing to Buddhist scripture itself as not denying a self.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: unmarked · types: dispute, consciousness-mind_
