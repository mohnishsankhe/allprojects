one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); Madhva's concise commentary on Bādarāyaṇa's Brahmasūtras, reading them as establishing Viṣṇu, full of qualities and free of defects, as the Brahman they inquire into, and as teaching difference, gradation of souls and liberation by grace won through knowledge. It supports its readings with many quotations from Vedic, Pañcarātra and Purāṇic sources.
