one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); Madhva's concise commentary on Bādarāyaṇa's Brahmasūtras, reading them as establishing Viṣṇu, full of qualities and free of defects, as the Brahman they inquire into, and as teaching difference, gradation of souls and liberation by grace won through knowledge. It supports its readings with many quotations from Vedic, Pañcarātra and Purāṇic sources.

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-madhva-1-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Having bowed to Nārāyaṇa, full of all qualities and free of defects, the meaning of the sūtras is stated. In the Dvāpara age, when knowledge had everywhere become confused, the Lord Nārāyaṇa, entreated by Brahmā, Rudra, Indra and others, descended as Vyāsa; he divided the lost Veda and composed the Brahmasūtras to settle its meaning (quoting the Skanda).

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-madhva-1-1-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Quoting the Bhāgavata-tantra:) Those entitled (adhikārin) are of three grades, low, middle and highest. By birth, the best among humans are the lowest grade, sages and gandharvas the middle, gods the highest; by qualities, one devoted to Viṣṇu who merely studies is the lowest, one with calm and the other virtues the middle, and the highest is one who, knowing everything from Brahmā to a blade of grass to be worthless and impermanent, has become dispassionate, takes Viṣṇu's feet as his sole refuge and has renounced all works.

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-madhva-1-1-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Quoting the Vyoma-saṃhitā:) Even the outcaste (antyaja) who are devotees are entitled to the knowledge of the Name; women, śūdras and degraded brāhmaṇas are entitled to the knowledge of the Tantra (Pañcarātra) in part, as taught by others, not from the text itself; the three twice-born classes who have right devotion to Hari are entitled to what the Veda teaches; and superior women such as Urvaśī, Yamī and Śacī are said to be entitled to the Vedic as well.

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-madhva-1-1-1-4"></a>

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-madhva-1-1-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Quoting the Nāradīya:) Grace won by ritual action is the lowest, grace won by hearing and the like is middling, and grace from fullness of knowledge is the highest; from the lowest comes heaven, from the middling worlds such as Janaloka, and the highest alone gives liberation. Hearing, reflection, meditation and devotion are the principal means to the fullness of knowledge; without them no one has ever gained knowledge.

### 3.1.8 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-madhva-3-1-8"></a>

### 4.3.15 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-madhva-4-3-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Those who do not rely on a (limited) symbol are led to the Supreme. (Quoting the Gāruḍa:) those whose vision of God is confined to their own body 'rely on a symbol'; the gods, a hundred sages, a hundred kings, a hundred gandharvas and the like are entitled to see him as all-pervading, others not; one who strives for a vision he is unfit for falls even from what he had. Those who see Brahman in their own body reach the world of Brahmā and, with Brahmā, the supreme abode at the dissolution.

### 4.4.19 <a id="tea-brahma-sutra-bhasya-madhva-4-4-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Quoting the Gāruḍa:) Having worshipped Hari as the Self, some are released here and do not depart; some abide here, some in the atmosphere, heaven, Mahar, Jana, Tapas or Satya worlds; those of great knowledge go to the Milk Ocean, and there too come nearer by degree of knowledge. Their liberation is sālokya, sārūpya, sāmīpya and yoga (sāyujya). Kings, human and divine gandharvas, ancestors, gods by birth and by merit, the presiding deities of the principles, Prajāpati, Rudra and Brahmā are each a hundredfold above the one before in eternal bliss, enjoyment, knowledge and lordship, and are honoured by those below; Brahmā is honoured by all. Those in union (sāyujya) enter the Supreme Self and come out again, and enjoy enjoyments of consciousness (and some others).
