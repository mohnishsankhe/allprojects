Jayanta Bhaṭṭa's large independent exposition of Nyāya in twelve āhnikas, organized around the means of knowledge and the objects of knowledge; it defends the authority of the Veda and of the Lord, criticizes Buddhist, Mīmāṃsaka and Cārvāka positions, and discusses the validity of the Āgamas.

## Teachings (6: skeleton 6)

### 1.carvaka-dhurta <a id="tea-nyayamanjari-1-carvaka-dhurta"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The rogue Cārvāka (cārvāka-dhūrta), having promised 'now we shall explain the principle', explained as the principle only this: that the number, definitions and scope of the means and objects of knowledge cannot be fixed; and he pointed to kinds of knowing not produced by perception or the other means.

> cārvākadhūrtas tu 'athātas tattvaṃ vyākhyāsyāmaḥ' iti pratijñāya pramāṇaprameyasaṅkhyālakṣaṇaniyamāśakyakaraṇīyatvam eva tattvaṃ vyākhyātavān

### 1.pramana-sankhya <a id="tea-nyayamanjari-1-pramana-sankhya"></a>

### 7.susiksita-carvaka <a id="tea-nyayamanjari-7-susiksita-carvaka"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The well-educated Cārvāka says: let there be one knower lasting as long as the body, capable of recollection — who quarrels about that? But what proof is there that it exists beyond the body? The knower does not pass from the former body to another, for then it would remember its past births as it remembers childhood; so give up the fairy-tale of the other world rooted in the eternal-self doctrine and live at ease: 'while life lasts let one live happily; none is beyond death's reach; once the body is burnt to ashes, how can it return?' Jayanta replies that, having granted a knower lasting through the body, he cannot show a cause of its destruction, so it is eternal.

> yāvaj jīvaṃ sukhaṃ jīven nāsti mṛtyor agocaraḥ / bhasmībhūtasya dehasya punarāgamanaṃ kutaḥ
