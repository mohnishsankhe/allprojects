# Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta

**Availability:** digitized-original

The second discourse, to the five monks at Isipatana: the five aggregates are not-self, since they lead to affliction and cannot be controlled; they are impermanent and suffering, hence not fit to be regarded as 'mine, I, my self'; seeing thus the five are liberated.

## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 2.1-2.5 <a id="tea-anattalakkhana-sutta-2-1-2-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Form is not-self. For if form were self, it would not lead to affliction, and one could have it of form: 'let my form be thus, let my form not be thus'.

> “Rūpaṁ, bhikkhave, anattā. Rūpañca hidaṁ, bhikkhave, attā abhavissa, nayidaṁ rūpaṁ ābādhāya saṁvatteyya, labbhetha ca rūpe: ‘evaṁ me rūpaṁ hotu, evaṁ me rūpaṁ mā ahosī’ti. Yasmā ca kho, bhikkhave, rūpaṁ anattā, tasmā rūpaṁ ābādhāya saṁvattati, na ca labbhati rūpe: ‘evaṁ me rūpaṁ hotu, evaṁ me rūpaṁ mā ahosī’ti.

### 2-5 <a id="tea-anattalakkhana-sutta-2-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Form is not-self; were form self, it would not lead to affliction and one could say 'let my form be thus, let it not be thus'; because form is not-self it leads to affliction and cannot be so commanded. Likewise feeling, perception, formations and consciousness.

### 6 <a id="tea-anattalakkhana-sutta-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Is form permanent or impermanent? — Impermanent. Is what is impermanent suffering or happiness? — Suffering. Is what is impermanent, suffering and subject to change fit to be regarded as 'this is mine, this I am, this is my self'? — No, venerable sir.

> Taṁ kiṁ maññatha, bhikkhave, rūpaṁ niccaṁ vā aniccaṁ vā”ti? “Aniccaṁ, bhante”. “Yaṁ panāniccaṁ dukkhaṁ vā taṁ sukhaṁ vā”ti? “Dukkhaṁ, bhante”. “Yaṁ panāniccaṁ dukkhaṁ vipariṇāmadhammaṁ, kallaṁ nu taṁ samanupassituṁ: ‘etaṁ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā’”ti? “No hetaṁ, bhante”. “Vedanā … saññā … saṅkhārā … viññāṇaṁ niccaṁ vā aniccaṁ vā”ti? “Aniccaṁ, bhante”. “Yaṁ panāniccaṁ dukkhaṁ vā taṁ sukhaṁ vā”ti? “Dukkhaṁ, bhante”. “Yaṁ panāniccaṁ dukkhaṁ vipariṇāmadhammaṁ, kallaṁ nu taṁ samanupassituṁ: ‘etaṁ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā’”ti? “No hetaṁ, bhante”.

### 7-10 <a id="tea-anattalakkhana-sutta-7-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Therefore any kind of form whatever, past, future or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near, should be seen as it really is with right wisdom: 'this is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self'.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: practice, ultimate_

### 11-12 <a id="tea-anattalakkhana-sutta-11-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Seeing thus the instructed noble disciple becomes disenchanted with the aggregates, through disenchantment dispassionate, through dispassion liberated, and knows 'birth is ended'; while this was spoken the minds of the five monks were liberated from the effluents by not clinging.
