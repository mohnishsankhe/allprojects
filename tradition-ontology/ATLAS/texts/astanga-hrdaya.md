# Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya

`src:astanga-hrdaya` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Aṣṭāṅgahṛdayasaṃhitā, Vāgbhaṭa, the Heart of Medicine
**Original title:** अष्टाङ्गहृदयसंहिता
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** shared
**Lineages:** [Āyurveda](../lineages/ayurveda.md), [The Aṣṭavaidya physician families of Kerala](../lineages/kerala-astavaidya.md)
**Authors:** 
  - [Vāgbhaṭa](../teachers/vagbhata.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: Vāgbhaṭa, son of Siṃhagupta, 'lord of physicians' (colophons), who drew the essence from the tantras of Agniveśa and the others (Sū 1.3-5; Utt 40.80).; scholarly: c. 600 CE, Sindh; Buddhist affiliation argued from the opening salutation and the ten sinful acts (Sū 2.21-22); translated into Tibetan (Tengyur).; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 7th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** Six sthānas, 120 chapters: Sūtra 30, Śārīra 6, Nidāna 16, Cikitsita 22, Kalpasiddhi 6, Uttara 40
**Availability:** digitized-original

The most widely studied compendium, a verse synthesis of Caraka and Suśruta covering all eight limbs: the doṣas and their five-fold divisions (Sū 12), daily and seasonal routine and conduct (Sū 2-3), urges (Sū 4), diagnosis, therapeutics, surgery, rejuvenation (Utt 39) and virility (Utt 40). It opens with a salutation to the 'unprecedented physician' who destroyed the diseases of passion and the rest, and closes by urging impartiality: accept what is well said, whoever says it (Utt 40.86-88).
**Commentaries on this text:** [Āyurvedarasāyana](ayurveda-rasayana.md), [Sarvāṅgasundarā](sarvangasundara.md)

### su.1.3-5 <a id="tea-astanga-hrdaya-su-1-3-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Brahmā, remembering the Veda of life, taught it to Prajāpati; he to the Aśvins; they to the thousand-eyed (Indra); he to Atri's son and other sages; they to Agniveśa and others, who composed separate tantras; from these, much scattered, this 'Heart of the Eight Limbs' is made, the choicest essence, neither too brief nor too long. Its eight limbs are body (internal medicine), children, seizers (grahas), the parts above the clavicle, surgery, fangs (toxicology), old age (rejuvenation) and virility.

### su.1.6-10 <a id="tea-astanga-hrdaya-su-1-6-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In brief the doṣas are three — vāyu, pitta and kapha; disturbed they destroy the body, undisturbed they sustain it; though pervasive they reside below, between and above the heart and navel, and predominate at the end, middle and beginning of life, day, night and digestion. By them the fire is irregular, sharp or weak, balanced when they are balanced; so too the bowel. As a worm born in poison is not harmed by it, the doṣa predominant in semen and ovum at conception gives the constitution — inferior, middling or best when single, blameable when of two doṣas, best when balanced.

### su.3.1-7 <a id="tea-astanga-hrdaya-su-3-1-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There are six seasons of two months each from Māgha: late winter, spring, summer, rains, autumn and early winter. The three from late winter make the northern course, the 'taking' period, which daily takes away men's strength: sun and wind, sharp, hot and dry, deplete the earth's cool qualities and the bitter, astringent and pungent tastes grow strong; it is fiery. The rains and the next two make the southern course, the 'releasing' period: the moon is strong, the unctuous sour, salt and sweet tastes prevail; it is cool. Strength is greatest in the cold, least in the rains and summer, middling in the others.

### su.11.37-41 <a id="tea-astanga-hrdaya-su-11-37-41"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ojas is the supreme essence of the dhātus ending with semen; seated in the heart yet pervading, it binds the body's stability; unctuous, of the nature of Soma, pure, slightly reddish-yellow; with its loss death is certain, while it remains life remains, and from it the body's various states arise. It is diminished by anger, hunger, worry, grief and fatigue; in its loss one is fearful, weak, anxious, with afflicted senses, poor lustre, dejected, dry and emaciated; life-giving herbs, milk and meat-juices are its medicines; with its increase come contentment, nourishment and strength.

### su.12.4-18 <a id="tea-astanga-hrdaya-su-12-4-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Vāyu is fivefold: prāṇa in the head, moving in chest and throat, upholding intellect, heart, senses and mind, governing spitting, sneezing, belching, breathing and swallowing; udāna in the chest, moving in nose, navel and throat, governing speech, effort, energy, strength, complexion and memory; vyāna in the heart, moving through the whole body, governing movement, flexion, extension and blinking; samāna near the fire, which takes, digests, separates and releases food; apāna in the lower region, governing semen, menses, faeces, urine and the delivery of the foetus. Pitta is fivefold: pācaka between the stomachs, which digests food and supports the other pittas; rañjaka in the stomach, colouring rasa; sādhaka in the heart, accomplishing intellect, intelligence and self-regard; ālocaka in the eye; bhrājaka in the skin. Kapha is fivefold: avalambaka in the chest, supporting the other seats of kapha; kledaka in the stomach, moistening food; bodhaka in the tongue, perceiving taste; tarpaka in the head, nourishing the senses; śleṣaka in the joints, binding them.

### utt.39.1-4 <a id="tea-astanga-hrdaya-utt-39-1-4"></a>

### utt.40.60-74 <a id="tea-astanga-hrdaya-utt-40-60-74"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Asked why some self-possessed patients with every support waste away while others do not, so that the fruit of observing the wholesome seems uncertain, Punarvasu explained: treatment and non-treatment cannot be equal; the physician's art is a hand held out to those sunk in the mire of disease; not all diseases need means, nor are the curable cured otherwise; untimely death is established in all systems — how else could it be warded off by effort? ### utt.40.86-88 <a id="tea-astanga-hrdaya-utt-40-86-88"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high
