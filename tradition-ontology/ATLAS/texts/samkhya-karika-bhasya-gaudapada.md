A concise commentary on SK 1–69. It presents Kapila as one of Brahmā's seven mind-born sons, born with virtue, knowledge, dispassion and power, who taught the twenty-five principles to Āsuri; names the three kinds of suffering; explains dharma through compassion, giving and the Pātañjala yamas and niyamas; lists the eight powers; details the fifty categories of the creation of cognitions; and argues that neither Īśvara, nature (svabhāva) nor time, but prakṛti, is the world's cause.

## Teachings (14: skeleton 14)

### 1 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Kapila is a son of Brahmā, one of the seven great sages named in a verse: Sanaka, Sananda, Sanātana, Āsuri, Kapila, Voḍhu and Pañcaśikha. Virtue, knowledge, dispassion and power were born together with him; seeing the world sunk in blind darkness, out of compassion he told the knowledge of the twenty-five principles, which destroys suffering, to an inquiring brahmin of the Āsuri clan.

### 1/2 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-1-2"></a>

### 1/3 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The three sufferings are: internal (ādhyātmika), bodily — from imbalance of wind, bile and phlegm, like fever and dysentery — and mental — from separation from the dear and union with the undesired; from other beings (ādhibhautika), from humans, animals, birds, reptiles, insects and plants, born of womb, egg, sweat or sprout; and from the divine (ādhidaivika), such as cold, heat, wind, rain and lightning. Medicine and protection are seen remedies, but they do not remove suffering with certainty or permanently.

### 2 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Vedic means (ānuśravika) are like the seen means: the Veda promises immortality through soma ('we have drunk soma, we have become immortal') and that one who performs the horse sacrifice crosses death and sin, but these means are impure through the killing of animals, perishable, and graded; the better means is the knowledge of the manifest, the unmanifest and the knower.

### 23 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dharma is marked by compassion, giving and the restraints and observances taught in the Pātañjala (non-harming, truth, non-stealing, continence, non-possession; purity, contentment, austerity, study, devotion to Īśvara). Knowledge is external — the Vedas with their six limbs, Purāṇas, Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, Dharmaśāstra — leading only to worldly esteem, or internal — knowledge of prakṛti and puruṣa — leading to liberation. Dispassion is external — aversion to seen objects from seeing the faults of acquiring, guarding, loss, attachment and harm — or internal, seeing even pradhāna as like a dream or magic show. Power is eightfold: becoming minute, great, light, reaching, irresistible will, lordship, control, and going wherever one wishes.

### 43 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-43"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The dispositions are innate (sāṃsiddhika), as the four born with Kapila at the first creation; natural (prākṛta), as in Brahmā's four sons Sanaka, Sanandana, Sanātana and Sanatkumāra at sixteen; and acquired (vaikṛta), as in us, where knowledge arises with the teacher's form as its occasioning cause, from knowledge dispassion, from dispassion virtue, from virtue power.

### 48 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-48"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The sixty-two forms of error: darkness (eightfold) is dissolving into the eight prakṛtis (pradhāna, buddhi, ahaṃkāra, the five subtle elements) and thinking 'I am liberated'; delusion (eightfold) is attachment to the eight powers, through which gods like Indra fail to reach liberation and fall back; great delusion (tenfold) is attachment to the five objects, divine and human; gloom (eighteenfold) is taking delight in the prosperity of the eight powers and ten objects and not accepting their loss; blind gloom (eighteenfold) is the great suffering of one who dies amid enjoyment or falls from the powers.

### 50 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-50"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The contentments: 'nature' — knowing prakṛti and resting content, without liberation; 'means' — taking up the triple staff, water-pot and the like, thinking liberation comes from them, without knowing the principles; 'time' — thinking liberation will come in time, so why practise the principles; 'fortune' — thinking it will come by luck; and five external ones from turning away from the five objects on seeing the faults of acquiring, guarding, loss, attachment and harm. Their names in another treatise are ambhas, salila, ogha, vṛṣṭi, sutamas, pāra, sunetra, nārīka and anuttamāmbhasika.

### 51 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-51"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The accomplishments: reasoning (one who constantly reflects 'what is true, what is highest, what is the final good' comes to know that puruṣa is other than pradhāna); words (knowledge from hearing); study (of the Vedas and other texts, gaining knowledge of the twenty-five principles); the three destructions of suffering (approaching a teacher for instruction to destroy the three sufferings); gaining a friend who has knowledge; and giving (serving the venerable with shelter, medicine, staff, water-pot, food and clothing, and receiving knowledge from them). Error, incapacity and contentment are a goad that holds the world in ignorance like an elephant; abandoning them, one should resort to accomplishment, from which knowledge of the principles and liberation follow.

### 61 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-61"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Some say Īśvara is the cause ('this unknowing creature, not master of its own pleasure and pain, goes to heaven or hell impelled by Īśvara'); others say nature (svabhāva) ('who whitened the swans, who painted the peacocks?'), or time. The Sāṃkhya teachers reply: how could beings with guṇas be born from Īśvara, who is without guṇas, or from puruṣa, also without guṇas? As white cloth comes from white threads, the three worlds with the three guṇas come from pradhāna with the three guṇas. Time is included in the manifest, nature merges into it; so prakṛti alone is the cause.

### 66 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-66"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Prakṛti is one only, the principal cause of the three worlds; there is no second prakṛti. Though prakṛti and puruṣa, both all-pervading, remain conjoined, there is no purpose for creation, since both purposes — experience of objects and apprehension of the difference between the guṇas and puruṣa — are fulfilled.

### 67 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-67"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Though perfect knowledge of the twenty-five principles has arisen, the yogin remains bearing a body through the force of impressions, as a potter's wheel keeps turning after the pot is made. The seven binding forms, burnt by right knowledge like seeds burnt by fire, can no longer sprout; knowledge burns future karma, present karma is exhausted, and with the exhaustion of impressions liberation comes at the fall of the body.

### 69 <a id="tea-samkhya-karika-bhasya-gaudapada-69"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The supreme sage is the blessed sage Kapila; the knowledge is secret; through considering the persistence, manifestation and disappearance of beings arises the understanding that discriminates the twenty-five principles. A closing verse says the Sāṃkhya taught by the sage Kapila is the cause of release from saṃsāra, set out in these seventy āryās, with a bhāṣya made by Gauḍapāda.
