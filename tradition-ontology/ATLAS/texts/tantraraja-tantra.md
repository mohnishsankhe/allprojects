# Tantrarāja Tantra

**Availability:** digitized-original

The 'king of tantras', called the Kādimata ('doctrine of Kādi'): a complete, self-sufficient tantra of the sixteen Nityās, taught by Śiva to the Goddess — their mantras, yantras and worship, the nine nāthas, the Nityās as the lunar days and the flow of time and breath, rites for particular ends, and, in the last chapters, the inner meaning (vāsanā) and the teaching on one's own self.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 1.2-4 <a id="tea-tantraraja-tantra-1-2-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Goddess: the nine tantras of the sixteen Nityās depend on one another and bewilder the mind; tell me a tantra of theirs that needs no other. Śiva: hear the Kādimata tantra, complete without dependence on others, to be guarded with every effort, as tantric teaching enjoins.

### 1.5 <a id="tea-tantraraja-tantra-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Asked why it is named Kādimata, Śiva says that the two Śaktis Kādi and Kālī had earlier been taught by him in tantras named after their doctrines, and hence the name.

### 1.16-18 <a id="tea-tantraraja-tantra-1-16-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The table of contents ends: the thirty-third paṭala gives yantras for different fruits, the thirty-fourth the names, the thirty-fifth the inner meaning (vāsanā), the thirty-sixth the teaching on one's own self; each chapter has about a hundred verses — such is the compass of the tantra.

> ṣaṭtriṃśe svātmakathanam adhyāyās tattvavigrahāḥ / pratyadhyāyaṃ śataślokāḥ syād evaṃ tantrasaṅgrahaḥ

### nityas-and-time <a id="tea-tantraraja-tantra-nityas-and-time"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The sixteen Nityās are the digits of the moon and the lunar days; time itself — the days and their divisions down to the breath — is their form, and the practitioner worships the Nityā presiding over each day.
