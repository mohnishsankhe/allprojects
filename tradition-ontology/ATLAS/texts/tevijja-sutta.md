The brahmins versed in the three Vedas cannot show a path to union with Brahmā whom none of them has seen (a line of blind men); the Buddha teaches the four brahmavihāras as the path to companionship with Brahmā.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 15 <a id="tea-tevijja-sutta-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The brahmins versed in the three Vedas who teach a path to union with Brahmā though neither they nor their teachers back to the seventh generation have seen Brahmā are like a line of blind men holding each other: the first does not see, the middle one does not see, the last does not see.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: dispute, teacher-transmission_

### 76-81 <a id="tea-tevijja-sutta-76-81"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The monk who abides pervading the four directions, above, below and all around with a mind of love, compassion, rejoicing and equanimity — abundant, exalted, immeasurable — leaves no limited deed remaining, as a strong conch-blower is heard in all directions; this is the path to companionship with Brahmā.
