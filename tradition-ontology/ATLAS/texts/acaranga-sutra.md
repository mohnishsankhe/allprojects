The first Aṅga, on the conduct (ācāra) of the mendicant. Book I (Bambhacerāiṃ, 'the discipline of chastity'; nine lectures, the seventh, Mahāparijñā, lost) teaches non-harm to the six classes of living beings (earth, water, fire, plants, mobile beings, wind), detachment and vigilance, and ends with the Upadhānaśruta, the account of Mahāvīra's austerities. Book II (four cūlās) gives detailed rules on alms, lodging, walking, speech, clothing and bowls, and a life of Mahāvīra with the twenty-five bhāvanās of the five great vows.

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-acaranga-sutra-1-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Some people do not know from which direction they have come, or whether their soul is one that is reborn. One who knows, by his own insight or from others, 'my soul is reborn; I came from this direction' — he believes in the soul, in the world, in karma and in action.

### 1.1.2-7 <a id="tea-acaranga-sutra-1-1-2-7"></a>

The six lessons that follow describe the harm done to earth-bodied, water-bodied, fire-bodied, plant-bodied, mobile and wind-bodied beings by those who act for their own ends, and teach that one who understands this harm renounces causing it, having it caused, and approving it.

### 1.5.6 <a id="tea-acaranga-sutra-1-5-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The liberated one cannot be described: all sounds recoil from it, reasoning does not reach it, the mind cannot grasp it. It is not long or short, round or square, black or white, fragrant or foul, bitter or sweet, heavy or light, cold or hot; it has no body, is not reborn, has no attachment; it is not female, male or neuter. It perceives, it knows; there is no analogy for it; its being is formless; there is no condition of the unconditioned, no word for it.

### 1.9 <a id="tea-acaranga-sutra-1-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The 'Pillow of Righteousness' (Upadhānaśruta) describes how the Venerable Ascetic wandered: he wore the one robe for thirteen months and thereafter went naked; he meditated with eyes fixed, silent, avoiding company; in the pathless land of Lāḍha he was beaten and set upon by dogs and bore it; he took no medicine, fasted for long periods, and, free from sinfulness, meditated in the posture of kāyotsarga, not seeking comfort.
