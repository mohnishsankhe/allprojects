The third Aṅga, a numerical compendium arranging doctrinal, cosmological and monastic topics in ten sections from 'ones' to 'tens'; among much else it lists the seven schismatics (nihnavas) and the ten 'wonders' (accheras) of the present era, such as a woman Tīrthaṅkara and the transfer of Mahāvīra's embryo.

### 4 <a id="tea-sthananga-sutra-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

There are four assemblies of disputants: those who affirm action, those who deny it, the ignorant, and the disciplinarians.

### 7 <a id="tea-sthananga-sutra-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Among its sevens, the Sthānāṅga names the seven schismatics (nihnava) of Mahāvīra's teaching — Jamāli, Tiṣyagupta, Āṣāḍha, Aśvamitra, Gaṅga, Rohagupta (Ṣaḍulūka) and Goṣṭhāmāhila — each of whom denied one point of doctrine.

### 10 <a id="tea-sthananga-sutra-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Among its tens, the Sthānāṅga lists ten 'wonders' (accheraga) of this descending age — events that should not happen but did: among them a woman becoming a Tīrthaṅkara (Mallī), the removal of Mahāvīra's embryo to another womb, an attack on a Jina (Gośāla's), Mahāvīra's first assembly in which no one took vows, and the worship of the unrestrained.
