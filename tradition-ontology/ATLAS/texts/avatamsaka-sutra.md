The 'Flower Ornament' sūtra: a vast compilation in which the Buddha, just awakened, is revealed as Vairocana filling the dharmadhātu; bodhisattvas describe the interpenetration of all worlds in each atom, the stages of the bodhisattva path (ten abodes, practices, dedications, grounds), the 'arising of the Tathāgata' present in all beings, Samantabhadra's practices, and, in its final part (the Gaṇḍavyūha), Sudhana's pilgrimage.

### 1 <a id="tea-avatamsaka-sutra-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the assembly just after the awakening, the Buddha is seen as Vairocana whose body fills the dharmadhātu; worlds as numerous as atoms appear in each pore and in each atom; bodhisattvas and gods praise him.

### 11 <a id="tea-avatamsaka-sutra-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pure practice (jingxing): the bodhisattva turns every daily act into a vow for beings — on waking, dressing, washing, eating, walking, seeing trees or water, he wishes that all beings may attain the corresponding good (some 140 such vows).

### 16 <a id="tea-avatamsaka-sutra-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

At the time of the first arousal of the mind (of awakening) one attains unsurpassed perfect awakening, knowing that all dharmas are the mind's own nature and becoming the wisdom-body without depending on another's awakening.

### 20 <a id="tea-avatamsaka-sutra-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Verse of the bodhisattva Juelin: the mind is like a skilled painter who paints all the aggregates; there is nothing in all the worlds not made by it; mind, buddha and beings — these three are without difference; whoever wishes to know all buddhas of the three times should contemplate the nature of the dharmadhātu: all is made by mind alone.

### 37 <a id="tea-avatamsaka-sutra-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Tathāgata's wisdom is present in full in the bodies of all beings, but beings, deluded and clinging, do not know it — like a great sūtra-scroll recording the whole trichiliocosm contained within a single atom, which a wise man breaks open to benefit all; the Buddha teaches them the noble path to remove false thinking.
