### 2.4.8-18 <a id="tea-moksopaya-2-4-8-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Everything in this world is attained by rightly applied human effort (pauruṣa); fate (daiva), unlike effort, is not seen and is imagined by the deluded — nothing called fate exists. Effort is the activity of mind and body guided by the instruction of the good and by scripture; anything else is the behaviour of a madman. Whoever desires a thing and strives steadily gets it unless he turns back; by effort beings became Indra, Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva. Effort is of two kinds, former and present, and the present quickly overcomes the former.

> prāktanaṃ caihikaṃ ceti dvividhaṃ viddhi pauruṣam / prāktano 'dyatanenāśu puruṣārthena jīyate

### 2.6.1-6 <a id="tea-moksopaya-2-6-1-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Fate is nothing but one's own former effort; give it up from afar and lift the self by holy company and true scripture. As effort is, so is the fruit — there is only effort; call it fate if you like. 'Alas, fate!' is only an exclamation of pain like 'alas, misery!'. There is no fate other than one's own former deeds, and it can be overcome as a strong man overcomes a child; yesterday's misdeed is set right by today's good conduct. Those who rely on fate are deluded.

> tasmāt prākpauruṣaṃ daivaṃ nānyat tat projjhya dūrataḥ / sādhusaṅgamasacchāstrair jīvam uttārayed balāt

### 2.11.56-61 <a id="tea-moksopaya-2-11-56-61"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

The mind is fickle, the monkey of the forest of saṃsāra: restrain it in the heart and listen to the teaching on the supreme. Keep far from the undiscriminating and the lovers of bad company; honour the holy; from constant contact with the good discrimination arises, whose fruits are enjoyment and liberation. ### 3.97.14-19 <a id="tea-moksopaya-3-97-14-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

There are three spaces: the space of mind (cittākāśa), the space of consciousness (cidākāśa) and the elemental space (bhūtākāśa). Consciousness-space is the knower present inside and outside all beings, revealing existence; mind-space is the creator that pervades all through its fancies; elemental space is the extended space that supports wind and clouds. ### 3.118.1-7 <a id="tea-moksopaya-3-118-1-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Hear the seven-stepped ground of knowledge, knowing which you will not sink again in the mud of delusion. Disputants speak of the stages of yoga in many ways; these seven are the ones I approve. Knowledge is awakening in seven stages; liberation lies beyond the seven. Liberation and awakening to truth are synonyms. The stages are: good desire (śubhecchā), inquiry (vicāraṇā), thinning of mind (tanumānasā), attainment of sattva (sattvāpatti), non-attachment (asaṃsakti), non-perception of objects (padārthābhāvanī) and the reaching of the fourth (turyagā); at their end is liberation, reached which one grieves no more.

> jñānabhūmiś śubhecchākhyā prathamā samudāhṛtā / vicāraṇā dvitīyātra tṛtīyā tanumānasā // sattvāpattiś caturthī syāt tato 'saṃsaktināmikā / padārthābhāvanī ṣaṣṭhī saptamī turyagā smṛtā

### 3.118.16-26 <a id="tea-moksopaya-3-118-16-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

The turya state is found in those liberated while living; beyond it lies the state beyond the fourth (turyātīta), the domain of the bodiless liberated. Those who reach the seventh stage, the great souls delighting in the self, are not immersed in pleasure and pain; they do or do not do ordinary tasks, and when prompted follow customary conduct like one half-asleep, unaffected. These stages are within reach of the intelligent, not of animals, plants or barbarian-minded; yet any animals or barbarians who attain this state of knowledge are liberated, with or without body.
