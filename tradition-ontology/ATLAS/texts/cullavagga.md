### 5.8.2 <a id="tea-cullavagga-5-8-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Piṇḍola Bhāradvāja rises into the air to take down a sandalwood bowl set on a pole as a challenge; the Buddha rebukes him for displaying a superhuman power to laypeople 'for the sake of a miserable wooden bowl' and forbids such displays, having the bowl broken up for eye-ointment.

### 5.33.1 <a id="tea-cullavagga-5-33-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Two brothers of brahmin birth with fine voices (Yameḷa and Kekuṭa in this edition) complain that monks of various origins corrupt the word of the Buddha in their own dialect and propose to put it into (Vedic) metre (chandaso); the Buddha refuses — the word of the Buddha is not to be put into metre — and allows it to be learned each in his own language (sakāya niruttiyā).

### 7.3.14 <a id="tea-cullavagga-7-3-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Devadatta asks the Buddha to make five practices compulsory for life — forest dwelling, alms-food only, rag robes, the root of a tree, no fish or meat; the Buddha refuses, allowing each as optional (and fish and meat when not seen, heard or suspected to have been killed for the monk); Devadatta uses this to split the Saṅgha.

### 10.1.3 <a id="tea-cullavagga-10-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ānanda asks whether a woman who goes forth in the Dhamma and discipline is capable of realising the fruit of stream-entry, once-return, non-return and arahantship; the Buddha answers that she is capable.

> “bhabbo nu kho, bhante, mātugāmo tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajitvā sotāpattiphalaṁ vā sakadāgāmiphalaṁ vā anāgāmiphalaṁ vā arahattaphalaṁ vā sacchikātun”ti? “Bhabbo, ānanda, mātugāmo tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajitvā sotāpattiphalampi sakadāgāmiphalampi anāgāmiphalampi arahattaphalampi sacchikātun”ti. “Sace, bhante, bhabbo mātugāmo tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajitvā sotāpattiphalampi sakadāgāmiphalampi anāgāmiphalampi arahattaphalampi sacchikātuṁ;

### 10.1.6 <a id="tea-cullavagga-10-1-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

If women had not obtained the going forth in this Dhamma and discipline, the holy life would have lasted long, the true Dhamma would have stood for a thousand years; now that they have gone forth, the true Dhamma will stand only five hundred years.

> “Sace, ānanda, nālabhissa mātugāmo tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajjaṁ, ciraṭṭhitikaṁ, ānanda, brahmacariyaṁ abhavissa, vassasahassaṁ saddhammo tiṭṭheyya. Yato ca kho, ānanda, mātugāmo tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajito, na dāni, ānanda, brahmacariyaṁ ciraṭṭhitikaṁ bhavissati. Pañceva dāni, ānanda, vassasatāni saddhammo ṭhassati.

### 11.1.1 <a id="tea-cullavagga-11-1-1"></a>

### 11.1.9-10 <a id="tea-cullavagga-11-1-9-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ānanda reports that the Buddha allowed the Saṅgha to abolish the lesser and minor rules, but he had not asked which; the elders disagree on which are meant, and Mahākassapa resolves that nothing unlaid-down be laid down and nothing laid down be abolished. The elders charge Ānanda with faults, including having urged the going forth of women; he confesses them out of faith in the elders while not seeing them as faults.

### 12 <a id="tea-cullavagga-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

A hundred years after the parinirvāṇa the Vajjiputtaka monks of Vesālī practised ten points (salt in a horn, eating when the shadow is two fingers past noon, going to another village to eat again, separate observances within one boundary, consent obtained afterwards, following precedent, unchurned milk after the meal, unfermented palm wine, a rug without fringe, accepting gold and silver); Yasa Kākaṇḍakaputta objected; a council of seven hundred elders judged all ten unallowable.

### 12.1-12.2 <a id="tea-cullavagga-12-1-12-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

A century after the parinibbāna the Vajjian monks of Vesālī proclaim ten points (salt in a horn, eating when the shadow is two fingers past noon, accepting gold and silver, and others); Yasa Kākaṇḍakaputta objects; a council of seven hundred at Vesālī, with Revata and Sabbakāmī, judges all ten points not to be in accord with the Dhamma and discipline.
