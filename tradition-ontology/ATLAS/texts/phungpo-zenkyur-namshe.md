### cautions <a id="tea-phungpo-zenkyur-namshe-cautions"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Chöd practised without the view of emptiness and without compassion becomes a mere rite that can provoke harm; one does not go to fearful places before the teacher permits and before one has stability; spirits are to be treated with compassion as guests, not attacked or exorcised; and the pride of thinking oneself powerful is itself the māra of conceit.

### four-maras <a id="tea-phungpo-zenkyur-namshe-four-maras"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

There are four māras: the obstructive māra — sense-objects that provoke attachment and aversion; the unobstructive māra — the thoughts that arise in the mind; the māra of elation — attachment to one's own attainments and good fortune; and the māra of conceit — self-fixation. The last is the root of the others; when it is cut, all four are cut.

### guests <a id="tea-phungpo-zenkyur-namshe-guests"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The body, the object of strongest attachment, is offered in visualization to four kinds of guests: the Three Jewels and the lineage, out of devotion; the protectors, for their qualities; the six classes of beings, out of compassion; and harmful spirits and karmic creditors, to repay debts. All are satisfied, and giver, gift and recipient are seen as empty. (Restricted practice (visualized body-offering): summary only, no procedure.)

### life <a id="tea-phungpo-zenkyur-namshe-life"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Machig was born in Lab, became a renowned reciter of the Perfection of Wisdom, and gained realization while reading its teaching on māras; she received instruction from Kyotön Sönam Lama and from Padampa Sangye, lived as a householder with the Indian yogin Thöpa Bhadra, and taught at Zangri Khangmar. She is an emanation of the Great Mother Prajñāpāramitā; in her previous life she was an Indian paṇḍita whose consciousness passed to Tibet.

`skeleton` · confidence low

Signs of accomplishment in Chöd: fear no longer arises in frightening places, spirits become friendly helpers, clinging to the body falls away, and illnesses — one's own and others' — are pacified by the practice.

### tibet-to-india <a id="tea-phungpo-zenkyur-namshe-tibet-to-india"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Indian scholars came to Tibet to test Machig; after questioning and demonstration they accepted her teaching and took it back with them. Thus Chöd is the one Dharma that went from Tibet to India, whereas all other teachings came from India to Tibet.
