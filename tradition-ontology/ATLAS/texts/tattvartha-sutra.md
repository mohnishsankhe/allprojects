# Tattvārthasūtra

`src:tattvartha-sutra` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Tattvārthādhigamasūtra (Śvetāmbara title), Mokṣaśāstra (Digambara title)
**Original title:** तत्त्वार्थसूत्र
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** ascetic
**Lineages:** [Jainism (Jaina dharma)](../lineages/jainism.md), [Śvetāmbara](../lineages/svetambara.md), [Digambara](../lineages/digambara.md)
**Genre:** sūtra
**Authors:** 
  - [Umāsvāti](../teachers/umasvati.md) — role: author; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: Śvetāmbara: Umāsvāti (with his own bhāṣya); Digambara: Umāsvāmin, pupil in Kundakunda's line (also given as Gṛdhrapiccha); scholarly: a single author Umāsvāti is generally accepted; his sectarian affiliation and the date are debated; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Digambara lineage lists place him soon after Kundakunda; Scholarly account: c. 2nd–5th c. CE (estimates vary; many place it c. 4th–5th c.); (confidence low)
**Structure:** 10 chapters; 357 sūtras in the Digambara recension (Sarvārthasiddhi), about 344 in the Śvetāmbara recension (bhāṣya); numbering differs within several chapters
**Availability:** digitized-original

The first Sanskrit summa of Jain doctrine: ten chapters of aphorisms covering the path (the three jewels), knowledge and standpoints (1), the soul (2), the lower and middle worlds (3), the gods (4), the non-soul substances (5), inflow (6), vows (7), bondage (8), stopping and shedding with meditation (9) and liberation (10). Accepted by all Jain sects.
