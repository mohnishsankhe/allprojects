A vṛtti on the SK that opens with the story of Kapila — an avatāra of the primal Person, son of Kardama and Devahūti — testing the brahmin Āsuri at thousand-year intervals before accepting him; it traces the teaching from Pañcaśikha through Bhārgava, Ulūka, Vālmīki, Hārīta and Devala to Īśvarakṛṣṇa, and enumerates the sixty topics of the Ṣaṣṭitantra.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 1 <a id="tea-mathara-vrtti-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The blessed great sage Kapila, endowed with innate virtue, knowledge, dispassion and power, an avatāra of the ancient Person, was born to Prajāpati Kardama and Devahūti, daughter of Svāyambhuva Manu. Moved by compassion for the world in blind darkness, he found the brahmin of the Āsuri clan, who had sacrificed for a thousand years, and asked 'Āsuri, do you delight in the householder's life?' — 'I do.' He returned after another thousand years and again after a third; then Āsuri answered 'I do not delight', and, struck by the three sufferings, gave up house, wife and children and became Kapila's disciple.

### 70 <a id="tea-mathara-vrtti-70"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The knowledge is a purifier because it saves from falling into hells, ghost-realms and animal births, and foremost — above Veda, Purāṇa, Bhārata and the law-books — because it removes suffering certainly and finally. Kapila gave it to Āsuri, having recognized his fitness, out of compassion, as a friend who helps all without expecting return; Āsuri gave it to Pañcaśikha, who divided the doctrine and gave it to many pupils.

### 71 <a id="tea-mathara-vrtti-71"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The knowledge came from Kapila to Āsuri, then to Pañcaśikha, from him to Bhārgava, Ulūka, Vālmīki, Hārīta, Devala and others, and from them to Īśvarakṛṣṇa, who, afraid of lengthy books, condensed the Ṣaṣṭitantra in āryā verses. The Ṣaṣṭitantra is the treatise in which sixty topics are set out.

### 72 <a id="tea-mathara-vrtti-72"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The sixty topics are the fifty cognitions (five errors, twenty-eight incapacities, nine contentments, eight accomplishments) and ten fundamental ones: existence (of pradhāna), its oneness, its purposiveness, (puruṣa's) otherness, (prakṛti's) being for another, cessation, conjunction, separation, the plurality of puruṣas, and the persistence of the body (after knowledge).
