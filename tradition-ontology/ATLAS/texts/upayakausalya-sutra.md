The Sūtra on Skillful Means: how the bodhisattva's apparently wrong acts are done out of skillful means and compassion — including the story of the Buddha in a past life killing a man who was about to murder five hundred merchants, to save both.

## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### robber <a id="tea-upayakausalya-sutra-robber"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In a past life as the captain Great Compassionate, the Buddha, knowing that a robber on the ship intended to kill five hundred merchants (bodhisattvas), killed him out of compassion — to save the merchants and to spare the robber the hell-karma of the deed — accepting the karmic consequence himself; this is presented as skillful means.
