Hemacandra's Sanskrit epic of the sixty-three 'illustrious persons' of this era — the twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras, twelve world-emperors, nine baladevas, nine vāsudevas and nine prativāsudevas — in ten books, ending with a full life of Mahāvīra; written at the request of King Kumārapāla.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1 <a id="tea-trisastisalakapurusacaritra-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The first book tells the twelve previous births of Ṛṣabha — from the caravan-leader Dhana, who first gained right faith by giving alms to monks, to his last birth — and his life as first king and first Tīrthaṅkara, with Bharata's conquests and Bāhubali's renunciation.

### 10 <a id="tea-trisastisalakapurusacaritra-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The tenth book, the life of Mahāvīra: his twenty-seven previous births (from the village headman Nayasāra, through Marīci, to the god in the Puṣpottara palace), his birth, austerities, hardships, omniscience, preaching, the gaṇadharas, the conflict with Gośāla, and his nirvāṇa, with a prophecy of the future.
