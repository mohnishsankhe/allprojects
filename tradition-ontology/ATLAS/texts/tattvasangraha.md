Śāntarakṣita's encyclopaedic 'Compendium of Realities' in c. 3,600 verses and 26 examinations: it sets out and refutes primal matter, Īśvara, the Word-brahman, the self of the Naiyāyikas, Mīmāṃsakas, Sāṃkhyas, Jains, Upaniṣadic thinkers and Vātsīputrīyas, permanent things, the categories, the Lokāyata denial of other worlds, the authorless Veda and the Mīmāṃsā denial of an omniscient being, and defends momentariness, apoha, Dharmakīrti's two means of knowledge and the Buddha's omniscience.

### 1456 <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-1456"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Some wrong-viewed people say that inference is not a means of knowledge — yet in saying so with words they convey their intention, which is itself known by inference.

### 1857-1864 <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-1857-1864"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Lokāyata case: if no continuing entity exists, there is no other world, for there is no one to go there; body, mind and senses perish every moment and nothing else is admitted; therefore from particular elements, as the power of fermented liquor, cognition is produced or manifested; the elements in a particular arrangement are called body and senses, and there is no other principle; cognitions in different bodies are not cause and effect; the dying mind of one with passion does not link to another mind; so 'cognition arises from the body alone, sustained by in-breath and out-breath' — as Kambalāśvatara said.

> kāyād eva tato jñānaṃ prāṇāpānādyadhiṣṭhitāt / yuktaṃ jāyata ity etat kambalāśvataroditam

### 1865-1964 <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-1865-1964"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Śāntarakṣita refutes the Lokāyata: cognition in the embryo is not absent; a cognition arises only from a preceding cognition of the same kind, so the first cognition of a life presupposes a former one; the body is not the material cause of mind; passion and the rest come from prior habituation; hence mind is a beginningless continuum and there is another world.

### anumanapariksa-patrasvamin <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-anumanapariksa-patrasvamin"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the examination of inference Śāntarakṣita sets out and answers the view of the Jain Pātrasvāmin that the single mark of a valid reason is its 'otherwise-impossibility' (anyathānupapannatva): where it is present, what use are the three characteristics? where it is absent, what use are the three?

### atindriyarthadarsipariksa <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-atindriyarthadarsipariksa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the examination of one who sees supersensible things, the Mīmāṃsā denial of an omniscient being is answered: the Buddha's omniscience is possible because the mind, freed by cultivation of selflessness from the afflictions, can know all; the Veda has no authority without a trustworthy speaker.

### isvarapariksa <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-isvarapariksa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the examination of Īśvara, a permanent creator is refuted: an eternal cause would produce all its effects at once or never, and if it depends on auxiliary causes it is not the cause; the order of the world is explained by karma and conditions.

### lokayatapariksa <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-lokayatapariksa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the examination of the Lokāyata, the materialist denial of other worlds is refuted: consciousness cannot arise from the body alone, for each moment of awareness arises from a preceding moment of awareness, so the series continues beyond death.

### Śabdabrahma-parīkṣā <a id="tea-tattvasangraha-sabdabrahmapariksa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

In the examination of the Word-brahman Śāntarakṣita argues that an eternal, single Word-brahman cannot account for successive and various things: if the world were its transformation it would be non-eternal and many; if it were only its appearance, the appearance would be unreal and the Word-brahman unproved; and no means of knowledge shows that cognition is interwoven with words.
