Sanatsujāta, an ever-youthful sage called by Vidura, answers Dhṛtarāṣṭra's questions about death and the imperishable: heedlessness (pramāda) is death and heedfulness immortality; the Vedas do not save the deceitful; the true brāhmaṇa, silence (mauna), the virtues and faults, the quarters of brahmacarya, and the shining Brahman whom 'the yogins see, the eternal Lord'.

### 5.42-43 <a id="tea-sanatsujatiya-5-42-43"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Sanatsujāta teaches that the Vedas do not save the deceitful; that the brāhmaṇa is one who knows Brahman, not one who merely recites; that silence (mauna) is the self; and he names the virtues — truth, self-restraint, freedom from envy, modesty, forbearance, sacrifice, giving and the like — and the faults such as anger, desire and greed.
