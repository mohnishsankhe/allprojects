**Availability:** digitized-original

The most authoritative siddhānta: a dialogue in which a being born of the Sun's portion teaches the asura Maya the motions of the planets. It gives the units of time, the yugas and the kalpa, mean and true planetary positions, eclipses, conjunctions, heliacal risings, the cosmography of the earth-globe held in space by Brahman's power, instruments, and the nine measures of time.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Gūḍhārthaprakāśa](gudharthaprakasa.md)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-1-1"></a>

Homage to Brahman, whose form is unthinkable and unmanifest, who is without qualities yet has the qualities as its self, whose embodiment is the support of the whole world.

### 1.2-9 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-1-2-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When little of the Kṛta age remained, the great asura Maya, wishing to know the supreme, holy and secret knowledge — the foremost Vedāṅga, the whole cause of the courses of the lights — propitiated the Sun with severe austerity. Pleased, the Sun himself gave Maya the account of the planets: 'I have no time to tell it and none can bear my brilliance; this person, my portion, will tell you all.' Having appointed that portion he vanished, and the man spoke to the bowing Maya: 'Hear the highest knowledge told by Vivasvant himself to the great seers in age after age. This is that same original śāstra which the Sun spoke of old; only the difference of time arises through the turning of the ages.'

### 1.10-11 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-1-10-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Time is of two kinds: the time that makes an end of the worlds, and another time whose nature is reckoning. The latter is twofold, gross and subtle, called 'embodied' and 'unembodied': the embodied begins with the prāṇa (the time of a breath), the unembodied with the truṭi; six prāṇas make a vināḍī and sixty of these a nāḍī.

> lokānām antakṛt kālaḥ kālo 'nyaḥ kalanātmakaḥ | sa dvidhā sthūlasūkṣmatvān mūrtaś cāmūrta ucyate ||

### 1.12-14 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-1-12-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sixty nāḍīs make a sidereal day-and-night; thirty civil days (by sunrises) make a civil month; a lunar month is reckoned by tithis and a solar month by the sun's ingress; twelve months make a year, which is one day of the gods. The day and night of gods and asuras are reversed; 360 of their days make a divine (and asura) year.

### 1.15-21 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-1-15-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Twelve thousand divine years make a caturyuga, that is 4,320,000 solar years, including its dawn and twilight portions. The arrangement of Kṛta and the rest follows the feet of dharma: a tenth of the yuga multiplied by four, three, two and one gives Kṛta, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali, each with a sixth as its twilights. Seventy-one yugas make a manvantara, with a twilight of a Kṛta's length at its end, a flood; fourteen Manus with their junctions, and a junction at the start, make a kalpa of a thousand yugas — a day of Brahmā, destroying beings, with a night of equal length. His life is a hundred such years; half of it has passed, and this is the first kalpa of the remainder.

### 1.22-24 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-1-22-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Of this kalpa six Manus with their junctions have passed, and of the seventh (Vaivasvata) twenty-seven yugas; of the twenty-eighth yuga the Kṛta has passed. Before the planets began to move, the Creator spent 47,400 divine years (a hundredfold) creating planets, asterisms, gods, demons and all that moves and stands.

### 2.1-5 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-2-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Forms of time with invisible bodies, stationed in the zodiac and called the swift apogee, slow apogee and node, are the causes of the planets' motion. The planets, bound to them by cords of air, are drawn forward or backward by their right and left hands toward whichever is nearer; the wind called Pravaha drives them toward their own apogees. Drawn this way and that, they attain their various motions.

### 12.1-11 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-12-1-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Maya, bowing to the one born of the Sun's portion and worshipping him with devotion, asks the measure, shape and support of the earth, the seven netherworlds, the cause of day and night for gods and asuras, why the lords of day, year, month and hour differ, what supports the circling zodiac with its planets, the heights of the orbits and why the sun burns in summer. Hearing this question asked in devotion, the teacher announces a secret chapter 'called adhyātma': to those who are very devoted nothing of mine is to be withheld.

### 12.12-21 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-12-12-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Vāsudeva is the supreme Brahman; his embodiment is the supreme Person, unmanifest, without qualities, peaceful, beyond the twenty-five principles, imperishable. Entering nature, as Saṅkarṣaṇa he first created the waters and cast his seed into them; it became a golden egg wrapped in darkness, in which Aniruddha first became manifest — the Hiraṇyagarbha of the Veda, called Āditya as the first and Sūrya as the begetter. He is the highest light beyond darkness, the self of time and maker of time, the all-pervading self in whom all rests; the Ṛk verses are his disc, the Sāmans his rays, the Yajus his form. Mounting a chariot whose wheel is the year, yoked with the seven metres as horses, he circles the worlds. Of his threefold immortal hidden being one quarter became manifest; he created Brahmā as the ego-principle for the creation, gave him the Vedas, set him in the egg and himself circles, sustaining.

### 12.22-28 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-12-22-28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Brahmā, bearing the form of the ego-principle, set his mind on creation: from his mind the moon was born, from his eyes the sun, treasury of light; from mind came ether, then air, fire, water and earth, each with one more quality. Fire-and-Soma are the sun and moon; then Mars and the rest were born in order from fire, earth, ether, water and air. The Lord again divided himself twelvefold as the signs and twenty-sevenfold as the asterisms; then he made the moving and unmoving world, beginning with the gods, and assigned to planets, stars, earth, gods, demons, men and siddhas their places according to quality and action, as seen in the Veda.

### 12.29-36 <a id="tea-surya-siddhanta-12-29-36"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The egg of Brahmā is hollow, and within it the worlds bhūr, bhuvaḥ and the rest are like two joined bowls forming a sphere. Within it the orbits lie one below another — Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, the Sun, Venus, Mercury, the Moon — and below them siddhas, vidyādharas and clouds. In the middle of the egg the earth-globe stands in space, bearing the supreme supporting power of Brahman. Within it are the seven netherworlds of nāgas and asuras; Meru, a golden mountain, passes through the middle of the globe and projects on both sides; above it dwell the gods with Indra and the great seers, below it the asuras, hostile to one another; the ocean surrounds the earth like a girdle dividing gods from asuras.

> madhye samantād aṇḍasya bhūgolo vyomni tiṣṭhati | bibhrāṇaḥ paramāṃ śaktiṃ brahmaṇo dhāraṇātmikām ||
