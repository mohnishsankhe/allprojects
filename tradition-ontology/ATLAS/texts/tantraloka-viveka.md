### 1.18 <a id="tea-tantraloka-viveka-1-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Explaining 'ten' and 'eighteen', Jayaratha quotes the Śrīkaṇṭhīya: in the upper stream knowledge is twofold, called Śiva and Rudra — Kāmaja, Yogaja, Cintya, Maukuṭa, Aṃśumat, Dīpta … (lacuna) … are the Śiva-divisions; the Rudra-divisions are Vijaya, Niḥśvāsa, Madgīta, Pārameśvara, Mukhabimba, Siddha, Santāna, Nārasiṃha, Candrāṃśu, Vīrabhadra, Āgneya, Svayambhuva, Visara, Raurava, Vimala, Kiraṇa, Lalita and Saurabheya — twenty-eight tantras issuing from the upper stream; the Śiva-division was spoken by Śivas, the Rudra-division by Rudras.

### 1.18 <a id="tea-tantraloka-viveka-1-18-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Jayaratha quotes the Śrīkaṇṭhīya's sixty-four Bhairava tantras in eight groups — Bhairava, Yāmala, Mata, Maṅgala, Cakrāṣṭaka, Śikhāṣṭaka, Bahurūpa, Vāgīśa: (1) spoken by Bahurūpa: Svacchanda, Bhairava, Caṇḍa, Krodha, Unmattabhairava, Asitāṅga, Mahocchuṣma, Kapālīśa; (2) Brahmayāmala, Viṣṇuyāmala, Svacchanda, Ruru, (fifth not named in the quoted text), Ātharvaṇa, Rudra, Vetāla; (3) the Mata group, 'divisions of Caṇḍa': Rakta, Lampaṭa, Lakṣmīmata, (a fourth, 'Mata'), Cālikā, Piṅgala, Utphullaka, Viśvādya; (4) the Maṅgala group, spoken by Krodheśa: Bhairavī arising from the Picu tantra (twofold), a third, Brāhmī Kalā, Vijayā, Candrā, Maṅgalā, Sarvamaṅgalā; (5) spoken by Asitāṅga: the Mantra-, Varṇa-, Śakti-, Kalā-, Bindu-, Nāda-, Guhya- and Kha-cakras; (6) spoken by Ruru: Andhaka, Rurubheda, Aja, Mūla, Varṇabhaṇṭha, Viḍaṅga, Jvālin, Mātṛrodana; (7) spoken by Kapālīśa, the Vāgīśa group: Bhairavī, Citrikā, Haṃsā, Kadambikā, Hṛllekhā, Candralekhā, Vidyullekhā, Vidyumān; (8) spoken by Unmatta, the Śikhā group: Bhairavī Śikhā, Vīṇā, Vīṇāmaṇi, Sammoha, Ḍāmara, Atharvaka, Kabandha, Śiraścheda. 'This is the wheel of Sadāśiva in eight groups of eight.'

### 1.18 <a id="tea-tantraloka-viveka-1-18-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Jayaratha explains that the ten Śiva-divisions arise from the combinations of the Īśāna, Tatpuruṣa and Sadyojāta faces and are dominated by difference (bheda); the eighteen Rudra-divisions, from their combination with Vāmadeva and Aghora, are dominated by difference-and-non-difference; and the sixty-four Bhairava-divisions, when the other four faces are absorbed into the southern face, are dominated by non-duality. Thus scripture arose threefold — called Śiva, Rudra and Bhairava — because knowledge itself unfolds as difference, difference-and-non-difference and non-difference.
