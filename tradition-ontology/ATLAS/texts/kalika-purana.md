For the Bengal–Assam lineage: the Upapurāṇa of Kāmarūpa, telling Satī's death, Śiva's grief and the scattering of her body into the pīṭhas (feet at Devīkūṭa, thighs at Uḍḍīyāna, yoni at Kāmagiri in Kāmarūpa, breasts at Jālandhara, shoulders and neck at Pūrṇagiri) (ch. 18 in the Vaṅgavāsī ed.); the worship of Kāmākhyā and of Tripurā; and the chapter on offerings (the 'rudhirādhyāya', ch. 67 in that edition), with its substitutes and prohibitions (summary only).

## Teachings (6: skeleton 6)

### 18.36-47 <a id="tea-kalika-purana-18-36-47"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mad with grief, Śiva wandered eastward carrying Satī's corpse on his shoulder; since the corpse would not decay through contact with Hara's body, Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śani entered it invisibly by yogic power and let it fall piece by piece: the two feet first at Devīkūṭa, the thighs at Uḍḍīyāna, the circle of the yoni at Kāmagiri in Kāmarūpa (and the navel there too), the breasts with their golden necklace at Jālandhara, the shoulders and neck at Pūrṇagiri, the head beyond; other small parts were carried by the wind to the heavenly Gaṅgā; wherever a part fell, Mahādeva himself stayed there in the form of a liṅga, bound by love for Satī, and the gods worshipped her feet and other parts.

### 18.48-50 <a id="tea-kalika-purana-18-48-50"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

At Devīkūṭa the Goddess is sung as Mahābhāgā, Yoganidrā mother of the world absorbed in Satī's two feet; Kātyāyanī at Uḍḍīyāna; Kāmākhyā, of the form of desire, in Kāmarūpa; Pūrṇeśvarī at Pūrṇagiri; Caṇḍī at Jālandhara; Dikkaravāsinī at the eastern end of Kāmarūpa, and Lalitakāntā — all Yoganidrā.

### 67.1-12 <a id="tea-kalika-purana-67-1-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

The offering chapter (the 'rudhirādhyāya'): the Lord tells his sons the order of offering (bali) according to the Vaiṣṇavī Tantra; birds, tortoises, crocodiles, fish, deer, buffalo, goats and other animals — and, the text says, a man, and one's own blood — are named as offerings to Caṇḍikā and Bhairava; by offerings liberation and heaven are attained and a king conquers his enemies; the Goddess's satisfaction is said to last longer according to the victim. ### 67.19-23 <a id="tea-kalika-purana-67-19-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

Blood purified by mantra becomes nectar; the Goddess partakes of the head and the flesh; hence in worship the head and blood are offered and the flesh used in food and fire offering; a pumpkin, a stick of sugarcane, and wine or spirits are declared equal to a victim and satisfy her like a goat.

### 67.46-52 <a id="tea-kalika-purana-67-46-52"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

Restrictions on offering: a king offers a horse only in the horse sacrifice and an elephant only in its own rite; a brāhmaṇa must never offer wine to the Great Goddess; a brāhmaṇa who offers a lion, tiger or man goes to hell and in this life loses longevity and happiness; offering one's own blood incurs the sin of self-slaughter; by offering wine a brāhmaṇa falls from brāhmaṇa status; kṣatriyas and others must not offer the black antelope.

### 67.113-117 <a id="tea-kalika-purana-67-113-117"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

Where wine is required, the twice-born should offer coconut water in a bronze vessel or honey in a copper one, and never wine even in distress; offerings of a man are restricted to the king's household and officers and are sinful without the king's permission. Restricted: summary only.
