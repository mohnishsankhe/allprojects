A short Maitreya text distinguishing dharmas — the appearance of duality and of names, which is saṃsāra — from their nature (dharmatā) — suchness without the division of grasped and grasper, which is nirvāṇa — and teaching the entry into non-conceptual wisdom and the transformation of the basis in ten aspects.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1 <a id="tea-dharmadharmatavibhaga-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

All that is to be known is summed up in two: dharmas and their nature (dharmatā). Dharmas are saṃsāra, characterized by the appearance of duality and of verbal designation, arising from unreal imagination; dharmatā is nirvāṇa, characterized by suchness without the division of grasped and grasper, of object of expression and expression.

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: ultimate_

terms: [dharmatā](../terms/dharmata.md) · concepts: [Phenomena and their nature (dharma and dharmatā)](../concepts/dharma-dharmata.md) · teachers: [Maitreyanātha](../teachers/maitreyanatha.md), [Asaṅga](../teachers/asanga.md)

### 2 <a id="tea-dharmadharmatavibhaga-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Entry into non-conceptual wisdom proceeds through the non-apprehension of objects on the basis of apprehending mind-only, and the non-apprehension of mind-only on the basis of not apprehending objects; the transformation of the basis is explained under ten headings (nature, substance, persons, distinctive features, requirements, basis, attention, application, drawbacks of its absence and benefits).

_level: bridging · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation, knowledge · stage: advanced · types: practice, karma-liberation_
