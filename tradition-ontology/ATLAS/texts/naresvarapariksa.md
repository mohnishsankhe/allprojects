Sadyojyoti's 'Examination of the Soul (nara) and the Lord (īśvara)': argues against Buddhists, Cārvākas, Mīmāṃsakas and others that a permanent, conscious soul exists distinct from body, senses and mind, that souls are many, and that an omniscient Lord distinct from souls exists and governs the unconscious māyā and karma.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1 <a id="tea-naresvarapariksa-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Against the Buddhists and materialists, there is a permanent soul distinct from body, senses and cognitions: recognition, memory and the unity of experience require a lasting knower whose consciousness is its own nature.

### 2 <a id="tea-naresvarapariksa-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Souls are many, not one: their experiences, bondage and release differ, and one soul's release does not release all.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: consciousness-mind, dispute_

disputes: [Are the individual souls one with Brahman/God, eternally distinct from him and from one another, or both different and non-different?](../debates/souls-one-or-distinct.md)

### 3 <a id="tea-naresvarapariksa-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The unconscious māyā and karma cannot organize themselves to give each soul its due fruits; an omniscient, omnipotent Lord, distinct from souls, must be their director — as the potter is of clay.
