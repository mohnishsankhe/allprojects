Seventy verses with Nāgārjuna's own commentary answering two objections: that the statement 'all things are empty' is itself empty and so cannot negate anything, and that things are established by the means of knowledge. Nāgārjuna answers that his words, though empty, function like a magically created man stopping another; that he has no thesis of his own; that the means of knowledge are established neither by themselves nor by their objects; and that for whom emptiness is possible, everything is possible.

### 31-33 <a id="tea-vigrahavyavartani-31-33"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

If the means of knowledge establish their objects, what establishes the means of knowledge? If other means of knowledge, there is an infinite regress, and neither beginning, middle nor end is established; if they are established without means of knowledge, your position is abandoned.
