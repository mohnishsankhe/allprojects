Dōgen's first instruction (in Chinese, 1227; autograph Tenpuku manuscript 1233; later revised) on seated meditation: the Way is fundamentally complete, so why practise? — yet the slightest gap and heaven and earth are separated; step back and turn the light inward; sit in full or half lotus, upright; think of not-thinking — how? — non-thinking; this is the essential art of zazen; zazen is not learning meditation but the Dharma gate of ease and joy.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### complete <a id="tea-fukanzazengi-complete"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Way is fundamentally complete and pervades everywhere — why depend on practice and realization? Yet if there is the slightest gap, heaven and earth are far apart; even the Buddha sat six years and Bodhidharma nine years facing the wall.

### non-thinking <a id="tea-fukanzazengi-non-thinking"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sit in full or half lotus with the body upright, the left hand on the right in the cosmic mudrā, eyes open, breath passing gently through the nose; think of not-thinking — how? — non-thinking: this is the essential art of zazen. Zazen is not learning meditation; it is the Dharma gate of ease and joy.
