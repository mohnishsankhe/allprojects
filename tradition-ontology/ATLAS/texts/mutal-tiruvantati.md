Poykai Āḻvār's hundred veṇpās, the 'first' antāti, sung (in the tradition's account) in the dark vestibule at Tirukkōvalūr: it opens by lighting a lamp with the earth as bowl, the sea as ghee and the sun as flame.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1 <a id="tea-mutal-tiruvantati-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

With the earth as the lamp's bowl, the surging sea as its ghee and the hot-rayed sun as its flame, I have laid a garland of words at the feet of the one who bears the flaming discus, that the sea of sorrow may pass away.

### 44 <a id="tea-mutal-tiruvantati-44"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Whatever form his devotees delight in, that form is his; whatever name they love, that name is his; in whatever way they contemplate him without blinking, in that way the discus-bearer becomes.
