### 27.80-81 <a id="tea-manimekalai-27-80-81"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The teachers of the schools are Bṛhaspati (for the Lokāyata), the Jina, Kapila, Akṣapāda, Kaṇāda and Jaimini, and the means of knowledge now current are perception, inference, testimony, comparison, presumption and non-apprehension.

### 27.108-165 <a id="tea-manimekalai-27-108-165"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Asked by Maṇimēkalai 'who is your lord and what are the principles of your text?', Pūraṇaṉ, who knows the Ājīvaka texts, answers: the lord is the boundless knower who is present in all things everywhere and always; the principles are five — life (uyir) and four kinds of atom (earth, water, fire, air); the atoms are beginningless and indestructible, never newly born, never entering one another or changing kind, never splitting; they combine into mountains, trees and bodies and separate again, and the knowing of them is called life; a single atom is seen only by the divine eye. Beings pass through six births — black, dark blue, green, red, golden and white — and, born in due order, reach release in the supremely white birth; gain and loss, meeting and parting, pleasure and pain, birth and death are all fixed while in the womb. 'This is the kind of the Maṟkali text.'

### 27.263-286 <a id="tea-manimekalai-27-263-286"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Bhūtavādin says: as intoxication arises when dhātakī flowers, jaggery and other ingredients are combined, so consciousness arises in the elements, and when the elements break up it perishes, like the sound of a drum dying away; some elements are joined to life and consciousness, some are not; this is the true doctrine, the Lokāyata's understanding: perception alone is a means of knowledge, inference fails; this life and its fruit are in this birth alone; another world and the experiencing of the fruit of deeds there are false. Maṇimēkalai laughs: how does he know his own father and mother except by inference?
