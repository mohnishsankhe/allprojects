one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); the seventh of the ten short treatises (daśa-prakaraṇa); it a thirteen-verse discrimination of realities: independent and dependent; Śrī as eternally free; souls graded in liberation and in bondage; eternal and non-eternal insentient things; and the relation of a thing to its properties (no difference for lasting properties; difference-and-identity for temporary ones). It ends: one who knows all this dependent reality to be always under Hari's control is freed from saṃsāra.

## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 1-3 <a id="tea-tattvaviveka-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The knowable is of two kinds, independent and dependent; the independent is the Lord Viṣṇu, free of defects and full of all good qualities. The dependent is positive or negative; negation is threefold (prior, posterior, constant). Mutual non-existence (difference) is not a separate negation, because it is the very nature of positive and negative entities. Positive being is sentient or insentient.

### 4-7 <a id="tea-tattvaviveka-4-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The dependent sentient is either eternally free — Śrī alone — or bound to transmigration, and the latter are liberated or unliberated. Among the liberated, from the lowest up to Brahmā, each class is a hundred times greater than the one before; Ramā exceeds them in all qualities, and Hari is infinitely greater than she. The unliberated are of three kinds — high, middle and low: the high are fit for liberation, the middle ever return (in saṃsāra), the low are fit for eternal darkness.

### 8-9 <a id="tea-tattvaviveka-8-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

By the division of eternal and non-eternal: space, time and śruti, and the subtle forms of the elements, senses, vital airs and qualities are eternal; their modifications are non-eternal; sentient beings are eternal. Qualities, actions, universals and the like are all properties of the thing.

### 10-12 <a id="tea-tattvaviveka-10-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A thing's form (rūpa) is of two kinds: that which lasts as long as the thing, and that which is split off (temporary). With temporary forms there is both difference and identity; with lasting forms there is no difference. This holds between a modification and what is modified, effect and cause, a quality and its possessor, an action and its agent, a universal and the particular, the qualified and the pure, and a part and the whole.
