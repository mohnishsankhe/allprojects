Nārāyaṇatīrtha's extensive vṛtti. Its opening verses give the transmission (Pitāmaha taught yoga; Śeṣa in the sūtras; Vyāsa in the bhāṣya; Vācaspati Miśra and Vijñānabhikṣu clarified the bhāṣya) and argue that Vyāsa's Brahma Sūtras refuted pradhāna's independence, not yoga as meditative cultivation; the introduction treats deep contemplation (nididhyāsana) as samādhi, 'also called rājayoga', and subsumes many named yogas under the eight-limbed yoga.

### intro.2 <a id="tea-yogasiddhantacandrika-intro-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pitāmaha taught yoga; then the Lord Śeṣa himself in the sūtras; Vyāsa, known to all, [expounded] the whole in the guise of a bhāṣya; the wise Miśra and then the Bhikṣu clarified that bhāṣya — yet its secret remains hard for the dull to grasp.

### intro.4-6 <a id="tea-yogasiddhantacandrika-intro-4-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In the Brahma Sūtras he himself composed, Vyāsa refuted the independence and reality [claimed] for pradhāna and a real difference of consciousness (by the statements of non-difference), but not the yoga called meditative cultivation (bhāvanā); the wise sage also expounded yoga as directed to the self in the bhāṣya and elsewhere; the Lord in the Gītā holds yoga to be higher, and Śuka and others practised it.

### intro.prose <a id="tea-yogasiddhantacandrika-intro-prose"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Liberation, the highest human aim, is reached by hearing, reflection and deep contemplation, of which deep contemplation (nididhyāsana) is chief; it is samādhi, otherwise called rājayoga. Its means — kriyā-yoga, caryā, karma-, haṭha-, mantra-, jñāna-, advaita-, lakṣya-, brahma-, śiva-, siddhi-, vāsanā-, laya- and dhyāna-yoga and the yoga of loving devotion — are all comprised, as general and particular, in the eight-limbed yoga.
