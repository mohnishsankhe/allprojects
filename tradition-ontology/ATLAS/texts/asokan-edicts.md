Aśoka's rock and pillar inscriptions (3rd c. BCE). Rock Edict 12 urges honour to all sects and restraint of speech in praising one's own; Rock Edict 13 says there is no land except among the Greeks where brāhmaṇas and śramaṇas are not found; Pillar Edict 7 appoints the dhamma-mahāmātras to the affairs of the Saṅgha, the brāhmaṇas, the Ājīvikas, the Nigaṇṭhas and other sects.

### re12 <a id="tea-asokan-edicts-re12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The king honours all sects, ascetics and householders alike, with gifts and honours, but values most the growth of their essentials; its root is restraint of speech — not praising one's own sect or disparaging another's without cause; one who praises his own sect out of devotion harms it; concord (samavāya) is good, that all may hear and follow one another's dharma.

### re13 <a id="tea-asokan-edicts-re13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

There is no land, except among the Greeks, where these orders — brāhmaṇas and śramaṇas — are not found, and none where people do not hold to some sect.
