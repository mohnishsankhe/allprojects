Rāmānuja's prose act of surrender, set on the day of Paṅguni Uttiram at Śrīraṅgam: he takes refuge in Śrī, then in Nārāyaṇa, asks for supreme devotion, knowledge and love (parabhakti, parajñāna, paramabhakti), and records the Lord's reply granting all and bidding him dwell at Śrīraṅgam reciting the dvaya.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1 <a id="tea-saranagati-gadya-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Rāmānuja first takes refuge in Śrī — whose form, qualities and glory befit the Lord's delight, who is inseparable from him, the mother of all, who teaches the good of the whole world — as his refuge (the mediator), before approaching Nārāyaṇa.

### prayer to Nārāyaṇa <a id="tea-saranagati-gadya-prayer"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Having recited the Lord's infinite auspicious qualities and confessed his own countless offences, Rāmānuja, with no other refuge, takes refuge at the Lord's feet and asks for supreme devotion, supreme knowledge and supreme love (parabhakti, parajñāna, paramabhakti) and for eternal service.

### the Lord's reply (closing) <a id="tea-saranagati-gadya-closing"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Lord replies that by his grace all this is granted: the refugee's sins are gone, he will attain the Lord's service at the end of this body; let him have no doubt, and dwell happily at Śrīraṅgam, reciting the dvaya with its meaning, until the body falls.
