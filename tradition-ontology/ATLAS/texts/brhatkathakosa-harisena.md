Hariṣeṇa's collection of 157 Sanskrit verse stories (931–932 CE), linked to the Bhagavatī Ārādhanā; its story of Bhadrabāhu gives the earliest full Digambara account of the famine, the migration south and the rise of the clothed monks.

## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### 131 <a id="tea-brhatkathakosa-harisena-131"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Bhadrabāhu, at Ujjayinī, heard a child cry 'go, go' and knew that a twelve-year famine was coming; he sent the saṅgha south under Viśākhācārya to the Punnāṭa country and himself ended his life nearby; the monks who stayed in Sindhu took to a half-cloth (ardhaphālaka), from which, in time, came the Śvetāmbaras and others.
