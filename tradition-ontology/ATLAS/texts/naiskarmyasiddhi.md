Sureśvara's treatise (verse with prose) establishing 'actionlessness': ch. 1 refutes the view that works, alone or combined with knowledge, lead to liberation; ch. 2 separates the self from the not-self by agreement and difference; ch. 3 explains how 'tat tvam asi' gives immediate knowledge; ch. 4 summarizes and describes the knower, whose virtues arise without effort and who cannot act licentiously.

## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 1 <a id="tea-naiskarmyasiddhi-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Works, whether alone or combined with knowledge, cannot bring liberation, which is the self's eternal nature; works purify the mind and turn it inward, and are then left behind — they are not co-causes with knowledge.

### 2 <a id="tea-naiskarmyasiddhi-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

By the method of agreement and difference (anvaya-vyatireka) — the self persists through waking, dream and sleep while the body, senses and mind come and go — the self is separated from the not-self.

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: intermediate · types: practice, consciousness-mind_

### 3 <a id="tea-naiskarmyasiddhi-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The sentence 'tat tvam asi', its word-meanings purified by exclusion of contradictory elements, gives immediate knowledge of the inner self as Brahman; no further act (meditation) is needed to make it effective.

### 4.62 <a id="tea-naiskarmyasiddhi-4-62"></a>

### 4.69 <a id="tea-naiskarmyasiddhi-4-69"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate
