### 2.52-2.75 <a id="tea-buddhavamsa-2-52-2-75"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The ascetic Sumedha lays himself face down in the mud so that the Buddha Dīpaṅkara and his monks may walk over him; he reflects that he could realise the Dhamma then and there but will rather attain omniscience and carry the world across on the ship of Dhamma; eight conditions make the aspiration succeed (human birth, the male sex, the cause, seeing a teacher, going forth, attainment of qualities, an act of devotion and strong desire); Dīpaṅkara, standing at his head, declares that after countless aeons he will be the Buddha Gotama.

### 2.59 <a id="tea-buddhavamsa-2-59"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Human birth, the (male) sex, the cause, seeing the Teacher, going forth, attainment of qualities, an act of devotion and desire: by the combination of these eight things the aspiration succeeds.

> Manussattaṁ liṅgasampatti, hetu satthāradassanaṁ; Pabbajjā guṇasampatti, adhikāro ca chandatā; Aṭṭhadhammasamodhānā, abhinīhāro samijjhati’.

### 2.116-2.165 <a id="tea-buddhavamsa-2-116-2-165"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Seeking the qualities that make a Buddha, Sumedha discerns the ten perfections one after another — giving, virtue, renunciation, wisdom, energy, patience, truthfulness, determination, loving-kindness and equanimity — each with a simile (an overturned water-jar, a yak guarding its tail, a prisoner leaving jail, a monk begging from every house, a lion, the earth that bears all, the morning star keeping its course, a rocky mountain unmoved by wind, water cooling good and bad alike, the earth indifferent to what is thrown on it), and resolves to fulfil them.
