one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); a long verse work that re-expounds the Brahmasūtras adhikaraṇa by adhikaraṇa, supplementing the Brahmasūtrabhāṣya with extended argument against Advaita, Buddhist, Jain, Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, Sāṃkhya and other positions; it defends the reality of the world through the witness (sākṣin) and ends with Madhva's verse naming himself Vāyu's third form. Jayatīrtha's Nyāyasudhā is its great commentary.

### 1.1.3-7 <a id="tea-anuvyakhyana-1-1-3-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Hari, manifest as Vyāsa at the request of Brahmā, Śiva and others, made the unsurpassed science called the supreme knowledge; Bādarāyaṇa is the teacher of teachers and the source of the śāstras. The Brahmasūtras have a threefold great authority — as the word of a trustworthy speaker, as rooted in śruti and as rooted in reasoning — found together nowhere else; so nothing is held more authoritative, and although (Madhva) has commented on them already, this further explanation is made for clarity.

### 1.4.95-98 <a id="tea-anuvyakhyana-1-4-95-98"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

That the witness (sākṣin) establishes what is real is fully agreed by both parties, so the doctrine of apparent transformation (vivarta) cannot stand. Śrutis such as 'viśvaṃ satyam' declare the world real. The reality of space and the rest is established by the witness's perception; what the witness establishes is never sublated, since it is free of defect.

### 1.4.99-103 <a id="tea-anuvyakhyana-1-4-99-103"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

That something is unsublated at all times is known by the witness alone. Time is directly perceived by the witness, even in deep sleep; past and future time too are within its range, and inference cannot reach them. Everything seen or remembered is set in time established by the witness, so without it nothing could be remembered or seen. Hence the reality of what is apprehended without defect is certified by the witness, and by it the reality of examination and the rest.

### 2.2.236-240 <a id="tea-anuvyakhyana-2-2-236-240"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

If the property-bearer, the counter-positive and their difference are grasped together, where is the conflict? The difference is grasped with the thing's own nature, and 'this is different from that' is then grasped specifically. So the charge of mutual dependence in grasping difference fails; those who raise it answer only with sophistry.

### 2.2.241-242 <a id="tea-anuvyakhyana-2-2-241-242"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The māyā-teacher and the Mādhyamika are both to be ignored by those who seek welfare: what is the void for the voidist is Brahman for the māyā-teacher. Their definitions do not differ, both (ultimates) being without attributes, and for both the defining mark is merely being opposed to the unreal and the like.

### 4.2.40-48 <a id="tea-anuvyakhyana-4-2-40-48"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The voidists call liberation the attributeless, self-shining, unconditioned void, free of the many concealments (saṃvṛti), and transmigration the state concealed by them. The māyā-teachers, whether they hold one soul or many, say the same: their māyā is the voidists' saṃvṛti, and by calling Brahman attributeless the void is Brahman — there is no difference. Being-consciousness-bliss is of no use to one who holds an impartite meaning; since both exclude only the unreal and the like, there is no difference between Brahman and the void.
