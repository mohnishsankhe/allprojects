## Teachings (8: skeleton 8)

### 1-3 <a id="tea-trisvabhavanirdesa-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The imagined, the dependent and the perfected are the three natures, the deep object of the wise. What appears is the dependent, because it occurs in dependence on conditions; how it appears is the imagined, because it is mere imagination; the perfected is the constant non-existence of the appearing thing in the way it appears, because it is never otherwise.

> kalpitaḥ paratantraśca pariniṣpanna eva ca / trayaḥ svabhāvā dhīrāṇāṃ gambhīrajñeyamiṣyate // yat khyāti paratantro 'sau yathā khyāti sa kalpitaḥ / pratyayādhīnavṛttitvāt kalpanāmātrabhāvataḥ

### 6-9 <a id="tea-trisvabhavanirdesa-6-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mind is twofold as cause and effect: the consciousness called ālaya, and the operative consciousness, sevenfold. The first is called 'mind' (citta) because it is accumulated (cita) with the seeds of defilement and imprints; the second because it occurs with variegated (citra) images. Unreal imagination is threefold: maturational (the root consciousness), causal, and appearing (the operative consciousness, seen, seer and seeing).

### 11-13 <a id="tea-trisvabhavanirdesa-11-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The imagined is both existent and non-existent, since it is grasped as existent and is utterly non-existent; the dependent is both, since it exists as error and does not exist as it appears; the perfected is both, since it exists as non-duality and is the non-existence of duality.

### 22-25 <a id="tea-trisvabhavanirdesa-22-25"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The three natures are taught in an order for the sake of convention and of entry: first one enters the dependent as the absence of duality; then one enters it as mere imagination, non-existent duality; then one enters the perfected, the being of the absence of duality — which is therefore said both to exist and not to exist.

### 27-30 <a id="tea-trisvabhavanirdesa-27-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

As a magical illusion produced by the power of a spell appears as an elephant — only the shape is there, the elephant not at all — so the imagined nature is the elephant, the dependent its shape, and the absence of the elephant there is the perfected. Unreal imagination appears from the root mind as duality; the duality does not exist at all; there is only the shape. The root consciousness is like the spell, suchness like the wood, imagination like the elephant's shape, duality like the elephant.

> māyākṛtaṃ mantravaśāt khyāti hastyātmanā yathā / ākāramātraṃ tatrāsti hastī nāsti tu sarvathā

### 31-34 <a id="tea-trisvabhavanirdesa-31-34"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

At the penetration of the truth of things, the three characteristics are simultaneously understood, abandoned and attained: understanding is non-apprehension, abandonment is non-appearance, attainment — which is also direct realization — is the apprehension without an image; by not apprehending duality the dual appearance vanishes, and with it the perfected, the absence of duality, is reached — just as in a magic show the non-apprehension of the elephant, the vanishing of its shape and the apprehension of the wood happen at once.

### 35 <a id="tea-trisvabhavanirdesa-35"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Liberation comes without effort through seeing that cognition obstructs the contrary cognition, through seeing the futility (of the object) by understanding, and through the continuation of the three knowledges.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: karma-liberation_

### 36-38 <a id="tea-trisvabhavanirdesa-36-38"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Through the apprehension of mind-only comes the non-apprehension of knowable objects; through the non-apprehension of knowable objects comes the non-apprehension of mind; through the non-apprehension of both comes the apprehension of the dharmadhātu; through the apprehension of the dharmadhātu comes mastery; and having mastery and accomplishing his own and others' aims, the wise one attains unsurpassed awakening, whose nature is the three bodies.

> cittamātropalambhena jñeyārthānupalambhatā / jñeyārthānupalambhena syāccittānupalambhatā // dvayoranupalambhena dharmadhātūpalambhatā / dharmadhātūpalambhena syād vibhutvopalambhatā
