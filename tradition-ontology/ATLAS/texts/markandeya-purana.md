A Purāṇa of dialogues: the birth, death and after-death journey of beings; the hells; Madālasā's teaching to her sons; Dattātreya's yoga for Alarka (posture, breath, dhāraṇā, obstacles, powers, Oṃ, the signs of death); creation, time and the fourteen manvantaras; and the Devī Māhātmya.

### 10.46-78 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-10-46-78"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Sumati describes death: the body's heat, stirred by a violent wind, breaks the vital points, and the udāna breath moves upward; those who gave water and food, told no lies and kept dharma die easily, liars and deniers of the Veda in terror; Yama's stinking, club-bearing men appear, the dying one trembles, cries to his kin, his speech fails and his sight whirls; leaving the body he takes another body born of his deeds, for suffering, not born of parents; bound by the messengers he is dragged south over thorns, stones and burning ground, while givers of umbrellas, sandals, clothes and food travel in ease; for twelve days he sees his house and eats the water and piṇḍas his relatives give, and after twelve days reaches the iron city and sees Yama in his terrible form.

### 11 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-11"></a>

Sumati on birth: the jīva, released from hell or heaven, enters the seed; the embryo grows through stages, nourished by a channel at the navel, lying curled in the womb, remembering its past births and suffering, until it is born and forgets.

### 13-15 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-13-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The righteous king Vipaścit of the Janaka line, brought briefly to hell for a small fault, found that the breeze from his body relieved the tormented; refusing to leave while his presence helped them - for no joy in heaven equals the joy of relieving the suffering - he gave all his merit so that they were freed from hell, and thereby gained a still higher world.

### 25.10-18 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-25-10-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Madālasā, pretending to lull her crying son, teaches him: you are pure, dear child; you have no name - it was just now imagined for you; this body of five elements is not yours, nor are you its; why do you cry? you neither grow nor decline; do not be deluded when this garment, the body, wears out; honour the host of beings you call father, son, mother, wife, mine and not-mine.

### 38.6-16 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-38-6-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dattātreya to Alarka: 'mine' is the root of suffering and 'not mine' of peace; the great tree of mine-ness sprouts from 'I', its trunk is 'mine', its branches house and field, its leaves wife and children, its fruit pleasure and pain; those who cut it with the axe of knowledge sharpened on the stone of good company reach the cool forest of Brahman; neither you nor I are the gross body, the subtle elements or the inner organ - the knower of the field is other than the aggregate of the guṇas.

> mameti mūlaṃ duḥkhasya na mameti ca nirvṛteḥ

### 39.1-35 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-39-1-35"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dattātreya's yoga: sitting in the lotus, half or svastika posture with Oṃ in the heart, body even, heels not touching the genitals, head slightly raised, teeth apart, gazing at the nose-tip without looking about, covering tamas by rajas and rajas by sattva, the yogin draws in senses, breaths and mind as a tortoise its limbs; by breath-restraint (prāṇāyāma), withdrawal (pratyāhāra) and holding (dhāraṇā) all faults perish; he should climb each ground only after mastering the one below, as one climbs a house.

### 39.47-61 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-39-47-61"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Yoga is not to be practised when overfed, hungry, tired or agitated, in great cold or heat, in wind, or in noisy places, near fire, in a ruined cowshed, at crossroads, on dry leaves, by rivers, in cremation grounds, among snakes, near wells or anthills; one who ignores these gets deafness, dullness, loss of memory, dumbness, blindness and fever; remedies are prescribed for such faults, e.g. warm oily gruel, holding the mind on a great mountain when trembling, on the ear when deaf, and the body must be protected since it is the means of all four aims.

### 39.62-65 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-39-62-65"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The yogin's attainments should be kept hidden, for through telling and wonder knowledge is lost; the first signs of progress in yoga are lightness (freedom from greed), health, gentleness, a pleasant smell, scant excreta, radiance, clarity and a pleasant voice; people praise him in his absence, creatures do not fear him, and he is not troubled by heat and cold.

### 40.1-13 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-40-1-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When the self has been seen, obstacles (upasarga) arise: longing for ritual rewards, women, heaven, divinity, elixirs, flying, entering water or fire, the fruits of śrāddha and gifts - the yogin must turn the mind back from these to Brahman; then come five more: prātibha (all learning appears), śrāvaṇa (hearing sounds from a thousand yojanas), daiva (seeing like the gods), bhrama (the mind wanders unsupported) and āvarta (knowledge whirls like a whirlpool); even the gods, their yoga destroyed by these, return again and again.

### 40.14-28 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-40-14-28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Covering himself with a white blanket made of mind, the yogin should contemplate the supreme Brahman; he should hold seven subtle dhāraṇās at the head - earth, water, fire, air, space, mind and intellect - experiencing and then abandoning the subtlety of each; attachment to any element destroys him, and he returns; passing beyond all seven, he goes where he wills.

### 40.29-41 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-40-29-41"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The eight powers - aṇimā, laghimā, mahimā, prāpti, prākāmya, īśitva, vaśitva and going where one wishes - are indicators of nirvāṇa; then the yogin is not born, does not grow or perish, cannot be cut, wetted or burnt; his faults burnt by the fire of yoga, he becomes one with Brahman as gold fuses with gold, fire with fire, water with water, never again separate.

### 41.2-26 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-41-2-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The yogin's conduct: honour and dishonour, which agitate people, work in reverse for him - dishonour is nectar and honour poison; he steps where the eye has purified, drinks water filtered by cloth, speaks what truth has purified, thinks what the intellect has purified; begs food after the household meal; offers the first mouthfuls to the five breaths; keeps five vows (non-stealing, celibacy, renunciation, non-greed and non-harming) and five observances (freedom from anger, service to the teacher, purity, light food, daily study); he should seek the essential knowledge, not many knowledges; the three staffs are control of speech, action and mind; better than Vedic rites is japa, better than japa knowledge, better than knowledge meditation free of attachment.

_level: conventional · standpoint: ethical-social · path: action, meditation · stage: intermediate · types: ethics, practice_

### 42.1-17 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-42-1-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

To attain the supreme self one should repeat the one syllable Oṃ: its three measures a, u and m are sāttvika, rājasa and tāmasa (and correspond to the three worlds bhūr, bhuvaḥ, svar), while the half-measure above them, touched like the movement of an ant at the crown, is beyond the guṇas; the breath is the bow, the self the arrow, Brahman the target; the one who knows and meditates on this imperishable Brahman called Oṃ leaves the wheel of saṃsāra and dissolves in the supreme self; one whose karma is not yet exhausted should know the signs of death so as to remember yoga at the time of departure.

### 43.1-2 <a id="tea-markandeya-purana-43-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The signs of approaching death (ariṣṭa), by which the knower of yoga knows his own death: one who no longer sees the path of the gods (the Milky Way), the pole-star, Venus, the moon's shadow or Arundhatī will not live beyond a year; the chapter lists further signs with shorter spans (a rayless sun, dream images, loss of one's shadow, bodily changes).
