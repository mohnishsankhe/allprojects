# Seven-Point Mind Training (blo sbyong don bdun ma)

`src:seven-point-mind-training` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Blo sbyong don bdun ma, The Seven Points of Mind Training, Root text of the Seven Points
**Language:** Tibetan
**Family:** ascetic
**Lineages:** [Kadam](../lineages/kadam.md), [Kagyu (the Kagyu transmissions: Marpa / Dakpo Kagyu, with the Shangpa grouped by name)](../lineages/kagyu.md), [Sakya](../lineages/sakya.md), [Gelug](../lineages/gelug.md)
**Genre:** mind-training slogans (blo sbyong)
**Authors:** 
  - [Chekawa Yeshe Dorje](../teachers/chekawa.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: Chekawa's arrangement of instructions received through Sharawa and traced to Atiśa, Serlingpa and ultimately to Śāntideva and Mañjuśrī; scholarly: the root text circulated in several arrangements (Sé Chilbu's, Tokmé Zangpo's, Jamgön Kongtrul's); the 'fifty-nine slogans' is the count of the later arrangement; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Scholarly account: 12th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** seven points; 59 slogans in the arrangement followed here (Kongtrul's, as in modern editions); numbering here is point.slogan
**Availability:** digitized-translation

Chekawa Yeshe Dorje's instruction on training the mind in bodhicitta, arranged in seven points — preliminaries; the main practice of ultimate and relative bodhicitta (sending and taking); transforming adversity into the path; a lifetime's practice (the five powers); the measure of training; its commitments; and its precepts — in terse slogans (fifty-nine in the arrangement of later editions).
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: original; name: Tibetan: in Mind Training: The Great Collection and many prints
  - kind: translation; name: English: Thupten Jinpa (Mind Training: The Great Collection, 2006); Chögyam Trungpa, Training the Mind (1993)
**Commentaries on this text:** [Sé Chilbu's commentary on the Seven Points (blo sbyong don bdun ma'i 'grel pa)](seven-point-commentary-se-chilbu.md)
