The hymns (patikams, usually of ten or eleven verses, set to melodic modes called paṇ) of the three samayācāryas — Campantar (Tirumuṟai 1–3), Appar (4–6) and Cuntarar (7) — sung at some 274 temples of the Tamil land and beyond; praise of Śiva's forms, deeds and shrines, confession, surrender, and polemic against Jains and Buddhists. Sung daily in temples by ōtuvārs.

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-tevaram-1-1-1"></a>

Campantar's first hymn: 'He with the ear-ring, riding the bull, crowned with the pure white moon, smeared with the ash of the burning ground — the thief who stole my heart' is the Lord of Brahmapuram (Cīkāḻi).

### 1-3.10 <a id="tea-tevaram-1-3-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In most of Campantar's patikams the tenth verse denounces the Jains and Buddhists (their nakedness or robes, their denial of Śiva) and warns against their teaching; the eighth recalls Rāvaṇa crushed under Kailāsa and the ninth Brahmā and Viṣṇu failing to find Śiva's crown and feet.
