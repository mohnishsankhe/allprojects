# Nyāyakusumāñjali

**Language:** Sanskrit

**Authors:** 
  - [Udayana](../teachers/udayana.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Dates:** Scholarly account: late 10th – 11th c. CE; (confidence low)
**Structure:** 5 stabakas of kārikās with Udayana's prose; verse numbering varies by edition
**Availability:** unknown

Udayana's 'handful of flowers of reasoning' offered to the Lord: in five clusters (stabaka) of verses with his own prose, it answers five objections to Īśvara — that there is no supersensible means to the other world, that such means can work without Him, that there are proofs of His non-existence, that even if He exists He is not an authority, and that there is no proof of Him — and gives, in 5.1, the classic list of proofs. It presents reasoning about the Lord as a form of worship and opens with a passage on the names under which the schools worship Him.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Nyāyakusumāñjaliprakāśa](kusumanjali-prakasa.md)

### stabaka 1 (statement of the five objections) <a id="tea-nyaya-kusumanjali-1-five-vipratipatti"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Lord's existence is questioned for five reasons, answered in the five clusters: that there is no supersensible means to the other world; that such means can be performed even without Him; that there are proofs of His non-existence; that even if He exists He is not an authority; and that there is no proof establishing Him.

### stabaka 1, opening prose <a id="tea-nyaya-kusumanjali-1-opening-prose"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Whatever human aim they seek, all worship Him: the Upaniṣadic thinkers as the pure, awakened by nature; the Kāpilas (Sāṃkhya) as the first knower, the perfected one; the Pātañjalas as untouched by afflictions, karma, its fruition and residues, who assumes a created body to reveal the tradition and shows grace; the Mahāpāśupatas as undefiled and independent; the Śaivas as Śiva; the Vaiṣṇavas as Puruṣottama; the Paurāṇikas as the Grandfather; the ritualists as the Person of the sacrifice; the Saugatas as the omniscient; the Digambaras as the unobstructed; the Mīmāṃsakas as what is taught as the object of worship; the Cārvākas as what is established by worldly usage; the Naiyāyikas as one endowed with whatever (qualities) are shown to fit; even artisans worship Him as Viśvakarman.
