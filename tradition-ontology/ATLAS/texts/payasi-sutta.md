## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 2 <a id="tea-payasi-sutta-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The chieftain Pāyāsi holds the view: 'there is no other world, there are no spontaneously born beings, there is no fruit or result of good and bad deeds'.

### 5-29 <a id="tea-payasi-sutta-5-29"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The chieftain Pāyāsi holds that there is no other world, no spontaneously born beings and no fruit of good and bad deeds; Kumārakassapa answers each of his 'experiments' with similes — the sun and moon, the condemned bandit, the man in the cesspit, the blind man who denies colours, the dream, the conch-blower — showing that the other world cannot be seen with the fleshly eye.

### 6-12 <a id="tea-payasi-sutta-6-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pāyāsi's arguments: his wicked relatives, told they would go to hell, and his virtuous relatives, told they would go to heaven, promised to return and tell him, but none came back or sent a messenger; and virtuous ascetics who want to live and fear death do not kill themselves to reach the heaven they believe in. Kumāra Kassapa answers with similes: a condemned bandit is not allowed leave to visit his kin; a man pulled from a cesspit would not wish to go back into it; a day in the Thirty-three is a century here; the blind from birth deny colours; and a pregnant woman who cuts open her belly to hasten the birth destroys herself and the child — the virtuous do not force the unripe to ripen.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: polemical · path: general · stage: unmarked · types: death-dying, karma-liberation, dispute_

### 14-20 <a id="tea-payasi-sutta-14-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pāyāsi's experiments on condemned criminals: one was sealed alive in a jar and heated, and on opening no soul was seen leaving; one was weighed alive and weighed after being strangled, and was heavier dead; one was killed without harming skin, flesh or bone, and no soul was seen leaving; one was cut open layer by layer, and no soul was seen. Kassapa answers: you do not see the soul of a dreaming man going and coming; a hot iron ball is lighter than a cold one, as a body with life, warmth and consciousness is lighter; a conch sounds only when blown by a man; and the boy who split the fire-sticks to find fire never found it.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: polemical · path: general · stage: unmarked · types: body-layers, death-dying, dispute_

### 29-30 <a id="tea-payasi-sutta-29-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Pāyāsi says he will not give up his view for fear of what people would say; Kassapa tells of the man who, carrying hemp, would not exchange it for linen, silver or gold. Pāyāsi then admits he was delighted with the very first simile and argued only to hear more, and takes refuge.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: general · stage: beginner · types: dispute, teacher-transmission_
