Kauṇḍinya's commentary reads the Pāśupata Sūtra as teaching five categories (effect, cause, union, rule, end of suffering), defines the Lord, the soul and the bonds, the three means of knowledge, the yamas and niyamas, the six 'doors' of feigned behaviour and the exchange of merit and sin, and correlates the sūtras with a five-stage path (residence, livelihood, strength, action, purification and gain for each stage). It holds that the end of suffering comes by the Lord's grace and that the Lord does not depend on karma.

## Teachings (21: skeleton 21)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-pancarthabhasya-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The first sūtra names, in brief, the five categories: the end of suffering (implied by 'atha', the answer to the disciple's question), the effect (the paśus), the cause (the Pati), union (yoga) and the rule (vidhi). The Pati 'reaches' and 'protects' the paśus by his infinite power of knowledge and his lordship. The end of suffering is attained by his grace, not merely by knowledge, dispassion, merit, lordship or renunciation. Yoga is the conjunction of the self with the Lord.

> evaṃ saduḥkhāntaḥ kāryaṃ kāraṇaṃ yogo vidhir iti pañcaiva padārthāḥ samāsata uddiṣṭāḥ

### 1.1 <a id="tea-pancarthabhasya-1-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Lord, in human form, took a brahmin's body and descended at Kāyāvataraṇa; he went on foot to Ujjayinī and, having taken the sign known in the 'beyond the āśramas' observance, stayed on an ash-altar in a temple. Kuśika, prompted by Rudra, came, clasped his feet, declared his birth, lineage, learning and freedom from debts, and asked whether there is a final and absolute removal of all sufferings - bodily-mental, from beings and from the gods.

### 1.1 <a id="tea-pancarthabhasya-1-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Paśus are all conscious beings except the Lord, both those joined to bodies and organs (sāñjana) and those free of them (nirañjana). Their paśu-state is bondage, a beginningless lack of lordship. They are called paśus because they 'see' (only the body) and because they are 'bound' by the kalās called effects and instruments. As it is said: those liberated by Sāṃkhya-Yoga, the lords of Sāṃkhya-Yoga, and all from Brahmā down to the animals are declared paśus.

> sāṅkhyayogena ye muktāḥ sāṅkhyayogeśvarāś ca ye | brahmādayas tiryagantāḥ sarve te paśavaḥ smṛtāḥ

### 1.1 <a id="tea-pancarthabhasya-1-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There are three means of knowledge: perception (of the senses and of the self), inference (seen - 'as before' and 'from the remainder' - and 'seen in general'), and scripture (āgama), which is the teaching come down through the succession of teachers from Maheśvara. The Lord is the one who causes valid knowledge, the person is the knower, the five categories are what is known, and knowing is awareness (saṃvit).

### 2.6 <a id="tea-pancarthabhasya-2-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Does the Lord produce the effect all at once or in sequence, depending on karma or not? As he wishes. The one who wills is the Lord, his will is his desire, what is willed is the effect beginning with vidyā; he produces it without sequence or in sequence as he wishes, because he wills. Hence his independence of karma is established: those who desire the fruits of action depend on Maheśvara, but the Lord depends neither on karma nor on the person. Therefore the Lord is not dependent on karma.

> karmakāminaś ca maheśvaram apekṣante na tu bhagavān īśvaraḥ karma puruṣaṃ vāpekṣate | ato na karmāpekṣa īśvaraḥ

### 2.18 <a id="tea-pancarthabhasya-2-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ordinary gifts of cows, land and gold are poor gifts, since their fruits are uncertain, not final and graded; the supreme gift is the gift of oneself, because it brings the certain and final attainment of Rudra's proximity, whose fruit is non-return. When he gives he also worships and performs austerity. The 'supreme goal' is a name for yoga; it lies beyond the goals of Sāṃkhya-Yoga and of worldly prosperity.

### 3.12-17 <a id="tea-pancarthabhasya-3-12-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The 'wretch' of the sūtra is a living man, not a dead one: unwashed, smeared with dirt, with beard, nails and hair grown, without grooming, so that the marks of caste and āśrama are cut off. Once he has gained knowledge, his impurity is reduced and he has the teacher's leave, he goes among people and, while awake, acts as if asleep with a snoring sound in the throat; or deliberately shakes his limbs as if struck by wind; or walks as if lame; or, standing neither too near nor too far from a group of women, looks at a young and beautiful one and makes signs of desire; or handles sticks and clods as one who cannot tell pure from impure - acts not conflicting with the yamas; or utters meaningless, repeated or contradictory words. People then say he is asleep, diseased, lame, unchaste, heedless or senseless and despise him, and by this false charge their merit comes to him and his sin goes to them.

### 5.29 <a id="tea-pancarthabhasya-5-29"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

For each stage the text gives a residence, livelihood, strength, action, purification and gain: first, in the temple, living on alms, with eightfold celibacy as strength, bathing and laughter etc. as action, removal of impurity as purification, and knowledge as gain; second, among people, living on discarded food, closing the doors of the senses and acting like a madman, destruction of sin, the whole of dharma as gain; third, in an empty house or cave, on alms, enduring like cows and deer, study and meditation, the gain of constant union with the god and conquest of the senses; fourth, in the cremation ground, on what comes, remembrance as action, removal of forgetfulness, union with Rudra as gain; fifth, the vigilance, grace, removal of suffering and attainment of the qualities. Verses list five gains (knowledge, austerity, constant union with the god, yoga, arising of the qualities), five impurities (ignorance, demerit, habituation to objects, non-attainment of steadiness, lack of lordship), five means (residence, meditation, restraint of all organs, remembrance, grace) and five places (the dwelling, the world, the empty house, the cremation ground, Rudra).

### 5.46 <a id="tea-pancarthabhasya-5-46"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In summing up: the causes of other systems - primal matter (pradhāna), the person (puruṣa), time, nature - are here effects, because they are paśus or are bound; the effect is vidyā, kalā and paśu. Where others teach isolation (kaivalya), here the distinction is the power of acting without organs and a lordship beyond kaivalya; the powers are knowing (beginning with hearing) and acting (beginning with swiftness of mind).
