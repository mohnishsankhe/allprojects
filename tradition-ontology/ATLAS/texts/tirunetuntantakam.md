Tirumaṅkai Āḻvār's thirty long tāṇṭakam verses: first in his own voice, then in the voice of the mother describing her love-sick daughter, then in the voice of the girl (Parakāla-nāyakī) herself. By tradition the text through which Parāśara Bhaṭṭar won over the Advaitin who became Nañjīyar, hence recited at the opening of the recitation festival.

### 1-30 <a id="tea-tirunetuntantakam-1-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The poem moves from the Āḻvār's own voice praising the Lord's forms to the voice of a mother lamenting her love-sick daughter, and then to the daughter herself, Parakāla-nāyakī, who has given her heart to the Lord of the sacred sites.
