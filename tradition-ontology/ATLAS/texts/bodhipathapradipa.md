# Lamp for the Path to Awakening (Bodhipathapradīpa; byang chub lam gyi sgron ma)

**Availability:** digitized-original

Atiśa's verse treatise composed at Tholing at Jangchub Ö's request: the three kinds of person (small, middling, supreme); refuge and offerings; generating aspiring bodhicitta and taking the engaging (bodhisattva) vow on a basis of prātimokṣa ethics; the three trainings; superknowledges requiring calm abiding; the union of method and wisdom; wisdom as the knowledge of emptiness established by the reasonings of Nāgārjuna; the grounds; and the rules for secret mantra, including the prohibition of the secret and wisdom initiations for celibates.

## Teachings (28: skeleton 28)

### 62-64 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-62-64"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

If one wishes to complete the accumulations for awakening easily by the powers of mantra — pacifying, increasing and the rest — and the eight great attainments such as the good vase, the secret conduct taught in the kriyā, caryā and other tantras is desired; then, for the master's consecration, please the true guru by gifts of jewels and by obeying all commands; when the guru is pleased, by the complete master's consecration one is purified of all evil and becomes fit for attainments.

> prasanne ca gurau bhūte pūrṇācāryābhiṣekataḥ | sarvapāpaviśuddhātmā siddhibhāgī bhaviṣyati ||

### 65-67 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-65-67"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Because it is strictly forbidden in the great Ādibuddha tantra, the secret and wisdom consecrations are not to be taken by one who keeps celibacy; if those established in celibate austerity take that consecration, doing what is forbidden, their austerity and vows are destroyed; the pārājika downfalls arise for that vow-holder, he surely falls into the bad destinies and never gains attainment.

### 68 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-68"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### v2-5 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-v2-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Persons are to be known as of three kinds — lesser, middling and supreme. Whoever by whatever means seeks only the happiness of saṃsāra for their own sake is the lesser person. Whoever turns their back on the pleasures of existence, turns away from harmful deeds, and seeks only their own peace is the middling person. Whoever, through the suffering belonging to their own continuum, wholly wishes for the complete ending of all the suffering of others is the supreme person.

### v22-26 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-v22-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

By the rite well taught in the chapter on ethics of the Bodhisattvabhūmi, take the vow from a good teacher who has the right qualities: know as a good teacher one who is skilled in the rite of the vow, abides in the vow himself, and has the patience and compassion to confer it. ### v27-32 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-v27-32"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

Before the protectors I generate the mind of complete awakening, invite all beings as my guests and will free them from saṃsāra. From now until I reach supreme awakening I shall not harbour harmful intent, anger, avarice or envy. I shall live in pure conduct, abandon wrongdoing and desire, and with joy in the vow of ethics train following the Buddhas. I shall not be eager to reach awakening quickly for myself, but remain to the end of time for the sake of even a single being. I shall purify immeasurable, inconceivable fields and remain in the ten directions for those who call my name; I shall purify all actions of body and speech, and of mind, and not do non-virtuous deeds.

### v36-38 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-v36-38"></a>

### v39-41 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-v39-41"></a>

If the limbs of calm abiding are lost, even meditating with great effort for thousands of years one will not achieve concentration. ### v42-44 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-v42-44"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

When the yogin has achieved calm abiding the superknowledges also arise; but without the yoga of the perfection of wisdom the obscurations are not exhausted. Therefore, to abandon without remainder the obscurations of afflictions and of the knowable, always meditate on the yoga of the perfection of wisdom together with method. Wisdom without method and method without wisdom are both called bondage; therefore do not abandon either.

### v49-51 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-v49-51"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

It is not reasonable for an existent to arise; a non-existent, like a sky-flower, does not arise either; both faults would follow for 'both', so both together do not arise either. Things do not arise from themselves, nor from others, nor from both, nor without a cause — therefore they are without own-nature by essence. ### v56-60 <a id="tea-bodhipathapradipa-v56-60"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high · [AI-translated]

This existence arisen from conceptualization has conceptualization as its nature; therefore the abandoning of all conceptualization is supreme nirvāṇa. So the Blessed One said that conceptualization is great ignorance, which makes one fall into the ocean of saṃsāra; abiding in non-conceptual concentration, the non-conceptual is clear like the sky.
