Shinran's systematic anthology: the true teaching is the Larger Sūtra, whose purport is the primal vow and whose substance is the name; the great practice is saying the name of the Tathāgata of Unhindered Light; great shinjin, the entrusting given by Amida, is the true cause; realization is necessary attainment of nirvāṇa (eleventh vow) and the return to save beings; the true Buddha and land are of immeasurable light and life; other practices and self-power nenbutsu are provisional gates leading to transformed lands; non-Buddhist paths and deities are not to be relied on.

## Teachings (12: skeleton 12)

### gyo <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-gyo"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The great practice is to say the name of the Tathāgata of Unhindered Light; it contains all good and all roots of virtue and comes from the vow that all buddhas praise the name (seventeenth); it is not a practice the ordinary being performs and transfers by self-power but one transferred by Amida (non-transferring practice); calling the name breaks the ignorance of beings and fulfils their aspirations.

### keshindo.gods <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-keshindo-gods"></a>

Quoting sūtras, Shinran teaches that one who has taken refuge in the Buddha should not take refuge in other gods and spirits, nor observe auspicious days and divination; non-Buddhist teachings are to be distinguished from the true.

### keshindo.mappo <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-keshindo-mappo"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The teachings of the holy path are suited to the time when the Buddha was in the world and to the right Dharma age, not to the semblance and last ages; reckoning the years since the Buddha's passing, Shinran finds the present in the last age, and quotes the Lamp for the Last Age: in the last age there are only monks in name, who are nonetheless to be honoured.

### keshindo.three-vows <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-keshindo-three-vows"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Those who practise various goods (nineteenth vow) or say the name by self-power (twentieth) are born in the transformed land — the borderland, the womb-palace, the realm of indolence and pride. Shinran confesses that, following the treatise-master and the master's guidance, he left the provisional gate of the myriad practices and good acts, turned to the true gate of the self-power nenbutsu, and then left it to enter the ocean of the selected vow, the eighteenth.

### kyo <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-kyo"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There are two aspects of Amida's transfer of merit, going and returning; in the going aspect there are true teaching, practice, shinjin and realization. The true teaching is the Larger Sūtra of Immeasurable Life: its essential purport is Amida's primal vow and its substance is the name; the Buddha appeared in the world precisely to reveal it.

### postscript <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-postscript"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The monks of the holy path in the temples, blind to the teaching, and the scholars of the capital, confused about true and false, together with the ruler and ministers, acting against the Dharma, punished the master Hōnen and his disciples without trial — some were executed, others defrocked and exiled; Shinran was one. Therefore he is now neither monk nor layman and has taken 'Toku' (bald-head) as his surname. In 1201 he abandoned the miscellaneous practices and took refuge in the primal vow; in 1205 he was permitted to copy the Senchakushū and the master's portrait. He writes with joy and gratitude at having met the teaching.

### preface <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-preface"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The universal vow difficult to fathom is a great ship bearing us across the sea hard to cross; the unhindered light is the sun of wisdom dispelling the darkness of ignorance. When the occasion for the Pure Land teaching had matured, Devadatta led Ajātaśatru to harm, and Śākyamuni led Vaidehī to choose the Land of Peace; the auspicious name that embodies all virtues and the true shinjin are the way for those of the last age. How joyful I am, Gutoku Shinran, to have met the teaching of India, China and Japan.

### shin.ajatasatru <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-shin-ajatasatru"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Quoting the Nirvāṇa Sūtra at length, Shinran shows that those difficult to cure — those who slander the Dharma, commit the grave offences, or lack the seed of buddhahood — are healed by the Buddha's compassion, as Ajātaśatru, who killed his father, was freed of his remorse and illness on meeting the Buddha and awakened 'rootless faith'.

### shin.cause <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-shin-cause"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Great shinjin is the true cause of birth; it is hard to attain, not because the practice is difficult but because shinjin is difficult. The three minds of the eighteenth vow — sincere mind, entrusting and aspiration for birth — are all Amida's true mind given to beings and are one mind, the single mind of Vasubandhu; there is no true mind in the foolish being. Shinjin arises in 'one thought-moment', the ultimate brevity of time, when the person enters the rightly established state; this is crosswise transcendence.

### shin.ten-benefits <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-shin-ten-benefits"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

One who attains the diamond-like true mind gains ten benefits in the present life: protection by unseen beings, possession of supreme virtue, turning of evil into good, protection by all buddhas, praise by all buddhas, constant protection by the Buddha's light, much joy of heart, awareness of the Buddha's benevolence and its repayment, constant practice of great compassion, and entry into the rightly established state.

### shinbutsudo <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-shinbutsudo"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The true Buddha is the Tathāgata of inconceivable light and the true land is the land of immeasurable light, established by the vows of light and life (twelfth and thirteenth); both are the reward of the vow; there are true and provisional (transformed) buddhas and lands.

### sho <a id="tea-kyogyoshinsho-sho"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

True realization is the wondrous fruit of perfect benefit for others, from the vow of necessary attainment of nirvāṇa (eleventh): the ordinary being full of passions who attains the shinjin of the going aspect joins the rightly established group in this life and necessarily reaches nirvāṇa, which is eternal bliss, dharma-body, suchness, one-thusness; from suchness appear the bodies of the Buddha. The returning aspect (twenty-second vow) is the benefit of teaching others after birth.
