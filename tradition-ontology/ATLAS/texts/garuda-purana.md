**Availability:** digitized-original

A Vaiṣṇava encyclopedia best known for its Pretakalpa: the process of dying, Yama's messengers, the thumb-sized person, the year-long road to Yama's city past sixteen towns and the Vaitaraṇī river, judgment, the preta state and its signs, and the rites that give the dead a body and join them to the ancestors.

### 1.142 <a id="tea-garuda-purana-1-142"></a>

### 2.2.40-51 <a id="tea-garuda-purana-2-2-40-51"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When death arrives, by the power of time some illness arises; the senses fail and strength goes; the pain is like the sting of countless scorpions; consciousness fades into insensibility; the breaths are moved by Yama's attendants standing near; with the breath in the throat the face froths; then the thumb-sized person (aṅguṣṭha-mātra puruṣa), crying 'alas', is taken from the body by Yama's messengers, looking back at his home; the truthful and faithful die easily, liars and betrayers in delusion; the messengers are terrifying, foul-smelling, with staffs and hammers in hand.

### 2.5.30-37 <a id="tea-garuda-purana-2-5-30-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The ten piṇḍas offered by the sons over the first days are each divided into four parts: two parts make the preta's body, the third feeds Yama's attendants, the fourth nourishes the preta; the body is completed in nine days - the first piṇḍa makes the head, the second ears, eyes and nose, the third neck, shoulders, arms and chest, the fourth navel, genitals and anus, the fifth knees, shanks and feet, the sixth all vital points, the seventh the channels, the eighth teeth and hair, the ninth vigour, and the tenth completeness, satiety and the reversal of hunger.

### 2.5.39-40 <a id="tea-garuda-purana-2-5-39-40"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 2.5.49-55 <a id="tea-garuda-purana-2-5-49-55"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The joining with the ancestors (sapiṇḍīkaraṇa) may be done on the twelfth day, after three fortnights, at six months or at the year; because the body is impermanent, the twelfth day is praised; until it is done the dead is addressed as 'preta'; the sixteen śrāddhas come in three sets (the 'soiled', 'middle' and 'highest' sixteen).

### 2.5.85-154 <a id="tea-garuda-purana-2-5-85-154"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The road of Yama: dragged by Yama's men with nooses, the preta travels about two hundred yojanas a day over thorns, stones, fire and burning sun, feeling pain when his body is burned; on the way lie sixteen cities - Yāmya, Sauripura, Nagendrabhavana, Gandharva, Śailāgama, Krauñca, Krūrapura, Vicitrabhavana, Bahvāpada, Duḥkhada, Nānākrandapura, Sutaptabhavana, Raudra, Payovarṣaṇa, Śītāḍhya and Bahubhīti - in each of which he eats the monthly piṇḍa given by his kin and laments the good he did not do; after a year he reaches Yama's city, with thirteen doorkeepers called Śravaṇas; sinners see Yama terrible, the virtuous see him gentle, crowned and radiant.

### 2.5.123-126 <a id="tea-garuda-purana-2-5-123-126"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

On the road flows the Vaitaraṇī, a hundred yojanas wide, full of pus and blood; boatmen there say: if you gave the Vaitaraṇī cow, board this boat and cross in ease; one who gave the cow crosses easily, the one who did not is dragged through by the hand and pecked by birds; the gift of the Vaitaraṇī at the end of life is beneficial for men and burns away sin.

### 2.10.75-82 <a id="tea-garuda-purana-2-10-75-82"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Immediately after death the embodied being takes an airy (vāyavīya) body, as a leech moves to the next blade of grass only when its front is firmly placed; the deluded do not see it leaving, staying or enjoying, but those with the eye of knowledge do; this airy body is called the conveyance-body (ātivāhika); when the ten piṇḍas are offered by sons, the piṇḍa-born body and the airy body become one.

### 3.1.50-56 <a id="tea-garuda-purana-3-1-50-56"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Brahmakāṇḍa grades the Purāṇas: sāttvika - the Viṣṇu, Bhāgavata and Garuḍa (highest), the Vāyu (middle), the Matsya and Kūrma (lowest sāttvika); rājasa - the Skanda, Padma, Vāmana, Varāha, Agni and Bhaviṣya; tāmasa - the Brahmāṇḍa, Liṅga, Brahmavaivarta, Mārkaṇḍeya, Brāhma and Āditya; the rājasa and tāmasa ones are not to be heard by seekers of liberation except for their sāttvika portions.
