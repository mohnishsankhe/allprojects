Yāmuna's hymn to Nārāyaṇa (c. 65 verses) opening with homage to Nāthamuni, Parāśara and Nammāḻvār, praising the Lord's supremacy and qualities, and culminating in the poet's confession of helplessness (ākiñcanya) and surrender at the Lord's feet.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1 <a id="tea-stotraratna-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Homage to the sage Nāthamuni, a mass of inconceivable, wonderful, effortless knowledge and dispassion, an ocean of deep devotion to the Lord.

> namo 'cintyādbhutākliṣṭa-jñāna-vairāgya-rāśaye | nāthāya munaye 'gādha-bhagavad-bhakti-sindhave ||

### 5 <a id="tea-stotraratna-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Yāmuna bows his head to the glorious feet, lovely with vakula flowers, of the first head of our clan (Nammāḻvār), which are for all his descendants mother, father, wives, children and wealth — everything.

> mātā pitā yuvatayas tanayā vibhūtiḥ sarvaṃ yad eva niyamena mad-anvayānām | ādyasya naḥ kulapater vakulābhirāmaṃ śrīmat tad-aṅghri-yugalaṃ praṇamāmi mūrdhnā ||

### 22 <a id="tea-stotraratna-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'I am not established in dharma, nor a knower of the self, nor have I devotion to your lotus feet; having nothing (akiñcana), with no other refuge, O refuge of all, I take refuge at the root of your feet.'

> na dharma-niṣṭho 'smi na cātma-vedī na bhaktimāṃs tvac-caraṇāravinde | akiñcano 'nanya-gatiḥ śaraṇya tvat-pāda-mūlaṃ śaraṇaṃ prapadye ||
