# Vimalakīrtinirdeśa

The layman Vimalakīrti of Vaiśālī, a bodhisattva of inconceivable skillful means, feigns illness; the Buddha's disciples decline to visit him, recalling how he had corrected them; Mañjuśrī goes, and in Vimalakīrti's empty room they discuss the bodhisattva's illness born of compassion, inconceivable liberation (Sumeru in a mustard seed), a goddess who turns Śāriputra into a woman, the passions as the family of the Tathāgata, and the entrance into non-duality, which Vimalakīrti shows by silence. The purity of the buddha-field follows the purity of the mind.

### 1.14 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-1-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A bodhisattva who wishes to purify his buddha-field should strive to purify his own mind, for as the bodhisattva's mind is pure, so his buddha-field is pure.

> yādṛśī bodhisatvasya cittapariśuddhis tādṛśī buddhakṣetrapariśuddhiḥ sambhavati

### 1.15 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-1-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Śāriputra wonders whether the Buddha's mind was impure, since this world looks impure; the Buddha answers that the sun is not at fault if the blind do not see it, and touching the ground with his toe shows the world as a jewelled pure land — it was always so, but appears according to beings' minds.

### 2.3 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-2-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Vimalakīrti wears the white clothes of a layman yet keeps the monk's discipline; lives at home but is unattached to the three realms; shows wife and children yet is always chaste; is seen in gambling halls, brothels and taverns to mature beings there.

> avadātavastradhārī śramaṇeryāpathasampannaḥ | gṛhavāsasthitaḥ kāmadhāturūpadhātvārūpyadhātvasaṃsṛṣṭaḥ

### 2.7 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-2-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

By skillful means Vimalakīrti shows himself ill; to the townsfolk who visit him he teaches that the body is impermanent, weak, like foam, a bubble, a mirage, and that they should seek the Tathāgata's body, which is born of the perfections and is the dharmakāya.

### 4.7 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-4-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mañjuśrī asks the cause of Vimalakīrti's illness: as parents are ill when their only son is ill, so the bodhisattva, loving all beings as an only child, is ill as long as beings are ill; his illness arises from great compassion.

> sa satvaglānyena glāno bhavati, satvārogyāt tv arogaḥ

### 4.12 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-4-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

How the sick bodhisattva should regard his mind: the illness comes from clinging to 'I' and 'mine'; he should see that even the notion of dharmas is an inversion and the great illness; he should remove the illness, not the dharmas, and see that as his illness is unreal, so is that of all beings — and then compassion arises free of sentimental views.

### 4.16 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-4-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Bondage and liberation: to seize rebirth without skillful means is the bodhisattva's bondage, to go into rebirth with skillful means his liberation; to relish the taste of meditation and samādhi without skillful means is bondage, with skillful means liberation; wisdom not joined to means is bondage, and means not joined to wisdom.

> anupāyād dhyānasamādhyāsvādanatā bodhisatvasya bandhaḥ, upāyena dhyānasamādhyāsvādanatā mokṣaḥ

### 6.1 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-6-1"></a>

How should a bodhisattva regard beings? As the moon in water, a face in a mirror, a mirage, an echo, a cloud in the sky, foam, a bubble, a plantain's core — and yet cultivate true love (maitrī) for them, a love that is peaceful, non-dual and without grasping.

### 6.14 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-6-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The goddess in Vimalakīrti's house, asked by Śāriputra why she does not change her woman's state, answers that in twelve years she has looked for 'womanhood' and not found it; she turns Śāriputra into a woman and herself into Śāriputra, then back: 'as you are not a woman yet appear as one, so all women appear as women but are not women; the Buddha said all dharmas are neither male nor female'.

### 7.2 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-7-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

What is the family (gotra) of the Tathāgatas? The body-view, ignorance and craving, passion, hatred and delusion, the four inversions, the five hindrances, the ten unwholesome acts — in short the sixty-two views are the Tathāgata's family. One who has seen the unconditioned and entered the certainty of the disciples cannot raise the thought of awakening; as lotuses grow not on dry highland but in mud, the buddha-qualities grow in beings in the mud of the passions.

> satkāyaḥ kulaputra tathāgatānāṃ gotram

### 8.33 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-8-33"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Asked in turn for his teaching on entry into non-duality, Vimalakīrti remained silent; Mañjuśrī praised him: 'Excellent! This is the bodhisattvas' entry into the door of non-duality, where there is no movement of letters, sounds or ideas.' Five thousand bodhisattvas attained the acceptance of the non-arising of dharmas.

> atha vimalakīrtir licchavis tūṣṇīm abhūt

### 10 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-10"></a>

The door of the destructible and indestructible: the bodhisattva neither destroys the conditioned nor abides in the unconditioned — he does not abandon great compassion and saṃsāra, yet does not abide in the conditioned.

### 11 <a id="tea-vimalakirtinirdesa-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Asked how he sees the Tathāgata, Vimalakīrti says: as I see the true nature of my own body — not coming from the past, not going to the future, not abiding in the present; he then brings Akṣobhya's world Abhirati into this world in his hand.
