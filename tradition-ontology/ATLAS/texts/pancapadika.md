Padmapāda's commentary on the adhyāsa-bhāṣya and the first four sūtras of Śaṅkara's BSBh: analyses the definition of superimposition, presents ignorance as an insentient power (avidyā-śakti) that is the material cause of superimposition, glosses 'mithyā' as 'indescribable', and treats the jīva as a reflection of Brahman in the ego.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Pañcapādikāvivaraṇa](pancapadika-vivarana.md)

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1 <a id="tea-pancapadika-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Ignorance is an insentient power (avidyā-śakti), the material cause of superimposition; the word 'mithyā' in the adhyāsa-bhāṣya means 'indescribable' (anirvacanīya).

### 2 <a id="tea-pancapadika-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The jīva is the reflection of Brahman's consciousness in the ego (ahaṃkāra), as a face is reflected in a mirror; the reflection is not other than the original.
