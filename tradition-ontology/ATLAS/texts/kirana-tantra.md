# Kiraṇatantra

**Alternate titles:** Kiraṇa, Kiraṇāgama
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Śaiva Siddhānta](../lineages/saiva-siddhanta.md), [Mantramārga (the Path of Mantras)](../lineages/mantramarga.md)

**Authors:** 
  - [Bhaṭṭa Rāmakaṇṭha (Rāmakaṇṭha II)](../teachers/ramakantha.md) — role: commentator; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: revealed by Sadāśiva from the upper (Īśāna) face; first received by Devavibhava (Kiraṇa: Devapitṛ), then the sage Saṃvartaka (Kāmika pūrva 1.30–92; Kiraṇa vidyāpāda 10); scholarly: an early scripture (see dating); confidence: moderate

**Structure:** Rudrabheda no. 27 in the Kāmika's order; 9 upabhedas in the Kāmika's list
**Availability:** digitized-original

No. 27 of the 28 Śaiva Siddhānta mūlāgamas (Rudrabheda). According to the Kāmika it was first received by Devavibhava (Kiraṇa: Devapitṛ), then the sage Saṃvartaka. The Kāmika assigns it 9 upabheda(s): Gāruḍa, Nairṛta, Nīla, Rūkṣa, Bhānuka, Dhenuka, Prabuddha, Buddha, Kāla. A dialogue in which Garuḍa questions Śiva. An old Nepalese palm-leaf manuscript is dated 924 CE (scholarly report). Its vidyāpāda (12 paṭalas: paśu, māyā, karma, pati, śaktipāta, dīkṣā, mantra, bhuvana, śivatattva, tantrāvatāra, mātṛkā, yantra) is an early statement of Siddhānta doctrine.

## Teachings (13: skeleton 13)

### 1.12-13 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-1-12-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Lord tells Garuḍa: hear the great tantra called Kiraṇa, which gives the bliss of supreme nectar, favours all, is pure, clear and illuminates the categories; it sets out the investigation of the knowledge of the bound soul, the bonds and the Lord (paśu, pāśa, pati), is endowed with ritual (kriyā) and conduct (caryā), and bears yoga and the abundance of its powers.

### 1.15-17 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-1-15-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The bound soul (paśu) is eternal, formless, ignorant, inactive, without qualities, without lordship, pervasive, lodged within māyā and intent on the means of experience. To this impure soul comes the connection of kalā (the printed text reads 'from Śiva'); by it its consciousness is stirred up; vidyā makes its objects known; coloured by rāga and by prakṛti made of the guṇas, and through connection with the host of instruments beginning with buddhi, the soul is bound.

### 5.1-8 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-5-1-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Garuḍa asks why, if Śiva's power is always present in the soul, bondage does not always end, and what 'the time' of the descent is. The Lord answers that words are used figuratively: as the cutting of bonds is spoken of, so the 'descent of power' (śaktinipāta) in the Śiva-scripture is figurative; as the sudden fall of a thing causes fear, the descent of power is said to cause fear of worldly existence. As a teacher wakes sleeping pupils with a stick, so Śiva by his power awakens those asleep in the sleep of delusion; when there is knowledge of one's own nature it is said to have 'fallen'. Its time is the equality of karma (karmaṇaḥ tulyatā).

### 5.9-13 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-5-9-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Asked how that equality is known, the Lord says that at whatever time there is subtle equality, then the power quickly awakens (the soul) to its own nature by the force of the signs of awakening; where one portion of karma is greater it gives its experience first. Because excess and deficiency are absent, the soul goes to Śakti: this is to be understood as the 'descent' (pāta), and its devotion (bhakti) is distinctive.

### 5.14-20 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-5-14-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Because of the soul's beginningless connection with karma, Śiva waits for the time, as one aiming at a moving target waits for a moment; were there no such equality, all would be liberated at once. Yet Śiva is the lord (prabhu) here: as the sun is called the opener of lotuses though they open only in their time, so, though the equality of karma is the time, the Lord is the cause of the descent of power.

### 6.1-4 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-6-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Garuḍa asks whether, since the classes beginning with the twice-born stand higher and lower, the rite (saṃskāra) gives no different fruit. The Lord: the rite is not of the caste (jāti) nor of the body but of the living being; if it were of the caste, initiating one person would initiate all of that caste; it cannot be of caste or body, since these are inert; because Śiva favours all — children, the foolish and those given to enjoyment — grace is to be given, and it is preceded by the rite.

### 6.5-8 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-6-5-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Garuḍa: if, as the tantra teaches, liberation is by the rite alone, ritual, knowledge, vows and the other means would not be causes. The Lord: the Lord grants his grace to beings as they are placed; some here are fit for ritual and their liberation is by that, others are fit for knowledge, others for conduct; for each, liberation as taught comes through being joined by the Lord, and initiation (dīkṣā) is held to be the cause of the means beginning with knowledge.

### 6.10-13 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-6-10-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

For women and others who are incapable, (the guru) should purify away the observances (samaya); there is no fault through ignorance, but knowingly it would be a great fault; hence their liberation is through the yoga of devotion in the Śaiva scripture. For the capable, the observances are not purified away; to them knowledge and the rest are to be revealed; otherwise the order laid down in the Śaiva scripture would be broken.

### 6.14-16 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-6-14-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Garuḍa: initiation is said to be done by separating the bonds, yet no separation is seen. The Lord: destruction of the bonds is accomplished by their paralysis (stobha) by perfected mantras, which are beyond thought — as poison is destroyed in a body, and as someone is accomplished merely by the recitation of a name, so by the chief mantras, even from afar, karma is destroyed here.

### 6.17-19 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-6-17-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Garuḍa: if all bonds are separated by initiation, how does the body remain once the goal is reached? The Lord: as the potter's wheel keeps turning after the pot is made, carried by the previous impulse, so this body remains; the karma of many births is burnt like seeds, the future is blocked, and that by which this (body) exists is (spent) through experience.

### 6.20-23 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-6-20-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Garuḍa: why is not even a slight sign seen in one freed from bonds? Devotion is said to be its sign, yet nowhere is a sign clear, and where clearly seen it proves inconstant. The Lord: its sign is steadiness of disposition (vāsanā-niṣṭhā) and absence of wavering in that (prescribed) action.

### 10.3-27 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-10-3-27"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Right after creation the Lord produced ten Śivas and, dividing the one knowledge, told it to them in as many texts: Kāmika to Praṇava, Yogaja to Sudhā, Acintya to Dīpta, Kāraṇa to Kāraṇa, Ajita to Suśiva, Sudīptaka to Īśa, Sūkṣma to Sūkṣma, Sahasraka to Kāla, Suprabha to Daśeśa and Aṃśumat to Aṃśu, each then passing through further teachers (e.g. Praṇava → Trikala → Hara). Having produced eighteen Rudras he taught them: Vijaya (Anādi), Pārameśa (Śrīrūpa), Niśvāsa (Daśārṇa), Prodgīta (Śūlin), Mukhabimba (Praśānta), Siddha (Bindu), Santāna (Śivaniṣṭha), Siṃha/Nārasiṃha (Saumyeśa), Candrabhāsa (Ananta), Bhadra (Sarvātman), Svāyambhuva (Nidhana), Vīraja (Tejas), Raurava (Brahmaṇeśa), Makuṭa (Śiva), this Kiraṇa (Devapitṛ), Lalita (Ālaya), Āgneya (Vyoman) and Saurabheya (Śiva); each was received in turn by a sage, god or gaṇa (e.g. the Niśvāsa by the Mountain-born goddess, the Kiraṇa by the sage Saṃvartaka, the Saurabheya by Mahākāla).

### 10.28-31 <a id="tea-kirana-tantra-10-28-31"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The division of the tantras has been told; a division within these divisions is called a sub-division (upabheda), very brief or extensive, (belonging to) sages, gods or gaṇas. (In answer to Garuḍa) the division is said to arise from differences of mind, of people's engagement and of the hearers; it is figuratively imagined: no difference of fruit is to be imagined here — only a difference of knowledge.
