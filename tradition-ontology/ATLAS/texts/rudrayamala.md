A late eastern tantra (the extant 'Uttaratantra' is distinct from the old Rudrayāmala cited in Kashmir) on the six centres, kumārī worship, the bhāvas and siddhis; its seventeenth paṭala tells how Vasiṣṭha, failing to win the Goddess by Vedic austerity, was sent to Mahācīna, where the Buddha taught him the Kula way (17.105-140).

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 17.105-124 <a id="tea-rudrayamala-17-105-124"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Vasiṣṭha, son of Brahmā, practised yoga and austerity for ages in a lonely place but had no direct vision of the Mahāvidyā; angered, he was about to curse her; she appeared and told him he did not know her worship or the Kula āgama — how could yoga practice alone win the sight of her feet? — and sent him to Mahācīna, the Buddhist land, where by seeing her 'great disposition' he would become a great Siddha knowing her Kula.

### 17.125-140 <a id="tea-rudrayamala-17-125-140"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

In Mahācīna Vasiṣṭha, seeing Siddhas drinking and with women beyond the Veda, asks the Buddha how siddhi can come without Vedic rites; the Buddha teaches him the Kula path: first one must be pure, steady, discriminating, established in the paśu disposition, alone, free of desire and anger, practising restraint and yoga, relying on the Vedic path and learned in its meaning, holding the breath and dissolving the mind in the breath's path, advancing gradually.
