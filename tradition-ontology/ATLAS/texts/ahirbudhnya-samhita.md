# Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā

**Alternate titles:** Ahirbudhnya, Tantrarahasya (colophon title)
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Pāñcarātra](../lineages/pancaratra.md)
**Genre:** saṃhitā (Pāñcarātra)
**Attribution:** tradition: revealed by Bhagavān (Nārāyaṇa/Vāsudeva); one of the traditional 108 saṃhitās; confidence: moderate
**Structure:** 60 adhyāyas
**Availability:** digitized-original

A 60-chapter Pāñcarātra saṃhitā in which Ahirbudhnya (Śiva) teaches Nārada, centred on Sudarśana as Viṣṇu's will-power (saṅkalpa). It defines the six guṇas (2.53–62), derives the three vyūhas from pairs of guṇas (5.16–19), lists 39 vibhavas (5.50–56), summarises the Sāṃkhya Ṣaṣṭitantra as Kapila's Vaiṣṇava teaching (12.18–30), calls the Lord's grace śaktipāta (14.28–34) and defines six-limbed surrender (37.27–29).

## Teachings (6: skeleton 6)

### 2.53-62 <a id="tea-ahirbudhnya-samhita-2-53-62"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Free from all pairs of opposites and devoid of all limiting adjuncts, the ṣāḍguṇya is the supreme Brahman, cause of all causes. Nārada asks what the ṣāḍguṇya is and how, if without qualities, it is called six-qualitied. Ahirbudhnya: it is called 'without qualities' with reference to the qualities of prakṛti, which do not touch it. Knowledge — non-inert, self-aware, eternal, all-penetrating — is the first quality, and it is both Brahman's nature and its quality; power (śakti) is its being the material cause of the world; lordship (aiśvarya) is its agency, full of independence; strength (bala) is its never tiring though ever making the world; virility (vīrya) is its undergoing no change though it is the material cause, also called 'not falling' (acyutatva); splendour (tejas) is its needing no auxiliary. These five are qualities of knowledge; knowledge alone is the supreme form of Brahman, the highest Self. That ṣāḍguṇya, the supreme Brahman, swelled by its own power, takes up the resolve 'may I be many' — that is Sudarśana.

> सर्वद्वन्द्वविनिर्मुक्तं सर्वोपाधिविवर्जितम् । षाड्गुण्यं तत् परं ब्रह्म सर्वकारणकारणम् ॥ २-५३ ॥

### 5.16-19 <a id="tea-ahirbudhnya-samhita-5-16-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The pure creation proceeds from the six qualities by three pairs: from the pair of knowledge and strength arises Hari's form as Saṅkarṣaṇa; from the union of lordship and virility the form called Pradyumna; from the pre-eminence of power and splendour Aniruddha's body. These vyūhas, made of power, are marked by the manifestation of their qualities; yet they are embodiments of all six qualities, the remaining two pairs following along in each.

### 5.50-59 <a id="tea-ahirbudhnya-samhita-5-50-59"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The vibhavas are thirty-nine, beginning with Padmanābha: Padmanābha, Dhruva, Ananta, Śaktyātman, Madhusūdana, Vidyādhideva, Kapila, Viśvarūpa, Vihaṅgama, Kroḍātman, Baḍabāvaktra, Dharma, Vāgīśvara, the one lying on the single ocean, the Lord as tortoise, Varāha, Nārasiṃha, the nectar-taker, Śrīpati, Kāntātman, the nectar-bearer, the conqueror of Rāhu, the slayer of Kālanemi, the taker of the Pārijāta, Lokanātha, Śāntātman, Dattātreya, the one lying on the banyan, the one-horned, the dwarf, Trivikrama, Nara, Nārāyaṇa, Hari, Kṛṣṇa, Rāma with the blazing axe, Rāma with the bow, Vedavid, Kalkin and the one lying in Pātāla. Their origin, activity, weapons, forms, stations, ornaments, garments and powers are all told in the Sātvata teaching.

### 12.18-30 <a id="tea-ahirbudhnya-samhita-12-18-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Lord's resolve in the form of Sāṃkhya, uttered long ago by the sage Kapila, is the tantra called Sāṃkhya with sixty divisions in two circles. The natural (prākṛta) circle has thirty-two: the tantras of Brahman, of Puruṣa, three of Śakti, Niyati and Kāla, three of the guṇas, of the Imperishable, of Prāṇa, of the Agent, of the Master, five of the knowledge-senses, five of the action-organs, five of the subtle elements and five of the elements. The modified (vaikṛta) circle has twenty-eight sections: five on duties, and sections on experience, conduct, five afflictions, three means of knowledge, discernment (khyāti), dharma, detachment, lordship, the guṇas, the marks (liṅga), views, scriptural tradition, suffering, powers, the 'dyed' (kāṣāya), conventions (samaya) and liberation. This sixty-fold Sāṃkhya is made of Hari's Sudarśana.

### 14.28-34 <a id="tea-ahirbudhnya-samhita-14-28-34"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

As the soul whirls in the wheel of transmigration by its own actions, afflicted by suffering, a certain compassion of Viṣṇu arises. That compassion is the fifth power already mentioned, of the form of Viṣṇu's will — the power of grace; those who hold to the āgamas call it Viṣṇu's descent of power (śaktipāta). When the soul is accepted by Viṣṇu with this power of grace, made of Sudarśana, a rain of compassion, and is looked upon by him, it attains equality of karmas (karmasāmya); that descent of power lifts the soul out of saṃsāra, the two karmas, being equal, falling silent — as robbers stop plundering and stand aside when the traveller has given up his belongings.

### 37.25-29 <a id="tea-ahirbudhnya-samhita-37-25-29"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Whatever desired thing is not attainable by other means — what the seeker of liberation cannot reach by Sāṃkhya, by yoga or by devotion — the supreme abode from which there is no return, and the supreme Self, Puruṣottama, are attained by surrender (nyāsa) alone. Knowers of the Veda say it is sixfold: the resolve to do what is agreeable, the avoidance of what is disagreeable, the faith that 'he will protect', the choosing (of him) as protector, the laying down of oneself, and helplessness — this is the sixfold refuge (śaraṇāgati). In this context the word 'śaraṇa', used for 'means' and for 'house' and 'protector', denotes the means alone.

> आनुकूल्यस्य सङ्कल्पः प्रातिकूल्यस्य वर्जनम् । रक्षिष्यतीति विश्वासो गोप्तृत्ववरणं तथा ॥ आत्मनिक्षेपकार्पण्ये षड्विधा शरणागतिः ।
