Haribhadra's Prakrit hundred verses on yoga: yoga in the ultimate sense (niścaya) is the union of the three jewels in the soul, in the conventional sense (vyavahāra) their causes such as serving the teacher; the practitioner's qualification and the teacher's adapting of instruction to the student.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 2 <a id="tea-yogasataka-haribhadra-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the ultimate sense (niścaya), yoga is the connection of the three — right knowledge and the others — [in the soul], because it joins [the soul] to liberation; so taught the lord of yogins.

### 3-4 <a id="tea-yogasataka-haribhadra-3-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the conventional sense (vyavahāra), yoga is also the causes of these — serving the teacher, hearing the scripture according to one's capacity, and the rest — since the cause is spoken of as the effect.
