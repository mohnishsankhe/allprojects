### 2.12 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-ramanuja-2-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Lord declares that he, Arjuna and the kings never were not and never will not be: the plurality of eternal selves is real and taught by the Lord who knows the highest truth, and so is not due to ignorance or adjuncts.

### 7.16-18 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-ramanuja-7-16-18"></a>

Four kinds of virtuous people worship the Lord — the distressed seeking lost wealth, the seeker of wealth, the seeker of the self (kaivalya), and the wise one (jñānī) who seeks him alone; the wise one is dearest and is, the Lord says, his very self, for he cannot sustain himself without him.

### 13.2 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-ramanuja-13-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

'Know me also as the knower of the field in all fields': the Lord is the self of all knowers, who are his body; hence co-referential statements name the embodied soul together with the Lord its self.

### 15.16-18 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-ramanuja-15-16-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 18.4-11 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-ramanuja-18-4-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

True relinquishment (tyāga) in action is to give up the sense that one is the agent, the sense that the act is one's own, and desire for its fruit, regarding the Lord as the agent and the owner; relinquishing the acts themselves is not meant.

### 18.66 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-ramanuja-18-66"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Rāmānuja gives two readings of 'abandoning all dharmas, take refuge in me alone': (1) perform karma-, jñāna- and bhakti-yoga while abandoning the sense of agency, ownership and fruit, taking the Lord alone as the agent, object and means; (2) for one who feels unable to begin bhakti-yoga because of the sins obstructing it, or through lack of qualification, take refuge in the Lord in place of the expiations and he will free you from those sins — do not grieve.

### introduction <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-ramanuja-intro"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Nārāyaṇa, the Lord of Śrī, opposed to all evil and the sole abode of auspicious qualities, beyond the reach of Brahmā and the gods, descended as Kṛṣṇa to be accessible to all; in the Gītā, using Arjuna as occasion, he teaches bhakti-yoga, prepared by karma-yoga and jñāna-yoga.
