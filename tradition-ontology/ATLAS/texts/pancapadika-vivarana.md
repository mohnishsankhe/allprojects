Prakāśātman's sub-commentary on the Pañcapādikā, founding text of the Vivaraṇa school: Brahman is the locus and object of a positive (bhāva-rūpa) indescribable ignorance, established by perception, inference and presumption; the jīva is the reflection of Brahman, real and identical with its prototype; hearing is the principal means, enjoined by a restrictive injunction; the mahāvākya yields immediate knowledge.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### bhava <a id="tea-pancapadika-vivarana-bhava"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ignorance is a positive (bhāva-rūpa) entity, though indescribable, known through perception ('I am ignorant', 'I did not know anything' on waking from sleep), inference and presumption; it is removed by knowledge.

### locus <a id="tea-pancapadika-vivarana-locus"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ignorance has pure consciousness (Brahman) as both its locus and its object; the insentient cannot be the locus of ignorance and the jīva is itself a product of ignorance.

### reflection <a id="tea-pancapadika-vivarana-reflection"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The reflection (the jīva) is real and identical with the prototype (bimba), as the face in the mirror is the face itself; only the adjunct makes it appear different. Īśvara is the prototype consciousness.

### sravana <a id="tea-pancapadika-vivarana-sravana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Hearing (śravaṇa) — the ascertainment of the Upaniṣads' purport — is the principal means, enjoined by a restrictive injunction (niyama-vidhi); reflection and contemplation are its auxiliaries; the sentence itself produces immediate knowledge.
