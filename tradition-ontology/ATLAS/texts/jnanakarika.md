A short work in three chapters — on liberation (mokṣādhikāra), on dharma and adharma, and on conduct (caryā) — teaching that bondage is support (āśraya) and liberation is supportlessness, that dharma and adharma are two nooses to be cut with the sword of knowledge, and that the 'fourth' mind beyond the three guṇas is marked by nirvāṇa.

### 1.9-10 <a id="tea-jnanakarika-1-9-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

When the mind is agitated, attached to sense-objects, beings deluded by the impurity of Māyā wander in saṃsāra: this is the settled account of saṃsāra as the pairs.

### 1.12-16 <a id="tea-jnanakarika-1-12-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Liberation is free of all saṃsāra, unperturbed, settled, motionless, even and beyond the pairs, mindless and free of thought. Dharma rests on the mind (citta), and the supportless (nirāśraya) gives liberation; god, goddess and all else are beyond dharma and adharma; what rests on dharma and adharma is the bondage of saṃsāra — support is bondage, liberation is supportless.

### 2.6-9 <a id="tea-jnanakarika-2-6-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mind is of three kinds — tāmasa, restless and petty; rājasa, coming and going; sāttvika, joined to dharma and steady — and there is a fourth mind, free of the three guṇas, always marked by nirvāṇa, the best of all, beyond the senses and imperishable.

### 2.12-14 <a id="tea-jnanakarika-2-12-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

As long as there is restlessness the mind is unsteady; the unsteady is called 'being' (bhāva), the steady is the mark of liberation; when the mind is joined to thought it is restless, when mind goes beyond mind it is certain and steady; freed from all thought it is still in non-being.

### 2.20-23 <a id="tea-jnanakarika-2-20-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Consciousness and non-consciousness, imagining and cultivation, concentrations on the circles of sun, moon and fire, air and space, Brahmā, Rudra and the gods — all are called conceptions (kalpanā); those joined to conceptual knowledge wander like a water-wheel and fall quickly into saṃsāra; those in the highest state do not come here.

### 2.24-28 <a id="tea-jnanakarika-2-24-28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Dharma and adharma are the two nooses; cutting the double bond with the sword of knowledge, whither would yogīs go? Bound by that noose they rise and fall and enjoy the store of karma — heaven by dharma, hell and the rest by adharma; the pair dharma-adharma binds the three worlds, and when both are abandoned men are quickly released.
