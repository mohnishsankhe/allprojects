# Yoga Vāsiṣṭha

**Attribution:** tradition: composed by Vālmīki, author of the Rāmāyaṇa, and told to Bharadvāja: the dialogue in which Vasiṣṭha teaches Rāma, with Viśvāmitra present, at Daśaratha's court; scholarly: the vulgate is a Vedāntic revision of the Kashmiri Mokṣopāya (src:moksopaya), an anonymous 10th-c. philosophical poem; its non-dualism is close to but not identical with Śaṅkara's Advaita and shares vocabulary with Kashmiri Śaiva and Buddhist idealist thought; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: composed by Vālmīki; the teaching given by Vasiṣṭha to the young Rāma in the Tretā age; Scholarly account: the Kashmiri Mokṣopāya c. 10th c. CE (Slaje, c. 950); the Vedānta-oriented Yoga Vāsiṣṭha vulgate is a later revision (c. 11th-14th c.); older proposals ranged from the 6th to the 13th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 6 prakaraṇas: Vairāgya (dispassion, 33 sargas), Mumukṣu-vyavahāra (conduct of the seeker, 20), Utpatti (origination, 122), Sthiti (persistence, 62), Upaśama (quiescence, 93), Nirvāṇa (liberation, pūrvārdha 128 + uttarārdha 216). The text names itself Mokṣopāya and claims 32,000 verses (2.17.6); the printed vulgate has roughly 27,000-29,000
**Availability:** digitized-original

Vasiṣṭha teaches the despondent young Rāma that the world is a projection of mind within the one consciousness (cit), arising like a dream; that human effort (pauruṣa) guided by scripture and the good overrides fate, which is only past effort; that liberation comes by inquiry (vicāra) with the four gatekeepers (peace, inquiry, contentment, holy company), by the weakening of latent tendencies (vāsanā) and the stilling of mind and breath, through seven stages of knowledge to liberation while living. The teaching is carried by dozens of stories (Līlā, Karkaṭī, Lavaṇa, Śukra, Dāśūra, Prahlāda, Bali, Gādhi, Uddālaka, Suraghu, Vītahavya, Bhuśuṇḍa, Cūḍālā and Śikhidhvaja, the stone and others).
