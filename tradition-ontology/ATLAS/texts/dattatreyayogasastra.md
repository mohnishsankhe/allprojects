# Dattātreyayogaśāstra

`src:dattatreyayogasastra` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Yogaśāstra (of Dattātreya), Dattātreya's discourse on yoga
**Original title:** दत्तात्रेययोगशास्त्र
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md), `lin:datta-sampradaya`
**Genre:** yoga dialogue
**Authors:** 
  - [Dattātreya](../teachers/dattatreya.md) — role: revealer; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: taught by Dattātreya to the sage Sāṅkṛti in the Naimiṣa forest; scholarly: anonymous, probably composed in a Vaiṣṇava milieu, c. 13th c.; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Dattātreya's teaching to Sāṅkṛti; Scholarly account: c. 13th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** vulgate of 154 verses (sanskritdocuments/Kokaje); Mallinson's critical text c. 334 verses
**Availability:** digitized-original

The first text to teach a system it calls haṭhayoga. Dattātreya names four yogas — mantra, laya, haṭha and rāja (the best) — and four stages; he teaches the eight-limbed yoga 'known to Yājñavalkya and others' and the haṭha of 'Kapila and the others' (mahāmudrā, mahābandha, mahāvedha, khecarī, the three bandhas, viparītakaraṇī, vajrolī with amarolī and sahajolī). Success comes from practice to anyone — brāhmaṇa, śramaṇa, Buddhist, Jain, Kāpālika or materialist.

### 14-24 <a id="tea-dattatreyayogasastra-14-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Layayoga is the dissolution of mind, which arises through 'indications' (saṅketa); Ādinātha — Śiva — taught eight crore of them to his attendants on various mountains. Among them: meditating on the void day and night while standing, walking, sleeping and eating; fixing the gaze on the tip of the nose; meditating on the back of the head; fixing the gaze between the brows; absorption on the big toes; lying on the ground like a corpse, relaxed, in a solitary place. Whoever's mind dissolves by these or other indications — that is layayoga.

### 31-35 <a id="tea-dattatreyayogasastra-31-35"></a>

### 39-42 <a id="tea-dattatreyayogasastra-39-42"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Success comes to one who practises; how to one who does not? No success arises from merely reading śāstras. The shaven head, the staff, the ochre robe, reciting 'Nārāyaṇa' or 'namaḥ śivāya', the matted locks and ash, outer worship, worship of the twelve places — how can one without practice, or a cruel person, attain success? Wearing a garb is not the cause of success.

### 55-61 <a id="tea-dattatreyayogasastra-55-61"></a>

### 77-80 <a id="tea-dattatreyayogasastra-77-80"></a>

Thus through practice success arises in the beginning stage; then comes the ghaṭa stage for one who constantly practises the breath, when prāṇa and apāna, mind and breath, the individual self and the supreme self unite without opposition — this is the ghaṭa ('conjunction') stage known to yogins.

### 100-110 <a id="tea-dattatreyayogasastra-100-110"></a>

### 120-124 <a id="tea-dattatreyayogasastra-120-124"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Now the view of the siddhas such as Kapila (the haṭha of mudrās); the difference lies in practice, the fruit is the same. Mahāmudrā, taught by Bhairava: place the left heel at the perineum, stretch out the right leg and hold it firmly with the hands, set the chin on the chest, fill with breath, hold as able and release, on the left then the right side. Placing the stretched leg on the thigh is mahābandha; seated in it one gently strikes the buttocks (the vulgate calls this too mahābandha; it corresponds to mahāvedha).

### 129-132 <a id="tea-dattatreyayogasastra-129-132"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Viparītakaraṇī destroys all diseases; for its daily practitioner the digestive fire grows, so ample food must be provided — if food is scanty the fire burns (the body); sun above, moon below: head down and feet up for a moment on the first day, a little more each day; after six months wrinkles and grey hair are not seen; one who practises it daily for a watch knows yoga.
