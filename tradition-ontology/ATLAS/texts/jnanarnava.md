Śubhacandra's large Sanskrit treatise on meditation (c. 39–42 sections): the twelve reflections, the meditator and the place, the vows, the conquest of passions, breath-control (with warnings), and the four objects of meditation (piṇḍastha with the five concentrations, padastha on mantras and letters, rūpastha on the Arhat, rūpātīta on the formless Siddha), culminating in pure meditation.

### anupreksa <a id="tea-jnanarnava-anupreksa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The twelve reflections are the ladder to meditation: by contemplating impermanence, the lack of refuge, the round of births, aloneness and the rest the mind becomes detached and fit for meditation.

### asad-dhyana <a id="tea-jnanarnava-asad-dhyana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Meditation and mantra used to harm, attract, subjugate or kill others is evil meditation (asad-dhyāna); it belongs to the sorrowful and cruel meditations and binds karma.

### dhyatr <a id="tea-jnanarnava-dhyatr"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The meditator should be detached, of steady vows, free of the passions, knowing the realities, and should choose a solitary, undisturbed place — a mountain, cave, forest or temple — and a steady posture.

### padastha <a id="tea-jnanarnava-padastha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Word-based meditation contemplates the letters and sacred mantras — the vowels on a sixteen-petalled lotus, the syllable 'arhaṃ', the praṇava, the homage to the five supreme beings — as pure objects leading the mind inward.

### pindastha <a id="tea-jnanarnava-pindastha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In embodied meditation the yogin performs the five concentrations — earth, fire, wind, water and the essential — burning the lotus of the eight karmas with the fire arising from the syllable 'arhaṃ' and finally seeing his own self as the pure, omniscient Siddha.

### pranayama <a id="tea-jnanarnava-pranayama"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low · _restricted: summary only_

Breath-control may steady the body and reveal omens, but those who pursue it for powers fall into distress; it agitates the mind and is not the cause of liberation, which is reached by the mind's own stillness.

### rupastha-rupatita <a id="tea-jnanarnava-rupastha-rupatita"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Form-based meditation contemplates the omniscient Arhat in his glory; formless meditation contemplates the bodiless Siddha of pure consciousness, until meditator and meditated are not experienced as two.
