# Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā

`src:isvarapratyabhijna-karika` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Pratyabhijñāsūtra, ĪPK
**Original title:** ईश्वरप्रत्यभिज्ञाकारिका
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Pratyabhijñā (the philosophy of recognition)](../lineages/pratyabhijna.md), [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
**Genre:** kārikā
**Authors:** 
  - [Utpaladeva](../teachers/utpaladeva.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: Composed by Utpala, son of Udayākara, 'for the effortless attainment of people' (ĪPK 4.18).; scholarly: Utpaladeva, c. 925-975.; confidence: high
**Dates:** Scholarly account: c. 925-975 CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 4 adhikāras: jñāna (8 āhnikas, 88 kārikās), kriyā (4 āhnikas, 53), āgama (2 āhnikas, 31), tattvasaṅgraha (18) = 190 kārikās
**Availability:** digitized-original

Utpaladeva's verse exposition of the 'recognition of the Lord': in the section on knowledge he answers the Buddhist logician's denial of an enduring self (1.2) and shows that cognition, memory and exclusion require one conscious subject (1.3-1.4) whose essence is reflexive awareness (1.5); the section on action shows action and relation to be the Lord's; the section on scripture sets out the tattvas, the pramātṛs and the three malas (3.1-3.2); the summary section (4) closes with the simile of the unrecognized lover (4.17).
**Editions / translations:** 
  - kind: original; name: with Utpaladeva's Vṛtti (ed. and tr. R. Torella, 1994) - reference
**Commentaries on this text:** [Īśvarapratyabhijñākaumudī](isvarapratyabhijna-kaumudi.md), [Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī](isvarapratyabhijna-vimarsini.md), [Īśvarapratyabhijñāvivṛti (Ṭīkā)](isvarapratyabhijna-vivrti.md), [Īśvarapratyabhijñāvṛtti](isvarapratyabhijna-vrtti.md)

### 3.2.4-5 <a id="tea-isvarapratyabhijna-karika-3-2-4-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The loss of freedom by consciousness, and the unconsciousness of freedom: this is the twofold āṇava impurity, the loss of one's own nature.
