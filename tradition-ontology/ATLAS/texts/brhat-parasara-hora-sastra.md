A dialogue in which Parāśara teaches Maitreya the horā: creation from Viṣṇu and his three powers, the planets as avatāras of Janārdana who give beings the fruits of karma, planets and signs, the sixteen divisional charts, sign-aspects, the houses, special ascendants, kārakas, yogas, longevity and death-inflicting planets, a great many planetary-period systems (with the Viṃśottarī as chief), aṣṭakavarga, the five great men, renunciation yogas, curses from past births, and a long series of pacificatory rites (śānti) for planets and inauspicious births.

### 1.9-20 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-1-9-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Viṣṇu is one, unmanifest, beginningless, the Lord, pure sattva, without qualities yet endowed with the three; with one portion he creates and protects the whole world in play, while three quarters of him are immortal. As Vāsudeva he is both manifest and unmanifest; with his powers Śrī (sattva), Bhū (rajas) and Nīlā (tamas) he takes the forms Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha; from them come mahat, ahaṃkāra and Brahmā; the threefold ahaṃkāra yields the gods, the senses and the elements. With Śrī Viṣṇu protects, with Bhū Brahmā creates, with Nīlā Śiva devours.

### 1.21-24 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-1-21-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The supreme Self shines in all beings, and all this rests in the supreme Self. In all beings there are two portions, the portion of the individual soul (jīvāṃśa) and the portion of the supreme Self (paramātmāṃśa). The Sun and the other planets, and Brahmā, Kāma, Dveṣa and others, have the supreme Self's portion predominant, as do the powers Śrī and the rest; in others the jīva-portion predominates.

### 2.4-7 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-2-4-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 2.8-10 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-2-8-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Those in whom the supreme Self's portion is greater are called sky-goers (divine); those in whom the jīva-portion is greater are called jīvas. From the planets come forth the portions of the supreme Self as Rāma, Kṛṣṇa and the other avatāras, who merge back into them when their work is done; from their jīva-portions men and others are born, who also merge there and into the unmanifest.

### 32.1-4 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-32-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The significators beginning with the Self (ātmakāraka) are seven, from the Sun to Saturn, or eight including Rāhu (used when two planets have equal degrees; some always count eight). The ātmakāraka is the planet with the most degrees in its sign (then minutes, then seconds); the one with the least is the last kāraka; Rāhu's degrees are counted backward from thirty.

### 46.12-15 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-46-12-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Counting from Kṛttikā, three times over, the lords of the periods are the Sun, Moon, Mars, Rāhu, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury, Ketu and Venus; the remainder of the count from Kṛttikā to the birth-asterism divided by nine gives the first lord. A full life is 120 years, so in the Kali age the Viṃśottarī is the chief period-system. The years are, in order: 6, 10, 7, 18, 16, 19, 17, 7 and 20.

### 79.1-5 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-79-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The yoga by which people leave home for another religious order: when four or more strong planets are in one sign, renunciation arises according to the strongest — from the Sun a tapasvin, from the Moon a kāpālika, from Mars one wearing red cloth, from Mercury an ekadaṇḍin, from Jupiter a yati, from Venus a cakradhara, from Saturn a nirgrantha. If those planets are set in the Sun's rays, even though strong, the persons are not initiated but devoted to that order.

### 83.1-6 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-83-1-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Maitreya asks: the śāstras say there is no good destination for the sonless — by what sin is one sonless, how can astrologers know it from the ascendant, and what is the remedy? ### 84.1-3 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-84-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Asked for the worship that pacifies the planets' faults, Parāśara says: the planets beginning with the Sun — on them depend the happiness and unhappiness of all creatures in the world. ### 84.4-13 <a id="tea-brhat-parasara-hora-sastra-84-4-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The planets' images are to be made of copper, crystal, red sandalwood, gold (two), silver, iron, lead and bronze, or painted on cloth in their colours in their directions: the Sun two-armed on a lotus seat with lotuses in a seven-horsed chariot; the Moon white, in white, with a club, on a ten-horsed chariot; Mars four-armed, red, with spear, trident and club, on a ram; Mercury yellow, on a lion, with sword, shield and club; Jupiter and Venus yellow and white, four-armed, with staff, rosary and water-pot; Saturn dark blue, on a vulture, with trident, bow and arrow; Rāhu on a lion, fierce-faced, blue; the Ketus smoke-coloured on vultures — all crowned and granting boons.
