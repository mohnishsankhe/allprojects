Bhāskara II's 'crest-jewel of siddhāntas' in four parts — Līlāvatī (arithmetic), Bījagaṇita (algebra), Grahagaṇita (planetary computation) and Golādhyāya (the sphere) — with his own commentary Vāsanābhāṣya. The Golādhyāya teaches that the earth rests in space by its own power without other support and has a power of attraction.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### gola.bhuvanakosa.2 <a id="tea-siddhanta-siromani-gola-bhuvanakosa-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The earth has no other support: by its own power alone it stands fixed in space, and on its surface all around dwell for ever the whole world of gods, demons, men and dānavas.

### gola.bhuvanakosa.6 <a id="tea-siddhanta-siromani-gola-bhuvanakosa-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The earth has a power of attraction: a heavy thing in the sky, drawn by it toward itself, appears to fall; since the sky is the same all round, where could the earth itself fall?
