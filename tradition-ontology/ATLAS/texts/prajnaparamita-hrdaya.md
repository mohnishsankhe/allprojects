## Teachings (26: text-verified 24, skeleton 2)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-prajnaparamita-hrdaya-1-1"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence low

This is the imperial preface (title: 大明太祖高皇帝御製序, "preface composed by the Emperor Taizu of the Great Ming") to the sūtra, not part of Xuanzang's translation. It opens from the order of rulership (the three bonds and five constants, 三綱五常, backed by the five punishments) and says that the Buddha, born in the Western Regions, taught out of compassion and that what he expounded to his hearers was the nature-principle of the three bonds and five constants. It argues that the Buddha's teaching is real, not void: the emptiness the Heart Sūtra keeps speaking of is emptiness of appearances (相空), and apart from that emptiness what remains is original nature (本性). It lists six emptied appearances (of speech by the mouth, of form by the eye, of hearing by the ear, of smell by the nose, of taste by the tongue, of pleasure by the body) and says these are not the emptiness of true appearance but the emptiness of appearances made by deluded thought. It warns that clinging to empty appearance harms rulers and people, and says the sūtra is called the Heart Sūtra in order to remove evil thoughts of the mind and return to the right way; it closes by praising the good of the Buddha-dharma and "the marvel of form and emptiness" (色空之妙).

> 二儀久判，萬物備周，子民者君君，育民者法其法也。三綱五常以示天下，亦以五刑輔弼之。有等凶頑不循教者，往往有趨火赴淵之為，終不自省。是凶頑者，非特中國有之，盡天下莫不亦然。俄西域生佛，號曰釋迦，其為佛也，行深願重，始終不二。於是出世間脫苦趣為其效也，仁慈忍辱務明心以立命，執此道而為之，意在人皆在此利濟群生。今時之人罔知佛之所以，每云法空虛而不實，何以導君子、訓小人。以朕言之則不然。佛之教實而不虛，正欲去愚迷之虛，立本性之實，特挺身苦行，外其教而異其名，脫苦有情。昔佛在時，侍從聽從者皆聰明之士，演說者乃三綱五常之性理也。既聞之後，人各獲福。自佛入滅之後，其法流入中國，間有聰明者動演人天小果猶能化凶頑為善，何況聰明者知大乘而識宗旨者乎。如心經每言空不言實，所言之空乃相空耳。除空之外，所存者本性也。所以相空有六，謂口空說相，眼空色相，耳空聽相，鼻空嗅相，舌空味相，身空樂相。其六空之相又非真相之空，乃妄想之相為之空相，是空相愚及世人禍及今古，往往愈墮彌深不知其幾。斯空相，前代帝王被所惑而幾喪天下者，周之穆王，漢之武帝，唐之玄宗，蕭梁武帝，元魏主燾，李後主，宋徽宗。此數帝廢國怠政，惟蕭梁武帝、宋之徽宗以及殺身，皆由妄想飛升及入佛天之地。其佛天之地未嘗渺茫，此等快樂世嘗有之，為人性貪而不覺，而又取其樂人世有之者，何且佛天之地如。為國君及王侯者，若不作非為，善能保守此境，非佛天者何如。不能保守而偽為，用妄想之心即入空虛之境，故有如是，斯空相，富者被纏則婬欲並生喪富矣，貧者被纏則諸詐並作殞身矣，其將賢未賢之人被纏則非仁人君子也，其僧道被纏則不能立本性而見宗旨者也。所以本經題云心經者，正欲去心之邪念以歸正道，豈佛教之妄耶。朕特述此，使聰明者觀二儀之覆載，日月之循環，虛實之孰取，保命者何如。若取有道、保有方，豈不佛法之良哉！色空之妙乎！

### 2.1 <a id="tea-prajnaparamita-hrdaya-2-1"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Preface to the sūtra in this edition (heading: 般若波羅蜜多心經序). It says the nature of dharmas (法性) is boundless and cannot be measured by the mind, and suchness (真如) has no marks and cannot be conveyed by words; seeking widely through words and meanings is like looking for a form in a mirror, and quieting thought to contemplate emptiness is like fleeing one's shadow in the sun. The sūtra is compared to the great earth, from which everything arises, and the buddhas are said to point only to the one mind, on which every dharma depends. Simply to understand the mind-ground is called dhāraṇī (總持); to realize that dharmas do not arise is called wondrous awakening (妙覺); one moment of thought that transcends is not to be sought in elaborate treatises.

> 夫法性無邊，豈藉心之所度；真如非相，詎假言之所詮。是故眾生浩浩無窮，法海茫茫何極。若也廣尋文義，猶如鏡裏求形，更乃息念觀空，又似日中逃影。茲經喻如大地，何物不從地之所生，諸佛唯指一心，何法不因心之所立。但了心地，故號總持，悟法無生，名為妙覺。一念超越，豈在繁論者爾。

### 2.4 <a id="tea-prajnaparamita-hrdaya-2-4"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

"Śāriputra! These dharmas have the mark of emptiness: not arisen, not ceased; not defiled, not pure; not increasing, not decreasing. Therefore in emptiness there is no form, no feeling, perception, formations or consciousness; no eye, ear, nose, tongue, body or mind; no form, sound, smell, taste, touch or dharma; no eye-realm, and so on up to no mind-consciousness realm; no ignorance and no ending of ignorance, and so on up to no old age and death and no ending of old age and death; no suffering, origin, cessation or path; no knowledge and also no attainment."

### s6 <a id="tea-prajnaparamita-hrdaya-s6"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Here, Śāriputra, all dharmas have the mark of emptiness (śūnyatā-lakṣaṇa): unarisen, unceased, not stained, not free from stain, not deficient, not full. Therefore, Śāriputra, in emptiness there is no form, no feeling, no perception, no formations, no consciousnesses; no eye, ear, nose, tongue, body or mind; no form, sound, smell, taste, touch or dharma; no eye-realm, and so on up to no mind-realm.

### thesis <a id="tea-prajnaparamita-hrdaya-thesis"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Thesis of the sūtra proper (2.2-2.8), by the six marks: (1) opening and closing: it opens with Avalokiteśvara seeing the five aggregates as empty and crossing beyond all suffering (2.2), and closes with the mantra of the perfection of wisdom (2.6-2.8); (2) repetition: the series of "no ... no ..." (2.4) and the repeated identity of form and emptiness, extended to the four other aggregates (2.3); (3) novelty: that form is not other than emptiness and that in emptiness there is no aggregate, no sense-base, no link, no truth, no knowledge and no attainment (2.3-2.4); (4) stated result: no hindrance, no fear, nirvāṇa, and the buddhas' anuttarā-samyak-saṃbodhi through reliance on the perfection of wisdom (2.5); the mantra removes all suffering (2.6); (5) praise: the epithets of the mantra (2.6); (6) reasoned demonstration: little beyond the "therefore" (是故, 故知) steps from the emptiness of dharmas to the negations (2.4) and from non-attainment to fearlessness (2.5). The thesis: the five aggregates are empty, and all dharmas have the mark of emptiness (not arising, not ceasing, not defiled, not pure, not increasing, not decreasing), so that in emptiness none of the listed dharmas is found, neither knowledge nor attainment; because there is nothing to attain, the bodhisattva who relies on the perfection of wisdom is without hindrance and fear and finally reaches nirvāṇa, and the buddhas of the three times attain supreme awakening. The two prefaces (1.1, 2.1) belong to this edition, not to the sūtra proper.
