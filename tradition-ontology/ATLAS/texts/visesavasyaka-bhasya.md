Jinabhadragaṇi Kṣamāśramaṇa's great verse commentary (c. 3,600 gāthās) on the first (sāmāyika) chapter of the Āvaśyaka: a systematic treatment of the five kinds of knowledge, the standpoints, the debates of Mahāvīra with the eleven gaṇadharas (gaṇadharavāda), and the eight schisms including the Boṭika (Digambara) origin story.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### botika <a id="tea-visesavasyaka-bhasya-botika"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The eighth schism, the Boṭika: at Rathavīrapura, 609 years after the nirvāṇa, Śivabhūti, angered when his teacher Āryakṛṣṇa cut up the precious blanket a king had given him, heard the teacher describe the naked Jinakalpa practice, declared that this alone was true renunciation and went naked; his sister Uttarā followed, but a courtesan covered her, and he taught that women cannot be without clothes and therefore cannot be liberated. So began the Boṭikas.

### ganadharavada <a id="tea-visesavasyaka-bhasya-ganadharavada"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

When the brahmin Indrabhūti comes to Mahāvīra doubting that the soul exists, Mahāvīra shows that the soul is directly known through the awareness 'I', that doubt itself is a quality needing a possessor, and that the Vedic passages he misread in fact affirm it; the other ten gaṇadharas' doubts (on karma, the body, the elements, rebirth, bondage, gods, hells, merit, the other world and liberation) are resolved in turn.

### ganadharavada <a id="tea-visesavasyaka-bhasya-ganadharavada-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The debates of the gaṇadharas: eleven learned brāhmaṇas came to defeat Mahāvīra, each secretly doubting one thing — the soul, karma, the soul's difference from the body, the reality of the elements, rebirth in like form, bondage and liberation, gods, hell-beings, merit and demerit, the other world, and nirvāṇa. Mahāvīra named each one's doubt and resolved it, often by showing that the Vedic sentence behind it (such as 'vijñānaghana evaitebhyo bhūtebhyaḥ samutthāya') had been misread; each became his disciple.

### kevala-krama <a id="tea-visesavasyaka-bhasya-kevala-krama"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Following the scriptures, the omniscient knows and perceives in alternating moments, not simultaneously, since two cognitive activities (upayoga) cannot occur at once.
