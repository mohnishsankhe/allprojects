## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 1.4-5 <a id="tea-abhisamayalamkara-1-4-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The perfection of wisdom is set out in eight topics: knowledge of all aspects, knowledge of paths, knowledge of all (bases); full realization of all aspects, the culminating realization, the serial realization, the instantaneous realization, and the dharmakāya.

> prajñāpāramitāṣṭābhiḥ padārthaiḥ samudīritā / sarvākārajñatā mārgajñatā sarvajñatā tataḥ

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: practice, ultimate_

concepts: [The eight topics and seventy points of the Abhisamayālaṃkāra](../concepts/eight-topics-abhisamayalamkara.md) · teachers: [Maitreyanātha](../teachers/maitreyanatha.md)

### 1.13-16 <a id="tea-abhisamayalamkara-1-13-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The full realization of all aspects includes the aspects, the trainings, qualities and faults, characteristics, the aids to liberation and to penetration (the four stages of the path of preparation), the irreversible assembly, the equality of existence and peace, the purification of fields and skill in means; the culminating realization has signs, increase, stability, mental abiding, the four antidotes to conceptions on the paths of seeing and cultivation, and the uninterrupted concentration.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: advanced · types: practice_

teachers: [Maitreyanātha](../teachers/maitreyanatha.md)

### 1.18 <a id="tea-abhisamayalamkara-1-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The dharmakāya together with its activity is taught as fourfold: the natural body, the enjoyment body, the emanation body and the (wisdom) dharmakāya.

_level: ultimate · standpoint: divine · path: general · stage: realized · types: ultimate_

terms: [dharmakāya](../terms/dharmakaya.md) · concepts: [The three bodies of a buddha (Yogācāra)](../concepts/three-bodies-yogacara.md) · teachers: [Maitreyanātha](../teachers/maitreyanatha.md)

### 1.19 <a id="tea-abhisamayalamkara-1-19"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The arising of the mind (of awakening) is the wish for perfect awakening for the sake of others; it is explained, briefly and in detail, according to the sūtra.

> cittotpādaḥ parārthāya samyaksambodhikāmatā

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: general · stage: beginner (saṃbhāra-mārga) · types: ethics, practice_

terms: [bodhicitta](../terms/bodhicitta.md), [cittotpāda](../terms/cittotpada.md) · teachers: [Maitreyanātha](../teachers/maitreyanatha.md)

### 5.21 <a id="tea-abhisamayalamkara-5-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There is nothing here to be removed and nothing to be added; reality should be seen as it really is; one who sees reality is liberated.

> nāpaneyamataḥ kiñcit prakṣeptavyaṃ na kiṃcana / draṣṭavyaṃ bhūtato bhūtaṃ bhūtadarśī vimucyate

_level: ultimate · standpoint: absolute · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: ultimate, karma-liberation_

teachers: [Maitreyanātha](../teachers/maitreyanatha.md)


_Notes: One of the 'five Dharmas of Maitreya' in the Tibetan list (Abhisamayālaṃkāra, Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra, Madhyāntavibhāga, Dharmadharmatāvibhāga, Ratnagotravibhāga). Chinese tradition instead attributes the Yogācārabhūmi to Maitreya and the Ratnagotravibhāga to Sāramati. Its own affiliation is contested: Haribhadra reads it as Yogācāra-Madhyamaka; Tibetan scholastics as Madhyamaka._
