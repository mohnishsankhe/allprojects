# Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta

`src:satipatthana-sutta` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Discourse on the Establishments of Mindfulness
**Language:** Pali
**Family:** ascetic
**Lineages:** [Early Buddhism (the Dhamma-Vinaya of the Nikāyas)](../lineages/early-buddhism.md), [Theravāda](../lineages/theravada.md)
**Genre:** sutta
**Part of:** [Majjhima Nikāya](majjhima-nikaya.md)
**Location in parent:** MN 10
**Availability:** digitized-original

The four establishments of mindfulness — contemplation of body (breathing, postures, clear comprehension, the parts, the elements, nine charnel-ground stages), feelings, mind and dhammas (hindrances, aggregates, sense bases, awakening factors, truths) — with the refrain of internal/external and arising/passing contemplation, 'the direct path' to purification and nibbāna.

### 2.1 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-2-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This is the direct path (ekāyana) for the purification of beings, for surmounting sorrow and lamentation, for the passing away of pain and grief, for attaining the method, for realising nibbāna: the four establishments of mindfulness.

### 3.1-3.5 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-3-1-3-5"></a>

### 4 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-4"></a>

### 5.1-5.3 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-5-1-5-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 14-31 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-14-31"></a>

### ch1 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-ch1"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

MN 10 in its own order: the frame (mn10:1); the announcement of the one-way path and the four establishments (mn10:2-3); contemplation of the body in fourteen sections (mn10:4-31: breath, postures, clear comprehension, repulsiveness, elements, nine charnel-ground contemplations), each closed by the refrain; contemplation of feelings (mn10:32-33); of mind (mn10:34-35); of dhammas in five sections (mn10:36-45: hindrances, aggregates of clinging, sense bases, awakening factors, four noble truths); the promised fruit for seven years down to seven days (mn10:46); the closing repetition, colophon and vagga mnemonic (mn10:47-48). Its purpose, as it states it, is the purification of beings and the realisation of nibbāna.

### mn10:3 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-mn10-3"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

The Buddha asks which four and answers. Here a monk abides contemplating the body in the body (kāye kāyānupassī), feelings in feelings (vedanāsu vedanānupassī), mind in mind (citte cittānupassī), and dhammas in dhammas (dhammesu dhammānupassī): in each case ardent (ātāpī), clearly comprehending (sampajāna) and mindful (satimā), having put away covetousness and displeasure (abhijjhādomanassa) with regard to the world. ### mn10:4 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-mn10-4"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

First section on the body, the breath (ānāpāna). The Buddha asks how a monk abides contemplating the body in the body. Here a monk, gone to the forest, or to the root of a tree, or to an empty hut, sits down, folding his legs crosswise, setting the body upright, and establishing mindfulness in front (parimukhaṃ satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā). Mindful he breathes in; mindful he breathes out. Breathing in long he knows 'I breathe in long'; breathing out long he knows 'I breathe out long'; likewise short in and short out. He trains: 'I shall breathe in (out) experiencing the whole body (sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī)'; he trains: 'I shall breathe in (out) calming the bodily formation (passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ)'. As a skilled turner or turner's apprentice, making a long turn knows 'I make a long turn', and a short turn 'I make a short turn', so the monk knows long and short breaths and trains in the two further steps.

> 1. Kāyānupassanā 1.1. Kāyānupassanāānāpānapabba Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā nisīdati, pallaṅkaṁ ābhujitvā, ujuṁ kāyaṁ paṇidhāya, parimukhaṁ satiṁ upaṭṭhapetvā. So satova assasati, satova passasati. Dīghaṁ vā assasanto ‘dīghaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, dīghaṁ vā passasanto ‘dīghaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti, rassaṁ vā assasanto ‘rassaṁ assasāmī’ti pajānāti, rassaṁ vā passasanto ‘rassaṁ passasāmī’ti pajānāti. ‘Sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘sabbakāyapaṭisaṁvedī passasissāmī’ti sikkhati. ‘Passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ assasissāmī’ti sikkhati, ‘passambhayaṁ kāyasaṅkhāraṁ passasissāmī’ti sikkhati. ### mn10:46 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-mn10-46"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

The stated result. Whoever would develop (bhāveyya) these four establishments of mindfulness in this way for seven years may expect one of two fruits: final knowledge (aññā) in this very life; or, if there is a residue of clinging (upādisesa), non-return (anāgāmitā). The Buddha then says: let seven years be; the same holds for six years, five, four, three, two, one year; for seven months, six, five, four, three, two, one month, half a month; and for seven days. In each case the same two fruits may be expected (the text abbreviates the run of durations with …pe…).

> Yo hi koci, bhikkhave, ime cattāro satipaṭṭhāne evaṁ bhāveyya satta vassāni, tassa dvinnaṁ phalānaṁ aññataraṁ phalaṁ pāṭikaṅkhaṁ diṭṭheva dhamme aññā; sati vā upādisese anāgāmitā. Tiṭṭhantu, bhikkhave, satta vassāni. Yo hi koci, bhikkhave, ime cattāro satipaṭṭhāne evaṁ bhāveyya cha vassāni …pe… pañca vassāni … cattāri vassāni … tīṇi vassāni … dve vassāni … ekaṁ vassaṁ … tiṭṭhatu, bhikkhave, ekaṁ vassaṁ. Yo hi koci, bhikkhave, ime cattāro satipaṭṭhāne evaṁ bhāveyya satta māsāni, tassa dvinnaṁ phalānaṁ aññataraṁ phalaṁ pāṭikaṅkhaṁ diṭṭheva dhamme aññā; sati vā upādisese anāgāmitā. Tiṭṭhantu, bhikkhave, satta māsāni. Yo hi koci, bhikkhave, ime cattāro satipaṭṭhāne evaṁ bhāveyya cha māsāni …pe… pañca māsāni … cattāri māsāni … tīṇi māsāni … dve māsāni … ekaṁ māsaṁ … aḍḍhamāsaṁ … tiṭṭhatu, bhikkhave, aḍḍhamāso. Yo hi koci, bhikkhave, ime cattāro satipaṭṭhāne evaṁ bhāveyya sattāhaṁ, tassa dvinnaṁ phalānaṁ aññataraṁ phalaṁ pāṭikaṅkhaṁ diṭṭheva dhamme aññā sati vā upādisese anāgāmitāti.

### mn10:47 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-mn10-47"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Closing. The Buddha says: 'This is the one-way path (ekāyana) for the purification of beings … namely the four establishments of mindfulness (the same words as at the opening); it was said on account of this.' The Blessed One spoke thus, and the monks, glad, rejoiced in his words. The colophon reads: the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta, tenth; the first chapter, the Mūlapariyāya vagga, ends.

> ‘Ekāyano ayaṁ, bhikkhave, maggo sattānaṁ visuddhiyā sokaparidevānaṁ samatikkamāya dukkhadomanassānaṁ atthaṅgamāya ñāyassa adhigamāya nibbānassa sacchikiriyāya yadidaṁ cattāro satipaṭṭhānā’ti. Iti yaṁ taṁ vuttaṁ, idametaṁ paṭicca vuttan”ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṁ abhinandunti. Satipaṭṭhānasuttaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ dasamaṁ. Mūlapariyāyavaggo niṭṭhito paṭhamo.

### thesis <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-thesis"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Thesis by the six marks. The sutta teaches that the four establishments of mindfulness (contemplating body in body, feelings in feelings, mind in mind, dhammas in dhammas, ardent, clearly comprehending and mindful, without covetousness and displeasure for the world) are the one-way path to purification and nibbāna. Opening and closing (upakrama-upasaṃhāra): the same sentence opens and closes it (mn10:2 and mn10:47), with the theme announced at mn10:3. Repetition (abhyāsa): after each of the twenty-one sections the refrain (internal, external, both; arising and passing away; bare knowledge and recollection; independence; clinging to nothing) recurs, and the whole is closed by the phrase 'thus too he abides contemplating' (mn10:5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, 25, 31, 33, 35, 37, 39, 41, 43, 45). Novelty (apūrvatā): the sutta does not itself mark anything as new. Stated result (phala): one of two fruits, final knowledge in this life or non-return if there is a residue, expected within seven years, or seven months, or seven days (mn10:46). Praise (arthavāda): the opening claim (mn10:2, 47) that this is the one-way path for purification, overcoming sorrow, ending pain and realising nibbāna reads as both a doctrinal statement and praise; the sutta gives no further argument for it. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti): none; the sutta instructs and does not argue, apart from the similes (turner, sack of grain, butcher, charnel-ground bodies).
