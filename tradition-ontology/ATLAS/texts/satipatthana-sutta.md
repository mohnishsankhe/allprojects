The four establishments of mindfulness — contemplation of body (breathing, postures, clear comprehension, the parts, the elements, nine charnel-ground stages), feelings, mind and dhammas (hindrances, aggregates, sense bases, awakening factors, truths) — with the refrain of internal/external and arising/passing contemplation, 'the direct path' to purification and nibbāna.

## Teachings (16: skeleton 16)

### 2.1 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-2-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This is the direct path (ekāyana) for the purification of beings, for surmounting sorrow and lamentation, for the passing away of pain and grief, for attaining the method, for realising nibbāna: the four establishments of mindfulness.

> “Ekāyano ayaṁ, bhikkhave, maggo sattānaṁ visuddhiyā, sokaparidevānaṁ samatikkamāya, dukkhadomanassānaṁ atthaṅgamāya, ñāyassa adhigamāya, nibbānassa sacchikiriyāya, yadidaṁ cattāro satipaṭṭhānā.

### 3.1-3.5 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-3-1-3-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A monk abides contemplating the body in the body, feelings in feelings, mind in mind, dhammas in dhammas — ardent, clearly comprehending and mindful, having removed covetousness and grief for the world.

> Katame cattāro? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā, vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ; vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā, vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ; citte cittānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā, vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ; dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā, vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṁ.

### 4 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Gone to the forest, the root of a tree or an empty hut, sitting cross-legged, body erect, mindfulness established, he breathes in and out mindfully, knowing long and short breaths and training to experience the whole body and to calm the bodily formation, like a skilled turner knowing a long or short turn.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation, body-breath · stage: all · types: practice_

### 5.1-5.3 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-5-1-5-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The refrain: he abides contemplating the body internally, externally and both; contemplating its nature of arising, of passing away, and of both; or mindfulness that 'there is a body' is simply established to the extent needed for knowledge and mindfulness; he abides independent, not clinging to anything in the world.

> Iti ajjhattaṁ vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati, bahiddhā vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati, ajjhattabahiddhā vā kāye kāyānupassī viharati; samudayadhammānupassī vā kāyasmiṁ viharati, vayadhammānupassī vā kāyasmiṁ viharati, samudayavayadhammānupassī vā kāyasmiṁ viharati. ‘Atthi kāyo’ti vā panassa sati paccupaṭṭhitā hoti. Yāvadeva ñāṇamattāya paṭissatimattāya anissito ca viharati, na ca kiñci loke upādiyati.

### 6-9 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-6-9"></a>

He knows 'I am walking', 'standing', 'sitting', 'lying down' however the body is disposed; and he acts with clear comprehension in going and returning, looking ahead and aside, bending and stretching, wearing robes, eating and drinking, defecating, walking, standing, sitting, falling asleep, waking, talking and keeping silent.

### 10-11 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-10-11"></a>

### 14-31 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-14-31"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

As if seeing a corpse in a charnel ground — one, two or three days dead, bloated and festering; devoured by crows, vultures, dogs and worms; a skeleton with flesh and blood held by sinews; without flesh; bones scattered; bones bleached white; heaped up; crumbled to dust — he compares his own body with it: 'this body too is of the same nature, will be like that, is not exempt from that fate' (the nine charnel-ground contemplations).

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation · stage: intermediate · types: practice, death-dying_

concepts: [The three marks](../concepts/three-marks.md) · practices: [The nine charnel-ground contemplations](../practices/navasivathika.md), [Contemplation of the unlovely (aśubhā-bhāvanā)](../practices/asubha-bhavana.md), [Mindfulness of death (maraṇassati)](../practices/maranassati.md)

### 32-33 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-32-33"></a>

### 34-35 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-34-35"></a>

### 36-37 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-36-37"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Contemplating dhammas as the five hindrances: when sensual desire is present he knows it is present; when absent he knows it is absent; he knows how the unarisen arises, how the arisen is abandoned, and how the abandoned does not arise again in future — and likewise for ill will, dullness and drowsiness, restlessness and remorse, and doubt.

### 46-47 <a id="tea-satipatthana-sutta-46-47"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Whoever develops the four establishments of mindfulness in this way for seven years — or even for seven days — may expect one of two fruits: final knowledge here and now or, if there is a residue, non-return.
