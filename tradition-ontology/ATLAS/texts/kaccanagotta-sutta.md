## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 2-3 <a id="tea-kaccanagotta-sutta-2-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This world mostly depends on the duality of existence and non-existence. For one who sees the origin of the world as it is with right wisdom, there is no 'non-existence' regarding the world; for one who sees its cessation, no 'existence'. Not taking up the notion 'my self', he has no doubt that what arises is only suffering arising and what ceases is suffering ceasing; this is right view — the middle teaching of dependent origination.

### 3.1-3.3 <a id="tea-kaccanagotta-sutta-3-1-3-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'All exists' is one extreme; 'all does not exist' is the second extreme; not approaching either extreme, the Tathāgata teaches the Dhamma by the middle.

> ‘Sabbamatthī’ti kho, kaccāna, ayameko anto. ‘Sabbaṁ natthī’ti ayaṁ dutiyo anto. Ete te, kaccāna, ubho ante anupagamma majjhena tathāgato dhammaṁ deseti:
