# Vaiśeṣika Sūtra

**Alternate titles:** Kaṇāda-sūtra, Vaiśeṣikadarśana

**Language:** Sanskrit

**Attribution:** tradition: Composed by the sage Kaṇāda (Kaṇabhuj, Ulūka, Kāśyapa).; scholarly: A layered text transmitted in divergent recensions; the recension commented on by Candrānanda is older than that of Śaṅkara Miśra's Upaskāra, which contains sūtras absent from it (e.g. Upaskāra 1.1.4 listing the six categories).; confidence: moderate

**Availability:** digitized-original

The root text of Vaiśeṣika in ten books. It announces the explanation of dharma — that from which prosperity and the highest good result — and grounds the Veda's authority; lists the nine substances, the qualities and the five motions and defines substance, quality and motion; treats being, universals and particulars, the elements, time, space, the self (with its marks and plurality) and the atomic mind; atoms and perception; motions caused by the unseen (adṛṣṭa); yoga and liberation; ethics of giving, food and ritual observances; inherence; absence; perception, inference and memory; and the seers' knowledge.
