The fourth Aṅga, continuing the numerical arrangement from one to a hundred and then to very large numbers; it describes the contents of all twelve Aṅgas (including the lost Dṛṣṭivāda), the thirty-four supernatural excellences (atiśaya) of a Jina and the thirty-five qualities of his speech, and lists the great persons of the era.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 34 <a id="tea-samavayanga-34"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The thirty-four supernatural excellences (atiśaya) of the Buddhas (Jinas): among them hair and nails that do not grow, a body free of disease and sweat, breath fragrant as a lotus, invisible eating and evacuation, a wheel of dharma moving before them, a voice in Ardhamāgadhī understood by all beings in their own speech, and the absence of plague and famine around them.

### dvadasanga <a id="tea-samavayanga-dvadasanga"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Samavāyāṅga's appendix describes the twelve-limbed scripture limb by limb, including the Dṛṣṭivāda with its five parts and fourteen Pūrvas, and lists the illustrious persons of the age.
