Jñāneśvar's independent work on non-dual experience: after saluting Śiva and Śakti as one and praising his guru Nivṛtti, it argues that the words sat, cit and ānanda, the word itself, ignorance and even knowledge fail before the self-luminous reality, which sports as the world; it ends in a devotion in which God, devotee and worship are one.

## Teachings (7: skeleton 7)

### ch.1 <a id="tea-amrtanubhava-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Jñāneśvar salutes the god and goddess, parents of the world, who are one reality appearing as a pair: the god is the being of the goddess and the goddess the manifestation of the god; in their embrace the world arises and their duality itself is lost.

### ch.5 <a id="tea-amrtanubhava-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The words existence (sat), consciousness (cit) and bliss (ānanda) do not describe the reality: each only excludes its opposite — non-existence, insentience, sorrow — and when the opposites fall away the words fall with them.

### ch.6 (refutation of the word) <a id="tea-amrtanubhava-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The word (śabda), though it removes ignorance and points to the hidden, cannot make the Self known, for the Self is self-evident and needs no proof; scripture's power ends before it.

### ch.7 (refutation of ignorance) <a id="tea-amrtanubhava-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ignorance cannot exist in the self-luminous reality: if it existed it would be known, and what is known is not ignorance; searched for, it vanishes like darkness searched for with a lamp.

### ch.8 (refutation of knowledge) <a id="tea-amrtanubhava-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Knowledge too, as a state opposed to ignorance, dissolves: where there is no ignorance, 'knower' and 'knowing' have nothing to do, and what remains is neither.
