Ānandaghana's hymns (stavana) to the twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras in old Gujarati-Rajasthani (he completed twenty-two; later poets supplied the last two): the soul's love-longing for the Jina as its only beloved, the difficulty of the true path, and the six darśanas as the limbs of the Jina.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1 <a id="tea-anandaghana-covisi-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ṛṣabha, lord Jina, is my beloved; I want no other husband; once pleased he never leaves me — a love with no beginning and no end.

The path of the Jina is hard to see; with human eyes it cannot be found, and argument, tradition and scriptures quarrel over it; only the divine eye of experience reveals it.

### 21 <a id="tea-anandaghana-covisi-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The six darśanas are called the limbs of the Jina: Sāṃkhya and Yoga his two feet, the Buddhist and the Mīmāṃsaka his two hands, the Lokāyata [the belly], and the Jaina his head; whoever unites the six limbs is the true seeker.
