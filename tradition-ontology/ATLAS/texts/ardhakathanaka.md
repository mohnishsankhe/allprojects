Banārsīdās's Braj Hindi verse autobiography (1641) of the first fifty-five years of his life — 'half' of a full span — including his religious wanderings, his fall into a one-sided niścaya view after reading the Samayasāra, and his recovery of balance when taught the guṇasthānas by Rūpcand Pāṇḍe.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### adhyatma-crisis <a id="tea-ardhakathanaka-adhyatma-crisis"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Having received the Samayasāra with Rājamalla's commentary, Banārsīdās and his friends took only the ultimate standpoint, gave up rites, vows and restraint, and behaved without discipline — which he later judges a fall into one-sidedness.

### rupcand-episode <a id="tea-ardhakathanaka-rupcand-episode"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

When Pāṇḍe Rūpcand taught the Gommaṭasāra and the guṇasthānas, Banārsīdās says his doubt was removed and his mind became balanced, holding the pure self together with the stages of conduct.
