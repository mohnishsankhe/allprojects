Sakya Paṇḍita's polemical verse treatise distinguishing the prātimokṣa, bodhisattva and tantric vows and criticising practices he held to be inauthentic — among them a self-sufficient 'white panacea' Mahāmudrā outside empowerment, which he compared to Chinese (Hwashang) teaching, and various rites and doctrines of his day.

## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### ch.3.chinese <a id="tea-domsum-rabye-ch-3-chinese"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Mahāmudrā of the present day and the Great Perfection of the Chinese tradition differ only in name — 'descending from above' and 'ascending from below', 'simultaneous' and 'gradual' — not in meaning.

### ch.3.karpo-chigtub <a id="tea-domsum-rabye-ch-3-karpo-chigtub"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

A fool's meditation on Mahāmudrā usually becomes a cause for rebirth as an animal; if not, one is born in the formless realm, or else falls into the cessation of the śrāvakas; the 'white self-sufficient remedy' (a single realisation said to suffice alone) is not the Buddha's teaching.

### ch.3.mahamudra <a id="tea-domsum-rabye-ch-3-mahamudra"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Mahāmudrā taught by the Buddha is the wisdom born of empowerment and the meditative absorption of the two stages; apart from this, the Buddha did not teach any other Mahāmudrā.

### structure <a id="tea-domsum-rabye-structure"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The three vows — of individual liberation, of bodhisattvas and of the mantra vehicle — must each be understood and kept according to its own rites and rules; mixing their procedures, or practising rites not found in the scriptures, is not the dharma.
