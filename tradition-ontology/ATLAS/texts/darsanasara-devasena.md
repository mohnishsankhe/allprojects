Devasena's short Prakrit account (Vikrama 990 = 933 CE, at Dhārā) of the origin of 'false doctrines' (darśana) — Buddhism, Śvetāmbaras, Yāpanīyas, the Drāviḍa, Kāṣṭhā and Māthura saṅghas and others — each with a founder and a date; it also mentions Padmanandin (Kundakunda) receiving knowledge from Sīmandhara.

### sec.kundakunda <a id="tea-darsanasara-devasena-sec-kundakunda"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

If Padmanandin (Kundakunda) had not been enlightened by the divine knowledge received from Sīmandharasvāmin, how would the monks know the true path?

### sec.svetambara <a id="tea-darsanasara-devasena-sec-svetambara"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Śvetāmbara 'false doctrine' arose at Valabhī 136 years after the death of King Vikrama, during a famine, when Jinacandra, pupil of Śāntyācārya, taught that monks may wear clothes, that women can be liberated and that the omniscient eats.
