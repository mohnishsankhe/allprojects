### Amṛtaratnāvalī, on rūpa and svarūpa (section not recalled) <a id="tea-amrtaratnavali-rupa-and-svarupa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Every man and woman has an outward form (rūpa) and an inner divine nature (svarūpa): in the man Kṛṣṇa, in the woman Rādhā; the practitioner attributes (āropa) the svarūpa to the rūpa, and so human love is lifted into the love of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.

### Amṛtaratnāvalī, on the sahaja mānuṣa (section not recalled) <a id="tea-amrtaratnavali-sahaja-manusa-in-the-body"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The innate Man (sahaja mānuṣa) dwells in the body in a hidden place (the lotus and lake within); he is reached not by outward worship but by the guru-taught inner practice.

### Amṛtaratnāvalī, on the three stages (section not recalled) <a id="tea-amrtaratnavali-three-stages-pravarta-sadhaka-siddha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The path has three stages: the beginner (pravarta), who relies on the name (nāma) and the guru's mantra; the practitioner (sādhaka), who relies on emotion (bhāva); and the perfected (siddha), who rests in love (prema).
