The treatise that made suggestion (dhvani) the soul of poetry: besides the expressed meaning there is a 'further', implied meaning in great poets' speech (1.4), whose highest form is suggested rasa; the Rāmāyaṇa's grief become verse shows it (1.5); the Mahābhārata's principal rasa is śānta and its principal aim liberation (4.5 vṛtti).
**Commentaries on this text:** [Locana (Dhvanyālokalocana) of Abhinavagupta](dhvanyaloka-locana.md)

## Teachings (6: skeleton 6)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-dhvanyaloka-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The wise have long declared that suggestion (dhvani) is the soul of poetry; some deny it, some call it a secondary sense, some say its nature cannot be told: therefore we explain its nature to delight the hearts of the sensitive.

> kāvyasyātmā dhvanir iti budhair yaḥ samāmnātapūrvaḥ

### 1.4 <a id="tea-dhvanyaloka-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There is something else, an implied meaning (pratīyamāna), in the speech of great poets, which shines out beyond the familiar parts as beauty (lāvaṇya) shines in women beyond their limbs; the vṛtti divides it into suggested fact, suggested figure and suggested rasa, the last never directly expressible.

> pratīyamānaṃ punar anyad eva vastv asti vāṇīṣu mahākavīnām | yat tat prasiddhāvayavātiriktaṃ vibhāti lāvaṇyam ivāṅganāsu ||

### 1.5 <a id="tea-dhvanyaloka-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

That meaning is the soul of poetry: so of old, the first poet's grief (śoka), born of the separation of the pair of krauñca birds, became verse (śloka).

> kāvyasyātmā sa evārthas tathā cādikaveḥ purā | krauñcadvandvaviyogotthaḥ śokaḥ ślokatvam āgataḥ ||

### 1.13 <a id="tea-dhvanyaloka-1-13"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Where the meaning, or the word, subordinating its own sense, suggests that (implied) meaning, the wise call that kind of poetry dhvani. The vṛtti: the first learned men are the grammarians, since grammar is the root of all sciences; they call the sounds that manifest the sphoṭa 'dhvani', and following them the poeticians call the suggesting word and meaning, and the suggested, dhvani.

### 3.26 vṛtti <a id="tea-dhvanyaloka-3-26-vrtti"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Śānta is a rasa, marked by the full development of the happiness that comes from the destruction of craving (tṛṣṇākṣaya-sukha), which, as the Mahābhārata verse says, sensual pleasure and heavenly happiness do not equal by a sixteenth part.

### 4.5 vṛtti <a id="tea-dhvanyaloka-4-5-vrtti"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the Mahābhārata, which has the form of a śāstra and of poetry, the principal rasa is śānta and the principal aim of man is liberation; the sage, by ending the story with the sad destruction of the Vṛṣṇis and the Pāṇḍavas, shows that he intends dispassion; the other rasas are subordinate to it.
