The central scripture of the Ekaśaraṇa tradition: Śaṅkaradeva's narrative songs for congregational singing, retelling the Bhāgavata — the avatāras, Kṛṣṇa's childhood and deeds, the rāsa, the story of Ajāmila and the power of the name, the offences against the name, the refutation of other paths — and praising the name of Kṛṣṇa as the one refuge.

### Ajāmilopākhyāna section <a id="tea-kirtana-ghosa-ajamila"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ajāmila, a fallen brahmin, called out to his son Nārāyaṇa at death and was saved from Yama's messengers: the name saves even when uttered unknowingly.

### Nāmāparādha section <a id="tea-kirtana-ghosa-namaparadha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The section on offences against the name warns against slighting devotees, holding other gods equal to Kṛṣṇa and sinning on the name's strength — yet the name itself, sung constantly, removes even these offences.

### Pāṣaṇḍa-mardana section <a id="tea-kirtana-ghosa-pasanda-mardana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The refutation of heretics: rites, sacrifices, pilgrimages and the worship of other gods do not save in the Kali age; the name of Kṛṣṇa is the one sure refuge and the true purport of the Vedas.
