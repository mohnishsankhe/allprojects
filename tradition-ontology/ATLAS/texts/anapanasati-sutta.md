## Teachings (51: skeleton 5, text-verified 46)

### 15.2-15.4 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-15-2-15-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mindfulness of breathing developed and cultivated fulfils the four establishments of mindfulness; these fulfil the seven factors of awakening; these fulfil true knowledge and liberation.

> Ānāpānassati, bhikkhave, bhāvitā bahulīkatā cattāro satipaṭṭhāne paripūreti. Cattāro satipaṭṭhānā bhāvitā bahulīkatā satta bojjhaṅge paripūrenti. Satta bojjhaṅgā bhāvitā bahulīkatā vijjāvimuttiṁ paripūrenti.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation, body-breath · stage: all · types: practice_

### 17-22 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-17-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The monk, gone to the forest, the root of a tree or an empty hut, sits cross-legged, body erect, mindfulness established, and ever mindful breathes in and out: knowing long and short breaths; training to breathe experiencing the whole body, calming the bodily formation; experiencing rapture, bliss, the mental formation, calming the mental formation; experiencing the mind, gladdening it, concentrating it, liberating it; contemplating impermanence, fading away, cessation and relinquishment — the sixteen steps.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation, body-breath · stage: all · types: practice_

### 23-28 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-23-28"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

How each tetrad fulfils one establishment of mindfulness: the first contemplates the body ('in-and-out breathing is a certain body among bodies'), the second feelings ('close attention to breathing is a certain feeling'), the third mind ('there is no development of mindfulness of breathing for one who is muddled'), the fourth dhammas, looking on with equanimity after seeing with wisdom the abandoning of covetousness and grief.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: analytic · path: meditation, body-breath · stage: all · types: practice_

### 26.6 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-26-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 29-43 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-29-43"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When the four establishments of mindfulness are developed, the seven factors of awakening arise in sequence — mindfulness, investigation of dhammas, energy, rapture, tranquillity, concentration, equanimity — each supported by seclusion, dispassion and cessation, ripening in relinquishment; so developed they fulfil true knowledge and liberation.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation · stage: advanced · types: practice, karma-liberation_

terms: [bojjhaṅga](../terms/bojjhanga.md) · concepts: [The seven awakening factors (satta bojjhaṅgā)](../concepts/seven-bojjhangas.md)

### mn118:1 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-mn118-1"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Setting. Thus have I heard. At one time the Blessed One was staying at Sāvatthī, in the Eastern Park (Pubbārāma), in the stilt longhouse of Migāra's mother (Migāramātupāsāda), together with many well-known senior disciples (therā sāvakā): the venerables Sāriputta, Mahāmoggallāna, Mahākassapa, Mahākaccāyana, Mahākoṭṭhika, Mahākappina, Mahācunda, Anuruddha, Revata and Ānanda, and other well-known senior disciples.

> Evaṁ me sutaṁ— ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde sambahulehi abhiññātehi abhiññātehi therehi sāvakehi saddhiṁ— āyasmatā ca sāriputtena āyasmatā ca mahāmoggallānena āyasmatā ca mahākassapena āyasmatā ca mahākaccāyanena āyasmatā ca mahākoṭṭhikena āyasmatā ca mahākappinena āyasmatā ca mahācundena āyasmatā ca anuruddhena āyasmatā ca revatena āyasmatā ca ānandena, aññehi ca abhiññātehi abhiññātehi therehi sāvakehi saddhiṁ.

### mn118:4 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-mn118-4"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

The Buddha says: 'I am satisfied (āraddhosmi) with this practice (paṭipadā); my mind is satisfied with this practice. ### mn118:8 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-mn118-8"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

The Buddha praises the assembly: it is without chatter (apalāpa) and without idle talk (nippalāpa), pure and established in the essence (sāra). Such a community, such an assembly, is worthy of gifts (āhuneyya), of hospitality (pāhuneyya), of offerings (dakkhiṇeyya), of reverential greeting (añjalikaraṇīya), the unsurpassed field of merit (puññakkhetta) for the world. A little given to such an assembly becomes much, and much becomes more. Such an assembly is rare for the world to see; it would be worth going many leagues (yojana), carrying a provision-bag, to see it.

> “Apalāpāyaṁ, bhikkhave, parisā; nippalāpāyaṁ, bhikkhave, parisā; suddhā sāre patiṭṭhitā. Tathārūpo ayaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhusaṅgho; tathārūpā ayaṁ, bhikkhave, parisā yathārūpā parisā āhuneyyā pāhuneyyā dakkhiṇeyyā añjalikaraṇīyā anuttaraṁ puññakkhettaṁ lokassa. Tathārūpo ayaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhusaṅgho; tathārūpā ayaṁ, bhikkhave, parisā yathārūpāya parisāya appaṁ dinnaṁ bahu hoti, bahu dinnaṁ bahutaraṁ. Tathārūpo ayaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhusaṅgho; tathārūpā ayaṁ, bhikkhave, parisā yathārūpā parisā dullabhā dassanāya lokassa. Tathārūpo ayaṁ, bhikkhave, bhikkhusaṅgho; tathārūpā ayaṁ, bhikkhave, parisā yathārūpaṁ parisaṁ alaṁ yojanagaṇanāni dassanāya gantuṁ puṭosenāpi.

### mn118:15 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-mn118-15"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

The Buddha declares that mindfulness of breathing (ānāpānassati), when developed (bhāvitā) and cultivated (bahulīkatā), is of great fruit and great benefit. Developed and cultivated, it fulfils the four establishments of mindfulness; the four establishments of mindfulness, developed and cultivated, fulfil the seven awakening factors; the seven awakening factors, developed and cultivated, fulfil knowledge and liberation (vijjāvimutti).

> Ānāpānassati, bhikkhave, bhāvitā bahulīkatā mahapphalā hoti mahānisaṁsā. Ānāpānassati, bhikkhave, bhāvitā bahulīkatā cattāro satipaṭṭhāne paripūreti. Cattāro satipaṭṭhānā bhāvitā bahulīkatā satta bojjhaṅge paripūrenti. Satta bojjhaṅgā bhāvitā bahulīkatā vijjāvimuttiṁ paripūrenti.

Here a monk develops the mindfulness awakening factor supported by seclusion (vivekanissita), by dispassion (virāganissita) and by cessation (nirodhanissita), ripening in relinquishment (vossaggapariṇāmī). The text abbreviates (…pe…): he develops the investigation-of-dhammas, energy, rapture, tranquillity and concentration awakening factors, and the equanimity awakening factor, in the same way.

> Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu satisambojjhaṅgaṁ bhāveti vivekanissitaṁ virāganissitaṁ nirodhanissitaṁ vossaggapariṇāmiṁ. Dhammavicayasambojjhaṅgaṁ bhāveti …pe… vīriyasambojjhaṅgaṁ bhāveti … pītisambojjhaṅgaṁ bhāveti … passaddhisambojjhaṅgaṁ bhāveti … samādhisambojjhaṅgaṁ bhāveti … upekkhāsambojjhaṅgaṁ bhāveti vivekanissitaṁ virāganissitaṁ nirodhanissitaṁ vossaggapariṇāmiṁ.

### mn118:43 <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-mn118-43"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence high

Thus developed and cultivated, the seven awakening factors fulfil knowledge and liberation (vijjāvimutti). The Blessed One spoke thus, and the monks, glad, rejoiced in his words. The colophon reads: the Ānāpānassati Sutta, eighth.

> Evaṁ bhāvitā kho, bhikkhave, satta bojjhaṅgā evaṁ bahulīkatā vijjāvimuttiṁ paripūrentī”ti. Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṁ abhinandunti. Ānāpānassatisuttaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ aṭṭhamaṁ.

### thesis <a id="tea-anapanasati-sutta-thesis"></a>
`text-verified` · confidence moderate

Thesis by the six marks. The sutta teaches mindfulness of breathing, in sixteen steps in four tetrads, as a practice which, developed and cultivated, is of great fruit and benefit and fulfils in turn the four establishments of mindfulness, the seven awakening factors, and knowledge and liberation (vijjāvimutti). Opening and closing (upakrama-upasaṃhāra): it opens with the community's advancement and the announcement of the chain (mn118:4, 15) and closes on its last link (mn118:41-43). Repetition (abhyāsa): each link is asked in the same form ('how developed, how cultivated ... fulfils'), and answered by the same formula (mn118:16, 22-23, 28-29, 40-41, 43); the awakening-factor sequence is repeated for each foundation (mn118:30-39). Novelty (apūrvatā): the sutta does not itself mark anything as new. Stated result (phala): great fruit and great benefit (mn118:15, 22), fulfilment of the four establishments, of the seven factors and of knowledge and liberation (mn118:28, 40, 43). Praise (arthavāda): the praise of the community and the list of practices its monks are engaged in (mn118:8-14) frame the teaching; the praise is of the assembly, not of the practice. Reasoned demonstration (upapatti): the mapping of each tetrad to a foundation is argued by the identification of the breaths as 'a body among bodies' and careful attention to the breaths as 'a feeling among feelings' (mn118:24-25), and the sequence of the seven factors is a conditioned sequence, each arising in dependence on the previous (mn118:30-36). The sutta sets out place and posture at mn118:17, and states its own condition at mn118:26: it does not speak of mindfulness of breathing for one of muddled mindfulness and without clear comprehension.
