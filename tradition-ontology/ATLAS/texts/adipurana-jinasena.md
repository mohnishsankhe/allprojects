Jinasena's Sanskrit purāṇa (first part of the Mahāpurāṇa, completed by Guṇabhadra) on the previous births and life of Ṛṣabha, who taught humanity the six means of livelihood, and of his sons Bharata and Bāhubali; Bharata's institution of the brāhmaṇas as Jain householders and the Jain life-cycle rites (kriyā).

### 16 <a id="tea-adipurana-jinasena-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Ṛṣabha, as the first king when the wish-fulfilling trees failed, taught the six means of livelihood — arms, writing, agriculture, learning (or arts), trade and crafts — and ordered society into kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras by occupation.

### 38 <a id="tea-adipurana-jinasena-38"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Bharata, wishing to honour the virtuous among the laity, had grass sprouts strewn on the path to his palace; those who would not walk on the living sprouts he made the brāhmaṇas (dvija), invested with the sacred thread as a mark of their vows, and taught them the life-cycle rites (kriyā) of the Jain householder.

### ch.4 <a id="tea-adipurana-jinasena-ch-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Some foolish men say a creator made the world; but if he made it, where was he before, what material did he use, and why would a perfect being create a world of suffering? The world is uncreated, like time, without beginning or end, made of the substances.
