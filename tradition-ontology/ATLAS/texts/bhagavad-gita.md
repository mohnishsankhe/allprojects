# Bhagavad Gītā

`src:bhagavad-gita` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Gītā, Śrīmad Bhagavad Gītā, Gītopaniṣad, Harigītā (as the Nārāyaṇīya refers to Kṛṣṇa's teaching)
**Original title:** भगवद्गीता
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Epic teaching (the Mahābhārata's Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Mokṣadharma milieu)](../lineages/epic-teaching.md), [Early Bhāgavata devotion (the Nārāyaṇīya / Sātvata–Ekāntika dharma)](../lineages/bhagavata-early.md), [Vedānta (Uttara Mīmāṃsā)](../lineages/vedanta.md)
**Genre:** gītā (dialogue); smṛti-prasthāna of Vedānta
**Part of:** [Bhīṣmaparvan](bhismaparvan.md)
**Location in parent:** Bhīṣmaparvan adhyāyas 23–40 (Critical Edition) = 25–42 (vulgate)
**Authors:** 
  - [Kṛṣṇa (Vāsudeva)](../teachers/krsna.md) — role: revealer; attribution: traditional
  - [Vyāsa (Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana)](../teachers/vyasa.md) — role: author; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: Spoken by Kṛṣṇa (the Lord) to Arjuna, heard and relayed to Dhṛtarāṣṭra by Sañjaya through the divine sight granted by Vyāsa (18.75), and set in the Mahābhārata by Vyāsa. The chapter colophons call it an Upaniṣad, a knowledge of Brahman (brahmavidyā) and a treatise on yoga (yogaśāstra).; scholarly: An integral part of the Bhīṣmaparvan whose unity and date are debated; many scholars see a single composition drawing on Upaniṣadic, Sāṃkhya-Yoga and Bhāgavata materials, others posit layers.; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: spoken on the first day of the Kurukṣetra war at the end of the Dvāpara yuga (before the Kali yuga, traditionally reckoned from 3102 BCE); Scholarly account: c. 2nd c. BCE – 2nd c. CE (estimates range from the 5th c. BCE to the 3rd c. CE); (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 18 chapters, 700 verses in the vulgate and the Critical Edition (a verse at the head of ch. 13 in some printed editions makes 701). The Kashmir recension (commented on by Abhinavagupta and Rājānaka Rāmakaṇṭha) has additional verses and many variant readings. A verse found in some manuscripts (the 'Gītāmāna') speaks of 745 verses (620 by Kṛṣṇa, 57 by Arjuna, 67 by Sañjaya, 1 by Dhṛtarāṣṭra); it is not in the Critical Edition.
**Availability:** digitized-original

The dialogue in which Kṛṣṇa, as charioteer, instructs Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukṣetra after Arjuna refuses to fight: the imperishable self, action without attachment to its fruits (karma-yoga), knowledge, meditation and devotion to the Lord as paths, the Lord's two natures, his descents and his universal form, the field and its knower, the three guṇas, the divine and demonic endowments, the threefold faith, and renunciation, ending in the call to take refuge in him alone (18.66).

### 1.28-30 <a id="tea-bhagavad-gita-1-28-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Seeing his kinsmen arrayed for battle, Arjuna, overcome by pity, says his limbs fail, his mouth dries, his body trembles and his hair stands on end; the bow Gāṇḍīva slips from his hand, his skin burns, he cannot stand and his mind seems to whirl.

`skeleton` · confidence high

### 2.11-30 <a id="tea-bhagavad-gita-2-11-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The teaching on the self: the wise grieve neither for the living nor for the dead; the embodied self was never non-existent and will never cease; it passes through the bodily stages and into another body; sense-contacts are transient and to be endured; the unreal has no being and the real never ceases; the self is unborn, eternal, not slain when the body is slain, not cut, burned, wetted or dried, unmanifest and unthinkable; therefore one should not grieve for any being.
