Six seekers approach the sage Pippalāda with fuel in hand and ask six questions: the origin of creatures (rayi and prāṇa; the two paths), the powers that support the body (the supremacy of prāṇa), the origin and distribution of prāṇa, sleep and dream, meditation on Om, and the person of sixteen parts.

### 1.1-2 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-1-1-2"></a>

Sukeśā Bhāradvāja, Śaibya Satyakāma, Sauryāyaṇī Gārgya, Kausalya Āśvalāyana, Bhārgava Vaidarbhi and Kabandhī Kātyāyana, devoted to brahman and seeking the highest brahman, came to the revered Pippalāda with fuel in hand. ### 1.3-8 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-1-3-8"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Kabandhī asks whence creatures are born. Prajāpati, desiring offspring, performed tapas and produced a pair, matter (rayi) and breath (prāṇa), thinking they would make creatures in many ways. The sun is breath, the moon matter; all that has form and is formless is matter.

### 1.9-10 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-1-9-10"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The year is Prajāpati; it has two courses, the southern and the northern. Those who venerate sacrifice and works of merit as what is done win only the world of the moon and return again; so the seers who desire offspring take the southern course, the path of the fathers, which is matter. Those who seek the self by austerity, celibacy, faith and knowledge win the sun by the northern course; that is the abode of breaths, the immortal, the fearless, the final goal; from there they do not return.

### 2.1-4 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-2-1-4"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Bhārgava Vaidarbhi asks how many deities support a being. Space, wind, fire, water, earth, speech, mind, sight and hearing boast that they hold the body up; breath, the chief, says: 'do not be deluded; I divide myself fivefold and hold it up.' They do not believe it; breath rises as if to leave, and all rise with it; when it settles, all settle — as bees follow the king bee — and they praise breath.

### 3.1-3 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-3-1-3"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

'You ask questions beyond the ordinary (atipraśna), but you are devoted to brahman, so I will answer: breath is born from the self; as a shadow is cast on a person, so it is spread on the self; it enters this body by the working of the mind.'

### 3.4-7 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-3-4-7"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

As a king appoints officers, breath appoints the other breaths: the downward breath (apāna) in the organs of excretion and generation; breath itself in the eye, ear, mouth and nose; in the middle the equalizing breath (samāna), which distributes food evenly. The self is in the heart; there are a hundred and one channels, a hundred in each, and seventy-two thousand branch channels in each of those; in them moves the diffused breath (vyāna). The up-breath (udāna), rising through one of them, leads by merit to a world of merit, by evil to an evil world, by both to the human world.

### 3.8-10 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-3-8-10"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The sun is the outer breath; the deity of the earth holds the downward breath; space is the equalizing breath, wind the diffused breath, heat the up-breath — so one whose heat has gone out is reborn with his senses merged in the mind. With whatever thought (citta) one has, with that one enters breath; breath joined with heat, together with the self, leads him to the world as it was conceived.

### 4.1-4 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-4-1-4"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Sauryāyaṇī Gārgya asks what sleeps and what wakes in a person, which deity sees dreams, whose is the happiness, in whom all are established. As the rays of the setting sun become one in its disc of light and spread out again when it rises, so the senses become one in the highest deity, the mind; then the person does not hear, see or speak — they say 'he sleeps'. The fires of breath stay awake in this city (the downward breath as the householder's fire, the diffused breath as the southern fire, breath as the offering fire...).

### 4.5-6 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-4-5-6"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

In dream this god (the mind) experiences its greatness: it sees again what it has seen, hears again what it has heard, experiences again what it has experienced in various places and regions — the seen and unseen, the heard and unheard, the experienced and unexperienced, the real and unreal. When it is overcome by light (tejas), the god sees no dreams; then happiness arises in the body.

### 4.7-11 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-4-7-11"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

As birds go to a tree to roost, all this goes to the highest self: earth and its subtle element, water, fire, wind, space and theirs; the eye and what is seen, the ear, smell, taste, skin, speech, hands, sexual organ, anus and feet with their objects; mind, intellect (buddhi), ego (ahaṃkāra), thought (citta), radiance and breath with theirs. This is the seer, toucher, hearer, smeller, taster, thinker, knower, agent — the person whose self is understanding; he rests in the highest imperishable self. One who knows the shadowless, bodiless, colourless, bright imperishable becomes all-knowing, all.

### 5.1-7 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-5-1-7"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Śaibya Satyakāma asks what world one wins by meditating on Om until death. Om is the higher and the lower brahman. Meditating on one measure, one is quickly brought back to earth and the ṛcs lead him to the human world; on two measures, the yajus lead him to the intermediate world, the moon, from which he returns; one who meditates on the highest person with the three measures of this syllable becomes one with the sun's light and, as a snake frees itself from its skin, freed from evil he is led up by the sāmans to the world of brahman and sees the person dwelling in the city (puriśaya), higher than the highest.

> etad vai satyakāma paraṃ cāparaṃ ca brahma yad oṃkāraḥ

### 6.1-4 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-6-1-4"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Sukeśā Bhāradvāja: Hiraṇyanābha, a prince of Kosala, asked me about the person of sixteen parts; I did not know; had I known I would have told him; one who speaks untruth withers to the root. Pippalāda: that person is here within the body, in whom the sixteen parts arise. He thought: 'in whose departure shall I depart, in whose staying stay?' He created breath; from breath faith, space, wind, light, water, earth, the senses, mind and food; from food strength, austerity, the mantras, rites, the worlds, and in the worlds name.

### 6.5-6 <a id="tea-prasna-upanisad-6-5-6"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

As rivers flowing to the ocean disappear on reaching it, their name and form destroyed, and are called simply 'ocean', so the sixteen parts of the seer, going to the person, disappear; he becomes partless and immortal. Know him in whom the parts are fixed like spokes in a hub, so that death may not disturb you.
