The domestic-rite manual of the Āśvalāyana Ṛgvedins in 4 adhyāyas: the three kinds of cooked offering (1.1.2), marriage — for which the customs of regions and villages are to be followed (1.7.1) — pregnancy and birth rites, initiation at eight, eleven or twelve (1.19), the Vedic student's vows, and in book 4 the treatment of the dying āhitāgni, cremation, collecting the bones and the ancestral rites.

### 1.1.2 <a id="tea-asvalayana-grhyasutra-1-1-2"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

There are three kinds of cooked-food offerings: those offered into the fire (huta), those offered not into fire (prahuta, the bali offerings), and those 'offered into Brahman' — the feeding of brāhmaṇas.

### 1.7.1 <a id="tea-asvalayana-grhyasutra-1-7-1"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The customs of peoples and of villages are high and low; one should follow them at a marriage; what is common to all we shall state.

### 1.19.1-7 <a id="tea-asvalayana-grhyasutra-1-19-1-7"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

One should initiate a brāhmaṇa in the eighth year (or the eighth from conception), a kṣatriya in the eleventh, a vaiśya in the twelfth; up to the sixteenth, twenty-second and twenty-fourth years respectively the time has not passed; after that they are 'fallen from the Sāvitrī'.

### 4.1-6 <a id="tea-asvalayana-grhyasutra-4-1-6"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The funeral rites of one who kept the sacred fires: when he falls ill he goes out with his fires; at death the body is carried to the cremation ground with the fires and burned with his sacrificial implements placed on his limbs; his widow lies beside him and is raised with a verse bidding her return to the world of the living; afterwards the bones are collected in an urn, and rites of appeasement and ancestral offerings follow.
