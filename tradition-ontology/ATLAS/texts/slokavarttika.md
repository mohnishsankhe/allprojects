### abhava.1-4 <a id="tea-slokavarttika-abhava-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Where the five (positive) means of knowledge do not arise with respect to a thing, non-apprehension (abhāva) is the means of knowing its non-existence. Absence is of four kinds: prior absence (curd in milk), destruction (milk in curd), mutual absence (horse in cow) and absolute absence (like a hare's horn).

### codana.110-112 <a id="tea-slokavarttika-codana-110-112"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### codana.117-118 <a id="tea-slokavarttika-codana-117-118"></a>

### sambandhaksepaparihara.42-47 <a id="tea-slokavarttika-sambandhaksepaparihara-42-47"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

At the beginning of creation there could be no action, and such a time is not accepted. To the suggestion that a Prajāpati first created the world with dharma, adharma and their means, and set forth the Vedas with their word-meaning relations: this is as hard to establish as omniscience. When nothing existed, what state was there? Where was Prajāpati, of what form? Who then knew him, to tell people? How is the first activity of the world known? Without a body, how could he even wish to create?

> yadā sarvamidaṃ nā''sīt kvā'vasthā tatra gamyatām| prajāpateḥ kva vā sthānaṃ kiṃrūpaṃ ca pratīyatām||45||

### sambandhaksepaparihara.66-68 <a id="tea-slokavarttika-sambandhaksepaparihara-66-68"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### sambandhaksepaparihara.102-104 <a id="tea-slokavarttika-sambandhaksepaparihara-102-104"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Knowledge as the cause of release is not known by the senses, and the Veda does not enjoin release through Sāṃkhya-type knowledge. 'The self is to be known' is not enjoined for the sake of release; self-knowledge is seen to be the cause of engaging in rites; once it is known to serve another, any statement of fruit for it is arthavāda, not a fruit other than heaven and the like.

> ātmā jñātavya ityetanmokṣārthaṃ na ca coditam| karmapravṛttihetutvamātmajñānasya lakṣyate||103||

### sambandhaksepaparihara.105-107 <a id="tea-slokavarttika-sambandhaksepaparihara-105-107"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

If release were an enjoyment of pleasure, it would be heaven under another name, and perishable; nothing that has a cause is known to be imperishable. So one is released only by the exhaustion of karma, the cause (of the body) being absent; only an absence can make release permanent, and an absence is not the fruit of any act.

> sukhopabhogarūpaśca yadi mokṣaḥ prakalpyate| svarga eva bhavedeṣa paryāyeṇa, kṣayī ca saḥ||105||

### sambandhaksepaparihara.108-111 <a id="tea-slokavarttika-sambandhaksepaparihara-108-111"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

For those who know the reality of the self, when earlier actions are exhausted by experience and no further accumulation exists, the body does not arise again, for the body arises to experience what karma produces. One seeking release should not engage in desire-prompted or prohibited acts, but should do the obligatory and occasional ones to avoid the demerit (of omission). A fruit comes only to one who desires it; since the self-knower does not, that knowledge is of use.

### sphotavada.134-137 <a id="tea-slokavarttika-sphotavada-134-137"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The cognition of meaning arises right after the cognition of the letters and from them, as fire is known from smoke; to deny the letters that role contradicts experience. Like a lamp, g and the other letters convey the cow; the effort (of this section) is to establish that effects rest on the real parts of the sentence (not on a sphoṭa).
