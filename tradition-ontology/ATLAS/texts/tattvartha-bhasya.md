Prose commentary on the Tattvārthasūtra that the Śvetāmbaras hold to be Umāsvāti's own (svopajña); it opens with verses on the purpose of the work and closes with a praśasti giving the author's lineage (Uccairnāgara branch) and place of composition (Kusumapura).

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-tattvartha-bhasya-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Of the three [view, knowledge, conduct], when an earlier one is gained the later is optional, but when a later one is gained the earlier is necessarily present.

### 9.11 <a id="tea-tattvartha-bhasya-9-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The Bhāṣya takes 'eleven in the Jina' literally: the eleven hardships arising from feeling-producing karma, among them hunger and thirst, do occur in the omniscient, who bears them.

### prasasti <a id="tea-tattvartha-bhasya-prasasti"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The closing verses name the author Umāsvāti, of the Uccairnāgara branch, disciple of Ghoṣanandi for initiation and of Mūla for study, son of Svāti and of Umā of the Vātsī clan, born at Nyagrodhikā, who composed the work at Kusumapura out of compassion for beings.
