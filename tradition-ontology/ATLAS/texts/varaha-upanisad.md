# Varāha Upaniṣad

`src:varaha-upanisad` · `sourced` · confidence high

**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Upaniṣadic tradition (the teaching of the early Upaniṣads)](../lineages/upanisadic.md), [Haṭha Yoga (the haṭha and rājayoga textual tradition)](../lineages/hatha-yoga.md), [Advaita Vedānta](../lineages/advaita-vedanta.md)
**Genre:** upaniṣad
**Attribution:** tradition: Śruti: revealed, without human author (apauruṣeya); the Muktikā presents it as one of the 108 Upaniṣads that Rāma teaches Hanumān, each belonging to a Vedic śākhā; scholarly: anonymous; assigned to a Veda by the Muktikā list, not by any surviving śākhā transmission; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Śruti: eternal and authorless; no human date; Scholarly account: Scholarly accounts differ by text. Several Yoga Upaniṣads (e.g. Amṛtabindu, Amṛtanāda, Kṣurikā, Tejobindu, Nādabindu, Dhyānabindu, Brahmavidyā, Yogatattva, Yogaśikhā, Haṃsa) survive in shorter northern versions that are older; the expanded southern recensions (as in this collection) borrow from medieval haṭha works and were probably compiled in South India, perhaps as late as the 18th c. (Bouy 1994).; (confidence low)
**Availability:** digitized-original

Varāha teaches Ṛbhu (ch.1–3) and Ṛbhu teaches Nidāgha (ch.4–5): the 96 tattvas; the fourfold means; Advaita teaching; seven bhūmikās; the paths of Śuka and Vāmadeva; eight-limbed yoga, channels, kuṇḍalinī and four stages.

### 1.1-5 <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-1-1-5"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Ṛbhu performs austerity for twelve divine years; the Lord appears as Varāha and offers a boon; Ṛbhu asks for the knowledge that frees from saṃsāra.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: general · stage: all · types: teacher-transmission_

teachers: [Ṛbhu](../teachers/rbhu.md), [Varāha (the Boar avatāra)](../teachers/varaha.md)

### 1.6-15 <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-1-6-15"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The principles are counted as 24, 36 or 96; the ninety-six add to the 24 the five elements, three bodies, three states, six modifications, six waves (hunger, thirst, grief, delusion, old age, death), six sheaths, six enemies, three jīvas, three guṇas, three karmas, five actions, four inner functions, the four sublime attitudes (friendliness, compassion, joy, equanimity) and fourteen deities.

_level: conventional · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: body-layers, consciousness-mind_

concepts: [The ninety-six principles](../concepts/ninety-six-tattvas.md) · obstacles: [Six waves (ūrmi)](../obstacles/six-urmis.md), [Six enemies (ṣaḍ-ripu)](../obstacles/six-enemies.md)

### 1.16-17 <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-1-16-17"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

Worshippers of Varāha become liberated while living; those who know the ninety-six are freed in any stage of life, whether matted, shaven or with topknot.

_level: bridging · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: intermediate · types: karma-liberation_

### 2.1-6 <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-2-1-6"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The fourfold means (discrimination, dispassion, the six virtues, desire for liberation) come by one's dharma of caste and stage, austerity and pleasing the guru.

### 2.39-40 <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-2-39-40"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

True fasting (upavāsa) is 'dwelling near' — the jīva abiding with the supreme Self — not drying up the body; beating the anthill does not kill the snake.

`sourced` · confidence high

### 2.78-79 (summary) <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-2-78-79-summary"></a>

### 2.80 <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-2-80"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

### 4.1-10 <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-4-1-10"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Nidāgha asks about the stages; Ṛbhu teaches seven: good desire (śubhecchā), inquiry (vicāraṇā), thinning of mind (tanumānasī), attainment of sattva (sattvāpatti), non-attachment (asaṃsakti), non-perception of objects (padārthabhāvanā) and the fourth (turyagā); each is defined.

### 4.34-42 <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-4-34-42"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Two paths: Śuka's path of the bird (vihaṅgama), immediate liberation through inquiry into the great sayings; Vāmadeva's path of the ant (pipīlikā), gradual liberation over births through yoga — a haṭha practitioner hindered by obstacles or powers is reborn in a great family and continues. Both reach Brahman.

_level: bridging · standpoint: seeker · path: knowledge, meditation · stage: intermediate · types: karma-liberation, dispute_

`sourced` · confidence moderate

The kuṇḍalinī lies in the triangle at the base; she is the supreme power from which breath, fire, bindu, nāda, haṃsa and mind arise; the six centres are the seat of Śakti, from the throat to the crown the seat of Śambhu.

`sourced` · confidence high

### 4 (opening prose, before v. 1) <a id="tea-varaha-upanisad-4-2-3-pranava-mapping"></a>
`sourced` · confidence moderate

The stages are mapped onto the measures of Oṃ (a, u, m, half-measure, each gross, subtle, seed and witness); the first three are the seeker's, the fourth makes a knower of Brahman, the fifth the excellent, the sixth the more excellent, the seventh the most excellent knower.
