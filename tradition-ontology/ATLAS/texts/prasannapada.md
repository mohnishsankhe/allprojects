## Teachings (7: skeleton 7)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-prasannapada-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

For one who is a Mādhyamika it is not right to state an autonomous inference, because he does not accept any other position; as Āryadeva said, one who has no position of existence, non-existence or both cannot be refuted.

> na ca mādhyamikasya sataḥ svatantramanumānaṃ kartuṃ yuktaṃ pakṣāntarābhyupagamābhāvāt |

### 13.8 <a id="tea-prasannapada-13-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Emptiness is the escape from all views, not another view: one who, told 'I will give you nothing to buy', says 'give me that nothing' cannot be made to grasp the absence of goods; and as a medicine that expels the illness but itself remains in the belly makes the patient worse, so emptiness taken as a view is incurable.

### 17.30 <a id="tea-prasannapada-17-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

We are not nihilists: rejecting both the doctrines of existence and of non-existence, we illuminate the non-dual path that leads to the city of nirvāṇa; we do not say that action, agent and fruit do not exist, but establish that they are without own-nature.

### 18.6 <a id="tea-prasannapada-18-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Lokāyatikas, not seeing a self that passes from this world to the next, denied it and so taught non-self in a wrong way ('the person is only as much as the range of the senses'); the Buddhas taught self for some, non-self for others, and for others neither self nor non-self.

### 18.7 <a id="tea-prasannapada-18-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Some object that the Mādhyamikas are no different from the nihilists, since they say that action, agent, fruit and the whole world are empty of own-nature; not so, for the Mādhyamikas teach dependent origination and say this world and the next lack own-nature because they are dependently arisen — which the nihilists do not.

### 24.8 <a id="tea-prasannapada-24-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Saṃvṛti means total covering: ignorance is called saṃvṛti because it completely covers the reality of all things; or it means mutual dependence; or it means convention, worldly usage.

> samantādvaraṇaṃ saṃvṛtiḥ | ajñānaṃ hi samantātsarvapadārthatattvāvacchādanātsaṃvṛtirityucyate | parasparasaṃbhavanaṃ vā saṃvṛtiranyonyasamāśrayeṇetyarthaḥ | atha vā saṃvṛtiḥ saṃketo lokavyavahāraḥ

### 24.18 <a id="tea-prasannapada-24-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The dependent origination that is the appearing of sprouts, consciousness and so on in dependence on causes and conditions is non-arising by own-nature, and the non-arising by own-nature of things is emptiness; this emptiness is a designation in dependence, as a chariot is designated in dependence on wheels and the other parts; being free of the two extremes of existence and non-existence, it is the middle way — these are special names of dependent origination.

> yo'yaṃ pratītyasamutpādo hetupratyayānapekṣya aṅkuravijñānādīnāṃ prādurbhāvaḥ, sa svabhāvenānutpādaḥ | yaśca svabhāvenānutpādo bhāvānāṃ sā śūnyatā |
