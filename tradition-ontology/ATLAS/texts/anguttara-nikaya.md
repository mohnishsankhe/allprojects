### 1.51-52 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-1-51-52"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The mind is luminous, but it is defiled by adventitious defilements; the uninstructed ordinary person does not understand this as it is, so there is no development of mind for him. The mind is luminous and it is freed from adventitious defilements; the instructed noble disciple understands this, so for him there is development of mind.

> “Pabhassaramidaṁ, bhikkhave, cittaṁ. Tañca kho āgantukehi upakkilesehi upakkiliṭṭhaṁ. Taṁ assutavā puthujjano yathābhūtaṁ nappajānāti. Tasmā ‘assutavato puthujjanassa cittabhāvanā natthī’ti vadāmī”ti. “Pabhassaramidaṁ, bhikkhave, cittaṁ. Tañca kho āgantukehi upakkilesehi vippamuttaṁ. Taṁ sutavā ariyasāvako yathābhūtaṁ pajānāti. Tasmā ‘sutavato ariyasāvakassa cittabhāvanā atthī’ti vadāmī”ti.

### 1.279 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-1-279"></a>

### 3.33 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-3-33"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Train so that there is no I-making, mine-making or underlying tendency to conceit in this conscious body or towards all external signs, and dwell in the liberation of mind and by wisdom in which they no longer occur: such a monk has cut off craving and made an end of suffering.

### 3.34 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-3-34"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

There are three sources for the origination of deeds: greed, hatred and delusion. A deed born of them ripens wherever one's individual existence arises, in this life, the next, or some later time; deeds born of non-greed, non-hatred and non-delusion, once greed etc. are gone, are abandoned, cut off at the root like a palm stump, like seeds burnt and scattered.

### 3.47 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-3-47"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The conditioned has three characteristics: arising is discerned, vanishing is discerned, and alteration of what stands is discerned. The unconditioned has three characteristics: no arising is discerned, no vanishing is discerned, no alteration of what stands is discerned.

Sattamaṁ. Asaṅkhatalakkhaṇasutta “Tīṇimāni, bhikkhave, asaṅkhatassa asaṅkhatalakkhaṇāni. Katamāni tīṇi? Na uppādo paññāyati, na vayo paññāyati, na ṭhitassa aññathattaṁ paññāyati. Imāni kho, bhikkhave, tīṇi asaṅkhatassa asaṅkhatalakkhaṇānī”ti.

### 3.101 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-3-101"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

As a goldsmith removes coarse, then middling, then fine impurities from gold ore until the gold is pliant and bright, so the monk intent on higher mind removes bad conduct, then sensual, malicious and cruel thoughts, then thoughts of family, country and reputation, then thoughts about the Dhamma, until concentration is unified; then he can direct the mind to the six direct knowledges.

### 3.136 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-3-136"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Whether Tathāgatas arise or not, that element stands — the stableness of the Dhamma, the fixed course of the Dhamma: all formations are impermanent; all formations are suffering; all phenomena are not-self. A Tathāgata awakens to this, then explains and teaches it.

> “Uppādā vā, bhikkhave, tathāgatānaṁ anuppādā vā tathāgatānaṁ, ṭhitāva sā dhātu dhammaṭṭhitatā dhammaniyāmatā. Sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā. Taṁ tathāgato abhisambujjhati abhisameti. Abhisambujjhitvā abhisametvā ācikkhati deseti paññāpeti paṭṭhapeti vivarati vibhajati uttānīkaroti: ‘sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā’ti. Uppādā vā, bhikkhave, tathāgatānaṁ anuppādā vā tathāgatānaṁ ṭhitāva sā dhātu dhammaṭṭhitatā dhammaniyāmatā. Sabbe saṅkhārā dukkhā. Taṁ tathāgato abhisambujjhati abhisameti. Abhisambujjhitvā abhisametvā ācikkhati deseti paññāpeti paṭṭhapeti vivarati vibhajati uttānīkaroti: ‘sabbe saṅkhārā dukkhā’ti. Uppādā vā, bhikkhave, tathāgatānaṁ anuppādā vā tathāgatānaṁ ṭhitāva sā dhātu dhammaṭṭhitatā dhammaniyāmatā. Sabbe dhammā anattā. Taṁ tathāgato abhisambujjhati abhisameti. Abhisambujjhitvā abhisametvā ācikkhati deseti paññāpeti paṭṭhapeti vivarati vibhajati uttānīkaroti: ‘sabbe dhammā anattā’”ti.

### 4.232 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-4-232"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Four kinds of kamma declared from direct knowledge: dark with dark result, bright with bright result, dark-and-bright with dark-and-bright result, and neither dark nor bright with neither dark nor bright result, which leads to the destruction of kamma.

> “Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, kammāni mayā sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā paveditāni. Katamāni cattāri? Atthi, bhikkhave, kammaṁ kaṇhaṁ kaṇhavipākaṁ; atthi, bhikkhave, kammaṁ sukkaṁ sukkavipākaṁ; atthi, bhikkhave, kammaṁ kaṇhasukkaṁ kaṇhasukkavipākaṁ; atthi, bhikkhave, kammaṁ akaṇhaasukkaṁ akaṇhaasukkavipākaṁ kammakkhayāya saṁvattati. Imāni kho, bhikkhave, cattāri kammāni mayā sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā paveditānī”ti.

### 4.233 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-4-233"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The four kinds of kamma explained: dark kamma is afflictive bodily, verbal and mental volition, by which one is reborn in an afflictive world and feels exclusively painful feelings, like beings in hell; bright kamma is non-afflictive volition, by which one is reborn in a non-afflictive world and feels exclusively pleasant feelings, like the gods of refulgent beauty (Subhakiṇha); mixed kamma leads to a mixed world (like humans, some gods and some beings in the lower realms); the volition to abandon all three is the kamma that is neither dark nor bright, leading to the destruction of kamma.

### 6.10 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-6-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When a noble disciple recollects the Tathāgata ('he is an arahant, fully awakened, accomplished in knowledge and conduct …'), the Dhamma ('well proclaimed, visible here and now …'), the Saṅgha ('practising the good way … the four pairs of persons'), his own virtue, his own generosity and the deities, his mind is not obsessed by lust, hatred or delusion; gladness, rapture, tranquillity, happiness and concentration arise — he dwells balanced among the unbalanced, having entered the stream of Dhamma.

### 9.36 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-9-36"></a>

### 10.176 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-10-176"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 11.15 <a id="tea-anguttara-nikaya-11-15"></a>
