A meditation manual for the disciples' path: the lineage and entry into the teaching, renunciation, the prerequisites of morality and sense-restraint, the four kinds of meditation object (pervasive, purifying temperament, skill, purifying defilement), the nine stages of settling the mind with six powers and four attentions, the seven attentions leading to the absorptions, and the mundane and supramundane paths.

### 1.gotra <a id="tea-sravakabhumi-1-gotra"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The lineage (gotra) of a disciple is the seed of the qualities of liberation already present; one who has it can attain nirvāṇa given the right conditions — human birth, a central land, intact faculties, faith, the appearance of a buddha, the teaching and a spiritual friend.

### 2.alambana <a id="tea-sravakabhumi-2-alambana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The meditation object is fourfold: the pervasive object, the object purifying one's conduct (temperament), the object of skill, and the object purifying defilements.

> vyāpyālambanaṃ, caritaviśodhanaṃ, kauśalyālambanaṃ, kleśaviśodhanaṃ ceti

### 2.carita <a id="tea-sravakabhumi-2-carita"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The objects that purify conduct are prescribed by temperament: the unattractive for one of lustful conduct, loving-kindness for the hateful, dependent origination for the deluded, the analysis of the elements for the proud, and mindfulness of breathing for the discursive.

> dveṣacarito vā punarmaitryāṃ, mohacarito vā idaṃ pratyayatāpratītyasamutpāde, mānacarito dhātuprabhede

### 2.yathavad <a id="tea-sravakabhumi-2-yathavad"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The limit of things (vastuparyantatā) is their extent — everything conditioned summed up in the five aggregates, all dharmas in the elements and bases, all knowables in the noble truths — and their reality, suchness, established by four kinds of reasoning: of dependence, of efficacy, of valid proof, and of the nature of things.

### 3.four-vipasyana <a id="tea-sravakabhumi-3-four-vipasyana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Insight is fourfold: a monk discerns dharmas, investigates them thoroughly, considers them and examines them — with respect to their extent and their reality.

### 3.nine-stages <a id="tea-sravakabhumi-3-nine-stages"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

By the ninefold settling of the mind one settles the mind inwardly: one places it, settles it firmly, re-settles it, settles it closely, tames it, calms it, calms it fully, makes it one-pointed and brings it to concentration. These are accomplished by six powers — hearing, reflection, mindfulness, awareness, vigour and familiarity — and accompanied by four attentions: forced, interrupted, uninterrupted and spontaneous.

> navākārayā cittasthityā adhyātmameva cittaṃ sthāpayati / saṃsthāpayati / avasthāpayatyupasthāpayati / damayati / śamayati / [vyupaśamayati] / ekotīkaroti / samādhatte

### 3.prasrabdhi <a id="tea-sravakabhumi-3-prasrabdhi"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

When calm is attained, pliancy of mind arises, and with it pliancy of body: the badness (dauṣṭhulya) that makes body and mind unworkable is removed, and a lightness is felt, as if in the head, spreading through the body.

### 3.seven-attentions <a id="tea-sravakabhumi-3-seven-attentions"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The yogin reaches freedom from desire for sense-pleasures by seven attentions: the attention discerning characteristics, that arising from resolve, that of seclusion, that gathering delight, that of examination, that of the completion of practice, and that which is the fruit of the completion of practice; the first discerns the coarseness of sense-pleasures and the peace of the first absorption.

> lakṣaṇapratisaṃvedī, ādhimokṣikaḥ, prāvivekyo, ratisaṃgrāhakaḥ / mīmānsā(māṃsā)manaskāraḥ / prayoganiṣṭhaḥ, prayoganiṣṭhāphalaśca
