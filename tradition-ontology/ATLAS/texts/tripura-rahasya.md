A Śākta-Advaita teaching in verse in which Dattātreya instructs Paraśurāma, narrated by Haritāyana; through stories such as that of Hemacūḍa taught by his wife Hemalekhā it teaches that the self is consciousness that knows itself (with the power of self-awareness, vimarśa), that the world is its reflection like an image in a mirror, and that realization is stable abidance beyond the alternation of samādhi and activity.

### jnana <a id="tea-tripura-rahasya-jnana"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Through the story of Prince Hemacūḍa, taught by his wife Hemalekhā, the text teaches that bondage is only lack of inquiry and that the self is pure consciousness which illumines and knows itself.

### jnana <a id="tea-tripura-rahasya-jnana-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The world appears within consciousness like a city reflected in a mirror; consciousness has the power of self-awareness (vimarśa) by which it knows itself and projects the world.

_level: bridging · standpoint: absolute · path: knowledge · stage: advanced · types: ultimate, world-fate_

### jnana <a id="tea-tripura-rahasya-jnana-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The highest knower is not one who must enter samādhi to be free, but one whose realization remains unbroken in activity and in samādhi alike (the natural state).
