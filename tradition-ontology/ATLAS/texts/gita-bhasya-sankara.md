The oldest extant complete commentary on the Gītā. Its introduction distinguishes the dharma of activity (pravṛtti) and of withdrawal (nivṛtti) and holds that the Gītā's purport is the highest good attained through steadfastness in knowledge of the self together with renunciation of all works; action performed as offering to the Lord purifies the mind for that knowledge; it rejects the combination of knowledge and works (jñāna-karma-samuccaya) as the means to liberation.

### 2.11 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-sankara-2-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Introducing the teaching, Śaṅkara argues at length that knowledge of the self cannot be combined with works as joint cause of liberation, since knowledge (the self is actionless) and works (which require the sense of agency) have opposite presuppositions.

### 4.18 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-sankara-4-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

One who sees inaction in action sees that the self is actionless and that action belongs to body and senses; one who sees action in inaction sees that inactivity too, when claimed as one's own, is an act — such a one is wise.

### 7.17 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-sankara-7-17"></a>

Of the four kinds of devotees, the knower (jñānī), ever united and devoted to the One, is the best; the Lord is dear to him and he to the Lord, since the knower is the Lord's very self.

### 13.2 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-sankara-13-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Lord is the knower of the field in all fields; the appearance of the embodied one as a transmigrating agent-enjoyer is due to ignorance, which is itself not the self's attribute but belongs to the field (the instrument).

### 18.66 <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-sankara-18-66"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

'Abandoning all dharmas' means renouncing all works, together with the notion of agency; taking refuge in the Lord alone means knowing oneself as the one Lord; knowledge alone, not works or their combination, is the means to the highest good.

### intro <a id="tea-gita-bhasya-sankara-intro"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The Vedic dharma is twofold: that characterized by activity (pravṛtti), which leads to prosperity and higher worlds, and that characterized by withdrawal (nivṛtti), which leads to liberation; the Gītā teaches both, and its special aim is the supreme good through knowledge of the self preceded by renunciation.
