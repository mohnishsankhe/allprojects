Lakṣmīdhara's commentary on the Saundaryalaharī, written from the Samaya standpoint. On verse 31 it names and judges the 64 tantras (Kaula, deceptive, aimed at worldly powers, for those outside Vedic eligibility), the 'mixed' group of eight Candrakalā tantras, and the Vedic 'five auspicious āgamas' (Śubhāgama-pañcaka) — the Vasiṣṭha, Sanaka, Śuka, Sanandana and Sanatkumāra saṃhitās.

## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### 31 <a id="tea-laksmidhara-31"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Lakṣmīdhara characterises the sixty-four tantras one by one (many as Kāpālika, left-handed or aimed at worldly powers) and concludes that they deceive even those who know them, being aimed only at powers in this world and far from the Vedic path. Paśupati made tantras for different classes: the sixty-four are for śūdras and mixed classes; the three higher classes are eligible for the eight Candrakalā tantras (Candrakalā, Jyotsnāvatī, Kalānidhi, Kulārṇava, Kuleśvarī, Bhuvaneśvarī, Bārhaspatya, Durvāsamata), which teach the right-hand way for them and the left-hand way for others and are called 'mixed'. The five auspicious āgamas — the saṃhitās of Vasiṣṭha, Sanaka, Śuka, Sanandana and Sanatkumāra — teach the Vedic way, called Samayācāra, which his commentary follows; 'the mixed and the Kaula paths are to be abandoned'.
