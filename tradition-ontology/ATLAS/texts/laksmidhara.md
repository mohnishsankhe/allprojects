**Availability:** digitized-original

Lakṣmīdhara's commentary on the Saundaryalaharī from the Samaya standpoint: it defines Samaya and Kaula worship (inner space vs external support), divides Kaula into pūrva and uttara, teaches the fivefold equality of Śiva and Śakti, identifies the Śrīcakra with the moon in the crown lotus, lists the five kinds of liberation, and classifies the tantras into the sixty-four, the eight 'mixed' Candrakalā tantras and the five Vedic Śubhāgamas.

### 8 <a id="tea-laksmidhara-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Worship of the cakra is of two kinds: in the outer space — drawing it on a seat, birch-bark, cloth or a plate of gold or silver — which the elders call Kaula worship; and in the space of the heart (dahara), which the Samayins call Samaya worship. The bindu between the four lower Śiva-yonis and the five upper Śakti-yonis is the ocean of nectar of the verse.

### 10 <a id="tea-laksmidhara-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The moon-disc in the head, known to all the yoga texts, is for the Samayins the Śrīcakra itself, since the moon has sixteen digits and the digits of Śrīvidyā wax and wane over the sixteen lunar days; even the outer moon is the Śrīcakra, a great secret. The Goddess drenches the practitioner's whole body with the nectar of her feet in the crown, then enters the base as a serpent and sleeps.

### 11 <a id="tea-laksmidhara-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The twenty-five principles are accepted by all because the Veda teaches them; the thirty-six-principle scheme is to be read in their light; beyond all principles is the conjoined pair of Śiva and Śakti, the twenty-sixth, from which the world arises; the one who knows it attains, by degrees of worship, the fivefold liberation of equal power, same world, nearness, same form and union (sārṣṭi, sālokya, sāmīpya, sārūpya, sāyujya).

### 23 <a id="tea-laksmidhara-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The verse may also state the uttara-Kaula doctrine: in it there is no Śiva-principle apart from the Śakti-principle, so Śiva is included in Śakti and she alone is to be worshipped — a view Lakṣmīdhara promises to examine at verses 35 and 41.

### 31 <a id="tea-laksmidhara-31"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Lakṣmīdhara characterises the sixty-four tantras one by one (many as Kāpālika, left-handed or aimed at worldly powers) and concludes that they deceive even those who know them, being aimed only at powers in this world and far from the Vedic path. Paśupati made tantras for different classes: the sixty-four are for śūdras and mixed classes; the three higher classes are eligible for the eight Candrakalā tantras (Candrakalā, Jyotsnāvatī, Kalānidhi, Kulārṇava, Kuleśvarī, Bhuvaneśvarī, Bārhaspatya, Durvāsamata), which teach the right-hand way for them and the left-hand way for others and are called 'mixed'. The five auspicious āgamas — the saṃhitās of Vasiṣṭha, Sanaka, Śuka, Sanandana and Sanatkumāra — teach the Vedic way, called Samayācāra, which his commentary follows; 'the mixed and the Kaula paths are to be abandoned'.

### 41 <a id="tea-laksmidhara-41"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Samayā is she who attains fivefold equality with Śambhu, and Śambhu too attains fivefold equality with the Goddess, so both are of equal primacy: equality of seat (both in the ādhāra), of action (both generate the world), of state (lāsya and tāṇḍava are one as dance), of form (both are red) and of name. For the Samayins the worship is of Samayā and Samaya in the thousand-petalled lotus; outer worship and the worship of a young woman's triangle are wholly rejected.
