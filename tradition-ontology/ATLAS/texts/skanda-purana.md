**Attribution:** tradition: Told by Skanda (Kumāra/Ṣaṇmukha); the largest Purāṇa, 81,100 verses by the traditional count (BhP 12.13.7); arranged either in seven khaṇḍas or in six saṃhitās (Sanatkumāra, Sūta, Śaṅkara, Vaiṣṇava, Brāhma, Saura).; scholarly: The printed Skanda (seven khaṇḍas) is a loose corpus of tīrtha- and vrata-māhātmyas of many dates and regions; a distinct early 'Skandapurāṇa' survives in Nepalese manuscripts, the oldest dated 810 CE (src:skandapurana-early).; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Vyāsa, end of Dvāpara; Scholarly account: components from c. 8th to 18th c. CE; (confidence low)
**Structure:** Venkateshwara print: seven khaṇḍas - Māheśvara (Kedāra, Kaumārikā, Aruṇācala), Vaiṣṇava (Veṅkaṭācala, Puruṣottama, Badarikāśrama, Kārttika, Mārgaśīrṣa, Bhāgavata-māhātmya, Vaiśākha, Ayodhyā, Vāsudeva), Brahma (Setu, Dharmāraṇya, Brahmottara), Kāśī, Āvantya (incl. **Availability:** digitized-original

The great compendium of sacred places and times: māhātmyas of Kedāra, Aruṇācala, Veṅkaṭācala, Puruṣottama, Badarī, Ayodhyā, Setu, Kāśī, Avanti and the Narmadā, Prabhāsa and Dvārakā; the months and vows; and teaching on the inner meaning of pilgrimage.
