# Mountain Doctrine: Ocean of Definitive Meaning (ri chos nges don rgya mtsho)

**Availability:** digitized-translation

Dolpopa's principal treatise on other-emptiness, written as final, uncommon instructions for mountain retreatants. It sets out buddha-nature (the matrix of the one gone thus) as the ultimate that is empty of all adventitious, conventional phenomena but not of its own nature, permanent and endowed with the buddha-qualities; distinguishes self-emptiness of the conventional from other-emptiness of the ultimate; and supports this with extensive citation of the tathāgatagarbha sūtras, the Maitreya texts, Nāgārjuna's hymns and the Kālacakra literature.

### alaya <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-alaya"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The ālaya (basis of all) is of two kinds: the ālaya-consciousness, which is conventional and the ground of saṃsāra's imprints, and the ālaya-wisdom, which is the ultimate, buddha-nature itself; confusing the two leads either to taking the ultimate as impermanent consciousness or to reifying consciousness.

### annihilatory-emptiness <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-annihilatory-emptiness"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

An emptiness that is nothing whatsoever — the emptiness of annihilation (chad stong) — is not the ultimate. The ultimate is the emptiness endowed with all supreme aspects, the unchanging great bliss of the Kālacakra, which is realized through the empty forms of the vajra-yoga and not by analysis alone.

### definitive <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-definitive"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The sūtras of the final wheel (the tathāgatagarbha sūtras), the Maitreya treatises, Nāgārjuna's hymns and the Kālacakra with its bodhisattva commentaries teach the definitive meaning. The middle wheel's statements that all phenomena are empty of own-nature are true of the conventional; applied to the ultimate they must be read with an intention.

### fruit <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-fruit"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The fruit is the dharmakāya made manifest with the qualities of freedom that were always present in it, together with the form bodies that appear for others; nothing that was not already there is created.

### ground <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-ground"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Buddha-nature — the matrix of the one gone thus, the dharmakāya, the dharmadhātu — is the ultimate truth. It is empty of all conventional phenomena, which are adventitious and other than it, but it is not empty of its own nature: this is emptiness of other (gzhan stong).

### ground <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-ground-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The ultimate is permanent, stable, peaceful and eternal (rtag brtan ther zug g.yung drung). The Buddha taught this in the sūtras of the final wheel and in the Kālacakra; to say that the dharmakāya is impermanent or a mere nothingness is to deprecate the ultimate.

### ground <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-ground-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Buddha-nature is present in every sentient being complete with the buddha-qualities — the powers and the rest — like a treasure beneath a poor man's house; the path removes what covers it and does not newly produce the qualities.

### not-dependent-arising <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-not-dependent-arising"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The ultimate is not a dependent arising: whatever arises in dependence is conventional, compounded and impermanent. The statements that whatever is dependently arisen is empty of own-nature therefore concern the conventional, not the dharmatā.

### path <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-path"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The path is to hear, reflect on and above all meditate on the definitive meaning in solitude — the 'mountain Dharma' for retreatants — and the supreme means of directly realizing the ultimate is the Kālacakra six-branch vajra-yoga.

### self <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-self"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The dharmakāya is the supreme self (bdag dam pa), the perfection of self spoken of in the sūtras; it is not the self imagined by the tīrthikas or by ordinary beings in the aggregates, which is refuted.

### two-truths <a id="tea-mountain-doctrine-two-truths"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Conventional phenomena are empty of their own nature (rang stong), like a rabbit's horns; the ultimate is empty of other. The two truths are not one entity, yet they are not two different entities either: they are different only in the sense that their being one is negated.
