## Teachings (4: skeleton 4)

### 7-20 <a id="tea-potthapada-sutta-7-20"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Perceptions arise and cease with a cause: by training some perceptions arise and some cease. Through the gradual training the monk passes from perception to perception through the jhānas and formless states; at the peak of perception he neither intends nor wills, and so perceptions cease and he touches cessation — the progressive cessation of perception attained with full awareness.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: experiential · path: meditation · stage: advanced · types: consciousness-mind, practice_

### 21-31 <a id="tea-potthapada-sutta-21-31"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Asked whether perception is a person's self, the Buddha shows that for any self one posits — gross, mind-made or formless — perception would be one thing and self another; and he declines to declare whether the world is eternal or the Tathāgata exists after death, because these are not beneficial, not the basis of the holy life, and do not lead to disenchantment and nibbāna; what he has declared is the four noble truths.

_level: unmarked · standpoint: polemical · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: dispute, ultimate_

terms: [avyākata](../terms/avyakata.md), [attā](../terms/atta.md) · concepts: [The undeclared questions](../concepts/undeclared-questions.md) · disputes: [Should the questions about the eternity and extent of the world, soul and body, and the Tathāgata after death be answered?](../debates/avyakata.md), [Is there a self (ātman, puruṣa, jīva) — an enduring conscious subject distinct from body and changing cognitions — or only a stream of impersonal factors designated 'person'?](../debates/is-there-a-self.md)

### 39-53 <a id="tea-potthapada-sutta-39-53"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Three kinds of acquired self (attapaṭilābha) — gross, mind-made and formless — are taught for their abandoning; like milk, curd and ghee, each is named for what is present at the time. 'These are the world's common usages, terms, expressions and descriptions, which the Tathāgata uses without grasping them.'

_level: bridging · standpoint: analytic · path: knowledge · stage: all · types: ultimate, sound-language_

### 53.5 <a id="tea-potthapada-sutta-53-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Tathāgata speaks with the world's common usages, terms, expressions and descriptions without grasping them.

> Imā kho, citta, lokasamaññā lokaniruttiyo lokavohārā lokapaññattiyo, yāhi tathāgato voharati aparāmasan”ti.
