# Vacana literature (vacana sāhitya)

**Alternate titles:** vacanas, śivaśaraṇara vacanagaḷu
**Original title:** ವಚನ ಸಾಹಿತ್ಯ
**Language:** Kannada
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Vīraśaiva / Liṅgāyata](../lineages/virasaiva.md), [Śaraṇa–vacana tradition](../lineages/sarana-vacana.md)
**Genre:** vacana
**Attribution:** tradition: the utterances of the śaraṇas (Basava, Allama Prabhu, Akka Mahādēvi, Cennabasava and some two hundred others, women among them), preserved in palm-leaf bundles and carried to Uḷavi after the Kalyāṇa upheaval; scholarly: a twelfth-century core with later accretions; much of the corpus is known through fifteenth–sixteenth-century Vijayanagara-period anthologies; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: 12th c. (the śaraṇa generation of Kalyāṇa); Scholarly account: 12th-c. core; later vacanakāras to the 17th–18th c.; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** about 20,000 vacanas ascribed to more than 200 named vacanakāras, including over thirty women (counts differ between editions)
**Availability:** digitized-original

The corpus of vacanas — short rhythmic Kannada prose-poems, usually closing with the author's signature name (aṅkita) for the Lord — composed by the twelfth-century śaraṇas of Kalyāṇa and by later vacanakāras. They teach devotion to Śiva as the liṅga, the six stages (ṣaṭsthala), the triad of guru, liṅga and jaṅgama, work as worship (kāyaka) and giving (dāsōha), and criticize caste, pollution rules, temple and Vedic ritualism and outward show in favour of lived experience (anubhava).
