### 1 (bahūdaka) <a id="tea-bhiksuka-upanisad-1-bahudaka"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The bahūdakas carry triple staff and water-pot, keep topknot and thread, wear ochre and take eight mouthfuls from the houses of brahmin sages, avoiding honey and meat.

### 1 (haṃsa) <a id="tea-bhiksuka-upanisad-1-hamsa"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high · _restricted: summary only_

The haṃsas stay one night in a village, five in a town, seven at a sacred place; they take cow's urine and dung and practise the lunar fast (cāndrāyaṇa).

_level: conventional · standpoint: ethical-social · path: general · stage: advanced · types: ethics_

### 1 (kuṭīcaka) <a id="tea-bhiksuka-upanisad-1-kuticaka"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Among mendicants seeking liberation there are four kinds. The kuṭīcakas — such as Gautama, Bharadvāja, Yājñavalkya and Vasiṣṭha — live on eight mouthfuls and seek liberation by the path of yoga.

_level: conventional · standpoint: ethical-social · path: general · stage: advanced · types: ethics, teacher-transmission_

concepts: [The kinds of renouncer](../concepts/six-kinds-of-renunciant.md) · teachers: [Yājñavalkya](../teachers/yajnavalkya.md), [Bharadvāja](../teachers/bharadvaja.md)

### 1 (paramahaṃsa) <a id="tea-bhiksuka-upanisad-1-paramahamsa"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The paramahaṃsas — Saṃvartaka, Āruṇi, Śvetaketu, Jaḍabharata, Dattātreya, Śuka, Vāmadeva, Hārītaka — take eight mouthfuls, live at tree-roots, empty houses and cremation grounds, clothed or naked; for them there is no dharma or adharma, pure or impure; they beg from all castes.
