The Mūlasūtra of the six obligatory daily duties (āvaśyaka): equanimity (sāmāyika), praise of the twenty-four Jinas (caturviṃśatistava, the 'Logassa'), veneration of the teacher (vandana), confession and repentance (pratikramaṇa), abandoning the body in standing meditation (kāyotsarga), and renunciation of specified things for a set time (pratyākhyāna). Its formulas, beginning with the Namaskāra, are the core of Jain liturgy; its niryukti and commentaries carry much narrative.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 1 <a id="tea-avasyaka-sutra-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The vow of equanimity: 'Venerable one, I undertake sāmāyika; I renounce all sinful activity for life (or for the set time), in three ways and by three means — I will not do it, nor cause it, nor approve it, in mind, speech or body; I repent of it, I censure it, I abandon that self (which committed it).'

### 2 <a id="tea-avasyaka-sutra-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The praise of the twenty-four Jinas (Logassa): 'I praise the Arhats who give light to the world, the makers of the ford of dharma, the conquerors — the twenty-four omniscient ones', naming Ṛṣabha and Ajita through Pārśva and Vardhamāna, and asking of them health, enlightenment and the highest concentration.

### 4 <a id="tea-avasyaka-sutra-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In confession (pratikramaṇa) the practitioner turns back from transgressions — 'may my wrongdoing be fruitless' (micchā mi dukkaḍaṃ) — and asks and grants forgiveness to all beings: 'I forgive all living beings; may all beings forgive me; I have friendship with all beings and enmity with none.'
