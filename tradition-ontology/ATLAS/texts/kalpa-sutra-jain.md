Originally the eighth chapter of the Daśāśrutaskandha: (1) the Jinacaritra — the lives of Mahāvīra (in detail: descent, the fourteen dreams, the transfer of the embryo, birth, renunciation, twelve years of austerity, omniscience, rainy seasons, nirvāṇa), Pārśva, Ariṣṭanemi and Ṛṣabha, with the intervals between the other Jinas; (2) the Sthavirāvalī, the line of elders from Mahāvīra's gaṇadharas; (3) the Sāmācārī, rules for monks during the rainy season. Mūrtipūjakas read it aloud during Paryuṣaṇa; richly illustrated manuscripts survive from the 14th c. onward.

## Teachings (7: skeleton 7)

### jinacaritra.mahavira <a id="tea-kalpa-sutra-jain-jinacaritra-mahavira"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Venerable Ascetic Mahāvīra descended from the Puṣpottara heavenly palace into the womb of the brāhmaṇī Devānandā at Brāhmaṇakuṇḍagrāma, who saw fourteen great dreams — an elephant, a bull, a lion, the anointing of Śrī, a garland, the moon, the sun, a banner, a full vase, a lotus lake, the ocean, a celestial palace, a heap of jewels and a smokeless fire. Śakra, reflecting that Arhats are never born in low, poor, beggarly or brāhmaṇa families but in noble kṣatriya clans, sent Hariṇaigameṣin to transfer the embryo to the womb of the kṣatriyāṇī Triśalā, who then saw the same fourteen dreams.

### jinacaritra.mahavira/2 <a id="tea-kalpa-sutra-jain-jinacaritra-mahavira-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Mahāvīra lived thirty years as a householder, then renounced; for twelve years and more he neglected his body, bore all hardships from gods, men and animals with equanimity, and lived homeless, possessionless, watchful in the five carefulnesses and three guards. In the thirteenth year, under a śāla tree near Jṛmbhikagrāma on the bank of the Ṛjupālikā, squatting in the sun after a fast without water, in pure meditation, he attained the infinite, supreme, unobstructed, complete and full knowledge and perception called kevala.

### jinacaritra.mahavira/3 <a id="tea-kalpa-sutra-jain-jinacaritra-mahavira-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

At Pāvā, in the palace of King Hastipāla's scribes, in his seventy-second year, Mahāvīra passed away, freed from all suffering. On that night the eighteen confederate kings of Kāśī and Kośala, saying 'since the light of intelligence is gone, let us make an illumination of material matter', lit lamps; on that night Indrabhūti Gautama cut the bond of affection for his teacher and attained omniscience. The community he left numbered 14,000 monks under Indrabhūti and 36,000 nuns under Candanā, with 159,000 laymen and 318,000 laywomen.

### jinacaritra.parsva <a id="tea-kalpa-sutra-jain-jinacaritra-parsva"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The life of Pārśva, the Arhat 'beloved of men': born at Vārāṇasī to Aśvasena and Vāmā, he renounced at thirty, attained omniscience after eighty-four days, taught for about seventy years and reached nirvāṇa on Mount Sammeta aged a hundred; his community had 16,000 monks and 38,000 nuns (under Puṣpacūlā).

### jinacaritra.rsabha <a id="tea-kalpa-sutra-jain-jinacaritra-rsabha"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The life of Ṛṣabha, 'the first king, the first mendicant, the first Jina, the first Tīrthaṅkara': born at the end of the third age, he taught the seventy-two arts, the sixty-four accomplishments of women and the hundred crafts, then renounced, and after omniscience taught for a vast period before nirvāṇa on Aṣṭāpada.

### samacari <a id="tea-kalpa-sutra-jain-samacari"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The rules of the rainy-season stay: a month and twenty nights after the rains begin the monks settle for the season and do not wander; they observe rules on alms, lodging and conduct; at Paryuṣaṇā they must forgive and ask forgiveness of one another — one who is reconciled is a 'fulfiller' (ārādhaka), one who is not is not, for the essence of monkhood is calm (uvasamasāraṃ khu sāmaṇṇaṃ).
