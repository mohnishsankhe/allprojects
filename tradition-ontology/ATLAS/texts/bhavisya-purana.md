**Attribution:** tradition: The Purāṇa of the future, spoken with the Aghora-kalpa (per the lists).; scholarly: Heterogeneous: the Brāhma-parvan preserves early material on sun worship and the Maga priests; the Pratisarga-parvan contains passages about second-millennium rulers and other late matter that scholars regard as 18th-19th c. additions; the Uttara-parvan is the Bhaviṣyottara (vrata-kathās).; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Vyāsa, end of Dvāpara; Scholarly account: layers from the mid 1st millennium CE to the 19th c. CE; (confidence low)
**Structure:** four parvans in the Venkateshwara print (Brāhma, Madhyama, Pratisarga, Uttara); 14,500 verses by the traditional count
**Availability:** digitized-original

The 'Purāṇa of the future': sun worship and the Magas, dharma and saṃskāras, prophecies of future kings, and (in the Uttara-parvan) the narratives of vows.
