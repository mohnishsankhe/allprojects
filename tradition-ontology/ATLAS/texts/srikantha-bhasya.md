Śrīkaṇṭha Śivācārya's Śaiva commentary on the Brahma Sūtras: Brahman is Śiva endowed with cit-śakti, material and efficient cause; souls and world are his body; the dahara (small space in the heart) is Śiva; Veda and Śaiva Āgama are equal in authority; the liberated attain equality with Śiva.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Śivārkamaṇidīpikā](sivarkamanidipika.md)

### 1.1.2 <a id="tea-srikantha-bhasya-1-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

On 'that from which the birth etc. of this (world) proceed': that cause is Parameśvara Śiva, who with his cit-śakti is both the material and the efficient cause of the world.

### 1.3.14 <a id="tea-srikantha-bhasya-1-3-14"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The small space (dahara) within the lotus of the heart (Chāndogya 8.1) is Brahman — Śiva as the supreme ether (paramākāśa), which is his cit-śakti — and is the object of the dahara meditation.

### 2.2.38 <a id="tea-srikantha-bhasya-2-2-38"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Śrīkaṇṭha declares that he sees no difference between the Veda and the Śivāgama: both are authoritative as coming from Śiva; what the sūtras reject is only a view of the Lord as merely the efficient cause.

### 4.4.17 <a id="tea-srikantha-bhasya-4-4-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The released soul attains all the Lord's powers except the activity of the world (creation, maintenance, dissolution): it becomes equal to Śiva in bliss and knowledge, not identical with him.
