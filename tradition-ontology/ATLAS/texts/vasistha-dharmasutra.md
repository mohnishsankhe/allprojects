A Dharmasūtra associated with the Ṛgveda. It places dharma in śruti and smṛti and, failing these, in the practice of the cultured, 'one free of desire' (1.4–6); teaches that the Vedas do not purify one without right conduct, and leave him at death as fledglings leave the nest (6.3); lets a student who has kept his vow enter whichever order he wishes (7.1–3); tells the renouncer to give up all rites but never the Veda (10.4); and ends: 'practise dharma, not adharma; speak truth, not untruth; look far, not near; look to the highest, not to what is not highest' (30.1).

### 1.4-6 <a id="tea-vasistha-dharmasutra-1-4-6"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Dharma is what is enjoined in śruti and smṛti; where these are lacking, the conduct of the cultured is authoritative; and the cultured one is he who is free from desire.

### 6.3 <a id="tea-vasistha-dharmasutra-6-3"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The Vedas do not purify one devoid of right conduct, even if he has learned them with all six limbs; at the time of death the metres leave him as fledglings leave the nest.

### 7.1-3 <a id="tea-vasistha-dharmasutra-7-1-3"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

There are four orders: student, householder, forest-hermit and wandering ascetic. Having studied one, two or all the Vedas without breaking his chastity, he may enter whichever of them he wishes.

### 10.4-5 <a id="tea-vasistha-dharmasutra-10-4-5"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

Let him renounce all rites, but never renounce the one Veda; by renouncing the Veda one becomes a śūdra. The one syllable is the supreme Brahman, breath-controls the highest austerity; begging is higher than fasting, compassion higher than giving.
