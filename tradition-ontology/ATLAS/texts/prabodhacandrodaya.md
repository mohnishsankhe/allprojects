An allegorical drama in six acts in which Viveka defeats Mahāmoha. In act 3 a Kāpālika appears beside a Digambara Jain and a Buddhist monk, boasts of worshipping Mahābhairava with human offerings and of his powers, and wins both over with liquor and his female companion; his doctrine is called Somasiddhānta.

## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 2 <a id="tea-prabodhacandrodaya-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The character Cārvāka tells his pupil that the Lokāyata is the only science: perception is the only means of knowledge; earth, water, fire and air are the principles; wealth and pleasure are the human aims; the elements themselves become conscious; there is no other world; death is release. Great Delusion says the doctrine was composed by Bṛhaspati and handed to Cārvāka, who spread it through his pupils.

### 3 <a id="tea-prabodhacandrodaya-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Kāpālika describes himself as adorned with human bones, dwelling in the cremation ground and eating from a human skull, and his worship of Mahābhairava as oblations of human flesh, brain and fat into the fire, breaking his fast with liquor drunk from a brahmin's skull, and offerings of human victims; his doctrine is called Somasiddhānta.
