A Dharmasūtra not attached to a surviving Kalpasūtra (associated with the Sāmaveda). It opens 'the Veda is the root of dharma' (1.1), reports that some allow a choice among the life-orders while 'the teachers' hold there is only one, the householder's, because only it is directly enjoined in the Veda (3.1, 3.36), lists the forty sacraments and the eight virtues of the self and declares that the virtues, not the sacraments alone, lead to Brahman (8.14–25), makes regional, caste and family customs authoritative where not contrary to the Veda (11.20), and gives the means of expiation (19.11–12).
**Commentaries on this text:** [Maskari-bhāṣya on Gautama](gautama-bhasya-maskarin.md)

### 8.14-25 <a id="tea-gautama-dharmasutra-8-14-25"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The forty sacraments are: the eight from conception to initiation, the four Vedic vows, the final bath, marriage, the five great sacrifices, and the seven cooked-food, seven oblation and seven Soma sacrifices. The eight virtues of the self are compassion for all beings, patience, freedom from envy, purity, calm, auspiciousness, freedom from meanness and freedom from greed. One who has the forty sacraments but not the eight virtues does not reach union with Brahman or his world; one who has only some sacraments but the eight virtues does.
