### 4.4 <a id="tea-platform-sutra-4-4"></a>

### 5.1 <a id="tea-platform-sutra-5-1"></a>

### 7.3 <a id="tea-platform-sutra-7-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The true teaching has no sudden or gradual; people's natures are sharp or dull — the deluded cultivate gradually, the awakened accord suddenly. From the beginning this gate has taken no-thought as its tenet, no-form as its substance and non-abiding as its root: no-form is being amid forms yet free of forms; no-thought is being amid thoughts yet not thinking; non-abiding is the original nature of people. No-thought does not mean thinking of nothing: if every thought is cut off one dies and is reborn elsewhere — a great error. 'Thought' means thinking of suchness; suchness is the substance of thought and thought its function.

> 先立無念為宗，無相為體，無住為本。無相者，於相而離相。無念者，於念而無念

### 8.1 <a id="tea-platform-sutra-8-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Seated meditation in this gate is not attaching to mind, not attaching to purity, and not immobility: mind is originally false; the nature is originally pure but covered by deluded thoughts; to set up an image of purity and call it practice is to be bound by purity. True non-movement is not seeing others' faults.

> 此門坐禪，元不著心，亦不著淨，亦不是不動

### 8.2 <a id="tea-platform-sutra-8-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

What is seated meditation (zuochan)? Outwardly, when thoughts do not arise toward any good or bad object, that is 'sitting'; inwardly seeing the unmoving self-nature is 'meditation'. What is dhyāna-samādhi? Being free of forms outwardly is dhyāna, not being confused inwardly is samādhi.

> 外於一切善惡境界，心念不起，名為坐；內見自性不動，名為禪
