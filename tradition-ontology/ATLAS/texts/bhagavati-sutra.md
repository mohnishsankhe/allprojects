The fifth and largest Aṅga, a vast collection of questions put to Mahāvīra, mostly by Gautama Indrabhūti, on souls, karma, matter, the cosmos, the gods and the path, with narrative sections: the questions of Skandaka and of the laywoman Jayantī, the schism of Jamāli, the followers of Pārśva who accept Mahāvīra, and (śataka 15) the history of Gośāla and his conflict with Mahāvīra. Its answers often distinguish respects (substance, place, time, state), foreshadowing the doctrine of standpoints.

### 1.1 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Gautama's first questions: is what is moving already moved, what is being produced already produced, what is being bound already bound, what is dying already dead? Mahāvīra answers yes — the action in progress is, in a respect, already accomplished.

### 2.1 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-2-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Skandaka's questions: Is the world finite or infinite? Mahāvīra answers by four respects — as substance the world is one and finite; in space it is finite; in time it is without end, having always been and always to be; in its modes (colours, forms and the rest) it is infinite. He answers likewise of the soul, of liberation (siddhi) and of the liberated one.

### 7.2 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-7-2"></a>

### 9.33 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-9-33"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Jamāli, Mahāvīra's nephew and son-in-law, falling ill asked his monks to spread a bed; hearing 'it is being spread' and then finding it not yet spread, he concluded that what is being done is not done, against Mahāvīra's teaching that what is being done is done. He would not accept correction and left with his followers; Priyadarśanā followed him, then returned.

### 12.2 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-12-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The laywoman Jayantī asks: is it better to sleep or to be awake? For the unrighteous sleep is better, for then they harm no one; for the righteous wakefulness is better. Is strength or weakness better? Likewise. Will all beings capable of liberation (bhavya) be liberated, and will the world then be empty of them? No — as the rows of the sky's points are endless, so the capable are never exhausted.

### 12.10 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-12-10"></a>

### 15 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-15"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Gosāla was the son of Maṅkhali, a picture-showman (maṅkha), and Bhaddā, born in the cowshed of the brāhmaṇa Gobahula at Saravaṇa, hence his name. In the second year of Mahāvīra's asceticism he attached himself to Mahāvīra at Nālandā as a disciple, and they wandered together for six years.

### 15 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-15-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

### 15/2 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-15-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

During their wandering Gosāla uprooted a sesame plant that Mahāvīra had said would bear pods; when it re-rooted and bore seeds as foretold, Gosāla concluded that all living beings are capable of reanimation (pauṭṭa-parihāra) — of dying and returning in the same or another body. Mahāvīra saved him with his cooling power from the fiery power (tejoleśyā) hurled at him by the ascetic Vesiyāyaṇa, whom Gosāla had mocked, and on his asking taught him how that fiery power is gained.

### 15/3 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-15-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

After leaving Mahāvīra, Gosāla gained the fiery power and learned the eightfold Mahānimitta (prognostication) from six 'wanderers of the directions' (disācara) who had extracted it from the Pūrvas; with it he could foretell gain and loss, pleasure and pain, life and death, and he proclaimed himself a Jina.

### 15/4 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-15-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Sixteen years before Mahāvīra's death, at Sāvatthī, Gosāla, living in the pottery of Hālāhalā, heard that Mahāvīra called him merely Maṅkhali's son and his former disciple. He warned Mahāvīra's disciple Ānanda with the parable of the merchants who, not content with the treasure of three anthills, broke open a fourth and were burnt by the glance of a serpent; then he confronted Mahāvīra, saying that the disciple Gosāla was dead and that he was Udāyi Kuṇḍiyāyaṇa, who had entered Gosāla's body as the seventh of his reanimations. He burnt two monks who rebuked him with his fiery power; the fire he hurled at Mahāvīra circled him and turned back into Gosāla's own body. Each foretold the other's death.

### 15/5 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-15-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

In his last days, burning with fever, Gosāla drank, sang, danced, repeatedly saluted Hālāhalā and sprinkled himself with clay-water; his followers explained these as the 'eight finalities' (carima): the last drink, last song, last dance, last salutation, the last great storm-cloud, the last sprinkling elephant, the last great battle of stones and thorns, and Gosāla himself as the last tīrthaṅkara of this age. He also taught four kinds of drink permitted and four not permitted (pāṇaga / apāṇaga), and answered the Ājīvika layman Ayampula's question in his delirium.

### 15/6 <a id="tea-bhagavati-sutra-15-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

On the seventh night Gosāla came to his senses, confessed that he was indeed Maṅkhali's son, Mahāvīra's disciple and not a Jina, that he had destroyed ascetics, and asked his followers to tie a rope to his left foot, drag his corpse through Sāvatthī and proclaim his falsity; the Ājīvikas performed this only symbolically inside the closed pottery and then gave him a great funeral.
