A commentary on the Sāṃkhyasaptati: liberation comes from knowing the twenty-five principles; the Saptati was written because the Ṣaṣṭitantra is too extensive; it quotes the verse that the knower of the twenty-five principles is liberated in whatever stage of life.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 1 <a id="tea-jayamangala-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The purpose of the treatise is liberation through knowledge of the principles, which are twenty-five: 'the knower of the twenty-five principles, delighting in whatever stage of life, with matted hair, shaven head or tuft, is liberated, no doubt'. Because the Ṣaṣṭitantra is extensive, the Seventy was begun for those who like brevity. Liberation brings the cessation of suffering; the one who does not know the principles, struck by sufferings, keeps transmigrating.

### 72 <a id="tea-jayamangala-72"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

What is said in the Seventy covers the topics of the entire Ṣaṣṭitantra, but without tales and without the objections of opponents — pure topics useful for bondage and liberation; hence the Seventy is complete.
