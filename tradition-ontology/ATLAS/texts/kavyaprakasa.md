Mammaṭa's synthesis of poetics in ten chapters: the purposes and causes of poetry (1.2–3), its definition as faultless word and meaning with qualities (1.4), the three powers of words (denotation, indication, suggestion), rasa and bhāva (ch. 4) — love toward a deity is a bhāva, not a rasa — and śānta as the ninth rasa with dispassion as its stable emotion; the most commented work of the tradition.

## Teachings (6: skeleton 6)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-kavyaprakasa-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Victorious is the speech of the poet (Bhāratī), which brings forth a creation free from the rules made by fate (niyati), made of joy alone, dependent on nothing else, and lovely with the nine rasas.

> नियतिकृतनियमरहितां ह्लादैकमयीमनन्यपरतन्त्राम्। नवरसरुचिरां निर्मितिमादधती भारती कवेर्जयति॥

### 1.2 <a id="tea-kavyaprakasa-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Poetry is for fame, for wealth, for knowledge of conduct, for warding off evil, for immediate supreme bliss (sadyaḥ paranirvṛti), and for instruction in the manner of a beloved wife (kāntāsammita); the vṛtti calls the bliss arising from relishing rasa, in which all other objects melt away, the crown of all these purposes.

> काव्यं यशसेऽर्थकृते व्यवहारविदे शिवेतरक्षतये। सद्यः परनिर्वृतये कान्तासम्मिततयोपदेशयुजे॥

### 1.3 <a id="tea-kavyaprakasa-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The cause of poetry is genius (śakti), skill (nipuṇatā) gained from observing the world, the sciences and poems, and practice under the instruction of those who know poetry.

> शक्तिर्निपुणता लोकशास्त्रकाव्याद्यवेक्षणात्। काव्यज्ञशिक्षयाभ्यास इति हेतुस्तदुद्भवे॥

### 4 (after the eight rasas) <a id="tea-kavyaprakasa-4-santa"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Śānta, whose stable emotion is dispassion (nirveda), is also the ninth rasa.

### 4 (bhāva kārikā) <a id="tea-kavyaprakasa-4-bhava"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Love (rati) directed to gods and the like (sages, teachers, kings, sons), and a transient state when made prominent, are called bhāva, not rasa; love directed to a beloved, when manifested, is the erotic rasa.

> रतिर्देवादिविषया व्यभिचारी तथाञ्जितः। भावः प्रोक्तः

### 4 (on the rasa-sūtra) <a id="tea-kavyaprakasa-4-rasasutra"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Mammaṭa sets out the four explanations of the rasa-sūtra — Lollaṭa's intensification, Śaṅkuka's inference, Bhaṭṭa Nāyaka's enjoyment and Abhinavagupta's manifestation — and adopts the last: rasa is the stable emotion manifested by generalized determinants and relished by the spectator.
