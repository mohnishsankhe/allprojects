## Teachings (3: skeleton 3)

### 2 <a id="tea-devadaha-sutta-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Some ascetics hold that whatever a person feels is due to past deeds; by burning up old deeds through austerity and doing no new ones, deeds, suffering and feeling come to an end. 'Such is the doctrine of the Nigaṇṭhas.' The Buddha questions them and shows the doctrine does not hold.

### 2-22 <a id="tea-devadaha-sutta-2-22"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Nigaṇṭhas hold that whatever a person feels is due to past deeds, so by burning away old deeds with austerity and doing no new ones all suffering will be exhausted. Asked whether they know they existed before, did evil, and how much remains, they cannot answer; if pleasure and pain were caused by past deeds, the Nigaṇṭhas who now feel severe pain must have done evil deeds. Their striving is fruitless.

_level: conventional · standpoint: polemical · path: action · stage: all · types: dispute, karma-liberation_

### 23-30 <a id="tea-devadaha-sutta-23-30"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

How striving is fruitful: a monk does not overwhelm himself with pain, nor reject righteous pleasure; knowing that the source of suffering is craving, he develops equanimity toward it, or striving, as an arrow-smith straightens a shaft by heat only until it is straight; then the gradual training up to the destruction of the effluents.

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: general · stage: all · types: practice_
