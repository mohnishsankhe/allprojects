### convergence <a id="tea-gyalwai-shunglam-convergence"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Though many names are given — co-emergent union, the amulet box, the fivefold, one taste, the four letters, pacification, cutting, the great perfection, the Madhyamaka instruction on the view — when examined by a yogin learned in scripture and reasoning and experienced in practice, they come down to one intent.

### mind <a id="tea-gyalwai-shunglam-mind"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Resting on the mind itself, the meditator finds it to be mere clarity and awareness, without form, in which anything can appear; then, examining that very mind, finds it lacking inherent existence — its conventional nature is clarity and knowing, its ultimate nature emptiness.

### two-approaches <a id="tea-gyalwai-shunglam-two-approaches"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Mahāmudrā is approached in two ways: the sūtra approach, meditating on emptiness as taught by Nāgārjuna and his followers, and the mantra approach, the clear light of great bliss arising when the winds enter, abide and dissolve in the central channel through skilful means.
