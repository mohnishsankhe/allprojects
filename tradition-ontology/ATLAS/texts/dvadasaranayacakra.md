Mallavādin's 'wheel of standpoints with twelve spokes': twelve philosophical positions (drawn from all the darśanas) are set out so that each refutes the one before it and is refuted by the next; no spoke stands alone, and the whole wheel is the Jain many-sided view. The root text is lost and is reconstructed from Siṃhasūri's commentary.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Nyāyāgamānusāriṇī (Siṃhasūri)](nyayagamanusarini.md)

## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### ara-1-12 <a id="tea-dvadasaranayacakra-ara-1-12"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Twelve positions — beginning with the view that all is to be known by practice alone and running through determinism, nature, time, Īśvara, the self, Sāṃkhya, Vaiśeṣika, Buddhist and grammarian views — are arranged as spokes of a wheel, each overturned by the next; the Jain view is the whole wheel, in which each is valid in its place.
