The Dharmasūtra of the Āpastamba Kalpasūtra (praśnas 28–29). It grounds dharma in the agreement of those who know it and in the Vedas (1.1.1.2–3), sets out the four classes and the student's life, contains a chapter on the attainment of the self and the removal of the faults that burn beings through yoga (1.8.22–23), treats the householder, inheritance and the king, lists four orders of life while criticizing the renouncer's claim of liberation by knowledge alone (2.9.21–24), teaches that the classes rise or fall in the next birth by conduct (2.5.11.10–11), and ends that the rest of dharma should be learned from women and from all classes (2.11.29.15).

### 1.1.1.4-8 <a id="tea-apastamba-dharmasutra-1-1-1-4-8"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

There are four classes — brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, vaiśya and śūdra; of these each earlier one is superior by birth. For those not śūdras and not of wicked deeds there are initiation, Vedic study and the setting-up of the fires, and rites bear fruit; the śūdra's is the service of the other classes; the higher the class, the greater its good.

### 1.1.1.9-17 <a id="tea-apastamba-dharmasutra-1-1-1-9-17"></a>

Initiation is a consecration, by Vedic authority, of one who seeks knowledge. One should seek as consecrator a teacher of good family and learning, composed, who has not strayed from dharma; he from whom one gathers the dharmas is the ācārya — never harm him, for he gives birth by knowledge, and that birth is the best.

### 1.8.23.3-6 <a id="tea-apastamba-dharmasutra-1-8-23-3-6"></a>
`sourced` · confidence high

The destruction of the faults in this life is rooted in yoga; having removed the faults that burn beings, the wise man attains safety. These are anger, exultation, wrath, greed, delusion, hypocrisy, malice, lying, gluttony, slander, envy, lust, rage, lack of self-control and lack of yoga; their removal is rooted in yoga. Freedom from anger and the rest — truthfulness, moderation, ungrudging compassion, contentment, honesty, gentleness, tranquillity, self-control, friendliness to all and non-injury — are agreed for all the orders.
