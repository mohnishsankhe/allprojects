Eight short 'gifts' (pāhuḍa) ascribed to Kundakunda: on faith (Daṃsaṇa), scripture (Sutta), conduct (Caritta), understanding (Bodha), inner state (Bhāva), liberation (Mokkha), the monastic sign (Liṅga) and virtue (Sīla). They state that dharma is rooted in right faith, that the naked form is the only path to liberation, that a clothed person cannot be liberated even if he were a Tīrthaṅkara, and that women cannot take full renunciation.

### darsana.2 <a id="tea-astapahuda-darsana-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Dharma has right faith (darśana) as its root, taught the Jina to his disciples; hearing this with your own ears, do not revere one without right faith.

### sutta.18 <a id="tea-astapahuda-sutta-18"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The monk has the form of a newborn child; he does not accept even a sesame husk in his hands; if he takes little or much, he goes to the lowest (nigoda) births.

### sutta.23 <a id="tea-astapahuda-sutta-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the Jina's teaching one who wears clothes is not liberated even if he were a Tīrthaṅkara; nakedness is the path to liberation, all others are wrong paths.

### sutta.24 <a id="tea-astapahuda-sutta-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

In women, in the private parts, between the breasts, at the navel and in the armpits subtle living beings are said to arise; how then can they have full renunciation (pravrajyā)?
