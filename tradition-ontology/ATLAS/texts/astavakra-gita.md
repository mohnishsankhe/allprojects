**Structure:** 20 chapters, 298 verses in the vulgate (1: 20, 2: 25, 3: 14, 4: 6, 5: 4, 6: 4, 7: 5, 8: 4, 9: 8, 10: 8, 11: 8, 12: 8, 13: 7, 14: 4, 15: 20, 16: 11, 17: 20, 18: 100, 19: 8, 20: 14). Some editions append a 6-verse 'saṅkhyākrama' listing the chapters and verse counts.
**Availability:** digitized-original

Aṣṭāvakra answers Janaka's question how knowledge, liberation and dispassion are attained: you are not the elements or the body but the one witness, pure consciousness; rest in awareness and you are free now; bondage and freedom are only notions ('as one thinks, so one becomes'). Janaka voices his awakening (ch. 2 and ch. 6-20 in part), and the text describes the liberated one (18) who is beyond effort, meditation, duty and even the idea of liberation.

_Notes: Distinct from the 'Aṣṭāvakrīya' episode of the Mahābhārata (Āraṇyakaparvan 3.132-134; see src:astavakriya-mahabharata), where Aṣṭāvakra defeats Bandin at Janaka's court; the 1896 Pañcadaśagītā collection prints that epic episode as an 'Aṣṭāvakra Gītā' in three chapters. In recent times the text was loved by Vivekananda and read in Ramana Maharshi's circle (recent reception)._
