Vijñānabhikṣu's extensive sub-commentary on the bhāṣya. It calls the bhāṣya 'the essence of all the Veda spoken by Vedavyāsa' and holds Sāṃkhya and the other darśanas to be contained in it like rivers in the ocean (intro.); cites the Yoga-Yājñavalkya for Hiraṇyagarbha as first teacher (1.1); argues for the mutual reflection of consciousness and buddhi against a single-reflection view (1.7); gives its own account of the four samprajñāta samādhis (1.17); and holds that knowledge of the Supreme Lord is the swift cause of liberation (1.51).

## Teachings (5: skeleton 5)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-yogavarttika-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'Anuśāsana' is the teaching of what has been taught, showing that the śāstra rests on a teacher: the Yoga-Yājñavalkya teaches that Hiraṇyagarbha is the first teacher ('Hiraṇyagarbha is the speaker of yoga, no other is ancient'). The bhāṣya on the whole śāstra is by the blessed Bādarāyaṇa (Vyāsa), for the good of all. Scripture and smṛti enjoin yoga as a cause of liberation; the Brahma-mīmāṃsā and Sāṃkhya examine knowledge at length but yoga only briefly, so Patañjali sets out yoga in detail.

### 1.7 <a id="tea-yogavarttika-1-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Against one who says that the result of cognition is a reflection of consciousness (cicchāyā) that belongs to the buddhi alone, Vijñānabhikṣu argues that this abandons the plain sense of the bhāṣya's word 'pauruṣeya', that a mere reflection cannot be a cognition or do the work of illumination, and that, since mutual reflection is established by śruti and smṛti, the result is consciousness itself as qualified by the reflection of the mind's activity.

### 1.17 <a id="tea-yogavarttika-1-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Ānanda is a particular joy (hlāda) arising from the preponderance of sattva as one ascends from the stage of reflection; in it the mind thinks 'I am happy'. Vijñānabhikṣu rejects the view of 'someone' that hlāda means the senses and that the bliss-samādhi has the senses as its object, and denies any subdivision into 'with bliss' and 'without bliss'. Asmitā is an awareness whose sole object is the self (ekātmikā saṃvit), and is twofold: with the individual self or with the Supreme Self as object. The Mokṣadharma verse on reasoning, discrimination and reflection arising in the first yoga is cited.

### 1.51 <a id="tea-yogavarttika-1-51"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Closing the first pāda: in the Sāṃkhya, liberation is taught from knowledge of the principle of the self (jīva) alone, without asamprajñāta yoga; in this śāstra, for a liberation free of obstacles, doubt and delay, knowledge of the Supreme Lord (parameśvara-vidyā) is shown as the swift cause of liberation, together with asamprajñāta yoga.
