The hymn of the long-haired one (keśin) who bears fire, poison(-drink) and heaven and earth, who is called this light; of the wind-girdled silent sages (munis) clad in soiled brown, who follow the wind's course when the gods enter them; 'ecstatic with munihood we have mounted the winds — you mortals see only our bodies'; the muni flies through the air seeing all forms, dwells by both oceans, knows the minds of apsarases, gandharvas and beasts, and drank from the cup with Rudra.

_Notes: Each verse is ascribed to one of seven wind-girdled munis: Jūti, Vātajūti, Viprajūti, Vṛṣāṇaka, Karikrata, Etaśa, Ṛśyaśṛṅga (checked in the local text's header)._
