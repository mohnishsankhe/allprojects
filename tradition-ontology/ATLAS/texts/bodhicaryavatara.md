Śāntideva's 'Entry into the Bodhisattva's Conduct' in ten chapters: the benefits of the awakening mind, confession, adopting the awakening mind, conscientiousness, guarding introspection, patience, vigour, meditation (with equalizing and exchanging self and other), wisdom (the two truths and the refutation of the realists, Yogācāra, Sāṃkhya, Nyāya and the self) and dedication.

### 8.1-4 <a id="tea-bodhicaryavatara-8-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 8.129-131 <a id="tea-bodhicaryavatara-8-129-131"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

All who are unhappy in the world are so through desiring their own happiness; all who are happy are so through desiring others' happiness. What need of more words? See the difference between the fool who works for himself and the sage who works for others. Without exchanging one's own happiness for others' suffering, buddhahood is not achieved, and even in saṃsāra there is no happiness.

> ye kecid duḥkhitā loke sarve te svasukhecchayā | ye kecit sukhitā loke sarve te 'nyasukhecchayā || bahunā vā kimuktena dṛśyatāmidamantaram | svārthārthinaśca bālasya muneścānyārthakāriṇaḥ || na nāma sādhyaṃ buddhatvaṃ saṃsāre'pi kutaḥ sukham | svasukhasyānyaduḥkhena parivartamakurvataḥ

### 8.134-136 <a id="tea-bodhicaryavatara-8-134-136"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Whatever calamities, sufferings and fears there are in the world all come from grasping at a self; what use is this grasping to me? Without giving up the self one cannot give up suffering, as one cannot avoid burning without giving up fire. Therefore, to calm my own suffering and the suffering of others, I give myself to others and accept others as myself.

### 9.33-35 <a id="tea-bodhicaryavatara-9-33-35"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

By habituation to emptiness the habit of taking things as real is removed; by habituation to 'nothing exists' that too is later abandoned. When no thing is found which is conceived as not existing, how can non-existence, without a basis, stand before the mind? When neither being nor non-being remains before the mind, then, since there is no other way, it is pacified without an object.

### 9.119-126 <a id="tea-bodhicaryavatara-9-119-126"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'Īśvara is the cause of the world' — then tell me who Īśvara is. If the elements, so be it — why labour over a mere name? But they are many, impermanent, inert, not divine, trodden on and impure; the earth and the rest are not Īśvara. If the Lord creates without wishing, he depends on another; if wishing, he depends on his wish — so where is his lordship in creating?
