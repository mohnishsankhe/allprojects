Abhinavagupta's 105 āryā verses retelling, 'by the view of Śiva's teaching' (v. 3), the older Ādhārakārikās / Paramārthasāra ascribed to Ādiśeṣa: the four eggs of manifestation, the Lord as the embodied enjoyer, the mirror image of the 36 tattvas, the kañcukas and the three coverings, bondage and liberation as Śiva's play, liberation as the manifestation of one's own power, the conduct and inner worship of the liberated, and the knower's death.

### 2-3 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-2-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A disciple wandering in the wheel of suffering from dwelling in the womb to death asked the Lord Ādhāra about the highest truth; the teacher answered with the Ādhāra-kārikās; Abhinavagupta tells their essence by the view of Śiva's teaching.

### 11-13 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-11-13"></a>

In the supreme principle - light, full, resting in itself, great bliss, filled with will, knowledge and action, free of all thought-constructs, without rise or dissolution - the universe of thirty-six principles shines; as a city appears in a mirror undivided yet as if divided, so the world appears from the stainless awareness of Bhairava.

### 16-17 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-16-17"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Taking on māyā, consciousness becomes stained - the person, the bound soul - tied by time, limited agency (kalā), restriction (niyati), attachment and limited knowledge: 'only now, only a little, only this, I know with all of myself' - māyā with the six sheaths is called the inner covering of the individual.

### 24 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-24"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

### 27-29 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-27-29"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The distinctions of cognition, inner controller, breath, cosmic body, body, class and individual are mere convention and in the highest sense do not exist; as the snake is not in the rope yet causes terror even to death, so merit and demerit, heaven and hell, birth and death, pleasure and pain, caste and stage of life arise in the self, though unreal, by the force of error.

### 60 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-60"></a>

### 69-71 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-69-71"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Eating whatever comes, clothed in whatever, peaceful, dwelling anywhere, the self of all beings is liberated; even performing a hundred thousand horse-sacrifices or killing a hundred thousand brāhmaṇas, the knower of the highest is not touched by merit or sin; free of pride, joy, anger, lust, despair, fear, greed and delusion, without hymns or oblations, he should wander as if dull.

### 74-80 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-74-80"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

For the liberated, the body of thirty-six principles, or any other body or a pot, is the temple; he worships there the supreme Bhairava with his power, using the pure substances of self-awareness; offering the seeds of difference into the fire of consciousness is oblation; the ever-arisen creation of forms is meditation; revolving the worlds and principles within awareness is japa; seeing all with equal vision, holding the skull of the knowable in the cremation ground of the universe, is his vow.

### 83 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-83"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Whether in a holy place or in the house of a dog-eater, even with memory lost, abandoning the body, one liberated at the moment of knowledge goes to isolation, sorrow destroyed.

> tīrthe śvapacagṛhe vā naṣṭasmṛtir api parityajan deham / jñānasamakālamuktaḥ kaivalyaṃ yāti hataśokaḥ

### 90-95 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-90-95"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The last moment is only a contributing factor for the deluded, not the cause of their destiny; at the time of knowledge the self appears as it is, and so it remains at the body's fall; though the senses fail, memory is lost and pain strikes the vital points at death, the knower does not fall from the supreme reality.

### 96-102 <a id="tea-paramarthasara-96-102"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One who receives this path from the teacher's mouth through a most intense descent of power is Śiva at once, without obstacle; one who ascends step by step becomes Śiva at the end; one who dies midway, fallen from yoga, enjoys worlds of pleasure and in a later birth completes the path.
