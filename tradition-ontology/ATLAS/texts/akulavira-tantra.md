# Akulavīratantra

**Original title:** अकुलवीरतन्त्र

**Lineages:** [Yoginī Kaula (Yoginīkaula) of Matsyendra](../lineages/yogini-kaula.md), [Nāth tradition (Nātha Siddhas; Nāth / Kānphaṭa Yogīs)](../lineages/natha.md)

**Availability:** digitized-original

A short tantra in two recensions praising the Akulavīra — the one reality beyond all distinctions, the same in everything, beyond mind, caste, ritual, fasting and pilgrimage. It teaches that the self is god, goddess, disciple and guru, that powers and alchemy are not to be grasped, and that the Kaula path is of two kinds, 'made' (kṛtaka: the aroused kuṇḍalī) and 'innate' (sahaja: abiding in samarasa).

### a.3-6 <a id="tea-akulavira-tantra-a-3-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

As all rivers go to the ocean, so all dharmas dissolve in the Akulavīra, the support of all; those who take refuge in all dharmas do not find innate bliss (sahajānanda); seized by impurities and covered by the blindness of great Māyā, content with nets of scripture, they do not find the peaceful state, the partless guru of the Kaulas.

### a.7-10 <a id="tea-akulavira-tantra-a-7-10"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Those who argue — Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika, Buddhist arhats, the Somasiddhānta, Mīmāṃsā, the five streams, the Left, the Siddhānta and the Right, the Itihāsa and Purāṇa, the Bhūta and Gāruḍa tantras, all the Śaiva Āgamas with their rituals — all are full of conceptual constructs (vikalpa), false and meaningless; lacking the Akulavīra they do not free from saṃsāra.

### a.15-23 <a id="tea-akulavira-tantra-a-15-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Akulavīra is not in the supports or aims, not in the heart, mouth, uvula or palate, not in iḍā or piṅgalā, not in the navel, throat or head, not in opening the eyes or gazing at the nose-tip, not in inhalation, retention or exhalation, not in piercing the bindu or the knots, not in invocation or dismissal, postures or seals; neither near nor far, full nor empty — where there is no aim (lakṣya), that is called the Akulavīra.

### a.24-26 <a id="tea-akulavira-tantra-a-24-26"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One who abides thus in samarasa is Brahmā, Hari, Īśa and Rudra, Īśvara and Śiva, the supreme god, sun, moon and fire; he is Sāṃkhya and the Purāṇas, the Arhat and the Buddha; himself goddess and god, himself disciple and guru, himself meditation and meditator, himself the deity everywhere.

### a.30-41 <a id="tea-akulavira-tantra-a-30-41"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

When one sees the great Akulavīra everywhere, inside and outside, of one form in all that moves and stands — waveless, free of appearance and of distinctions of state, beyond the principles, spotless, all-knowing, full, imperishable, free of cause and effect, beyond Māyā, supportless, beyond 'is' and 'is not', where there is no mind, intellect, time, power, Śiva or senses — then all is samarasa and full, the Akulavīra alone.

### a.42-48 <a id="tea-akulavira-tantra-a-42-48"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One in whom this liberation abides is freed from worldly bonds: for him there is no mother, father, kin or deity, no sacrifice, fasting, rite or distinction of caste; no recitation, worship, bathing, oblation or practice, no rule, ancestor-rite, pilgrimage or vow, no fruit of dharma or adharma. Abandoning worldly conduct and the made bonds of the pledges, the yogī is without conduct (nirācāra), free of the conduct of 'animals'.

### b.43-45 <a id="tea-akulavira-tantra-b-43-45"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

In the Kaula path there are two, the made (kṛtakā) and the innate (sahajā): the kuṇḍalī is to be known as the made, the innate abides in samarasa; the made consists in the relation of impelled and impeller and is brought down by seals and mantras, by oblation, ear-whispered mantra and incense.

### b.52-54 <a id="tea-akulavira-tantra-b-52-54"></a>

### b.76-78 <a id="tea-akulavira-tantra-b-76-78"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The Vedas, Siddhāntas and many kinds of śāstras are all delusion and bodily toil without purpose; the proud, seized by the conceit of learning and pondering many books, go to a bad end; how can the proud have knowledge even with hundreds of millions of books?
