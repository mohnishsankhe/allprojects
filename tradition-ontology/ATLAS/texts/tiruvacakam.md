Māṇikkavācakar's 'Sacred Utterance': 51 poems (c. 656 verses) of longing, confession, ecstatic surrender and praise of Śiva who came to him as guru at Tirupperunturai; opens with the Civapurāṇam; includes the Tiruvempāvai and Tiruppaḷḷiyeḻucci. The tradition says Śiva himself wrote it down.

## Teachings (8: skeleton 8)

### 1 <a id="tea-tiruvacakam-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Praise to namaccivāya, praise to the feet of the Lord; praise to the feet of him who never leaves my heart even for the blink of an eye.

> நமச்சிவாய வாஅழ்க நாதன்தாள் வாழ்க இமைப்பொழுதும் என்நெஞ்சில் நீங்காதான் தாள்வாழ்க

### 1 <a id="tea-tiruvacakam-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Born as grass, shrub, worm, tree, many beasts, bird, snake, stone, man, demon, spirit, asura, sage and god — in every moving and unmoving birth I have been born and am weary; today, seeing your golden feet, I am freed.

### 3 <a id="tea-tiruvacakam-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

Tiruvaṇṭappakuti: the countless universes are like motes in a sunbeam before the Lord; he is inside and outside all, and he came to me, a mere dog, and made me his.

### 5 <a id="tea-tiruvacakam-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Tiruccatakam: the devotee weeps and melts, his hair stands on end, he dances and sings — yet laments that his heart has not melted enough; the melting of the heart is the sign of grace.

_level: conventional · standpoint: experiential · path: devotion · stage: all · types: powers-experiences_

Tiruvempāvai: young women wake one another before dawn in the month of Mārkaḻi to bathe and sing Śiva's praise, and pray for husbands who are his devotees.
