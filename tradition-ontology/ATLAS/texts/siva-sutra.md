# Śivasūtra

`src:siva-sutra` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Original title:** शिवसूत्र
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Spanda (the doctrine of vibration)](../lineages/spanda.md), [Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual Śaiva tantra of Kashmir)](../lineages/kashmir-saivism.md)
**Genre:** sūtra (revealed)
**Authors:** 
  - [Vasugupta](../teachers/vasugupta.md) — role: revealer; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: Revealed by Śiva to Vasugupta: Paramaśiva told him in a dream of a secret on a great rock on Mahādeva mountain, which turned over at his touch (Kṣemarāja, Śivasūtravimarśinī intro.); the sūtras 'appeared by a siddha's command' (Bhāskara, Vārttika 1.3).; scholarly: Promulgated by Vasugupta, c. 850 CE.; confidence: high
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Revealed to Vasugupta.; Scholarly account: c. 850 CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** 3 sections: 22 + 10 + 45 = 77 sūtras in Kṣemarāja's recension (called unmeṣa); 23 + 10 + 46 = 79 in Bhāskara's (called prakāśa): he divides 1.16 in two, adds 3.15 'visargasvābhāvyād abahiḥ sthites tatsthitiḥ', and orders 3.22-23 differently.
**Availability:** digitized-original

Seventy-seven terse aphorisms (Kṣemarāja's recension) on consciousness as the self, bondage as limited knowledge, the states of waking, dream, sleep and the fourth, the powers and pitfalls of yoga, and the life of the realized yogin as a dancer on the stage of the inner self. Kṣemarāja reads its three sections as the śāmbhava, śākta and āṇava means.
