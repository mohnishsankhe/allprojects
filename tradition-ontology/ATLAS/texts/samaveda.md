# Sāmaveda Saṃhitā (Kauthuma)

**Alternate titles:** Sāmaveda, Chandas-ārcika
**Original title:** सामवेदसंहिता
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Vedic Śrauta tradition (the Saṃhitā lineages)](../lineages/vedic-srauta.md), [Kauthuma śākhā (Sāmaveda)](../lineages/sakha-kauthuma.md), [Rāṇāyanīya śākhā (Sāmaveda)](../lineages/sakha-ranayaniya.md)
**Genre:** saṃhitā (chant texts)
**Authors:** 
  - [Jaimini](../teachers/jaimini.md) — role: compiler; attribution: traditional
**Attribution:** tradition: authorless (apauruṣeya) revelation seen by the ṛṣis; arranged by Vyāsa; Jaimini received the Sāmaveda from Vyāsa (Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.4.21); scholarly: a liturgical anthology drawn largely from the Ṛgveda, arranged for the udgātṛ's chants; confidence: high
**Dates:** Scholarly account: collection c. 1200–1000 BCE (low confidence); (confidence low)
**Structure:** Pūrvārcika (āgneya, aindra and pāvamāna parvans; then the Āraṇyaka section and the Mahānāmnī verses; c. 650 verses) and Uttarārcika (c. 1,225 verses in groups, mostly triplets); about 1,875 verses in the usual Kauthuma count (c. 1,800 in Griffith's edition), only some 75 not found in the Ṛgveda (counts differ).
**Availability:** digitized-original

The Veda of chants: verses (ṛc), nearly all taken from the Ṛgveda (mostly books 8 and 9), arranged as the texts (yoni) of melodies (sāman) sung by the udgātṛ priests at the soma sacrifice. The verse-books (ārcika) are the libretto; the melodies themselves are in the song-books (gāna). The tradition treats the Sāmaveda as the root of chant and of music.
