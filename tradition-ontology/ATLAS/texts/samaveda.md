# Sāmaveda Saṃhitā (Kauthuma)

**Structure:** Pūrvārcika (āgneya, aindra and pāvamāna parvans; then the Āraṇyaka section and the Mahānāmnī verses; c. 650 verses) and Uttarārcika (c. 1,225 verses in groups, mostly triplets); about 1,875 verses in the usual Kauthuma count (c. 1,800 in Griffith's edition), only some 75 not found in the Ṛgveda (counts differ).
**Availability:** digitized-original

The Veda of chants: verses (ṛc), nearly all taken from the Ṛgveda (mostly books 8 and 9), arranged as the texts (yoni) of melodies (sāman) sung by the udgātṛ priests at the soma sacrifice. The verse-books (ārcika) are the libretto; the melodies themselves are in the song-books (gāna). The tradition treats the Sāmaveda as the root of chant and of music.
