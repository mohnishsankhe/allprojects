# Āryabhaṭīya

`src:aryabhatiya` · `skeleton` · confidence high

**Alternate titles:** Āryasiddhānta, Āryāṣṭaśata
**Original title:** आर्यभटीय
**Language:** Sanskrit
**Family:** vedic
**Lineages:** [Jyotiṣa (the science of the lights)](../lineages/jyotisa.md)
**Genre:** siddhānta
**Authors:** 
  - [Āryabhaṭa](../teachers/aryabhata.md) — role: author; attribution: accepted
**Attribution:** tradition: knowledge from Brahmā (Svayambhū), recovered by Āryabhaṭa at Kusumapura; scholarly: Āryabhaṭa (b. 476 CE); confidence: high
**Dates:** Tradition's account: Āryabhaṭa was 23 when 3600 years of the Kali age had passed (Kālakriyā 10); Scholarly account: c. 499 CE (the year the text names as its author's 23rd); (confidence high)
**Structure:** 4 pādas: Gītikā 13 (10 gīti verses + 3), Gaṇita 33, Kālakriyā 25, Gola 50 = 121 verses
**Availability:** digitized-original

Āryabhaṭa's 121 āryā verses in four parts: the Gītikāpāda (alphabetic numerals, the kalpa and planetary revolutions), Gaṇitapāda (mathematics), Kālakriyāpāda (time-reckoning, the equal quarters of the yuga, planetary motion) and Golapāda (the sphere, the rotation of the earth, eclipses by shadow). It opens in homage to Ka, the one and many, the supreme Brahman, and closes saying its knowledge was raised from the ocean of true and false knowledge by the grace of Brahmā.
**Commentaries on this text:** [Āryabhaṭīyabhāṣya of Bhāskara I](aryabhatiya-bhasya-bhaskara.md), [Āryabhaṭīyabhāṣya of Nīlakaṇṭha](aryabhatiya-bhasya-nilakantha.md)

## Teachings (9: skeleton 9)

### 1.1 <a id="tea-aryabhatiya-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Bowing to Ka (Brahmā) — one and many, the true deity, the supreme Brahman — Āryabhaṭa sets forth three things: mathematics, time-reckoning and the sphere.

> praṇipatyaikam anekaṃ kaṃ satyāṃ devatāṃ paraṃ brahma | āryabhaṭas trīṇi gadati gaṇitaṃ kālakriyāṃ golam ||

### 1.5 <a id="tea-aryabhatiya-1-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

A day of Brahmā contains fourteen Manus, and a Manu seventy-two yugas; from the beginning of the kalpa to the Thursday of the Bhārata (war), six Manus, twenty-seven yugas and three quarters of a yuga had elapsed.

### 3.9 <a id="tea-aryabhatiya-3-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The first half of the yuga is utsarpiṇī (ascending) and the latter half apasarpiṇī (descending); suṣamā is in the middle of the yuga and duṣṣamā at its beginning and end, reckoned from the moon's apogee.

### 3.11 <a id="tea-aryabhatiya-3-11"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The yuga, year, month and day all began together at the beginning of the bright fortnight of Caitra; this time is without beginning or end, and is inferred through the planets and stars in the sphere.

> yugavarṣamāsadivasāḥ samaṃ pravṛttās tu caitraśuklādeḥ | kālo 'yam anādyanto grahabhair anumīyate kṣetre ||

### 4.9 <a id="tea-aryabhatiya-4-9"></a>
