one of the thirty-seven works of Madhva collected as the Sarvamūla ('root of all'); Madhva's commentary on the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, reading it as teaching Viṣṇu's supremacy and his difference from souls and matter, often by etymological explanation of names and by quotations from other scriptures. Madhva's text of the Upaniṣad includes the verses that Advaita attributes to Gauḍapāda's Kārikā (read by him as śruti); he explains Viśva, Taijasa, Prājña and Turīya as four forms of Viṣṇu, and the tradition's account of its origin is Varuṇa, as a frog, praising the fourfold Nārāyaṇa.

### 2.8 <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-bhasya-madhva-2-8"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

When the soul, put to sleep by beginningless māyā, awakens, it knows the unborn, sleepless, dreamless, non-dual (Lord) — glossed (quoting the Prakāśikā): when the soul, put to sleep by Viṣṇu's will, the beginningless māyā, is awakened by that same will, it sees Viṣṇu.

### 2.9 <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-bhasya-madhva-2-9"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

On 'prapañco yadi vidyeta ... māyāmātram idaṃ dvaitam advaitaṃ paramārthataḥ': 'advaita' is the supreme Brahman, known as it truly is; 'dvaita' is what is known otherwise, wrongly, by the ignorant; 'māyāmātra' means made by his will (māyā = icchā); 'paramārthataḥ' means from the Supreme Lord. The 'prapañca' that ceases is the soul's relation of owner to body; this wrong knowledge, produced by his will, ceases by his will when Brahman is known.

### 3-7 <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-bhasya-madhva-3-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The Supreme Self, present in the bodies of all beings, is fourfold — Viśva, Taijasa, Prājña and Turīya — and all these forms are full of bliss and knowledge. Hari as Taijasa, abiding in the mind, makes the dream appear; as Prājña he is lord of deep sleep, in which the soul, having reached Hari and covered by darkness (tamas), neither desires nor sees; Viśva presides over waking.

### 7 <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-bhasya-madhva-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

(Quoting the Brahmāṇḍa:) Viṣṇu in his Turīya form abides at the dvādaśānta; it is the form attained by the liberated and is not seen in ordinary life; to those fully concentrated who have reached the sixteenth kalā and have direct vision, the Turīya state is sometimes seen. The Turīya gives all knowledge to the liberated and is the cause of all their activity; he stills the world of bodily bondage, and so is called 'prapañcopaśama' and 'advaita' (without equal).

### intro <a id="tea-mandukya-upanisad-bhasya-madhva-intro"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Homage to Viṣṇu, whose nature is full bliss, knowledge and power, the fourfold enjoyer of all. In this Upaniṣad Varuṇa, in the form of a frog (maṇḍūka), praises the fourfold Nārāyaṇa (quoting the Pādma); the syllable Om designates Hari, in whom all is woven, the full one, the same in past, present and future.
