Six chapters: the setting in the medicine city Tanaduk; the request; the basis (health and disease: the three nyepa, seven constituents, three excretions); diagnosis (seeing, touching, questioning); treatment (diet, behaviour, medicine, external therapy); and the summary of all by the image of three trees.

### 1 <a id="tea-gyushi-tsagyu-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Thus have I heard: in the city of medicine, Tanaduk ('beautiful to behold'), in a palace of five kinds of jewels surrounded by mountains on which medicines of warming and cooling power grow, the Medicine Buddha sat amid four retinues — gods, sages, non-Buddhists and Buddhists — and, entering the samādhi that dispels all illness, emanated the sage Rigpé Yeshe from his mind.

### 2 <a id="tea-gyushi-tsagyu-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence low

The sage Yilé Kyé, emanated from the Buddha's speech, requests the teaching: how is the science of healing to be learned? Rigpé Yeshe answers that it has eight branches — the body, children, women's diseases, spirit afflictions, wounds, poisons, old age (rejuvenation) and virility — and is taught in four tantras.

### 3 <a id="tea-gyushi-tsagyu-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The basis: the body in health and disease. The three nyepa (rlung, mkhris pa, bad kan), the seven bodily constituents and the three excretions: in balance they sustain the body, disturbed they are disease. Rlung dwells chiefly in the lower body, mkhris pa in the middle and bad kan in the upper.

### 4 <a id="tea-gyushi-tsagyu-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Diagnosis is by three means: looking (above all at the tongue and the urine), touching (above all the pulse) and questioning (about causes, conditions and symptoms).

### 5 <a id="tea-gyushi-tsagyu-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

Treatment is by four means — diet, behaviour, medicine and external therapy — applied to rlung, mkhris pa and bad kan disorders each in its own way; the gentler means come first.

### 6 <a id="tea-gyushi-tsagyu-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

All of this is summarized in the image of three trees — of the basis, of diagnosis and of treatment — with their roots, nine trunks, forty-seven branches and two hundred and twenty-four leaves; their two flowers are health and long life, their three fruits Dharma, wealth and happiness.
