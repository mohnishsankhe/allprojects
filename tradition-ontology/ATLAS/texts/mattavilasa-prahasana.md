A one-act farce set in Kāñcī. The drunken Kāpālika Satyasoma and his companion Devasomā lose their skull begging-bowl, accuse the Buddhist monk Nāgasena, appeal to the Pāśupata Babhrukalpa, and recover the bowl from a madman who took it from a dog. It satirises Kāpālika, Buddhist and Pāśupata ascetics.

## Teachings (2: skeleton 2)

### 6 <a id="tea-mattavilasa-prahasana-6"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The drunken Kāpālika tells his companion Devasomā that it is true that by austerity one gains the power to take any form, since by duly keeping the supreme vow she has suddenly become more beautiful.

### 7 <a id="tea-mattavilasa-prahasana-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'Liquor is to be drunk, the beloved's face gazed on, a naturally charming and outlandish garb worn - long live the Lord who holds the Pināka, who saw this path to liberation!' When his companion says the Jain ascetics describe the path differently, he retorts that they are wrong: they teach that happiness is the fruit of suffering, though an effect must resemble its cause.
