**Availability:** digitized-original

The Maitreya text that defines the Yogācāra middle way: unreal imagination exists, the duality of grasped and grasper within it does not, and emptiness exists within it; hence everything is neither empty nor non-empty. It analyses the obscurations, ten kinds of reality, the path with its five faults of meditation and eight remedies, and the ten perfections.

### 1.3 <a id="tea-madhyantavibhaga-1-3"></a>

### 1.4-5 <a id="tea-madhyantavibhaga-1-4-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Consciousness arises appearing as objects, beings, self and cognitions; its object does not exist, and since that is absent, it too is unreal. Hence it is established as unreal imagination — not in that it is wholly non-existent; its extinction is held to be liberation.

> arthasattvātmavijñaptipratibhāsaṃ prajāyate / vijñānaṃ nāsti cāsyārthastadabhāvāttadapyasat

### 1.13-16 <a id="tea-madhyantavibhaga-1-13-16"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Emptiness is explained by its characteristic, synonyms, their meaning, divisions and proof. Its characteristic is the non-existence of duality and the existence of that non-existence; it is neither existent nor non-existent, neither different from nor identical with (unreal imagination). Its synonyms are suchness, the limit of reality, the signless, the ultimate and the dharmadhātu — meaning unchanging, non-erroneous, cessation of signs, object of the noble ones' cognition, and cause of the noble qualities.

> dvayābhāvo hyabhāvasya bhāvaḥ śūnyasya lakṣaṇam

### 1.21-23 <a id="tea-madhyantavibhaga-1-21-23"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Emptiness is the non-existence of person and dharmas and the existence of that non-existence. If it were defiled, all embodied beings would be liberated; if it were pure, effort would be fruitless. It is neither defiled nor undefiled, neither pure nor impure, because the mind is luminous and the defilements are adventitious.

> na kliṣṭā nāpi va 'ākliṣṭā śuddhāśuddhā na caiva sā / prabhāsvaratvāccittasya kleśasyāgantukatvataḥ

### 4.3-5 <a id="tea-madhyantavibhaga-4-3-5"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Stability of mind for success in all aims arises from abandoning five faults and applying eight formations. The five faults are laziness, forgetting the instruction, sinking and agitation, non-application and (over-)application. The eight formations are the basis (desire-to-act), the based (effort), its cause (faith), its fruit (pliancy), non-forgetting of the object (mindfulness), noticing sinking and agitation (awareness), the application to remove them (intention), and calm flow when they have ceased (equanimity).

> kausīdyamavavādasya sammoṣo laya uddhavaḥ / asaṃskāro 'tha saṃskāraḥ pañca doṣā ime matāḥ

_level: conventional · standpoint: seeker · path: meditation · stage: intermediate (śamatha) · types: practice_
