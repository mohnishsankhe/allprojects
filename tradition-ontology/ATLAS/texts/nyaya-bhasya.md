Vātsyāyana's commentary, the oldest extant on the Nyāya Sūtra. It explains that a means of knowledge is purposeful because it makes action successful, defines nyāya as the examination of things by the means of knowledge, presents ānvīkṣikī as the fourth science distinct from the Upaniṣadic science of the self, reads the five members as a convergence of all four means of knowledge, catalogues the forms of false knowledge, rejects eternal pleasure in liberation, describes Īśvara as a distinct self who assists human effort, and prescribes yama, niyama, yoga and contemplation of the unlovely against passion.

## Teachings (24: skeleton 24)

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-1"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

A means of knowledge is purposeful, because when a thing is known through it, activity succeeds: without a means of knowledge there is no knowing of things, and without that no successful activity. The one who acts, impelled by the wish to obtain or avoid, is the knower; that by which he knows is the means of knowledge; what is known is the object of knowledge; the knowing is the cognition. Truth (tattva) is the being of what is and the non-being of what is not; the means of knowledge reveals both, as a lamp shows what is present and, by its not being seen, what is absent.

> pramāṇato 'rthapratipattau pravṛttisāmarthyād arthavat pramāṇam

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-1-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Four sciences are taught separately for the benefit of living beings, the fourth being this ānvīkṣikī, the science of reasoning (nyāyavidyā); its categories from doubt onward are stated separately because without them it would be merely a science of the self (adhyātmavidyā), like the Upaniṣads.

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-1-4"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

What is nyāya? The examination of things by the means of knowledge. Inference that rests on perception and scripture is 'anvīkṣā' (subsequent review) — the reviewing of what has been seen through perception and scripture — and the science that proceeds by it is ānvīkṣikī, the science of reasoning; an inference that contradicts perception and scripture is pseudo-reasoning (nyāyābhāsa).

> kaḥ punar ayaṃ nyāyaḥ? pramāṇair arthaparīkṣaṇam

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-1-6"></a>

### 1.1.1 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-1-7"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

This ānvīkṣikī, divided into the categories beginning with the means of knowledge, is declared in the enumeration of the sciences to be 'the lamp of all sciences, the means of all actions, the support of all dharmas'. Knowledge of truth and the attainment of the highest good are to be understood according to each science; here, in the science of the self, knowledge of truth is knowledge of the self and the rest, and the attainment of the highest good is the attainment of liberation.

> pradīpaḥ sarvavidyānām upāyaḥ sarvakarmaṇām / āśrayaḥ sarvadharmāṇāṃ vidyoddeśe prakīrtitā

### 1.1.2 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

False knowledge about the objects of knowledge takes many forms: of the self, 'it does not exist'; of the non-self, 'it is the self'; pain taken as pleasure, the impermanent as permanent, the unprotecting as protection, the fearful as safe, the loathsome as desirable, what is to be abandoned as not to be abandoned; of activity, 'there is no karma and no fruit'; of the faults, 'saṃsāra is not caused by faults'; of rebirth, 'there is no being, soul or self that dies and is reborn', or that rebirth is uncaused, or a mere cutting-off and re-linking of the series of body, senses, cognitions and feelings without a self; of liberation, 'this cessation of all activity is frightening — how could a wise person want a liberation that cuts off all pleasure and is without consciousness?'. Knowledge of truth is the opposite: liberation is peace, the cessation of all, in which much that is painful, terrible and evil is cut off; how could a wise person not want it? As food mixed with honey and poison is not to be taken, so pleasure bound up with pain is not to be taken.

> tad yathā, madhuviṣasaṃpṛktam annam anādeyam iti, evaṃ sukhaṃ duḥkhānuṣaktam anādeyam iti

### 1.1.2 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-2-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

From false knowledge arise attachment to the agreeable and aversion to the disagreeable, and from these untruth, envy, jealousy, pride, greed and other faults; impelled by them one acts. Bad activity: by body — injury, theft, forbidden sex; by speech — lying, harshness, slander, idle talk; by mind — malice, coveting others' goods, disbelief (nāstikya); this leads to adharma. Good activity: by body — giving, protecting, serving; by speech — truthful, beneficial, pleasant speech and recitation; by mind — compassion, freedom from craving, faith; this leads to dharma. Activity causes birth, high or low; birth is the appearance of body, senses, cognition and feeling; with it comes pain. These, from false knowledge to pain, running on without break are saṃsāra; when knowledge of truth removes false knowledge, the faults, activity, birth and pain depart in turn, and absolute liberation — the highest good — results.

### 1.1.3 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-3"></a>

### 1.1.3 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-3-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The means of knowledge may converge on one object (saṃplava) or be restricted (vyavasthā): fire is known from a reliable person's statement, inferred on seeing smoke nearby, and perceived when one is close; but heaven (known only from the Vedic injunction) and thunder's cause (only inferred) are restricted. Such cognition culminates in perception: one who has learned a thing from testimony seeks to infer it, and having inferred it wishes to see it; when it is perceived the wish to know ceases. The perception of one engaged in yoga arises from samādhi: 'from a particular conjunction of self and mind the self is perceived'.

> pramātuḥ pramātavye 'rthe pramāṇānāṃ saṃkaro 'bhisaṃplavaḥ, asaṃkaro vyavastheti

### 1.1.22 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-1-1-22"></a>

### 2.1.69 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-2-1-69"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

What is the validity of Āyurveda? That what it teaches — 'doing this one obtains what is desired, avoiding this one escapes what is undesired' — turns out so when practised. ### 4.1.21 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-4-1-21"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Īśvara assists human effort: for the person who strives for a fruit Īśvara brings the fruit about, and when He does not, human action is fruitless. Īśvara is a distinct self (ātmāntara) marked by qualities — free of demerit, false knowledge and heedlessness, and endowed with merit, knowledge and concentration; the fruit of his merit and concentration is the eightfold lordship beginning with minuteness; his merit, following his resolve, sets in motion the stores of merit and demerit residing in each self and the elements such as earth. He is like a reliable person; as a father is to his children, so is Īśvara to beings.

> puruṣakāram īśvaro 'nugṛhṇāti

### 4.2.2 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-4-2-2"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

One should first contemplate (prasaṅkhyāna) the objects of desire — colour and the rest — which, when falsely constructed, give rise to attachment, aversion and delusion; as one contemplates them the false construction regarding outer objects ceases; then one should contemplate the body and the rest within, and the I-notion regarding the inner ceases. One who dwells with a mind detached within and without is called liberated (mukta).

> so 'yam adhyātmaṃ bahiś ca viviktacitto viharan mukta ity ucyate

### 4.2.3 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-4-2-3"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

The cause of the faults is the notion of the whole — the notion 'woman' in a man and 'man' in a woman, with its embellishments — both the notion of the whole form (nimitta) and of its attractive details (anuvyañjana), which increases passion. It is removed by the notion of the parts: the notion of hair, body-hair, flesh, blood, bone, sinew, veins, phlegm, bile, excrement and so on, called the notion of the unlovely (aśubhasaṃjñā); as one cultivates it, lustful passion is abandoned. As with food mixed with poison, one notion is to be cultivated, the other avoided.

> keśalomamāṃsaśoṇitāsthisnāyuśirākaphapittoccārādisaṃjñā tām aśubhasaṃjñety ācakṣate

### 4.2.46 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-4-2-46"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

Restraint (yama) is the means of dharma common to all life-stages; observance (niyama) is particular; the purification of the self is the removal of demerit and the increase of merit. The method of the inner self is to be learned from the Yoga śāstra: austerity, breath-control, withdrawal of the senses, meditation and fixation; repeated contemplation of sense-objects for the abandonment of attachment and aversion; the means is the practice of yogic conduct.

### 4.2.47 <a id="tea-nyaya-bhasya-4-2-47"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence high

'Knowledge' means the science of the self; its 'grasping' is study and retention; 'repetition' is constant practice — study, listening and reflection; discussion with those learned in it is for the ripening of wisdom, which is the cutting of doubt, the understanding of what was not known, and the confirmation of what has been ascertained.
