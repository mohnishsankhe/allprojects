Vātsyāyana's commentary, the oldest extant on the Nyāya Sūtra. It explains that a means of knowledge is purposeful because it makes action successful, defines nyāya as the examination of things by the means of knowledge, presents ānvīkṣikī as the fourth science distinct from the Upaniṣadic science of the self, reads the five members as a convergence of all four means of knowledge, catalogues the forms of false knowledge, rejects eternal pleasure in liberation, describes Īśvara as a distinct self who assists human effort, and prescribes yama, niyama, yoga and contemplation of the unlovely against passion.
