**Attribution:** tradition: Sung by the twelve Āḻvārs; lost and recovered by Nāthamuni, who received all four thousand from Nammāḻvār in yogic vision at Kurukūr after reciting Madhurakavi's Kaṇṇinuṇ Ciṟuttāmpu 12,000 times.; scholarly: Compiled c. 10th c. CE from hymns composed c. 6th–9th c.; the four-part arrangement and the inclusion of the Irāmāṉuca Nūṟṟantāti reflect later Śrīvaiṣṇava redaction.; confidence: moderate
**Dates:** Tradition's account: hymns of the late Dvāpara and early Kali ages, recovered by Nāthamuni; Scholarly account: hymns c. 6th–9th c. CE; compiled c. 10th c. CE; (confidence moderate)
**Structure:** About 4000 verses in four 'thousands' (āyiram): (1) Mutalāyiram (c. 947: Tiruppallāṇṭu, Periyāḻvār Tirumoḻi, Tiruppāvai, Nācciyār Tirumoḻi, Perumāḷ Tirumoḻi, Tiruccanta Viruttam, Tirumālai, Tiruppaḷḷiyeḻucci, Amalaṉātipirāṉ, Kaṇṇinuṇ Ciṟuttāmpu); (2) Periya Tirumoḻi with Tirukkuṟuntāṇṭakam and Tiruneṭuntāṇṭakam (c. 1134); (3) Iyaṟpā, the recited portion (c. 817: the first three Tiruvantātis, Nāṉmukaṉ Tiruvantāti, Tiruviruttam, Tiruvāciriyam, Periya Tiruvantāti, Tiruveḻukkūṟṟirukkai, Ciṟiya and Periya Tirumaṭal, Irāmāṉuca Nūṟṟantāti); (4) Tiruvāymoḻi (1102). Each work is preceded by invocatory taṉiyaṉ verses.
**Availability:** digitized-original

The 'four thousand divine compositions': the collected hymns of the twelve Āḻvārs (with Tiruvaraṅkattu Amutaṉār's Irāmāṉuca Nūṟṟantāti), gathered and arranged by Nāthamuni, set to music for temple recitation, and revered by Śrīvaiṣṇavas as the Tamil Veda. It sings Tirumāl with Śrī in his temple images, the avatāras, surrender, service, the name, and love in the voices of Tamil love-poetry.

_Notes: Part counts (947 / 1134 / 817 / 1102 = 4000) as recalled, counting Ciṟiya Tirumaṭal as 40 and Periya Tirumaṭal as 78 units; placement of Tirukkuṟuntāṇṭakam/Tiruneṭuntāṇṭakam differs in some arrangements. Commentaries: see src:tiruvaymoli and the U14 commentary entries (src:arayirappati etc.). Periyavāccāṉ Piḷḷai commented on nearly the whole Prabandham._
