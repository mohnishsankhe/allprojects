Śākaṭāyana's companion treatise arguing that the omniscient (kevalin) takes food: as long as the body and the feeling-producing and life-span karmas remain, the body needs nourishment, and eating without desire does not diminish omniscience.

## Teachings (1: skeleton 1)

### whole <a id="tea-kevalibhukti-prakarana-whole"></a>
`skeleton` · confidence moderate

The omniscient eats: his body is the ordinary gross body, which lasts only by nourishment; the feeling-producing and life-span karmas that cause hunger remain until the final stages; eating without desire does not diminish omniscience or bliss, as the other bodily acts of the omniscient — walking, sitting, preaching — do not; and the scripture speaks of the Jina taking food.
