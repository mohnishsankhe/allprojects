## The Vedic and yogic reading

- Your words “i want to go straight back to it” fit only loosely what the Vedic and yogic texts call Rāga (attachment). Rāga is what follows upon pleasure (YS 2.7). Vyāsa: in one who has known pleasure, preceded by the memory of pleasure, the greed, thirst and craving (gardha, tṛṣṇā, lobha) for pleasure or for its means — that is rāga. _(Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra) 2.7; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.7)_

## The ascetic reading (Buddhist and Jain)

- Read through the Jain texts, your words “i want to go straight back to it” come closest to what they call Lobha (greed) (a partial match for Rāga (attachment)). Conduct-deluding karma includes the sixteen passions: anger, pride, deceit and greed, each in four grades named endless-binding (anantānubandhī), non-renunciation (apratyākhyāna), renunciation (pratyākhyāna) and smouldering (saṃjvalana) (TS 8.9, Digambara recension). _(Tattvārthasūtra 8.2; Tattvārthasūtra 8.9; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.7)_
- Read through the Buddhist texts, your words “i want to go straight back to it” come closest to what they call Kāmacchanda (sensual desire) (a partial match for Rāga (attachment)). Contemplating dhammas as the five hindrances: when sensual desire is present in him the monk knows 'there is sensual desire in me'; when absent, 'there is no sensual desire in me'; he knows how the unarisen sensual desire arises, how the arisen sensual desire is abandoned, and how the abandoned sensual desire does not arise again. _(Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta dn22:13; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:36; Visuddhimagga 4.p146; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.7)_

## How the two readings fit together

- YS: attachment rests on pleasure (YS 2.7); Vyāsa glosses it as greed, thirst and covetousness (gardha, tṛṣṇā, lobha) for the pleasure or its means, preceded by the memory of pleasure (YB 2.7). Gītā: from dwelling on objects comes attachment, from attachment desire (BhG 2.62); desire-anger is 'the enemy here' (BhG 3.37). Suttas: sensual desire (kāmacchanda) is known as present or absent, as arising and as abandoned (MN 10:36; DN 22:13); craving (taṇhā) that delights here and there is the origin of suffering, and its fading away is cessation (DN 22:19–20). TS: greed (lobha) is one of the four passions (TS 8.9), because of which the soul takes in karmic matter (TS 8.2). Under the one-truth principle, they describe it from different standpoints: the match is a partial match, not an identity. _(standpoint; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.7; Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra) 2.7; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.4; Bhagavad Gītā 3.37; Bhagavad Gītā 2.62; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:36; Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta dn22:13; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:34; Visuddhimagga 22.p683; Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta dn22:19; Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta dn22:20; Tattvārthasūtra 8.9; Tattvārthasūtra 8.2; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.9; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.25; Visuddhimagga 4.p146; Visuddhimagga 14.p468)_
- Where they differ: Rāga is not kāmacchanda, and neither is lobha. (1) Framework: YS rāga is one of five kleśas growing in the field of avidyā (YS 2.4). The Gītā's kāma is born of the guṇa rajas and is one enemy with anger (BhG 3.37). Kāmacchanda is a hindrance: a state to be noticed and abandoned in meditation (MN 10:36). Taṇhā is the origin of suffering (DN 22:19). Jain lobha is a passion-felt deluding karma (kaṣāya-vedanīya, TS 8.9) and a literal cause of karmic matter binding the soul (TS 8.2). (2) Scope: YS rāga covers any remembered pleasure and its means (YB 2.7). Kāmacchanda is sensual desire. Taṇhā in DN 22 also includes craving for being and for non-being (DN 22:19); the Yoga puts the wish to go on being under a different kleśa (abhiniveśa, YS 2.9). Jain lobha comes in four intensities, anantānubandhī to saṃjvalana (TS 8.9), a grading no other lens uses. The Theravāda lists grade it differently, by depth: a hindrance that keeps jhāna from arising (Vism IV), greed as a root of unwholesome consciousness (Vism XIV), and sensual lust as one of the five lower fetters (Vism XXII). (3) What is freed: the Yoga ends in the seer's aloneness (YS 2.25), the suttas in the cessation of craving (DN 22:20), the Jain path in the soul's release from karmic matter (TS 8.2). The self-question behind these three ends is not yet reconciled (row ot:provisional-self-question in one_truth.json). _(Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.7; Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra) 2.7; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.4; Bhagavad Gītā 3.37; Bhagavad Gītā 2.62; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:36; Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta dn22:13; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:34; Visuddhimagga 22.p683; Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta dn22:19; Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta dn22:20; Tattvārthasūtra 8.9; Tattvārthasūtra 8.2; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.9; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.25; Visuddhimagga 4.p146; Visuddhimagga 14.p468)_

## A gentle practice pathway

### Cultivating the opposite (pratipakṣa-bhāvanā, YS 2.33–34)

You wrote “i want to go straight back to it”. The texts pair what they call Rāga (attachment) with this practice.

1. Sit in a posture that is steady and comfortable (YS 2.46).
2. When a harmful thought arises, to harm, to lie, to take what is not given, name it as a harmful thought (vitarka) (YS 2.33 with Vyāsa).
3. See how it stands: done, caused to be done, or approved; rooted in greed, anger or delusion; mild, middling or intense (YS 2.34).
4. Reflect that such thoughts bear endless suffering and ignorance as their fruit (YS 2.34).
5. Turn the mind toward the restraint the thought opposes, such as non-harming or truthfulness (YS 2.33; 2.30).
6. Close gently.

Length: 5–10 minutes, 1 time(s) a day (product default for a beginner; the text gives no length).

**The texts' caution:** Practice becomes firm only when cultivated for a long time, without interruption and with earnestness (satkāra); the sūtra does not present it as quick (YS 1.14). _(Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 1.14)_

**The texts' caution:** Vyāsa's illustrations of the fruit of violence include births in hells (YB 2.34); the steps keep only the sūtra's own reflection. _(Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra) 2.34)_

**The texts' caution:** The posture is to be steady and comfortable (sthira-sukha); it is accomplished by relaxing effort, so that the limbs do not tremble. _(Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.46; Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 2.47; Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra) 2.47)_
