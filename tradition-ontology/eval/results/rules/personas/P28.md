- Why this fits: You wrote "At night my mind runs like a sewing machine"; the texts describe this as the mind moving off wherever it will, restless and unsteady (bhg 6.26, 6.34).
- How sure: moderate
- Texts: Bhagavad Gītā 6.26; Bhagavad Gītā 6.34

## The Vedic and yogic reading

- Your words “At night my mind runs like a sewing machine” may fit what the Vedic and yogic texts call Cañcala manas (the restless mind). Arjuna: the mind is restless, turbulent, strong and obstinate; I think it as hard to hold as the wind (BhG 6.34). Kṛṣṇa: no doubt the mind is hard to hold and moving, but it is held by practice and dispassion (6.35). The turbulent senses carry off by force the mind even of a wise man who strives (2.60); the mind that follows the roving senses carries off understanding as the wind a ship on the water (2.67). _(Bhagavad Gītā 2.60; Bhagavad Gītā 2.67; Bhagavad Gītā 6.34; Bhagavad Gītā 6.35)_

## The ascetic reading (Buddhist and Jain)

- The Buddhist texts have their own account of a pattern like the one above: Uddhacca-kukkucca (restlessness and remorse) (a partial match for Cañcala manas (the restless mind)). Contemplating dhammas as the five hindrances: when restlessness and remorse is present in him the monk knows 'there is restlessness and remorse in me'; when absent, 'there is no restlessness and remorse in me'; he knows how the unarisen restlessness and remorse arises, how the arisen restlessness and remorse is abandoned, and how the abandoned restlessness and remorse does not arise again. Your words were matched to Cañcala manas (the restless mind), not to this; it is shown so that both readings are in view. _(Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta dn22:13; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:36)_

## How the two readings fit together

- Each names a mind that will not stay. Gītā: the mind is restless, turbulent, strong and obstinate, as hard to restrain as the wind (BhG 6.34). It is held by practice and dispassion (BhG 6.35), and wherever it wanders it is to be brought back (BhG 6.26). Vyāsa: samādhi is a property of the mind at every level, and one of its grounds is the distracted (vikṣipta) (YB 1.1). Suttas: restlessness and remorse (uddhacca-kukkucca) is a hindrance known as present or absent (MN 10:36), and a scattered (vikkhitta) mind is known as scattered (MN 10:34). Under the one-truth principle, they describe it from different standpoints: the match is a partial match, not an identity. _(standpoint; Bhagavad Gītā 6.34; Bhagavad Gītā 6.35; Bhagavad Gītā 6.26; Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra) 1.1; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:36; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:34)_
- Where they differ: (1) Vyāsa's distracted mind is one ground in a scale of grounds of mind (YB 1.1). MN 10 only names the scattered mind as something to be known, with no scale (MN 10:34). (2) The hindrance joins restlessness and remorse (kukkucca) into one (MN 10:36). The Gītā passage speaks of restlessness alone. (3) The Gītā names the remedy, practice and dispassion (BhG 6.35). MN 10's approach in this passage is to know the hindrance as present, arising and abandoned (MN 10:36). (4) The Visuddhimagga defines restlessness by its mark of non-calming (Vism XIV). It counts restlessness among the fetters that remain until the arahat path (Vism XXII), a depth that the Gītā and Vyāsa passages do not grade. _(Bhagavad Gītā 6.35; Yogabhāṣya (Vyāsa's commentary on the Yoga Sūtra) 1.1; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:36; Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta mn10:34; Visuddhimagga 14.p469; Visuddhimagga 22.p682)_

## A gentle practice pathway

### Practice and dispassion (abhyāsa, vairāgya, BhG 6.35)

You wrote “At night my mind runs like a sewing machine”. This reading suggests the practice below for what the texts call Cañcala manas (the restless mind); the practice comes from the texts cited with it, and the pairing is this reading's, not theirs.

1. Sit on a firm seat in a clean place, neither too high nor too low (BhG 6.11), body, head and neck in line and still (6.13).
2. Practice (abhyāsa): wherever the restless mind wanders off, restrain it from there and bring it back (6.26).
3. Dispassion (vairāgya): recall that enjoyments born of contact have a beginning and an end and are wombs of sorrow; the wise one does not delight in them (5.22).
4. Come to rest little by little (śanaiḥ śanaiḥ), not all at once (6.25).
5. Close gently.

Length: 5–10 minutes, 1 time(s) a day (product default for a beginner; the text gives no length).

**The texts' caution:** Moderation: yoga is not for one who eats too much, nor for one who does not eat at all, nor for one who sleeps too much or keeps always awake; it is for one measured in food, recreation, effort, sleep and waking. _(Bhagavad Gītā 6.16; Bhagavad Gītā 6.17)_

**The texts' caution:** The Gītā asks that yoga be practised with resolve and with an undejected mind (anirviṇṇa-cetas). _(Bhagavad Gītā 6.23)_

**The texts' caution:** The Gītā grants that the mind is hard to restrain; it is held by practice and dispassion (6.35), and yoga can be attained by one who strives through the right means (6.36). _(Bhagavad Gītā 6.35; Bhagavad Gītā 6.36)_
