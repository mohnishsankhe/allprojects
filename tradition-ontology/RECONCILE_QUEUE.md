## RQ-020 — Does liberation contain bliss (and consciousness)?  
`dsp:bliss-in-liberation` · skeleton

- **lin:nyaya**: Mainstream Nyāya: liberation is absolute release from pain; there is no proof of an eternal pleasure manifested in it, and one who strives for liberation out of attachment to pleasure is not freed; the liberated self has neither pleasure nor cognition. Pleasure bound up with pain is to be relinquished like food mixed with honey and poison.
- **lin:vaisesika**: Liberation is the absence of contact with a body and of new appearance when adṛṣṭa is exhausted; a quiescence like fire whose fuel is burnt (no special qualities of the self remain).
- **lin:nyaya**: Bhāsarvajña (a minority within Nyāya): liberation is the absolute cessation of pain qualified by the experience of eternal pleasure.
- **lin:advaita-vedanta**: Liberation is the realization of the self as Brahman, being-consciousness-bliss; a liberation without consciousness or bliss would be no better than a stone's state — a charge pressed against Nyāya (e.g. in Śrīharṣa's Naiṣadhīyacarita, as usually cited).
- **lin:bhatta-mimamsa**: Pārthasārathi: no — release has no cognition and no bliss; it is like dreamless sleep without return, a human goal because pain ceases; Kumārila: release is not an enjoyment of pleasure, which would be a perishable heaven.
- **lin:bhatta-mimamsa**: Some Bhāṭṭas (reported in the Śāstradīpikā): yes — the self's innate bliss, overpowered in saṃsāra, becomes manifest and is enjoyed through the mind in release; Kumārila's verse states another's view.
- **lin:prabhakara-mimamsa**: No — scripture does not make known accomplished things, and no supreme bliss is experienced by the embodied; release is valuable as the end of sorrow.
- **lin:advaita-vedanta**: Yes — liberation is the realization of Brahman, whose nature is bliss.
- Candidate readings:
  - P2-standpoint / terminological: Nyāya denies pleasure as an adventitious quality produced by contact with objects; Advaita affirms bliss as the self's nature, not a produced quality — the two may partly talk past each other.
  - P1-level: Advaita could read Nyāya's account as describing liberation negatively from the empirical standpoint.
  - The Śāstradīpikā itself notes that on either view release is a human goal and differs from saṃsāra by the absence of pain.
  - P1-level: Advaita's bliss belongs to the absolute self, not to an experience of a knower — a reading the Mīmāṃsakas reject.
- Note: Not yet reconciled.

## RQ-039 — Is sound (the word) eternal?  
`dsp:eternality-of-sound` · skeleton

- **lin:nyaya**: No: sound has a beginning, is sense-perceived and is spoken of like produced things; it is a non-eternal quality of ākāśa. The word–meaning relation is conventional (later: the Lord's convention).

## RQ-062 — Does the liberated soul become identical with Śiva, or only equal to Śiva (śivasamatva) while remaining distinct?  
`dsp:liberation-identity-or-equality-with-siva` · skeleton

- **lin:saiva-siddhanta**: Sanskrit exegetes: the freed soul becomes a Śiva, equal in omniscience and omnipotence, its own powers manifested; it remains distinct and does not perform the cosmic acts; the liberations of other schools (isolation, extinction, merger) are refuted.
- **lin:tamil-saiva-siddhanta**: Tamil school: release is inseparable union (advaita as ananya) — the soul, its malas powerless, abides in Śiva enjoying his bliss, 'one, other and together'; it neither becomes Śiva nor stands as a second Lord; 'Śivasamavāda' and 'Saṅkrāntavāda' are refuted.
- **lin:saiva-siddhanta**: The liberated soul becomes equal to Śiva in omniscience and omnipotence but remains numerically distinct; the five cosmic acts remain Śiva's alone.
- **lin:pratyabhijna**: There is only one Maheśvara, the self of all beings (ĪPK 4.1); the bound individual already performs the five acts (PH 10); liberation is the recognition of that identity, culminating in lordship over the circle of one's own powers (PH 20). ## RQ-066 — Is the root impurity (āṇava mala) a real substance removable only by ritual initiation, or is it ignorance - a contraction of consciousness - removed by knowledge?  
`dsp:mala-substance-or-ignorance` · skeleton

- **lin:saiva-siddhanta**: Āṇava mala is a real, beginningless substance (dravya) that covers the soul's powers of knowing and acting; as a substance it cannot be destroyed by knowledge but only by an act - initiation (dīkṣā) empowered by Śiva - after its ripening; the liberated soul becomes equal to Śiva but remains distinct.
- **lin:trika**: Impurity is ignorance, the cause of the sprouting of saṃsāra (MVT 1.23); ignorance is not the absence of knowledge but its incomplete manifestation (TĀ 1.25-26); knowledge is the sole cause of liberation (TĀ 1.22); the āṇava impurity is consciousness losing its freedom (ĪPK 3.2.4); grace does not depend on the ripening of impurity or on a balance of karma but on the Lord's freedom (TĀ 13); initiation works because it is itself a form of knowledge.
- **lin:saiva-siddhanta**: Mala is a substance (dravya); knowledge cannot remove it any more than knowing a cataract cures it; Śiva removes it through the initiation rite.
- **lin:tamil-saiva-siddhanta**: Āṇava is eternal and is never destroyed; grace makes its power ineffective, like a snake's poison stilled by a mantra; knowledge given by grace (not the soul's own effort) accompanies this.
- Candidate readings:

- Note: Not yet reconciled: the two schools disagree on what mala is.

## RQ-068 — How many members does a demonstration (inference for others) need?  
`dsp:members-of-inference` · skeleton

- **lin:nyaya**: Five: proposition, reason, example, application, conclusion; together they embody all four means of knowledge. A ten-member view (adding desire to know, doubt etc.) is rejected; stating fewer is the point of defeat 'saying too little'.
- **lin:pramana-buddhist**: Fewer members suffice: Dignāga states thesis, reason and example; Dharmakīrti holds that for the competent the reason with its pervasion and presence in the subject is enough, and stating superfluous members is itself a fault in debate.
- **lin:advaita-vedanta**: Three members suffice — either the first three or the last three (Vedāntaparibhāṣā); Mīmāṃsakas also hold three.
- **lin:jainism**: The number depends on the listener: two members for the sharp, more (up to ten) for others (Jain logicians, as usually reported).
- Candidate readings:
  - P4-stage / P6-upaya: the number of stated members depends on the hearer's capacity — a principle the Jain side states explicitly and Nyāya approaches by distinguishing inference for oneself (no verbal members) from inference for others.
  - The shared logical core (pervasion plus the reason's presence in the subject) is common to all sides.
- Note: Not yet reconciled: each side treats the other's practice as a fault in debate.

## RQ-073 — Is the world false (mithyā), and can the Advaita definitions of falsity withstand Vyāsatīrtha's critique? (The Nyāyāmṛta–Advaitasiddhi controversy)  
`dsp:nyayamrta-advaitasiddhi` · skeleton

- **lin:dvaita**: The world is real. None of the five Advaita definitions of falsity is coherent; the inferences from 'perceivable', 'insentient' and 'limited' fail; perception, scripture and above all the witness (sākṣin) establish the world's reality, and what the witness establishes is never sublated. - **lin:advaita-vedanta**: The world is mithyā: not ultimately real, since it is sublated by the knowledge of Brahman, yet not unreal like a hare's horn, since it appears and functions; Madhusūdana defends each of the five definitions of falsity point by point.   - {"principle": "P2-standpoint", "reading": "Dvaita speaks from the standpoint of the dependent self and its objects, Advaita from the standpoint of the absolute; each is true within its standpoint."}

## RQ-075 — What are the objects of the bliss (ānanda) and I-am-ness (asmitā) forms of samprajñāta samādhi, and who are the videhas and prakṛtilayas?  
`dsp:objects-of-samprajnata` · skeleton

- **lin:patanjala-yoga**: Bhoja: sānanda is cultivation of the inner organ's sattva tinged by rajas and tamas (grasping-coalescence) — those content there are the 'videhas'; sāsmita is cultivation of pure sattva, mere being — those content there are 'prakṛtilayas'; discernment of the supreme puruṣa is grasper-coalescence.
- **lin:patanjala-yoga**: Vijñānabhikṣu: bliss is a joy arising from preponderant sattva, taken as object; it is not the senses; asmitā is awareness with the self alone as object (the individual self or the Supreme Self); there are no 'with bliss / without bliss' sub-varieties.
- **lin:patanjala-yoga**: Vyāsa: the bodiless (videha) are gods who experience a quasi-kaivalya with a mind using its own impressions (YBh 1.19); bliss is joy, I-am-ness awareness of a single self (YBh 1.17).

  - P2-standpoint: objective (grāhya–grahaṇa–grahītṛ) vs experiential (joy, 'I am') descriptions.

- Note: Not yet reconciled: the commentators assign different objects to the upper samprajñāta levels and identify the videhas differently.

## RQ-076 — Can any person be omniscient, in particular directly know dharma?  
`dsp:omniscience` · skeleton

- **lin:nyaya**: Īśvara is omniscient; yogic perception, arising from special merit, grasps the supersensible.
- Candidate readings:
  - P2-standpoint: Kumārila concedes that an omniscience by all six means of knowledge would not be refuted (ŚV codanā 111); if Buddhist and Jain claims are read as knowledge of what matters for liberation rather than perception of Vedic dharma, part of the dispute narrows — each tradition would reject this narrowing of its own claim.

- Note: Not yet reconciled; candidate readings listed.

## RQ-080 — Does the Lord act independently of the karma of souls?

## RQ-107 — Is the validity of a cognition intrinsic (svataḥ) or extrinsic (parataḥ)?  
`dsp:svatah-paratah-pramanya` · skeleton

- **lin:nyaya**: Validity is extrinsic: it is produced by an excellence in the causes of a cognition and is known not with the cognition itself but by inference — from the success of the activity it prompts or other marks; invalidity likewise comes from defects and is known extrinsically.
- **lin:mimamsa**: Every cognition is valid by itself: validity arises from the very conditions that produce the cognition and is apprehended with it; invalidity is extrinsic, known from a defect in the causes or a later sublating cognition. A power not present in a thing by itself cannot be produced by another.
- **lin:samkhya**: Both validity and invalidity are intrinsic — as reported in later doxography.
- **lin:pramana-buddhist**: Invalidity is intrinsic and validity extrinsic — as reported in the same doxographic scheme (Buddhist sources themselves are more differentiated).
- Candidate readings:
  - P2-standpoint: the Mīmāṃsaka thesis may describe the default acceptance of a cognition when no doubt has arisen, the Naiyāyika thesis the justification of validity once doubt arises.
  - P2-standpoint: both distinguish the arising (utpatti) from the ascertainment (jñapti) of validity; agreement may be possible on the first and not the second.
- Note: Not yet reconciled: the schools disagree about how validity is established.

- **lin:pramana-buddhist**: No: only the parts (ultimately momentary particulars) are real; the 'whole' is a conceptual construction over an aggregate.
- Candidate readings:
  - P2-standpoint: the Jain distinction of a standpoint of the enduring whole (dravyārthika) from one of parts and modes (paryāyārthika) would treat the two as partial views.
  - P1-level: Buddhist two-truth doctrine allows wholes conventional (not ultimate) reality.

## RQ-127 — May (or should) a widow follow her husband onto the pyre?  
`dsp:widow-anvarohana` · skeleton

- **lin:dharmasastra**: The widow's dharma is lifelong chastity and restraint (MDh 5.156–160); Medhātithi (on Manu) holds that following the husband in death is self-destruction, not dharma, comparable to a rite performed to harm.
- **lin:dharmasastra**: It is permitted as an option: chastity or ascending the pyre (Viṣṇusmṛti 25.14); the Mitākṣarā (on YS 1.86) defends it as a meritorious optional act.
- Candidate readings:
  - Medhātithi's Mīmāṃsā argument that a rite motivated by desire and involving killing is not dharma
  - the Mitākṣarā's reading as an optional (kāmya) act
  - later kalivarjya-style prohibitions and the Śākta tantras' prohibition (outside this unit)
- Note: Not yet reconciled.
