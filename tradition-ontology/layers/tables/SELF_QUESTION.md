# The self-question: jīva, puruṣa, ātman, not-self (RQ-U50-01)

**Decision.** The verdict is `related-under-P2-standpoint` and the status is **not yet reconciled**. Only one standpoint passes the test: the Jain teaching of standpoints (naya). Only the Jains hold it, and the other three reject it on their own verified texts. The Jain texts that apply it to other doctrines are still skeleton, so the product shows the four side by side (`self_question.json`, `product_wording`).

## 2. The positions, each in its own verified words
1. Jain. The five kinds of state are "the soul's own nature" (TS 2.1), and the karma-rising ones include the passions and wrong view (2.6). Being a soul is inherent (2.7). The existent arises, ceases and persists (5.30). The soul contracts and expands like a lamp (5.16). The liberated are set out by number (10.9).
2. Yoga. "The seer is seeing alone; though pure, it looks on cognitions" (YS 2.20). Vyāsa: the buddhi changes and the puruṣa does not. The seen goes on for other puruṣas (2.22), and the lord of the mind is unchanging (4.18).
3. Upaniṣadic. "That you are" (ChU 6.8.7). "I am brahman" (BAU 1.4.10). The seer of seeing cannot be seen (BAU 3.4.1–2). "Not this, not this" (4.2.4).
4. Theravāda. "All dhammas are not-self" (Dhp 279). "Even oneself is not one's own" (Dhp 62). A doctrine of self is a clinging (Vism 17.p569–570). Conceit is cut at the arahat path (22.p682).

The Heart Sūtra is Mahāyāna, not Theravāda. The aggregates are empty of own-being (s3), and in emptiness there is no knowledge and no attainment (s7).

## 3. The shared minimum, corrected for the Jain side
- The body: all four deny that it is the self (TS 8.2 with 8.11; YS 2.5 with Vyāsa; ChU 8.12.1–2 and the asuras' bodily self, 8.7.4–8.8.5; Dhp 279 with Vism 17.p570).
- The changing states: the Yoga, the Upaniṣads and the Theravāda put them outside the self or find no self in them. The Tattvārthasūtra does not. It calls them the soul's own nature (2.1) and denies only that the soul is its passing states alone (2.7, 5.30).
- So the minimum is this: none of the four takes the body, or any passing state of mind, to be what one is. Each has a practice for seeing through it (TS 9.7; YS 2.26; BAU 4.2.4; Vism 20.p638).
- Some words are shared but used in a different sense:
  - YS 2.5 and Vism 22.p683 name the same four inversions, including "not-self taken as self". The Yoga concludes that there is a seer and that the seen is mistaken for it. The Theravāda concludes that nothing is self.
  - "Not this, not this" (BAU 4.2.4) and Dhp 279 both refuse every graspable thing as the self. The Upaniṣad calls the ungraspable seer the self. The Theravāda calls nothing self.

## 4. Standpoints tested
The test has three parts: the standpoint is held by one of the four from its own texts, it relates all four, and it says more than the minimum.
- **P2, the Jain standpoints: passes.**
  - The texts: the existent arises, ceases and persists (TS 5.30, verified). Souls last as substance but not as modes (Bhagavatī 7.2). Each standpoint alone is false, and joined they are right (Sanmati, kāṇḍa 1). One-sided permanence, one-sided momentariness and absolute non-duality are all refuted (Āptamīmāṃsā ch. 3, 24–27).
  - On this standpoint, the unchanging seer, the one self and "all dhammas are not-self" are each true in one respect.
  - Every text here except TS 5.30 is skeleton.
- **P1, Gauḍapāda's levels: fails, because it relates three of the four.**
  - It is held by Advaita and its texts are verified. Non-duality is the ultimate and duality is its difference, so "this does not conflict" with the dualists (GK 3.17–18). The jīvas are the one self as pot-spaces are space (3.3).
  - It reaches the plural selves of the Yoga and the Jains, but not "all dhammas are not-self".
  - GK 4.99 says "this was not spoken by the buddha". Śaṅkara's comment on that verse says the non-dual truth was not the Buddha's teaching, but that comment is skeleton.
- **P1, the provisional row's reading: fails.** The reading is that the Buddhist negation reaches only the aggregates, never the seer or the witness. No party's text makes this reading. Dhp 279 says all dhammas, and the Heart Sūtra carries the negation into emptiness (s6–s7).
- **The Theravāda's own reading: fails.** Every doctrine of self is a clinging (Vism 17.p569–570). This covers all four, but only as views to be given up, and it falls under no reconciliation principle.
- **P4/P6 and P5/P6: fail.**
  - Haribhadra teaches one nirvāṇa under many names and teachings suited to the disciples (Yogadṛṣṭisamuccaya 129–134). This is skeleton, and it concerns the teachers and the goal, not the self.
  - The P5/P6 readings from MMK 18.6 and SN 44.10 are skeleton, and MMK is not one of the four parties.

## 5. Rule 5: what the P2 relation claims and who rejects it
- It does not claim that the four teach one thing or name one reality. It does not claim that souls are one, that there is no self, or that any party but the Jains accepts it. It is the Jain view of the others, not the ontology's verdict.
- The Theravāda rejects it. Nothing, in any respect, is self (Dhp 279, 62). The eternity view and the annihilation view are given up by seeing passing and arising (Vism 22.p694), and no substance lasting through modes is posited.
- The Yoga rejects it. The puruṣa is unchanging (YS 4.18 with Vyāsa; Vyāsa on 2.20), and what transforms is the seen (2.18). Vyāsa also argues against a mind that is only momentary (1.32).
- The Upaniṣads reject it. Being was one only, without a second (ChU 6.2.1–2), and whoever sees diversity here goes from death to death (KaU 2.1.10–11).
- Advaita rejects it. Duality is māyā (GK 1.17), and difference is censured (3.13).
- BS 2.2.33, which rejects the Jain doctrine itself, is skeleton.
