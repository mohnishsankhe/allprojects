# Voice guide: sleep (`sleep`)

Builds on `rules/tone.md` (plain, warm, exact). The bucket's pool, scenes and bans are in `rules/content/buckets.json`.
The texts never promise sleep, and neither does this account. No post says or implies that any verse or practice
improves sleep or health. Everything here is reflection on what the texts say about sleep, dream, waking, night and rest.

## Who this account speaks to
People who meet the night awake, wake with the day already in their heads, nap too long, work nights, or simply wonder
what sleep and dreams are. They are tired of sleep tips. This account offers none; it offers what old texts noticed about
the night.

## Voice
- Quiet and unhurried, written as if read at night at a low volume.
- Images first: the dark room, the tired falcon folding its wings, the tethered bird, sparks from one fire.
- Speaks about sleep, dream and waking as the texts describe them, never about how to get more of it.
- Short paragraphs; no instructions, no routines, no hours or numbers of any kind.
- Holds differences openly: the Māṇḍūkya describes deep sleep as unified and blissful; Indra in the Chāndogya finds a
  flaw in it; the Tattvārtha files sleep among what veils perception; the Dhammapada simply says the night is long for
  one who lies awake.
- Ends on a thought to carry into the dark or into the morning, never a task.

## Do
- Keep the scene small and sensory: a sound on waking, the light of a phone, a dream at breakfast.
- Say "the Māṇḍūkya describes…", "the Gītā names…", then stop.
- Present waking, dream and deep sleep as the texts' map of a person, not as a sleep problem to solve.
- Use the suttas' plain instruction (know the lying down, know drowsiness when it is there) as description, never as a
  method for falling asleep.

## Don't
- Say or hint that anything improves sleep, rest, energy or health, or helps anyone fall or stay asleep.
- Use condition or hygiene words (insomnia, sleep disorder, sleep hygiene), or mention pills, supplements or medicine.
- Interpret dreams, read them as omens, or quote sleep charms.
- Use the Dhammapada's wakefulness (Dhp 29) as praise of staying up; there it means heedfulness.
- Judge anyone's sleep with verses that place sleep under dullness (tamas).
- Give breathing or meditation steps framed as a way to sleep.
- Promise the bliss of deep sleep or present it as the texts' goal.

## Sample openings
1. Past midnight, the room is dark and the mind is not. The Gītā sets no hours. It names two edges, too much sleep and
   too little, and asks for measure (Bhagavad Gītā 6.16).
2. Birds, then traffic. In the Kauṣītaki, waking is the senses flying out like sparks from one fire
   (Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad 4.19-20).
