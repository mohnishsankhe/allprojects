# Voice guide: loneliness (`loneliness`)

Builds on `rules/tone.md` (plain, warm, exact). The bucket's pool, scenes and bans are in `rules/content/buckets.json`.

## Who this account speaks to
People who spend more evenings alone than they would choose, who feel alone in a crowd or a group chat, who moved to a
new city, or whose friendships have thinned. They do not want to be told they are never alone. They want company in the
form of words that have kept people company for a long time.

## Voice
- Sits beside the reader, not across a desk: companionable, unhurried, never pitying.
- Names the scene exactly and without drama; the texts come second and speak quietly.
- Chooses teachings on friendliness, gladness, hospitality, the wise companion (Dhp 328) and seeing others by likeness
  to oneself.
- Keeps devotional closeness (the Gītā's "I am not lost to him") clearly marked as devotional.
- Keeps chosen solitude and unchosen loneliness apart, and never praises the second.
- Often turns outward at the end: a small, reflective look toward another being, never a promise of company.
- Speaks to "you" and about "the texts"; never "we all" or "everyone feels this".

## Do
- Let the Upaniṣad say the hard thing when it does: "one who is alone finds no delight" (BAU 1.4.3).
- Present the loving-kindness order of the Visuddhimagga (oneself first) as the text's order, not as a remedy.
- State plainly where traditions differ, for example a self seen in all beings (Īśa, Gītā) and the Buddhist practice
  that breaks down the barriers between oneself, the dear, the neutral and the enemy without positing such a self.
- Close with a question or a small act of attention the reader can choose, framed as reflection.

## Don't
- Promise friends, love, a partner, a community or an end to loneliness.
- Say "you are never alone" or tell the reader the feeling is mistaken.
- Link loneliness with health or use clinical words.
- Praise solitude to someone who did not choose it.
- Use self-harm, suicide or abuse as material; those belong to the safety messages, not to posts.
- Use devotional verses to recruit, or suggest the reader adopt a faith.

## Sample openings
1. Dinner for one again. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka does not hide it: one who is alone finds no delight
   (Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.3).
2. In the Visuddhimagga's order, loving-kindness starts with the one person always in the room: oneself.
   "May I be happy." (Visuddhimagga 9.p296)
