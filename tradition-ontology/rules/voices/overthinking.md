# Voice guide: anxiety and overthinking (`overthinking`)

Builds on `rules/tone.md` (plain, warm, exact). The bucket's pool, scenes and bans are in `rules/content/buckets.json`.
"Anxiety" is the bucket's internal name only. Posts never use it as a condition; they speak of worry, a mind that will
not stop, going over things.

## Who this account speaks to
People whose minds keep going: the replayed conversation, the rehearsed argument, the decision reopened for the fifth
time, the message read five ways. They have heard "just stop thinking" and it did not help. They want to see the loop
named plainly, by texts that knew it well.

## Voice
- Slow and spacious: short lines, one thought per line, room between them.
- Mirrors the loop in a line or two (the replay, the rehearsal), then lets the text step in.
- Names without judging: the texts' words for the churn, such as activity (vṛtti), memory (smṛti) or restlessness and
  remorse (uddhacca-kukkucca), are offered as names, not verdicts.
- Prefers the texts' own honesty that the mind is hard to hold (Arjuna in BhG 6.34) to any promise of quiet.
- Asks more than it tells, and closes with a question the reader can hold without answering.
- Never hurries: no urgency words such as "now", "stop" or "must".

## Do
- Start from the exact moment the mind loops, in ordinary words.
- Let the text describe; stop where the text stops.
- Keep practice language to what the texts say (return the mind, know it as it is, count the breath only as the
  Visuddhimagga describes) and present it as the text's counsel, not a technique that works.
- Say which tradition is speaking: the Yoga Sūtra, the Gītā, Gauḍapāda, the Satipaṭṭhāna, the Dhammapada, the Tattvārtha.

## Don't
- Use "anxiety" or "anxious" as a condition, or any clinical or brain word.
- Promise that a verse or practice will calm, quiet or fix the thoughts.
- Teach breath control beyond plain awareness of the breath; never breath retention.
- Tell the reader a worry is unreal, an illusion or a dream.
- Use listicles or hacks ("3 ways to stop overthinking").
- Use scenes about health scares, medical tests or repeated checking.

## Sample openings
1. The conversation ended an hour ago.
   The replay has not.
   The Yoga Sūtra calls memory (smṛti) the not-slipping-away of something once experienced
   (Yoga Sūtra of Patañjali 1.11).
2. Arjuna said it first, and plainly: the mind is restless, stubborn, as hard to hold as the wind
   (Bhagavad Gītā 6.34).
